## 中文

当你已确认将去一个新的普通工作、学习或志愿环境，而**第一天还没开始**时，加载这份 Skill。结果是一张可执行的到场卡：**哪天何时、哪里、找谁、带什么、当天先做什么**，以及未确认项的求证路径。它不处理劳动权利、背景调查、证件真伪或专业岗位操作；这些遵循该组织正式说明。

### 准备与输入

拿出组织方最近一次正式通知，不靠转述的旧消息猜地点。核对名称、日期和当地时区/时间格式；有远程首日时确认会议入口与实际可用设备。写下自己的交通、行动、感官、餐食及休息需求，只有必要信息才向联系人说明，不用向陌生群聊公开私人情况。

### 执行

1. **建一张到场卡。** 写日期、准确时间、地址或远程入口、**具体入口/报到点**、接待人或部门、联系电话/已约定消息渠道，以及离开前该做什么。不要把地图上的建筑名当作接待地点。
2. **从官方消息核当日安排。** 看集合、介绍、培训、第一件任务、休息与结束时间是否已有说明；空白就写“待确认”，不自行编一个日程。不同组织给的表格或培训不同，不按别人的清单补自家要求。
3. **只准备明确要求的材料。** 按通知核要携带的普通文具、设备、笔记本或已明确要求的文件。证件、表格、健康/背景材料等只有组织明确要求时才按其安全提交方式处理；本篇不列“所有新单位都要带”的敏感材料清单。
4. **把缺口合并成一次短问询。** 对联系人的正式渠道问两三个会实质影响到场的问题，例如“请确认从哪个门入、找哪位、首日需要自带电脑吗？”若已有答复，更新到场卡并记来源。无人回复时保留未确认项，按已知正式地址和入口信息行动；入口/时间缺失到无法到场时继续联系而非猜。
5. **做一次现实演练。** 根据你自己的行动与交通条件预留到达余量，确认可访问入口、远程设备或替代联系方式。结束前检查卡能在需要时打开，别把唯一地址存在当天可能没网的链接里。

### 完成、常见问题与补救

你有一张含到场时间、位置、联系人、明确材料与首日已知安排的卡；关键未知项已发给正确联系人或明确标待确认。表格写满不等于组织方真的确认。

- **两封通知地点不同：** 按日期和发件组织核最新有效信息，向联系人确认冲突，不任选一封。
- **只有建筑地址没有入口：** 问报到点；若赶不上回复，保留官方总机/接待处联系方式并给找路余量。
- **没有材料清单：** 带普通低风险用品即可，涉及证件或私人材料先问正式渠道，不把猜测当要求。
- **远程链接当天可能失效：** 核主持人与备用联系办法；没有授权不另找陌生链接。
- **行动、听力、视力或饮食需要影响首日：** 提前向适当联系人提出具体可执行需求；不以“大家都一样”掩盖入口或形式限制。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

单位、学校和志愿组织的安排不同；本篇提供**核对问题**，不替组织发布入职、录取或培训要求。纸、可访问电子笔记或语音都能承载到场卡。准备完成后实际到场仍要看当天通知与交通情况；改动及时更新卡，不把旧截图当最终安排。

## English

Load this Skill after you have a confirmed first day in an ordinary new work, study, or volunteer setting and **before it begins**. Produce an arrival card: **when, where, whom to meet, what to bring, and what happens first**, plus a route for unresolved details. This does not decide employment rights, background checks, document validity, or specialist work; follow the organisation's official instructions for those.

### Preparation and inputs

Use the organiser's most recent official notice rather than an old forwarded message. Check name, date, and local time zone or time format. For a remote first day, check the meeting entry and a device that actually works. Note your travel, mobility, sensory, food, and break needs. Share only necessary information with the appropriate contact, not a public group chat.

### Execution

1. **Make an arrival card.** Record date, exact time, address or remote entry, **specific entrance or check-in point**, host or department, phone or agreed message route, and what you need before leaving. A building pin on a map is not necessarily the reception point.
2. **Check the day plan against official messages.** Look for assembly, introductions, training, first task, breaks, and end time. Mark blank parts “to confirm” instead of inventing a schedule. Organisations require different forms and training; do not import someone else's checklist.
3. **Prepare only requested materials.** Check the notice for ordinary stationery, equipment, notes, or files it explicitly names. Bring ID, forms, health, or background material only when formally requested and use the stated secure submission method. This Skill does not claim every new place needs the same sensitive documents.
4. **Send one short bundled clarification.** Through the official contact route ask two or three questions that materially affect arrival, such as “Which door and person should I report to, and must I bring a computer?” Update the card with actual replies and source. Without a reply, keep unknowns visible and use confirmed address and entry details. If time or entrance remains too unclear to arrive, keep contacting rather than guessing.
5. **Make a practical rehearsal.** Allow arrival margin for your travel and mobility conditions. Check an accessible entrance, remote device, or backup contact. Ensure the card opens when needed; do not leave the only address inside a link you may not be able to load that morning.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

You have a card with arrival time, place, contact, requested materials, and known first-day plan. You have sent important unknowns to the right contact or marked them to confirm. A filled card is not proof the organiser confirmed every field.

- **Two notices disagree on location:** Check date and organiser and ask for a resolution instead of choosing a random one.
- **Only a building address is given:** Ask for the check-in point. If no reply comes in time, keep the official reception contact and leave wayfinding margin.
- **No materials list exists:** Bring ordinary low-risk supplies; ask formally before carrying private documents on a guess.
- **A remote link may fail on the day:** Confirm host and a backup contact rather than finding an unauthorised replacement link.
- **Mobility, hearing, vision, or food needs affect the day:** Ask the appropriate contact for a concrete usable arrangement. A generic “everyone does this” instruction may not fit the actual entrance or format.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Employers, schools, and volunteer groups differ. This is a set of **questions to verify**, not their hiring, enrolment, or training policy. Paper, an accessible note, or audio can hold the card. Actual arrival still depends on current notice and travel conditions; update changes rather than treating an old screenshot as final.
