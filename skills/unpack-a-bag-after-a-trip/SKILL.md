## 中文

普通旅行回家后，你的包里混着食品、潮湿或待洗物、要马上用的东西和不急着处理的物品时，用这份 Skill **先按时效交给正确去向，再清空并收好包**。结束不是“把箱子挪到角落”；每件东西要么归位，要么进入一个有负责人与下一步的处理队列。它不替代食品安全判断、洗涤设置或损坏维修。

### 准备与输入

把包放在不会挡通道的稳定位置，确认一共有几件包、是否有寄存或同行者代管的包还没回家。准备四个去向：**现在处理、待洗/晾干、普通归位、待决定且写明原因**。没有收纳盒可用纸条或四块清楚的桌面区域。先想起一路有没有带需冷藏食品、漏液瓶、湿衣、游泳用品、临时借物或很快要再次使用的物品；具体内容以打开包后的实见为准。

### 执行

1. **先开所有包的易取层。** 数包件并看外袋、手提袋与箱顶，不只打开最大的箱子。把易腐食品和可能漏液、潮湿的物品先找出来；不要为了整理纪念品让它们继续闷在包里。对温度或停留时间不清的易腐食品，不凭“摸着还凉”断言安全，按可靠食品安全说明处理，必要时弃置。
2. **把湿物从干物和包体分开。** 湿衣、毛巾、鞋或沾水的袋子放到可晾、可按标签清洗的地方；查看包内衬和相邻物品是否也潮。先按衣物护理标签及现有洗衣流程分组，不把所有材质一股脑塞进机器。若包体湿了，打开并在合适位置尽快晾干后再收起；不要把潮湿包扣上拉链当完成。
3. **逐件清空并标去向。** 常用物放回平时真正能找到的位置；待洗物进洗衣流程，需进一步处理的物品写下“谁、做什么、何时再看”，不要无说明地扔进一个‘以后’袋。借来的普通物品标明应归还的对象与方式，不把它们误收成自己的。电子设备或材料若有可见损坏、漏液或异常，不自行拆修，另找适当的处理流程。
4. **对照下一次生活节点检查。** 看今晚/明早要用的钥匙、眼镜、充电器、工作用品是否已回到可用位置；同时数空包和拆出的待处理堆，确认没有遗留外袋、拉链夹层或随身小包。若少了一件普通物品，先按旅途最后实际持有处追查，不用“应该在包里”代替寻找。
5. **收尾并保留真实待办。** 清理包内可见碎屑，按材质说明让包保持干燥后归位；不对未知材料上强清洁剂。现在来不及全部洗或归位时，先完成易腐/湿物等时效项，再把剩余待办集中写在一处并定下下一次处理时点。一个满包改成三个无标签的袋子，不是部署成功。

### 成功条件

返程包的各层已实际清空并核对；易腐食品按可信规则处理、湿物离开封闭包体、常用物归位，其他东西有可追踪去向。所有包体干燥或明确处于晾干状态。若有未到家的包或不确定的物品，不把总拆包写成已完成，而是记下谁持有和下一次确认点。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只拆大箱，手提袋还藏着食物：** 重新按包件数检查每个外袋、随身包和寄存取回件。
- **湿泳衣被留在密闭袋里：** 立即与干物分开，按护理标签晾干或清洗；检查接触到的包内衬。
- **不知道冷藏食品在常温待了多久：** 不用气味或触感猜安全，按来源提供的安全条件判断；条件无法确认时不要把它当安全食物保存。
- **待处理堆越堆越大：** 每项写清动作与下一核对时点；没有动作的物品回到真实归位决定，不新增一个永久旅行箱。
- **自己的包少一件，可能在旅程中丢了：** 划出最后确定持有的时间与地点，转入正式失物追查；拆包清单不能证明物品曾经随包回家。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

本 Skill 只做普通物品返家的分流和包体收尾。食品的安全条件由实际种类、温度与时间决定；美国 USDA 对常温易腐食品提出两小时规则、高于 32°C 时一小时，但本文不凭未知旅途温度宣布食品可食。EPA 建议湿材料尽快干燥以降低霉菌风险；具体衣物按洗护标签。没有洗衣机、空间或体力时，先把湿物从密闭包里移到可安全晾放处，再安排可用的洗护协助。特殊化学品、医药品、危险物和设备维修不进入本流程。

## English

Use this Skill after an ordinary trip when the bags mix food, damp or dirty items, things you need soon, and items that can wait. **Route time-sensitive contents first, then empty and put away the bags.** Finish with every item either in its real place or in a task with an owner and next step. Moving the case to a corner is not unpacking. This does not make a food-safety decision for you, choose wash settings, or repair damage.

### Preparation and inputs

Put bags on a stable surface that does not block a route. Count all bags, including any still held by a storage service or companion. Set up four destinations: **act now, laundry/drying, ordinary home place, and a named decision pending**. Paper labels or four clear surface areas work without organizers. Recall whether the trip included chilled food, leaking containers, wet clothes, swimwear, borrowed items, or something needed soon. The actual contents you see override memory.

### Execution

1. **Open every bag's accessible layer first.** Count bags and inspect outside pockets, totes, and case tops, not just the largest case. Remove perishables and possible leaks or damp items before sorting souvenirs. For perishable food with unknown temperature or time, do not infer safety from “it still feels cool.” Apply reliable food-safety guidance, discarding when its conditions are not met or cannot be established.
2. **Separate damp from dry and from the bag itself.** Move wet clothes, towels, shoes, or pouches to a place where they can dry or be cleaned under their care labels. Check the lining and neighboring items for moisture. Sort clothes under their actual labels and your existing laundry process; do not send every fabric into one wash. If the bag is wet, leave it open in a suitable place to dry before storage. Closing a damp case is not a completed task.
3. **Empty item by item and mark destinations.** Put daily-use objects back where you can really find them. Route laundry into its process. For anything pending, write “who, what, when to check,” instead of sending it to an unnamed someday bag. Label a borrowed ordinary item with its owner and return route. For visible damage, leakage, or abnormal behavior in equipment or materials, do not take it apart; use an appropriate separate process.
4. **Check the next ordinary life node.** Confirm keys, glasses, charger, work items, or other things needed tonight or tomorrow are usable and reachable. Count empty cases and the pending piles, then check outside pockets, zipped layers, and small carry bags. If an ordinary item is missing, trace its last confirmed possession on the trip; “it should be in the bag” is not a search result.
5. **Close out honestly.** Remove visible crumbs from the bag and let it dry as its materials allow before putting it away; do not use a strong cleaner on an unknown material. If you cannot finish all washing or storage now, complete perishable and damp priorities first, then keep remaining tasks in one visible list with a next action time. Turning one full case into three unlabelled bags is not a successful deployment.

### Success

Every layer of every returned bag has been emptied and checked. Perishables have been handled under reliable rules; wet items are out of closed bags; daily-use things are home; the rest has a traceable destination. Bags are dry or explicitly drying. For a bag still held elsewhere or an uncertain item, record who holds it and the next check; do not mark the whole unpacking complete.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Only the large case was emptied; food remains in a tote:** Recheck every outer pocket, carry bag, and retrieved storage item against the bag count.
- **Wet swimwear remained sealed:** Separate it from dry goods now, dry or wash under its care label, and check the bag lining it touched.
- **The time chilled food spent warm is unknown:** Do not use smell or feel as proof of safety. Apply the source's safety conditions; if they cannot be established, do not store it as safe food.
- **The pending pile keeps growing:** Give each item an action and next check time. If no action exists, make a real home-placement decision instead of creating permanent suitcase storage.
- **An item is missing from your bags:** Identify the last time and place you definitely had it and move to a formal lost-property task. The unpacking list does not prove it arrived home.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This routes ordinary trip contents back into home processes and closes out the bags. Food safety depends on food type, temperature, and time. The US USDA gives a two-hour room-temperature limit for perishables, reduced to one hour above 32°C, but unknown journey conditions are not proof that food is safe. The US EPA advises prompt drying of wet materials to reduce mold risk; clothing care follows its own label. Without a washer, space, or enough strength, first move damp items out of closed bags to a safe drying place, then arrange available laundry help. Chemicals, medical items, dangerous goods, and equipment repair are outside this flow.
