## 中文

当你有标题、主图和一条补充信息等**几个已有要素**，却不知它们放在同一张小纸上时人会先看到什么，用这份 Skill 用纸笔安排**第一、第二、第三观看点**。完成物是可解释的版面草图，不是替你写文案、做最终海报，也不是只围绕一个主体画留白缩略图。

### 准备与输入

先列出真正要放的 3–4 个要素及用途，例如标题、主视觉、时间/说明；本篇只安排，不核实事实、费用或截止日期。若内容涉及安全、医疗、法律、财务等关键决策，改用相应专业设计与审核，别靠一次小草图声称信息可靠。确定预定观众的语言方向、可见尺寸和观看方式；左到右或上到下并非所有人统一。用纸画接近最终比例的框，必要时放大框、用高对比笔或触觉方块；不复制别人的付费版式或受保护图像。

### 执行

1. **排内容优先级。** 在要素旁标 1、2、3（多一项可标 4），分别回答“首先知道什么、随后理解什么、最后补什么”。若标题和主图都想当 1，选一个作为入口，另一个作为接续；不让所有东西同时喊话。
2. **只用块来布局。** 在纸框里用大小不同的方块放要素，不先抄长文或画精图。让第 1 项在预定观看条件下足够显眼，第 2 项能顺着位置或对比被找到，第 3 项仍可读；边缘和要素之间留空间，避免互相压住。
3. **追踪一次视线。** 用笔在块间画出你期望的 1→2→3 路径，然后盖住数字从预定阅读方向看：眼睛真会先到 1 吗？如果第 3 项抢先、第二项被隔开，就改一处大小、位置或间距。箭头是内部测试记号，不一定成为最终作品的一部分。
4. **检查小尺寸。** 把草图放到接近实际观看距离；当所有块都挤成一团，不要只把文字缩得更小。删次要项、分到下一张或放大载体，直到主要信息有足够位置。也保留给手写、印刷或触觉标记的真实空间。
5. **记录选择与疑点。** 写一句“我让____先出现，因为____；之后到____”。若有真实愿意的目标读者，可请其说第一眼看到什么；没有就只写“自检，尚无人读”，不假称人群体验已验收。保留草图供正式制作。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你有一张接近目标比例、能指明至少三个要素的优先次序与位置的纸图；要素之间可分辨，你能说明最重要的一处安排。若只能通过念出旁边的数字才知道顺序，布局本身还需调整。

- **每项都很大：** 按用途只留一项主入口，其他缩到仍可读而不争第一；必要时拆成两张。
- **空白被当成浪费：** 给要素之间留明确间距，让边界和次序可辨；无需填满每个角。
- **多语言或阅读方向改变：** 为实际观众重新测试顺序，不把一版简单镜像就称为验收。
- **纸图看着好，实际尺寸过小：** 按最终载体重画更接近尺寸的框；装不下就减内容，不靠微小字体硬塞。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这适用于个人小卡、普通展示页或手作说明，不替代专业信息设计、无障碍审核或公共服务告示。观看习惯和语言、视觉能力因人而异；纸上的路径是计划，真实观众可能从别处开始。可用大纸、粗线、可移动纸块或可信协助规划；你仍决定内容权重。产出是版面地图，正式内容制作与验证仍要做。

## English

Use this Skill when you already have several elements—perhaps a title, main image, and one supporting detail—but do not know what someone will notice first on a small page. Arrange a **first, second, and third viewing point** with paper and pencil. The result is an explainable layout sketch, not copywriting, a finished poster, or a thumbnail devoted only to one subject and its open space.

### Preparation and inputs

List the 3–4 real elements and their jobs, such as title, main visual, and time or explanation. This Skill arranges them; it does not verify facts, prices, or deadlines. If information drives a safety, medical, legal, or financial decision, use appropriate professional design and review instead of calling a small sketch reliable. Define the intended audience's reading direction, viewing size, and surface; left to right and top to bottom are not universal. Draw a frame roughly matching the final proportions. Use a larger frame, high-contrast marks, or tactile blocks if needed. Do not copy someone else's paid layout or protected imagery.

### Execution

1. **Rank the content.** Mark elements 1, 2, 3 (and perhaps 4): what should be known first, understood next, and added last? If title and picture both demand rank 1, choose an entry and a follow-on. Every element cannot shout first.
2. **Place blocks only.** Put differently sized blocks in the page frame without copying long text or drawing a full image yet. Make item 1 noticeable under planned viewing conditions, item 2 findable through position or contrast, and item 3 still readable. Leave space at edges and between blocks so they do not collide.
3. **Trace an eye path once.** Draw your intended 1→2→3 route, then cover the numbers and view from the intended reading direction. Do you really reach 1 first? If item 3 jumps ahead or 2 is isolated, change one size, position, or gap. The arrows are test marks, not necessarily part of the finished page.
4. **Check the real scale.** View the sketch at roughly the actual distance. If everything becomes cramped, do not only shrink the lettering. Remove a secondary item, move it to another page, or enlarge the surface until major content has room. Leave real space for handwriting, print, or tactile marks as relevant.
5. **Record the choice and uncertainty.** Write “I make ____ appear first because ____; then ____.” If a willing intended reader is available, ask what they noticed first. Otherwise label it “self-check; no reader yet,” not an accepted audience experience. Keep the map for making the final work.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You have a paper map near the intended proportions, with positions and priorities for at least three elements. They remain distinguishable, and you can explain the key layout choice. If only reading the written numbers reveals the order, revise the layout itself.

- **Everything is huge:** Keep one main entry and reduce other items only while they remain readable; split the page if needed.
- **Empty space feels wasted:** Leave intentional gaps so boundaries and sequence stay legible; filling every corner is not the goal.
- **Language or reading direction changes:** Retest for the actual viewers rather than call a mirrored version accepted.
- **The sketch works but real size is too small:** Redraw a frame nearer the final size. Reduce content instead of packing in tiny type.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This suits a personal card, ordinary display page, or craft instruction. It does not replace professional information design, accessibility review, or public service signs. Viewing habits, language, and sight vary; a paper path is a plan and a real reader may start elsewhere. Use large paper, bold lines, movable blocks, or trusted help while keeping the content priorities yours. The output is a layout map; final making and validation remain.
