## 中文

当你对一个新爱好感兴趣，却已经开始比较“终极套装”时，加载这份 Skill。先测试你是否喜欢核心动作，再决定买什么。拥有装备能证明你完成了支付，不能证明你喜欢这件事。

本 Skill 只适合低风险尝试。攀登、潜水、射击、飞行、高速/高空运动、危险化学品、重型工具等需要合格教练、场地和专用安全规范，不在这里自助启动。

### 准备与输入

确认：

- 你真正好奇的是什么：画、弹、种、拍、做、观察、收集，还是与人一起活动；
- 这个爱好的最小核心动作；
- 你愿意投入的试用时间、预算和储存空间；
- 能否借用、租用、参加体验课、使用公共设施或买二手基础品；
- 若借用、租用或去公共设施试用：你是否有资格、会不会另付押金/耗材费、是否有人教安全起步、何时何地归还；
- 行动、视觉、听觉、感官、过敏、场地和交通条件；
- 取消、退还或不继续的成本。

先写一个试用上限，例如“三次，每次四十分钟，累计支出不超过你预先写下的金额”。金额用你实际支付的币种和预算，不由本文语言决定。它是防止兴趣验证意外升级为采购系统的资源限制。

### 执行

1. **定义核心动作。** 摄影是观察和取景，不是先研究十支镜头；绘画是落笔和看结果，不是先拥有完整色号。用一句话写出你实际上要做什么。
2. **删去展示性依赖。** 区分“没有就无法安全开始”的必需品与“看起来像认真爱好者”的升级品。只保留前者。
3. **选最小可逆入口。** 优先顺序通常是：使用已有物品、借、公共设施/体验、租、买便宜或二手基础品，最后才是新全套。借/租之前核该机会的资格、耗材、费用、归还条件和教学支持；若你不会安全操作而现场不教，就换有指导的低风险体验，不借设备硬试。卫生、尺寸和安全装备不能因省钱被忽略。
4. **安排一到三次具体尝试。** 写进日历，给每次一个可完成任务，例如“学三个和弦”“画一个杯子”“照顾一盆容易存活的植物七天”。不要把“看教程”算作全部运行时间。
5. **第一次只学安全启动和核心循环。** 找可靠入门说明，完成准备—动作—收尾。遇到需要专业监督的步骤就停，不把勇气当认证。
6. **每次只记录三件事。**
   - 哪一段让你愿意继续；
   - 哪一段只是陌生，哪一段确实不适合；
   - 哪个具体限制来自装备、教学、环境或身体条件。
7. **第二次修一个瓶颈。** 如果握持、噪音、光线、空间、语言或节奏不适合，先换可访问方法或环境，不立刻用高价装备诊断所有问题。
8. **试用结束再做决策。** 选择继续、暂停、换一种相邻爱好或停止。继续时只买能解除已观察瓶颈的下一件东西。

### 成功条件

你实际完成至少一次核心动作，并能区分“喜欢做”“喜欢研究”“喜欢买”和“只是不想浪费已经花的钱”。你有下一次安排，或一个无需辩护的停止决定。

### 常见报错与补救

- **ERR_GEAR_BEFORE_EXPERIENCE：** 把购物车保存但不付款，先安排一次借用、体验或基础版本。
- **教程看了很多，手没开始：** 选一个十到四十分钟的最小作品/动作，在下一个教程前完成。
- **第一次很笨拙：** 把“新手摩擦”与疼痛、危险或不可访问区分。陌生可以再试；疼痛和危险要停止并调整。
- **体验课不友好：** 换老师、场地、时间或自定节奏版本。一个入口不好不等于爱好本身不可用。
- **已经买多了：** 在退货期内检查政策并退掉未使用的非必需品；无法退时停止追加，不用更多购买证明前面的购买正确。
- **没有继续的兴趣：** 正常结束，归还借品、妥善处理已有材料。停止是完整结果，不是运行崩溃。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

租借、二手、公共设施和体验课并非每个地区都可用；价格、卫生和无障碍条件也不同。没有这些选项时，选最小安全基础品并设置转售/转赠路径。体力、感官或社交负荷不合适时，可以改变节奏、工具、环境或选择相邻活动。

现实副作用是你可能真正得到一个爱好，也可能只得到一次试验和省下的一整套装备。两者都比在家存放未加载依赖更有信息量。

## English

Load this Skill when a new hobby interests you but research has already reached “ultimate setup” comparisons. Test whether you enjoy the core action before deciding what to buy. Owning equipment proves payment completed; it does not prove you like the activity.

This covers low-risk trials only. Climbing, diving, shooting, aviation, high-speed/high-altitude sport, hazardous chemicals, and heavy tools require qualified instruction, facilities, and dedicated safety rules.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm:

- what actually interests you: drawing, playing, growing, photographing, making, observing, collecting, or participating with people;
- the smallest core action;
- trial time, budget, and storage you accept;
- borrowing, renting, a taster class, public facilities, or used starter options;
- for a loan, rental, or public trial: eligibility, deposit or consumable costs, safe-start instruction, and when and where to return items;
- mobility, vision, hearing, sensory, allergy, space, and transport needs;
- the cost of cancellation, return, or stopping.

Set a trial ceiling such as “three sessions, forty minutes each, within a spending cap you write in advance.” Use the currency you will actually pay and a budget you accept; the language of this file does not set either. This resource limit keeps interest testing from silently migrating into a procurement system.

### Execution

1. **Define the core action.** Photography is observing and framing before comparing ten lenses. Drawing starts with making marks and seeing results before owning every colour. Write one sentence describing what you will actually do.
2. **Remove display dependencies.** Separate what is necessary to start safely from what mainly signals “serious hobbyist.” Keep the first list.
3. **Choose the smallest reversible entry.** A useful order is existing item, borrow, public/taster access, rent, cheap or used starter, then new full-price gear. Before borrowing or renting, check this option's eligibility, consumables, charges, return terms, and instruction. If you cannot operate it safely and nobody will teach you, choose a guided low-risk trial instead of borrowing gear to experiment blindly. Do not compromise hygiene, fit, or required safety equipment.
4. **Schedule one to three real trials.** Give each a finishable task: learn three chords, draw one cup, or keep one beginner plant for a week. Watching tutorials is preparation, not the entire runtime.
5. **Learn only safe startup and the core loop first.** Use a reliable beginner source and complete setup, action, and cleanup. Stop where qualified supervision is required; confidence is not certification.
6. **Record three things after each attempt.**
   - What made you want another session?
   - What was merely unfamiliar, and what genuinely did not fit?
   - Which exact limit came from gear, teaching, environment, or access?
7. **Repair one bottleneck on the second attempt.** Change grip, noise, light, space, language, pace, or environment before using expensive gear to diagnose every problem.
8. **Decide after the trial.** Continue, pause, choose an adjacent hobby, or stop. If continuing, buy only the next item that solves an observed bottleneck.

### Success

You performed the core action at least once and can distinguish liking doing, liking research, liking shopping, and disliking sunk cost. You have a next session or a stop decision that needs no defence.

### Common errors and recovery

- **ERR_GEAR_BEFORE_EXPERIENCE:** Save the cart without paying and schedule one borrowed, rented, public, or starter attempt.
- **Many tutorials, no action:** Pick a ten-to-forty-minute smallest work or movement and finish it before the next tutorial.
- **The first attempt feels clumsy:** Separate beginner friction from pain, danger, or inaccessibility. Unfamiliarity can be retried; pain and danger require stopping and adjustment.
- **The class or group is unwelcoming:** Change teacher, venue, time, or use a self-paced route. One bad entry point does not define the activity.
- **You already bought too much:** Check the return window and return unused nonessential items. If returns are impossible, stop adding purchases; more gear cannot validate the earlier gear.
- **Interest does not continue:** Finish cleanly, return borrowed items, and responsibly move materials along. Stopping is a complete result, not a crash.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Rental, used markets, public facilities, and taster classes are not available everywhere; cost, hygiene, and accessibility vary. If none exists, choose the smallest safe starter with a resale/give-away route. Adapt pace, tools, environment, or the activity itself for physical, sensory, or social load.

Known side effects include acquiring a real hobby or acquiring one experiment and saving the cost of a full setup. Both provide more information than storing unloaded dependencies at home.
