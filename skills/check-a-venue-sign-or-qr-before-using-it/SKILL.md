## 中文

你在普通公共场所看到指向地图、开放信息或访客指南的牌子/二维码，想用它决定下一步时，用这份 Skill判断它是否属于**场所管理方可核实的信息入口**。目标是从官方可核对页面或现场文字取得普通导向，或改用纸图/服务台；不替你在扫码页面登录账号、输入验证码或付款。一个贴着漂亮徽标的码也可以被别人覆盖，外观不是授权证明。

### 准备与输入

先明确你要查什么：楼层、开放入口、服务时间、排队位置等普通信息。看看二维码是印在管理方正式标牌上，还是松动/覆盖原码的贴纸，旁边有没有可读文字、场所名和其他官方指引。找一个与该码独立的来源，例如场所官网、入口柜台、工作人员或纸质地图。若手机没网、相机打不开、视觉/阅读不方便，直接走文字或人员渠道，不把扫码视为必需手续。

### 执行

1. **先读现场文字和牌面。** 核对场所名称、位置、用途与当前开放标识是否一致。看到覆盖式贴纸、陌生收费提示、急迫“马上扫码否则失效”或与旁边牌面冲突时先不扫，问正式人员。不要自行撕掉别人的贴纸来验证真假。
2. **扫码只看预览，不立即按提示操作。** 使用设备自带相机或可信扫码功能，先看打开前显示的完整网址/应用名，核域名拼写与真实归属；缩短链接、与场所无关的域名、相似字母和网页里再次跳转都需要更多确认。二维码把你带到第三方平台并非一定假：先从场所自己官网或正式人员确认它确实指定该平台，例如官方导览页可能链接到外部应用。
3. **只取本次普通信息。** 网址与场所独立来源一致、内容也符合信息牌用途时，读取所需楼层/时间/路线，再拿现场标识复核。若页面忽然要求账户密码、一次性验证码、身份证照、银行卡或付款，停止这份普通信息读取；若场所确有付费或认证服务，用其正式渠道另核，不在可疑码上试凭据。
4. **不确定就退回非扫码路径。** 找纸图、入口目录、管理方网站的自行搜索入口或服务台，说明“我只需〔普通信息〕，这个二维码是不是你们当前入口？” 用取得的信息判断下一步，但不让陌生网页覆盖现场关闭/限制标志。发现疑似覆盖码时按该场所/设施正式渠道报告其位置，不公开他人的扫码内容或自己账户资料。

### 成功条件

你通过场所可独立核对的入口读到了与现场一致的普通信息，或因为来源不明而安全改用纸图/工作人员并知道下一步。仅扫出一个能打开的网站或见到正确 Logo，不足以证明它属于管理方。

### 常见报错与补救

- **二维码是新贴在旧牌上的：** 不靠猜测扫码，找管理方现场人员/官网独立确认并报告位置。
- **预览网址看起来有品牌字样：** 检查真正的域名和拼写，不把 `official-name.example.com` 一段字当成场所官网证明。
- **官方场馆页确实导向第三方应用：** 从场馆自己的正式页面/人员核对该合作，而不是因为域名不同就盲断真伪。
- **页面突然要付款或登录：** 停止本次导向信息步骤，不给密码/验证码/卡号；改用正式服务台核实是否另有真正业务流程。
- **设备没网或无法扫描：** 用现场文字、纸图或有标识工作人员完成普通信息确认，不向路人交手机代扫。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

本 Skill 只处理可退出公共场所的普通信息牌/二维码来源核实，不提供支付验证、账号恢复、网络取证或诈骗后财务处理。FTC 与纽约交通局的提醒说明覆盖式假码确实存在；英国图书馆示例说明合法场馆也可能链接到外部导览平台，因此要从管理方独立核。网站和合作方会变，当前场所才有权说明真实入口。纸图和服务台是可及替代，跳过一个不明 QR 比把凭据填给它更可控。

## English

Use this Skill when a sign or QR at an ordinary public venue appears to lead to a map, opening information, or visitor guide and you want to use it for your next move. Verify that it belongs to an **information route independently confirmed by the venue manager**. Finish with ordinary directions from a checkable official page or on-site text, or use a paper map/staff desk. This does not log you in, submit one-time codes, or pay. A neat logo on a sticker is not authorization.

### Preparation and inputs

Decide what you need: floor, open entrance, hours, queue point, or similar ordinary information. Notice whether the code is printed on a manager's sign or is a loose sticker over another mark, with readable venue name and adjacent guidance. Find an independent source such as the venue's own site, entrance desk, identifiable staff, or paper map. With no data, camera, sight, or reading access, use text or staff directly; scanning is not a required rite.

### Execution

1. **Read the sign first.** Compare venue name, location, purpose, and current open-area notice. For an overlay sticker, unexpected payment demand, urgent “scan now or lose access,” or a contradiction with the sign, pause and ask formal staff. Do not peel a stranger's sticker to test it.
2. **Preview a scan without acting on it.** Use the built-in camera or trusted scanner and inspect the full URL or app name before opening. Check the real domain spelling and owner; a short link, unrelated domain, lookalike letters, or later redirect needs independent confirmation. A third-party destination is not automatically fake: verify from the venue's own site or staff that it intentionally uses that partner, as an official guide may use an external app.
3. **Read only the needed ordinary fact.** Once the URL matches an independent venue source and the content matches the sign's purpose, read the floor, hours, or route and cross-check on-site signs. If a page suddenly asks for password, one-time code, ID image, card, or payment, stop this ordinary information task. If a real paid or authenticated service exists, verify it separately through the venue's formal route rather than testing credentials in a suspicious scan.
4. **Use a non-QR route for uncertainty.** Find a paper map, entrance directory, independently navigated venue site, or staffed desk and ask whether the code is their current information entry point. Use the resulting directions, but do not let an unfamiliar page override a closed/restricted on-site sign. Report a suspected overlay to the venue or facility operator with its location, not your account data or a public copy of what you entered.

### Success

You read ordinary information through a venue-confirmed source consistent with current signs, or safely switched to paper/staff because ownership was unclear. A scannable site or convincing logo alone has not proved venue authority.

### Common errors and recovery

- **New QR sticker overlays an old sign:** Ask venue staff or its independently found site and report the location rather than scanning by guesswork.
- **Preview URL contains the brand's letters:** Check the actual domain and spelling; a brand-looking string inside someone else's domain is not proof.
- **Official venue site really links to a third-party guide:** Confirm the relationship from the manager's page or staff instead of assuming every external domain is bad.
- **Page suddenly asks for payment or sign-in:** Stop this information step without entering passwords, codes, or cards. Ask the formal desk about any separate real business process.
- **No connection or scanner:** Use printed text, a paper map, or identifiable staff. Do not hand your phone to a passerby for scanning.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This verifies low-risk public information signs or QRs in an escapable venue. It is not payment authentication, account recovery, network forensics, or financial response to a scam. FTC and NYC DOT document overlay-code risks, while the British Library shows that an official venue may use an external guide provider; check independently with the manager. Sites and partners can change. Paper and staff are accessible alternatives; skipping an uncertain QR is cheaper than entering credentials into it.
