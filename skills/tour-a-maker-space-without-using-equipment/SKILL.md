## 中文

当你想了解图书馆创客空间、社区手工房或类似场所能否支持自己的小兴趣，却不清楚哪儿可进、哪台设备要培训时，用这份 Skill 完成**一次不操作设备的参观**。结果是你知道一项想做的活动可否参与、需要哪些正式培训/预约/支持；参观证不等于机器操作证。

### 准备与输入

从空间官网或工作人员核开放时段、参观是否需要预约、年龄/会员资格、费用、拍照和可及条件。写下你最想了解的一种活动，例如纸艺、缝纫或数字制作，不为了这次参观列出所有机器。若空间只对特定人群开放，先确认自己符合要求；不借他人证件混入。需要无障碍通道、文字说明或安静介绍时提前询问。这里不教电动工具、激光、加热、化学或机械设备使用，设备操作只在该空间正式培训与监督下进行。

### 执行

1. **按正式入口到场。** 向前台说明“我来参观，想了解____活动”，遵守签到、访客标识和导览路线。若没有开放参观或工作人员说需预约，记正式入口并改日，不自行进工作区。
2. **分清三类区域。** 在工作人员允许范围内标出可公开看/用的区域、需培训或预约后可进入的区域、仅工作人员区域；指示不明时问，不以门开着推断自己可进。参观中手不触设备、未包装材料或别人作品。
3. **问一项活动的完整路径。** 对你选的活动逐项问：谁有资格、要先上哪种入门/安全说明、能否预约、材料和费用谁提供、第一次操作谁在场、哪些事必须交工作人员。把答复记成“已可做 / 先培训 / 暂不提供 / 待确认”，不把看到机器当已获使用权。
4. **核可及和退出办法。** 问台面高度、文字或听觉说明、辅助人员、可用时间、改约/取消渠道，以及不懂或设备异常时找谁。没有满足你需要的培训和支持时，把活动标暂缓，不自行摸索。
5. **离场并留一行下一步。** 按访客路线离开，记“想做____；本空间要求____；下次通过____正式办理”。不在现场运行设备、留下素材、拍到别人作品或替他人公开空间布局；若暂不适合，写明原因即可。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你实际走完一次获准参观，能说出至少一类公开区、一类受限区、目标活动的培训/预约或不提供状态，以及下一次正式联系人/渠道。单看网站设备列表或进门拍照不算理解使用边界。

- **工作人员忙、无法回答培训细节：** 留下官方联系渠道，标“资格待确认”，不要试开机器验证。
- **朋友说“平时随便用”：** 以场所当日工作人员和正式规则为准，不用口头传闻当授权。
- **参观区可见尖锐/热/电动工具：** 保持距离并走允许路线；本文不会把它们降成普通手作。
- **活动需要你暂不具备的资格或支持：** 改选可参与的低风险活动，或停止；不借账号、证件或安全培训记录。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

“创客空间”可以从开放纸艺台到受控设备实验室，规则差异很大；某馆允许自由使用材料，不代表另一馆允许。手部、视听、语言或行动条件可请正式导览、文字说明或可信协助，但设备权限仍由空间授予。是否参加培训或支付费用由你以后决定；本篇只交付有依据的空间使用地图，不进行高风险制作。

## English

Use this Skill when you want to know whether a library maker space, community craft room, or similar venue can support a small interest but do not know which areas are open or what training a machine requires. Make **one visit without operating equipment**. The result is knowing whether one activity is available, what formal orientation, booking, and support it needs, and the authorised next step. A visitor badge is not an equipment licence.

### Preparation and inputs

Check the space's official hours, whether tours need booking, age or membership eligibility, charges, photography, and accessibility conditions. Write down one activity you want to explore—paper craft, sewing, or digital making, for example—instead of trying to inventory every machine. Confirm you qualify if the space serves a limited group; do not borrow someone else's ID. Ask ahead about accessible routes, written explanations, or a quieter tour if needed. This Skill does not teach power, laser, heated, chemical, or mechanical equipment. Operate any such equipment only after that space's formal training and supervision.

### Execution

1. **Arrive through the official entrance.** Tell reception, “I am here to tour and learn about ____,” and follow sign-in, visitor badge, and guided route. If tours are unavailable or require a booking, note the formal route and return later rather than enter a workroom yourself.
2. **Separate three kinds of space.** Within the permitted route, identify public viewing or use areas, areas accessible only after training or booking, and staff-only areas. Ask if signs are unclear; an open door is not permission. Do not touch equipment, unpacked supplies, or other people's projects during the tour.
3. **Ask the full path for one activity.** For your chosen activity ask who qualifies, what introduction or safety briefing comes first, whether booking is needed, who supplies materials and pays charges, who supervises first use, and what must be left to staff. Note “available now / training first / not offered / unconfirmed” rather than treat a visible machine as permission.
4. **Check access and exit routes.** Ask about work-surface height, written or audio instructions, assistance, time slots, cancellation, and who to contact if instructions or equipment seem wrong. If training or support cannot meet your need, mark the activity on hold instead of experimenting alone.
5. **Leave one next step.** Exit as a visitor and note “I want to do ____; this space requires ____; next I use ____ official channel.” Do not run equipment, leave materials, photograph another person's work, or publish a layout of the workspace. “Not suitable now” with a reason is a valid result.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You completed an authorised tour and can name a public area, a restricted area, the training or booking status for your target activity, and the formal next contact or channel. Seeing an equipment list online or taking entrance photos is not understanding access boundaries.

- **Staff are busy and cannot confirm training:** Keep the official contact channel and mark eligibility unconfirmed. Do not turn on a machine to find out.
- **A friend says “people usually use it freely”:** Use today's space rules and staff guidance, not hearsay as permission.
- **Sharp, hot, or powered tools are visible:** Keep distance and stay on the visitor route; their presence does not make them an ordinary craft activity here.
- **The activity requires eligibility or support you lack:** Choose a permitted low-risk activity or stop, without borrowing an account, ID, or training record.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

A maker space may range from an open paper table to a controlled equipment lab, so one library's freely usable supplies say nothing about another's. Formal guides, written material, or trusted help may support hand, sight, hearing, language, or mobility needs, while equipment rights remain with the venue. Later training or payment is your decision. This Skill delivers an evidenced map of space permissions, not hazardous making.
