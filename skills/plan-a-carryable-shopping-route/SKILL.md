## 中文

当你已经知道要买什么，却需要去不止一处，且拎回家、换乘或冷藏要求会改变先后顺序时，加载这份 Skill。这里安排的是**采购与返家路线**；决定需求和数量可先用 [去菜市场前做计划](../plan-a-market-trip/SKILL.md)，进门后的保存交给 [买菜回家后收纳](../put-away-groceries/SKILL.md)。

### 准备与输入

把清单按购买地点标好，并确认每处当日营业时间、到达方法和回家方法。估计每站会增加的**体积与重量**，标出需冷藏/冷冻、生肉或海鲜、易压坏物和清洁用品。准备足够结实、能把这些类别分开的袋；需要保冷时，带可用的保冷袋和冰袋，并按商品标签及当地食品安全要求判断它能否满足整个返程。

把你的实际负重和行走、站立、上下车能力当作路线输入。若要用公共交通或带推车，查看当地运营方当前的携带和无障碍信息；查不到就不要把“应该能上车”当作路线成立。你可用纸、手机、放大的地图、语音导航或熟悉路线的人协助；不要求你能读小字或手拎全部物品。

### 执行

1. **先确定最后一段。** 从预定最后一站到家，写出交通方式、预计时间、需要的换乘或台阶，以及可能的等待。无法可靠回家或保冷时，先删去该次的易腐品或改成另一趟，不先付款再研究出口。
2. **给每站标记不可交换的条件。** 营业时间、预约取货时间、必须走的无障碍入口和返程班次比地图上的最短线优先。信息不确定时，用商店或运营方当日信息核实，并留一个能安全停下或直接回家的位置。
3. **把预计负重写在站与站之间。** 例如“第一站后两袋，第二站后再多一箱”。使用你自己已知能稳定携带或推动的范围；没有把握，就少买、分两趟、请合适的人协助，或在可用且费用可接受时选择配送。购物车轮子也不会替你通过不存在的电梯。
4. **再排购物顺序。** 通常先办轻、耐储存的日用品，再拿较重物品；冷藏、冷冻以及生肉海鲜尽量留到最后一站或最后一段，买完直接回家。若营业时间迫使易腐品排在前面，只有你能按标签要求可靠保冷且途中不会继续长时间停留，这条路线才成立；否则拆成两趟或放弃该项。
5. **在路线卡上写出取舍。** 每站只记“要买的类别、离开时预计负重、下一站或回家”；另标可删的非必需项。生肉、海鲜用防漏包装与即食食品分开，清洁用品与食品分开。选袋和分装时让易碎品不被重物压住，不让袋子或推车挡住交通工具通道。
6. **每站付款前复核。** 看实际购买量、袋子/推车状态、身体负担、返程与保冷条件。已超过可安全携带的范围，就先减掉可选商品，或使用可确认的配送/接应；条件仍不成立便停止后续采购并按安全可行的路线回家。已买易腐品后，先处理其返家，不继续追加绕路。

### 完成条件

你有一条从家出发、经过各站、再回家的可行顺序；知道每站后要带多少、何时开始携带冷藏品，以及在哪一站能删项或直接返家。完成的是可执行的路线，不是“地图上连起来了”。

### 常见报错与补救

- **店关门或交通临时变更：** 在尚未买易腐品时重排剩余站；若已经买了，先评估返家和可靠保冷，必要时跳过其余商店。
- **物品比预想重或袋子破损：** 停在安全处重新分装，舍弃未付款的可选项；已购买的物品需要接应、合适的运输或商家可提供的配送时先确认，再继续。不要靠“再走一段就好了”完成负重测试。
- **保冷条件或返程时间失控：** 停止继续采购易腐品，尽快转向可靠冷藏。时间、温度历史无法确认时，不凭外观或气味认定安全；按当地食品安全建议处理。
- **无障碍入口不可用：** 用已核实的替代入口、运输或协助；没有安全可行的替代方案就结束这一趟。

### 假设、替代与副作用

这份路线不假定你有车、能走楼梯、能提固定重量，或当地都有配送。商店、公交与商品规则随地区、时间和个人能力变化，按当日官方信息、包装要求和你自己的实际条件决定。可能的副作用是少买一件，或需要第二趟；这比把购物袋升级成耐力考试更诚实。

## English

Load this Skill when you already know what to buy, but more than one stop, the load home, a transfer, or chilled food changes the order. This Skill produces a **shopping and return route**. Use [Plan a Market Trip](../plan-a-market-trip/SKILL.md) to decide needs and quantities, and [Put Away Groceries](../put-away-groceries/SKILL.md) once you get home.

### Preparation and inputs

Assign each item on your list to a shop. Check today's opening hours, how you will reach each place, and how you will get home. Estimate the **size and weight added** at each stop. Mark chilled or frozen food, raw meat or seafood, crushable items, and cleaning products. Bring sound bags that can keep these groups apart. For perishables, bring a suitable cool bag and ice packs if needed, and judge the whole journey against the product label and local food safety guidance.

Your real capacity to carry, walk, stand, board, and use steps is an input. If you will use transit or a wheeled cart, check the operator's current carriage and accessibility information. An unverified assumption that a cart can board is not a route. Paper, an enlarged map, audio directions, or help from someone who knows the journey are all valid ways to plan it.

### Execution

1. **Work backward from home.** Write down the route from the proposed final stop to home, including travel time, transfers or steps, and likely waiting. If you cannot get home or keep perishables at their required condition reliably, remove them from this trip or make a separate trip before you pay for them.
2. **Mark the constraints that cannot move.** Opening hours, pickup windows, a usable accessible entrance, and the return service matter more than the shortest line on a map. Confirm uncertain details with the shop or operator's current information. Identify somewhere you can stop safely or head straight home.
3. **Record the load between stops.** For example, “two bags after stop one, plus a box after stop two.” Use the amount you know you can carry or move steadily. If unsure, buy less, split the trip, arrange suitable help, or choose delivery where available and affordable. A cart does not create an elevator where none exists.
4. **Order the purchases.** Generally do light, shelf-stable errands first, then heavier items. Leave chilled and frozen food, and raw meat or seafood, until the last stop or final leg, then head home. If opening hours force a perishable purchase earlier, keep that route only if you can reliably meet its labelled storage condition throughout and avoid a prolonged detour. Otherwise split the trip or skip the item.
5. **Make a small route card.** For each stop, note the item group, expected load on leaving, and next stop or home. Mark optional items you can drop. Keep raw meat and seafood in leak-resistant packaging away from ready-to-eat food, and cleaning products away from food. Do not crush fragile goods under heavier ones or block vehicle aisles with bags or a cart.
6. **Check again before paying at each stop.** Compare the actual load, bag or cart condition, your capacity, the return journey, and cooling. If carrying the load safely is no longer realistic, remove unpaid optional items or confirm a workable pickup or delivery. If that still fails, end the remaining shop and go home by a safe route. Once you have bought perishables, make their trip home the priority.

### Success

You have a workable order from home through each stop and back. You know the load after every stop, when the chilled-food journey starts, and where you can drop an item or turn home. A line connecting shops on a map is only a draft.

### Common errors and recovery

- **A shop closes or transit changes:** Reorder remaining stops if you have not bought perishables. If you have, assess the direct return and reliable cooling first; skip the other shops if needed.
- **The load is heavier than expected or a bag fails:** Stop somewhere safe and redistribute it. Leave unpaid optional goods behind. Confirm help, suitable transport, or shop delivery for goods already bought before proceeding. “Just a little farther” is not a carrying-capacity test.
- **Cooling or travel time becomes unreliable:** Stop buying perishables and get to reliable refrigeration promptly. If time or temperature history cannot be established, appearance or smell cannot establish safety; follow local food safety advice.
- **The accessible entrance is unavailable:** Use a verified alternative entrance, transport, or assistance. End this trip if none is safe and workable.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This route assumes neither a car, stair access, a universal carrying limit, nor available delivery. Shop hours, transit rules, labels, and your capacity vary by place, date, and person. Use current operator information, package instructions, and your actual conditions. You may buy one thing less or make a second trip; that is a more honest result than making a shopping bag into an endurance exam.
