## 中文

当你已经选定一份**有完整步骤和原产量**的普通家用配方，但要做的份数不同，尚未开始烹调时，用这份 Skill 产出一张实际可做的新用量表。它不替你发明医疗配方、罐藏或发酵比例，也不承诺烹调时间与火力可以按人数倍增。

### 准备与输入

找出原配方的“可供几份”、每项原料和单位、锅/模具尺寸、关键步骤、原本的熟度判断；写下这次目标份数、已有原料和实际器具。若原配方没有份数或只有模糊的“适量”，先用实际成品或可信同类配方确定基线，不编一个倍率。看不清小数或单位时用纸、计算器、放大或可信协助；涉及过敏、婴幼儿食品或经验证的安全配方时不要随意重算。

### 执行

1. **算一次倍率并标单位。** 目标份数 ÷ 原配方份数。例如原配方 4 份、要做 6 份，倍率为 1.5；若原来一种普通主料是 200 克，目标就是 300 克。把“克、毫升、个”写在数字旁，不用体积与重量凭直觉互换。
2. **把每项原料逐行重算。** 先复制完整原清单，再写“原量 × 倍率 = 目标量”。整只蛋、独立包装等难分割的项，先看是否可用原份数做一批或用可信目标份数配方；不要为了凑整随便改变敏感配方的比例。
3. **给敏感项单独检查。** 调味强度、膨松剂、增稠剂与烘焙中的结构比例可能不适合盲乘；查该配方作者或适用烹饪教育资料给出的调整方法。没有可靠说明时，保留原批量多做几批，或换已测试的目标份数配方，而不是把猜测写成精确新配方。
4. **核对实物容量。** 目标原料总量能否放进你实际的锅、烤盘或模具，且仍有正常操作空间？若不能，按原配方分批、用经确认适用的器具或缩回目标份数。别把锅盖当第二层锅壁。
5. **不机械缩放时间和温度。** 厚度、锅具和批数变化会影响烹调过程；保留原配方与产品说明的安全检查点，必要时选适用的新配方。不能确认成品安全判断或设备能力时，停止这次改份数，不靠“多煮一会儿”替代依据。
6. **写一张可操作的新配方卡。** 逐项列最终量和单位、目标份数、所用锅具、哪些步骤仍按原说明、哪些变化需要重新核对。开工前检查原料总量、空间与预计时间是否成立；分批会增加等待，交给多道菜顺序任务安排。

### 成功条件

你能用同一单位重现每项用量的计算，知道哪些部分不能按倍率自动变、器具容得下，且配方仍有可信的熟度与安全检查路径。若某个关键比例或设备条件无依据，完整结果是做原份数/可信替代配方或暂缓。

### 常见报错与补救

- **把分钟数也乘 1.5：** 撤回该时间线，按厚度、锅具与适用原配方的检查方式重排；总量扩大不保证每份受热相同。
- **同一行混用克和毫升：** 回到原配方单位或可核实的重量换算，信息不全就停止数值替换。
- **算完才发现锅放不下：** 分成可核实的原批次或改合适器具，不在满锅边缘测试容量。
- **调味或膨松比例无可靠依据：** 保留原配方批次、查配方作者说明，或选已经验证目标份数的配方；不要用精确小数给猜测披上制服。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这适用于普通家庭可调整的配方；烘焙、原料品牌、海拔、锅具与做法会改变可调整范围。这里不提供专业营养比例、罐藏、发酵、婴幼儿食品或过敏替代。量具不可及或操作困难时，可选择原批量、分批处理或经核验的更合适配方，避免要求无法准确分割的材料。结果可能多一批待做、更多器具占用或改变开饭时间，这些必须在开火前承认。

## English

Use this Skill when you have chosen an ordinary home recipe with **complete directions and a stated yield**, but need a different number of servings before cooking starts. Make a usable new amount card. It does not invent medical, canning, or fermentation formulas, and it does not promise that heat or cooking time multiplies with the number of diners.

### Preparation and inputs

Find the original serving count, every ingredient with units, pan or dish size, key steps, and doneness checks. Write down your target servings, ingredients you actually have, and real equipment. If the source has no yield or says only “to taste,” establish a credible baseline from an actual prior batch or another tested recipe before calculating. Use paper, a calculator, enlargement, or trusted help for small figures. Do not casually rescale allergy-critical, infant, or tested safety formulas.

### Execution

1. **Calculate one factor with units.** Target servings ÷ original servings. A recipe for 4 changed to 6 uses a factor of 1.5; 200 grams of an ordinary main ingredient becomes 300 grams. Keep grams, millilitres, and counts beside each number. Do not guess a weight-volume conversion.
2. **Recalculate every ingredient line.** Copy the full original list, then write “original amount × factor = target amount.” For whole eggs or sealed units that cannot be divided neatly, first consider making an original-size batch or finding a trusted recipe for the target yield. Do not round one structural ingredient by changing a sensitive formula at random.
3. **Check sensitive lines separately.** Seasoning strength, leavening, thickeners, and structural baking ratios may not tolerate blind multiplication. Check the original author's or suitable cooking educator's scaling advice. If none exists, repeat the original batch or use a tested recipe for the desired yield instead of presenting a guess as an exact new formula.
4. **Check physical capacity.** Will the total fit your actual pan, baking tin, or dish while leaving working room? If not, use separate original-size batches, verified suitable equipment, or fewer target servings. A lid is not a second wall for the pan.
5. **Do not scale time or temperature mechanically.** Thickness, equipment, and batch count change the cooking process. Preserve the original and product-specific safety checks, or choose an appropriate new recipe. If safe doneness or equipment capacity cannot be established, stop the resize rather than substituting “cook longer” for evidence.
6. **Write a new recipe card.** Record final quantities and units, target yield, actual pan, steps still governed by original directions, and changes needing review. Before starting, check food, space, and time. Multiple batches add waiting; sequence them in the separate multi-dish planning workflow.

### Success

You can reproduce the arithmetic for each ingredient in a consistent unit, identify what cannot scale automatically, fit the equipment, and retain a credible doneness and safety check. When a crucial ratio or capacity is unsupported, making the original yield, using a trusted alternative recipe, or deferring is a complete result.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You multiply the cooking minutes by 1.5:** Undo that timeline. Recheck thickness, pan, and the recipe's applicable cues; more food does not guarantee identical heating.
- **Grams and millilitres are mixed in one calculation:** Return to the original units or a verified conversion. Stop the numerical substitution if the information is missing.
- **The new batch will not fit the pan:** Split into verified original-size batches or use suitable equipment, rather than testing the rim of a full pot.
- **Seasoning or leavening has no supported ratio:** Keep the original batch, consult the recipe author, or use a recipe tested at the target yield. Do not dress a guess in precise decimals.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers adjustable ordinary home recipes. Baking, brands, altitude, pans, and technique affect what can be changed. It gives no medical nutrient ratios or canning, fermentation, infant-food, or allergy substitutions. If measuring or handling is inaccessible, choose the original yield, separate batches, or a verified better-fitting recipe rather than demanding impossible fractional ingredients. You may have more batches, occupied equipment, or a later serving time; acknowledge those before cooking.
