## 中文

当你已决定买某种普通食物或日用品，却在散装、小包装、大包装、多件装之间犹豫时，加载这份 Skill。这里决定**买多少**；商品是否适用、单位价格怎么算、到家怎么保存，分别是其他任务。它不处理药品、保健品、危险化学品或医疗饮食。

### 准备与输入

先确认这次购买要覆盖到什么时候（例如下次补货前），其间你**实际会用几次、每次多少**，以及手头已知可用的存量。若使用频率没有依据，就只按一次真实用途估算，不把“以后总会用到”算作需求。

对每个适用的候选包装，读实际净含量或件数，而非盒子的视觉大小；同时记下现场总价、日期、储存条件和开封后说明。确认你有足够的预算、放置空间和携带能力。看不清标签时，放大照片、查看商家可读的商品信息，或请店员读出具体数量与日期；仍不清楚就先不买。

### 执行

1. **写出缺口。** 用“次数 × 每次用量 − 已知可用存量”得到这段时间真正缺的量。例：两次各用 150 克，家里还有确定可用的 100 克，缺口是 200 克。存量状态不明时，先核实，别用想象抵扣。
2. **给每个包装设可用窗口。** 食物同时看预计使用日、包装日期、储存和开封后说明；能否冷冻或分装，只按该商品标签和你已有的可靠条件判断，不能把计划中的冰箱空间当作已经存在。标有 *use-by* 的食品按其安全界限处理；*best-before* 通常关乎品质，不自动等于安全截止日。其他地区按当地标签及权威解释，不从英文词汇猜规则。普通无日期日用品则按下一次补货前的真实用量和空间判断。
3. **算能用掉的量。** 同一种适用商品把包装净量换成同一单位，逐个问：在上述窗口内，我能用掉多少？大包装剩余部分若没有可信用途，就不计为“省到的量”。独立密封小包与一开封就受限制的整包，按各自标签分别判断，不假设保质条件相同。
4. **先排除不可执行的选项。** 装不下、拎不动、超出今天付款上限、日期赶不上，或必须靠未经确认的人接手剩余量才能成立的包装，都不进入最后比较。优惠券不会替多出来的东西安排日程。
5. **选能覆盖缺口且余量可解释的数量。** 可以是一包、几包小份、按需散买，或今天不买。若多个选项都合适，再用同一现场的实际总支出选你能承担的方案；精细的同单位价格比较归独立任务，不能凭“大包”二字推断划算。

### 成功条件

结账前你能说出：“我要买 **几包、每包实际多少、用于哪几次、何时用完、今天付多少**。”若没有可信包装满足缺口与条件，明确输出“少买、换可用规格或延后”，也算完成决定。

### 常见报错、补救与停止

- **ERR_FAMILY_SIZE：** 大包装单价看似低，但你只会用其中一部分。回到可用窗口和真实缺口，改看小包或散装；别为了证明折扣正确而临时发明需求。
- **ERR_OLD_BOX_MEMORY：** 你记得的“这盒够三次”与现售净含量不符。重新读数字；外观相同不保证里面一样多。
- **ERR_DATE_AFTER_OPEN：** 未开封日期够远，开封后却用不完。按开封后的说明重算，或选独立密封、可逐份使用的选项；条件不明就停止该包装的购买决定。
- **ERR_UNKNOWN_RATE：** 第一次使用，没有可靠消耗速度。先买能完成一次用途的最小合适量，用完后再按实际速度决定下次；不要承诺大包装会成为新习惯。
- **ERR_NO_ACCESS：** 标签太小、价格缺失或需要帮助拿取。用可访问的信息或请店员确认；关键数字仍拿不到，就不以猜测结账。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里假设你能决定一次普通采购，并能获得至少一种适用的商品信息。包装日期含义、价格单位、购物频率、身体负担和储存条件随地区与个人而变；用当地规则和你实际条件替换示例。行动不便时可选较轻的分次采购或配送，配送费用也要算进今天能否承担。若是食品安全、过敏或产品用途本身不确定，先解决那个问题，这份 Skill 不用“大概会用完”覆盖它。

现实副作用是你可能需要补货更频繁，也可能减少废弃；这两种成本都比一张醒目的促销标签真实。

## English

Load this Skill when you have chosen an ordinary food or household consumable but must decide between loose goods, small packs, large packs, or multipacks. You are deciding **how much to buy**. Product suitability, detailed unit-price comparison, and storage after you get home are separate tasks. This does not cover medicines, supplements, hazardous chemicals, or medical diets.

### Preparation and inputs

Set the period this purchase must cover, such as until your next realistic restock. Estimate **how many real uses and how much per use** fall within it, then note any stock you know is usable. If you have no evidence for a use rate, plan only one actual use; “I will probably need it someday” is not a quantity.

For each suitable option, read its net amount or count rather than judging the box by sight. Note today's total price, date marking, storage conditions, and any after-opening instructions. Confirm that you can pay for, carry, and place the pack. If print is hard to read, enlarge a clear photo, use accessible seller information, or ask staff for the exact amount and date. If a key fact stays unknown, pause the purchase.

### Execution

1. **Calculate the gap.** Use “number of uses × amount per use − known usable stock.” For example, two uses of 150 g with 100 g already usable leave a 200 g gap. Verify uncertain stock before subtracting it.
2. **Set a usable window for each pack.** For food, compare your planned use dates with the package date, storage directions, and after-opening instructions. Count freezing or portioning only when the product instructions and your actual facilities support it. For food marked *use-by*, respect its safety limit; *best-before* generally concerns quality and is not automatically a safety deadline. Check the local authority and label outside the UK rather than inferring rules from English words. For ordinary undated household goods, use your real demand until restock and available space.
3. **Estimate what you can use.** Convert net quantities of the same suitable product to one unit. Ask how much of each pack you would actually use within its window. Surplus without a credible use is not a saving. Check individually sealed portions and one container opened at once against their own instructions; do not assume they keep equally well.
4. **Remove impossible options.** A pack is out if you cannot store or carry it, it exceeds today's spending limit, its date does not fit, or it only works if an unconfirmed person takes the excess. A sale sticker cannot schedule the surplus for you.
5. **Choose a quantity that covers the gap with an explainable remainder.** That may be one pack, several smaller packs, a loose amount, or no purchase today. If several options work, compare their observed total spending with what you can afford. Detailed like-for-like unit-price ranking belongs to a separate task; a large pack is not proof of value.

### Success

Before checkout, you can state **how many packs, the actual amount in each, which uses they cover, when you will use them, and today's total cost**. If no suitable pack fits, “buy less, find another suitable size, or wait” is a completed decision.

### Common errors, recovery, and stop points

- **ERR_FAMILY_SIZE:** A large pack looks cheaper per unit, but you would use only part of it. Return to the gap and usable window; choose a smaller or loose amount instead of inventing extra demand.
- **ERR_OLD_BOX_MEMORY:** The familiar-looking pack no longer holds what you remember. Read its current net contents and recalculate.
- **ERR_DATE_AFTER_OPEN:** The unopened date is distant but you cannot finish the pack after opening. Recalculate from its opening instructions or choose separately sealed portions. Stop on that pack if the conditions remain unclear.
- **ERR_UNKNOWN_RATE:** It is your first use, so buy the smallest suitable amount for one real attempt. Use that experience to set the next purchase quantity.
- **ERR_NO_ACCESS:** Small print, missing price, or an unreachable shelf blocks a key input. Use accessible information or ask staff; if the number is still unknown, do not guess at checkout.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This assumes you can choose an ordinary purchase and obtain information for at least one suitable product. Date-label meanings, price units, shopping frequency, carrying ability, and storage vary by region and person. Use your local rules and actual constraints. If carrying is difficult, consider lighter repeat purchases or delivery, including its real cost in what you can pay today. Resolve food safety, allergies, or product suitability first; a plausible consumption plan does not resolve them.

You might shop more often or waste less. Both costs are more real than the type size on a promotion sign.
