## 中文

当你看得见物体的亮面和暗面，却一落笔就把每处涂成同样灰时，用这份 Skill 做一张**三档明暗观察图**。结果是能从纸上找到眼前物体的亮区、中间调和暗区，不追求光滑写实或画满所有细节。轮廓练习解决外形观察；这份练习解决光照分区。

### 准备与输入

选你可安全观察的普通静物，放在光线相对稳定、不需面对太阳直视的位置。自然窗光或已有室内照明即可，不为了本练习改接电线、使用热灯或购买设备。用纸笔画一个很简略的物体占位，或使用自己已有的轮廓；它不必比例完美。先画一个三格小色阶：纸白/最浅、轻压中灰、较深灰。若不便辨认灰度，可用三种明显不同的线纹或标记，并注明对应亮/中/暗；需要可信协助时让对方描述光区，不把描述当自己看见的证据。

### 执行

1. **先观察，再归类。** 在固定视角看物体与其附近的光源方向，指认一个最亮的面、一个最暗的面或投影，以及介于两者之间的区域。不要凭“球一定左亮右暗”之类记忆替代现场；对象和光源才是输入。
2. **保留亮区。** 在简略轮廓里先圈出最亮区域，暂不涂它；不用橡皮先把整张涂灰再硬擦高光。亮区可以很小，也不必是纯白，只要与你的中间调有区别。
3. **铺中间调，再压暗。** 用轻线或中档纹样标出大块中间区域，再只在观察到的暗面和投影处加深。边缘若渐变，用较少/较密的线缓和，不需要画成三个硬纸片。不要为了“画得像”凭空加不存在的影子。
4. **退一步对照。** 把纸放远一点，回看实物：纸上最亮与最暗的位置是否对应？光源变化或物体被移动后，重新观察并标注“光变了”，不要把两个时刻的阴影拼成一个固定状态。只修正最明显的一处错位。
5. **记一次观察。** 在角落写“光大致从____来；我发现____面比想象中暗/亮”。停止在三档关系可辨的位置，不靠加黑全页来制造“完成感”。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你能在同一实物上指出纸上的亮、中、暗三类区域，纸上最亮和最暗位置与当前观察大致对应，并有一处比较记录。不是必须有完美渐层；只是把轮廓加粗或整张平均涂灰还没有完成明暗分区。

- **所有地方一样灰：** 留一块观察到的亮区不涂，并只加深真正最暗的区域，重新拉开三档。
- **光一直变：** 换稳定现有光线或缩短观察时间，标注开始时的光方向；不要改装照明。
- **物体反光太强看不出中间调：** 换哑光普通物体，不盯强光或镜面反射。
- **视觉条件下无法可靠分档：** 用大画幅、高对比纹样和可信描述协助；若仍无法核对，暂停，不虚构三档结果。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这是低风险静物观察，不教专业灯光、电气布置或人体绘画。不同纸笔、光线与感官条件会改变你能分辨的细节；三档是入门比较工具，不是物理测光标准。用斜线/点纹代替灰度时写明图例。作品只要求你能解释观察到的分区，可与轮廓草图分开完成。

## English

Use this Skill when you can see the bright and shaded sides of an object but your pencil makes every part the same grey. Make a **three-value observation study** whose light, middle, and dark regions correspond to the object in front of you. Smooth realism and every detail are unnecessary. A contour exercise focuses on shape; this one focuses on light regions.

### Preparation and inputs

Choose an ordinary still object you can observe safely under fairly steady light, without looking toward the sun. Window light or existing room lighting is enough; do not rewire anything, use a hot lamp, or buy equipment for this exercise. Make a very rough initial outline on paper or use one you already made; perfect proportion is unnecessary. Draw a three-box key: paper white/lightest, gentle middle grey, and darker grey. If greys are hard to tell apart, use three clearly different patterns or symbols labelled light/middle/dark. Trusted help may describe the light regions when needed; distinguish that description from what you personally saw.

### Execution

1. **Observe and classify.** From one fixed view, notice the light direction and point to one brightest surface, one darkest surface or cast shadow, and an area between. Do not substitute a remembered rule such as “a sphere is always light on the left.” This object and light are the inputs.
2. **Reserve the light.** Mark the brightest region within the rough outline and leave it unshaded for now. Do not shade everything first and then struggle to erase a highlight. The light area can be small; it only needs to differ from the middle.
3. **Lay in middle, then dark.** Use light strokes or your middle pattern across larger intermediate areas. Deepen only the observed dark face or cast shadow. For gradual transitions, vary line spacing rather than force three rigid blocks. Do not invent a shadow solely to make the study look more finished.
4. **Step back and compare.** View paper and object again. Are their brightest and darkest locations roughly aligned? If the light changes or the object moves, observe again and note “light changed”; do not blend shadows from different moments into one supposedly fixed observation. Correct the most noticeable mismatch only.
5. **Record one observation.** Write “light came roughly from ____; I noticed ____ was darker/lighter than expected.” Stop when the three relationships can be distinguished. Darkening the entire page is not evidence of completion.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You can point to light, middle, and dark categories on the same real object and your page. The lightest and darkest places on paper roughly match this observation, and one comparison note remains. A perfect gradient is unnecessary. A thicker outline or uniformly grey page has not mapped values yet.

- **Everything is the same grey:** Leave one observed light area clear and deepen only the genuinely darkest area, then compare again.
- **Light keeps changing:** Use existing steadier light or shorten the session, noting the starting direction. Do not modify electrical lighting.
- **A shiny object has no clear middle:** Choose an ordinary matte object rather than stare at glare or a mirror reflection.
- **You cannot reliably distinguish the regions:** Try a larger view, contrasting patterns, and trusted description. Pause if a check remains impossible; do not invent a three-value result.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is low-risk still-life observation, not professional lighting, electrical setup, or figure drawing. Paper, pencil, light, and sensory conditions change what details you can see. Three values are a beginner comparison aid, not a physical light meter. If using hatch or dot patterns instead of grey, label the key. The work only needs an explainable observed division and can be separate from a contour sketch.
