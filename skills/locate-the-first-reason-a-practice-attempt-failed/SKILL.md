## 中文

当一项普通学习练习已经做完或做到停点，结果与可信答案/标准不符时，加载这份 Skill。目标是从**这一份原始尝试**找到第一处有证据的偏差，分辨题意误解、知识/方法缺口与执行步骤失误，再选一个可检验的下一动作。它不诊断学习障碍、不预测成绩；形成原始独立尝试归 H255，隔一段时间再测试归 H257。

### 准备与输入

保留原题、你没看答案时的作答和所用资料。准备可靠答案、评分标准、课堂解释或能核对的教师/同伴；答案本身不可靠时标“待核”，不要从两份互相矛盾的答案里任选一份。若是已评分材料且可能要依课程规则复核，不改动原件，在副本上标注。先只选一处错误，不把一次练习全盘写成个人能力结论。

### 执行

1. **重述原题要什么。** 用自己的话写结果形式、关键条件、限制词和必须使用的资料。把这句与题目逐项对照：若漏看“至少”“不能”“比较”或对象范围，先标为可能的**题意偏差**，而不是立刻说知识不够。
2. **并排放原作答与可信标准。** 标出最早出现不同的步骤或判断；只看最后答案对错，无法知道哪里开始偏离。没有逐步标准时，只标你能核实的条件，并把其余留待求证，不捏造标准解法。
3. **用一项小证据判断原因。**
   - **题意误解：** 你回答的其实是另一问题；重新圈原题条件，试说出正确问题，但先不算重做成功。
   - **知识/方法缺口：** 你知道题目在问什么，却不能说出该用哪个概念或为什么；回到可定位的课程说明，写下缺的那一条关系。
   - **执行步骤失误：** 方法选对，但在计算、顺序、单位、引用或表达中的某一步偏离；在原过程旁标出那一步和应检查的动作。
   几种原因可能并存，只选**最早且证据够**的一项作为本次优先修正；无法判断就写“未定位”，不要给自己贴标签。
4. **选一个匹配的修正动作。** 误读就重写题意并用新题核；概念缺口就先复查来源、用自己的话解释后再做相近新题；步骤失误就用检查点重走该步骤再独立试另一题。仅把正确答案抄在原题旁，不算修正。
5. **留一张短记录。** 写题目出处、第一处偏差、证据、优先原因、下一次要检查的动作与仍未知的问题。向教师/同伴求助时带这张记录和原尝试，别只说“不会”。下一次练习再检验修正是否有效，不凭这张分析卡宣称掌握。

### 完成、常见问题与补救

你有一条可指向原题/原作答位置的偏差证据和一个与之匹配的下一练习动作；或者明确标未定位并准备具体求助问题。答案改成对的但说不出原偏差，不算找到了原因。

- **答案有争议或标准看不到：** 保留两种解释和原题位置，找课程允许的核对人，不自行宣布对错。
- **一步错后后面全错：** 从第一处偏差开始，不把后面每一项都当独立知识缺口。
- **只是粗心吗？：** 写出实际可观察的漏条件或步骤；“粗心”太宽，不能指导下一次检查。
- **同一道题重做会了：** 换一题条件相近但不同的练习验证，避免记住答案冒充会迁移。
- **压力、疲劳或可及条件影响练习：** 记录当时条件并换合适形式/时长；一次错误不能推成医学或人格判断。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

题目和可核标准因课程、语言与媒介而异；文字、语音或可访问图示都能记录第一处偏差。未必每项错误都能归入单一类别，也可能是答案标准有问题。此 Skill 只做普通练习诊断；正式评分、申诉或特殊支持按课程规则和适当人员处理。

## English

Load this Skill after an ordinary study exercise is finished or reaches its stopping point and the result differs from a trustworthy answer or rubric. Find the **first evidenced divergence in your original attempt**, distinguish a misunderstood prompt, missing knowledge or method, and an execution slip, then choose one testable next action. This does not diagnose a learning disorder or predict grades. Making the first independent attempt belongs to H255; a later retention check belongs to H257.

### Preparation and inputs

Keep the original prompt, the response you made before seeing an answer, and the resources you used. Have a reliable answer, rubric, course explanation, or teacher or peer who can check it. If the reference itself is uncertain, mark it “to verify” rather than picking one of two conflicting keys. For graded material that may need formal review, leave the original untouched and annotate a copy under course rules. Pick one error first instead of making the whole exercise a verdict on your ability.

### Execution

1. **Restate what the prompt requires.** In your own words write the output form, key conditions, limiting words, and allowed materials. Compare with the exact prompt. If you missed “at least,” “not,” “compare,” or the target scope, mark a possible **prompt-reading difference** before deciding that your knowledge is missing.
2. **Place your original attempt beside the reliable reference.** Mark the earliest different step or judgement. The final answer alone cannot show where the path diverged. Without stepwise criteria, mark only conditions you can verify and leave the rest for a person or source to check; do not invent an official solution.
3. **Use one small piece of evidence to classify the cause.**
   - **Misread prompt:** You answered a different question. Circle the original condition and state the intended question, without counting this as a completed redo.
   - **Knowledge or method gap:** You understand what is asked but cannot explain which concept or method applies and why. Return to a locatable course explanation and name the missing relationship.
   - **Execution slip:** You chose a workable method but deviated at a calculation, sequence, unit, citation, or expression step. Mark that step beside the original and the check it needs.
   Several causes can coexist. Choose the **earliest supported** one to repair first. If evidence is insufficient, write “cause not located” instead of labelling yourself.
4. **Choose a matching repair.** For misreading, rewrite the requirement and test it on a new prompt. For a concept gap, check the source, explain it in your own words, then try a comparable new problem. For a slip, use a checkpoint at that step and independently try another problem. Copying the correct answer beside the old one is not a repair.
5. **Leave a brief record.** Note prompt source, first divergence, evidence, priority cause, next check, and remaining unknowns. Bring it and the original attempt when asking a teacher or peer for help instead of saying only “I can't do it.” Test the repair in another practice attempt; the analysis card alone is not proof of mastery.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

You can point to evidence in the prompt and original response for the first divergence and a matching next practice action, or explicitly mark the cause unresolved and prepare a specific question. Correcting the final answer without finding the original divergence is not cause analysis.

- **The key is disputed or unavailable:** Keep both interpretations and the prompt location and ask a course-approved checker instead of declaring a result.
- **One early mistake makes every later line wrong:** Start at the first divergence; do not count each downstream line as a separate knowledge gap.
- **“I was careless” seems enough:** Name the observable missed condition or step. “Careless” is too broad to guide the next check.
- **You can redo the same problem now:** Try a comparable but different prompt to avoid mistaking remembered answers for transfer.
- **Stress, fatigue, or access conditions affected practice:** Record the conditions and adjust form or duration. One error is not a medical or character diagnosis.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Prompts and checkable standards differ by course, language, and medium. Text, audio, or accessible diagrams can preserve the first divergence. Not every error fits one category; the answer key itself may be wrong. This is ordinary practice analysis. Grading appeals or specialised support follow course rules and appropriate people.
