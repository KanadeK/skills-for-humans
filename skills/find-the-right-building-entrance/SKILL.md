## 中文

当导航显示“已到达”，但眼前有几栋相似楼、多个门或正门与可及入口不同，你需要到达**这次真正开放、允许访客使用的入口**时加载。目标是核对楼栋、门位和当前开放状态，站到正确入口；进入后的楼层/房间路线另行处理。这里不教闯入员工门、尾随他人通过门禁、绕开围栏或根据陌生二维码登录。

### 准备与输入

从实际预约、活动或场所管理方给的信息抄下完整名称、街道门牌、楼栋/翼区/访客入口、到访时间及联系电话。看场所官方“如何到达/无障碍/临时关闭”页面或正式纸质指引；只有地图定位点时，承认它可能只指向楼体中心。手机离线可使用已保存的地址、外部门牌和公开标识；具体街道绕行交给安全步行流程。

### 执行

1. **先确认是这栋楼。** 在允许停留的人行位置，把门牌号、楼名、楼栋或翼区与预约信息对照。相邻楼共用广场或同一机构有多处场地时，名称相近也要核门牌；不要只看地图箭头压在建筑上的位置。
2. **查访客入口而非最近的门。** 读管理方当前入口说明、现场“Visitor/Main/Reception”或同义标识，确认开放时段与临时关闭通知。送货口、员工门、消防出口和关闭的旋转门不是可试开的捷径。若预约另写特定门或需登记，以这次真实说明为准。
3. **把可及入口与路线一起确认。** 若你需要无台阶、自动门、足够宽的通道或其他具体条件，查该场所官方给的可用入口及从公共道路到门前的路线；它可能不在最显眼的正面，也可能与普通入口相同。页面只写“设施可及”而没有门位时，向场所正式电话/接待问入口位置，不根据旁人的身体标签猜需求。
4. **沿开放的公共路径抵门再核一次。** 遵守现场路标与安全通道，核对门旁单位名称、是否为访客入口以及此时是否开放。门锁着、路被封或标牌写“改走另一门”，就用正式告示/管理方答复重新定位；不要翻栏、穿车道或跟着别人刷门禁。
5. **留下可复用的一句入口说明。** 记“__楼，__街一侧，访客入口在__，今日开放/待确认__”。若要与人会合，另约双方都能识别的标志；这份 Skill 只保证你能指出自己实际站的入口，不替场所授予进入权限。

### 完成与停止

你站在门牌/楼栋和访客标识均吻合、当前可用、满足你实际通行条件的入口，并知道若门关闭该联系谁，本轮完成。**到入口不等于已登记、获准进入或到达室内目的地。** 楼栋不符、入口开放状态或可及路线无法确认时，停在安全公共位置向场所正式渠道求证，不进入陌生私有门。

### 常见报错与补救

- **地图说已到，眼前只有建筑侧墙：** 回看完整门牌/场所入口说明，沿公开路标找访客门，不把定位点当门洞。
- **正门有台阶，另一侧可能可进：** 查场所官方可及入口与开放状态，再按公开可达路线过去；不推断任何后门都能用。
- **相邻两栋楼共用名称：** 对照楼号、翼区和预约地址，不从装修或人群猜哪栋。
- **访客门临时关闭：** 读当日正式替代指示或联系接待；没有可核验入口就暂停而非进员工门。
- **手机没网或提示要扫陌生码：** 用门牌、实体标识、已保存预约信息或场所正式电话/人员确认；不向未知页面交出账号或付款资料。

### 假设、替代与副作用

适用于普通城市中可退出的楼宇或公共设施，不涉及野外导航、紧急救援、法律无障碍合规判断或安保权限。入口、时段与施工会变化；官方场所页面与现场当天标识要一起看。视觉、行动、听觉或语言不便时，可用场所提供的文字/图片、无障碍入口资料、正式电话或可信陪同，描述你**具体需要的通行条件**。副作用是你可能要绕到另一侧，但这比一直盯着地图中心点有用。

## English

Load this Skill when a map says “arrived” but several nearby buildings or doors are plausible, or the main-looking door differs from the entrance you can use. Your result is reaching the **visitor entrance that is open and usable for this visit**, with building, door and current state checked. Indoor floors and rooms come later. This does not teach entry through a staff door, tailgating a controlled gate, bypassing barriers or signing into an unknown QR page.

### Preparation and inputs

From your real appointment, event or venue, keep the full name, street number, building or wing, visitor entrance, visit time and contact route. Read the venue's official directions, accessibility or temporary-closure information or a formal paper guide. A map pin alone may mark the building centre. Without data, use a saved address, external street number and public signs. Handle any street detour through the separate safe-walking task.

### Execution

1. **Confirm this is the building.** From a permitted place on the public footpath, match street number, building name and wing to the appointment. Adjacent buildings may share a forecourt or institution; similar names still need an address check. An arrow over a roof is not a doorway.
2. **Find the visitor entrance, not the nearest door.** Read the venue's current entry directions and signs such as Visitor, Main or Reception, including opening hours and temporary closures. Delivery, staff, emergency-exit and closed revolving doors are not shortcuts to try. If your booking names a different door or registration point, use the instructions for this visit.
3. **Check accessible entrance and route together.** If you need no steps, an automatic door, enough width or another specific condition, use the venue's official entrance and public-street route information. It may be away from the most visible front door, or be the same door. “Accessible building” without a door location is incomplete: ask the venue's official phone or reception, describing the entrance condition you need rather than guessing from a label about a person.
4. **Follow an open public path and verify at the door.** Use on-site signs and safe public routes. At the door, compare the organisation name, visitor notice and current open state. For a locked door, closure or “use another entrance” sign, recheck the formal notice or venue reply. Do not climb a barrier, cross live traffic or follow someone through access control.
5. **Keep one reusable entrance line.** Note “__ building, __ street side, visitor entrance at __, open now or still to confirm __.” For a meeting, agree on a recognisable landmark separately. This Skill identifies where you actually stand; it does not grant admission.

### Success and stop

You are at an entrance whose street or building identity and visitor signs match, which is currently open and meets your practical access needs, with a contact route if it closes. **Reaching the door is not registration, permission to enter or arrival at the indoor destination.** If building, hours or accessible route cannot be verified, pause in a safe public place and ask the venue officially rather than trying an unknown private door.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The map says arrived but shows a side wall:** Recheck full street number and entrance directions; follow public signs instead of treating the pin as a door.
- **The front has steps and another side may work:** Verify the venue's accessible entrance and current opening, then use a public reachable path. Do not assume every rear door is usable.
- **Nearby buildings share a similar name:** Match building number, wing and appointment address rather than guessing from the crowd or decor.
- **The visitor door is temporarily closed:** Read today's formal alternative or call reception. With no verified public entrance, pause rather than trying a staff door.
- **No data or an unfamiliar QR request:** Use street number, physical signs, saved booking details or the venue's official phone or staff. Do not give account or payment information to an unknown page.

### Assumptions, alternatives and side effects

This covers ordinary, exit-able city buildings and public facilities, not wilderness navigation, emergency response, accessibility-law compliance or security authorisation. Entrances, hours and construction change; use both venue information and today's signs. For sight, mobility, hearing or language needs, use the venue's text or photos, accessible-entry information, official contact or a trusted companion, stating the **specific route conditions** you need. You may have to go around a building, which beats staring at its centre pin.
