## 中文

当钥匙、常用包、交通卡或外套每次进门都落在不同地方，出门又要重新搜一遍时，加载这份 Skill。目标是让**你实际高频携带的几样东西**各有可找回的位置，并且门、通道和共用区域仍能正常通行。这里不负责找回已经丢失的物品、处理借用归属、重新设计所有抽屉，也不要求你买一套漂亮的收纳架。

### 准备与输入

先挑最近一周确实反复进出门的三到五样物品；没有这么多就只做已有的。观察你进门时哪只手拿着东西、先在哪儿脱鞋或放包，以及出门时从哪里转身拿回。确认位置是否属于你可使用的空间；楼道、公共疏散通道和别人专用的台面不自动成为你的收纳区。

优先使用已有的稳固架面、浅盒、挂点或包内固定口袋，不先购物或钻孔。位置要能安全到达、辨认和取用；若弯腰、够高处、辨别颜色或看小字困难，改用合适高度、固定顺序、触觉或大字标记。不要移动别人赖以定位的家具或物品而不告知。

### 执行

1. **留下进出门真正会用的物品。** 给每样写一个下次出门的用途。偶尔使用、待归还、待处理的物品另放到相应任务位置；别让玄关成为所有未决定问题的候诊室。
2. **沿动作选位置。** 选你进门顺手能放、出门按同一路径能拿到的固定小区域。钥匙等必须找得到的东西要让有权使用的人知道位置；具体放法还须符合你的住房安全与出入口安排，不把钥匙展示或留在门外可触及处。
3. **先保留通道，再放物品。** 放好后把门完整开关一次，并按你平常走、推行或使用辅助工具的方式通过。鞋、包、盒和临时快递不能挤占出入口或公共通道；放不下就缩小存放数量，改用门边之外的安全位置。
4. **给每样物品一个可识别的家。** 一个浅格、一个挂点或包内同一个口袋即可；靠位置、顺序和必要时的大字/触觉标记辨认。颜色不是唯一编码。不要在钥匙上写公开可识别的住址，也不要把位置清单发到公开场合。
5. **处理暂时不能进常驻位的东西。** 湿伞、泥鞋或刚带回的杂物需要一个不会挡路、能继续处理的暂放点；干燥、清洁或转交完成后再回常驻位。暂放点不是无限期收纳许可。
6. **走一次来回。** 模拟进门：把物品放好；离开区域，再按正常出门顺序取回。每样都能找到、通道可用、另一位需要使用位置的人也知道约定，才算完成。若某处总被跳过，只改那个位置，别先购买第二个系统。

### 完成与停止

你能不翻包、不挪开一堆别的东西就找到这几样高频物品；门和通道保持可用，位置不会强迫你够到危险高度。没有适合位置时，停止新增入口堆放，选一个现有、安全、稍远但能记住的地方。钥匙涉及的出入安全依你的住房与当地指引，不靠本篇决定锁具或逃生规则。

### 常见报错与补救

- **收纳盒很快变成杂物盒：** 只留本轮三到五样高频物品；“待处理”单独写下一步和去处，不让它永久占位。
- **进门时手里太多，固定位置总没用上：** 把落点移到实际先放东西的位置，或先给包一个稳固落点，再拿出小物。
- **家人换了摆放处，你找不到：** 先确认对方的需要和是否共用，再重新约定一个双方能辨认的固定位置；别把对方的物品悄悄移走。
- **湿物弄湿常驻区或挡路：** 先放到不滑、不挡门的临时点，安排干燥后回位；地面湿滑则先处理该处通行问题。
- **临出门才发现物品不在家：** 这已是查找任务；按有限范围寻找并设停止点，不把整个归位系统当场推倒重建。
- **布置到一半被打断：** 记下哪几样已有固定位置、哪些还没做来回测试；下次只补未完成的部分，别把临时堆放当作新规则。

### 假设、替代与已知副作用

住处大小、门的方向、共用规则、钥匙管理和身体可及性都不同；本篇只要求稳定、可辨认、可取用的少量位置，不规定统一家具或美学。视觉不可靠时可用一致位置和触觉标记；无法站立或提举时选择坐着也能触及的稳固位置。现实副作用是每次进门要多做一次归位，系统才会真的记住东西在哪里。

## English

Load this Skill when keys, a daily bag, a travel card, or a coat lands somewhere different after each return, so leaving begins with another search. Give **the few things you really carry often** findable places while keeping the door and route through it usable. This does not find an item already lost, settle ownership of a borrowed item, redesign every drawer, or require a matching set of organizers.

### Preparation and inputs

Choose three to five items you actually carried in and out repeatedly in the last week; use fewer if that is all you have. Watch which hand is occupied on arrival, where you first remove shoes or set down a bag, and where you turn to collect things before leaving. Confirm that you may use the proposed space. A shared corridor, escape route, or someone else's surface does not become yours by being convenient.

Use a stable existing shelf, shallow container, hook, or fixed bag pocket before buying anything or drilling holes. You must be able to reach, recognize, and retrieve the place safely. If bending, reaching high, distinguishing colours, or reading small print is difficult, choose a workable height, consistent order, tactile cue, or large label. Do not move furniture another person uses as a landmark without telling them.

### Execution

1. **Keep only items that belong to this exit routine.** Name each item's next real use. Move occasional, borrowed, and unresolved items to their own task location. The entryway need not become a waiting room for every undecided object.
2. **Choose places along your movement.** Pick a small fixed area you can use naturally on arrival and collect from along the same route when leaving. People entitled to use essential keys must know where to find them; the exact arrangement must also fit your home's safety and exit plan. Do not display keys or leave them reachable from outside.
3. **Preserve the route before adding storage.** Once items are placed, fully open and close the door, then pass through as you usually do, including any mobility aid or carried bag. Shoes, boxes, and deliveries must not occupy the doorway or shared passage. If everything will not fit, store fewer items or use another safe place away from the door.
4. **Give each item a recognizable home.** One shallow section, hook, or consistent bag pocket may be enough. Use location, order, and if needed large-print or tactile marking; colour alone is not a universal code. Do not label keys with a publicly identifiable address or publish a map of their location.
5. **Handle items that cannot go straight home.** A wet umbrella, muddy shoes, or a miscellaneous thing just brought in needs a temporary place that neither blocks the route nor becomes its final destination. After drying, cleaning, or handing it over, return it to its regular place. “Temporary” is not a storage duration.
6. **Run one round trip.** Simulate arriving and put the items away. Leave the area, then retrieve them in your normal departure order. Finish when you can find each, the route works, and anyone else who uses the space understands the agreement. If you repeatedly skip one place, change that place before buying another system.

### Success and stop conditions

You can retrieve these frequently carried items without emptying a bag or moving a pile, the door and passage remain usable, and no place requires a dangerous reach. If no suitable spot exists by the door, stop adding to the entrance and choose an existing, safe, slightly farther location you can remember. Follow your own housing and local guidance for key access and exits; this Skill does not set lock or evacuation rules.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The tray becomes a miscellaneous tray:** Keep only the few frequent items. Give “to handle” items a next action and another place instead of permanent occupancy.
- **Your hands are full and you skip the spot:** Move it to where you actually set things down, or give the bag a stable landing place before removing small items.
- **Someone moves the items and you cannot find them:** Check their needs and whether the place is shared, then agree on a fixed place both of you can recognize. Do not silently move their belongings.
- **Wet things soak the regular spot or block the way:** Use a temporary place that does not slip or obstruct the door, then return them when dry. Address a wet, slippery walking surface first.
- **An item is missing just before departure:** That is now a search task. Search within a bounded area and set a stop point rather than redesigning the entire arrangement on the spot.
- **You are interrupted halfway through setup:** Note which items have a fixed place and which still need the round-trip test. Resume only those unfinished parts; a temporary pile has not become the new rule.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Homes vary in size, door swing, sharing rules, key management, and access needs. This uses a few stable, recognizable, reachable places, not one required piece of furniture or aesthetic. If sight is unreliable, use consistent positions and tactile cues. If standing or lifting is difficult, choose a stable spot reachable while seated. The side effect is one extra return action each time; that is how the arrangement remembers where things are.
