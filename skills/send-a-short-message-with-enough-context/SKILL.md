## 中文

你要给一个人或小群体发一条普通短消息，但对方可能不在同一段实时谈话里，需要**看完这条就知道指哪件事、是否要行动**时，用这份 Skill。目标是选择合适渠道、核对收件对象、补足最少上下文并如实记录发送状态；不是要求对方立刻回复。它不处理账号验证、敏感资料、危机通知或正式法律/财务沟通，也不按不同聊天 App 拆成多份 Skill。

### 准备与输入

先问这条消息是**告知、询问、确认还是请求动作**，尽量只保留一个主要目的。选对方平常能收到且适合这件事的渠道；如果必须在很短时间内让人知道变化，普通异步文字未必可靠，应按你们已有的及时联系办法核对，而不是把“已发送”写成“已通知”。确认收件人或群聊名称与实际成员；私人细节不应因发错群而变成公共广播。

### 执行

1. **先给指向这件事的锚点。** 用人、活动或日期指出主题：“关于周六的图书交换……” 不只发“那个可以”“按之前的来”；即便你们昨天谈过，对方现在也可能在处理另几件事。若引用一条旧消息，确保引用对象正确且对方能看到。
2. **给事实和下一动作。** 简短写清这次变化或问题、相关地点/时间/数量，以及对方需要做什么；若无需回复，直接说“无需回复，除非地点变化”。有真实截止才写明日期、时间和所属地区/时区；异地人员先按时区流程核对，不凭“明天下午”猜他们看到消息的本地日期。
3. **发前从对方视角读一次。** 检查收件对象、称呼、日期与地点、附件是否真的附上，以及这条里是否有不该给所有收件人的地址、证件或完整私人行程。把一屏里五个不相干问题拆开或换合适的同步沟通；短不等于把关键信息砍掉。设备朗读或小屏阅读时，文字本身也要能独立表达要点，不全靠图中的小字。
4. **发送后保留真实状态。** 发送成功只是消息进入渠道；显示已读也不能证明对方同意或已完成请求。有行动依赖对方答复时，标待确认，等实际回复；若临近真实决策点，可做一次针对性的跟进。没有答复时按你能控制的备选行动处理，不连发“在吗”。
5. **误发或话题变复杂时修正。** 发错对象时立即按渠道可用功能纠正并清楚通知相关人，不假设撤回能消除别人已看见的内容；若双方理解越聊越分叉，提议换一个双方可用的通话/当面方式，结束后用一条短消息回写已实际确认的结果。

### 成功条件

你已把一条指明具体事情、关键事实和是否需要动作的消息发往正确对象的可用渠道；你知道它是“已发送 / 等待答复 / 对方明确确认 / 已实际完成”哪一种状态。可由消息本身理解，不要求读者查三十条旧聊天。发送、已读、同意和执行是四个不同事件；只按实际证据记状态。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只发“好，那个就这样”：** 补上具体活动、所指版本和这次要对方做什么。
- **发给同名联系人或错误群：** 立即在正确渠道重发并说明前一条误发；涉及私人资料时按实际渠道和相关方处理，不以撤回按钮保证未被看见。
- **“明天三点”跨了地区或午夜：** 改为日历日期、明确时区和具体开始时间，并读回核对。
- **正文写“见附件”却没有附件：** 补发实际文件，说明前条不完整；不要让对方依据空引用行动。
- **已读但没回应，你把行动写成已完成：** 改为“已读/待答”，对有真实时限的请求做一次具体跟进。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

本 Skill 适用于普通、低敏感短时文字沟通。平台的送达、已读、撤回和邀请功能不同，具体状态以实际工具为准；本文不声称所有软件都能保证收件。阅读、视觉或语言习惯不同的人可选择双方都能用的简明文字、语音或可访问格式；若用语音补充，也把影响行动的结论写回可读消息。多补一行上下文通常比让对方猜“那个”是哪一个更省时间。

## English

Use this Skill when you need to send one ordinary short message to a person or small group who may not be in the same live conversation. They should be able to tell **which matter it concerns and whether they need to act** from this message. Choose a usable channel, verify recipients, supply minimal context, and record the real send state. This does not require an immediate reply. Account verification, sensitive records, crisis notices, and formal legal or financial communication are outside this flow. Messaging apps are variants, not separate Skills.

### Preparation and inputs

Decide whether the main purpose is **informing, asking, confirming, or requesting an action**. Prefer one main purpose. Choose a channel the person actually uses and that fits the matter. If a change must be known very soon, ordinary asynchronous text may not be enough; use your established timely contact method and verify, rather than treating “sent” as “notified.” Check the actual recipient or group membership. A wrong group can turn a private detail into a broadcast.

### Execution

1. **Anchor the matter first.** Name the person, event, or date: “About Saturday's book swap...” Avoid “That works” or “Do it as before.” Even yesterday's discussion may compete with several others in the reader's day. If quoting an earlier message, check that the reference is the right one and visible to them.
2. **Give the fact and next action.** Briefly state the change or question, relevant place, time, or amount, and what the other person should do. If no reply is needed, say so: “No reply needed unless the venue changes.” Give a deadline only when real, with calendar date, time, and region or time zone. For people elsewhere, use the timezone task; “tomorrow afternoon” may mean a different local date when they read it.
3. **Read it once from their side before sending.** Check recipient, name, date and place, whether the attachment is actually attached, and whether an address, ID, or full private itinerary is inappropriate for every recipient. Split five unrelated questions or switch to a mutually usable live exchange. Short does not mean deleting vital context. Make the text carry its own point for small screens or screen readers rather than relying entirely on tiny words inside an image.
4. **Keep the real state after sending.** A successful send means the message entered a channel. “Read” does not prove agreement or that the requested action happened. If the action depends on an answer, mark it pending and wait for the actual reply. Near a real decision point, make one targeted follow-up. Without an answer, use an alternative you control rather than sending repeated “Are you there?” messages.
5. **Repair a wrong send or expanding thread.** If sent to the wrong person, correct through the available channel and tell the relevant people clearly. Do not assume an unsend button erased what someone saw. If interpretations diverge across a long thread, propose a call or in-person method both can use, then send one short written recap of what was actually agreed.

### Success

You sent a message to the right recipient through a usable channel that identifies the matter, key facts, and whether an action is needed. You know whether it is “sent / awaiting reply / explicitly confirmed / actually completed.” The message can stand without thirty earlier chat lines. Sent, read, agreed, and done are four different events; record only what the evidence supports.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Only “Okay, that one” was sent:** Add the actual event, version, and requested action.
- **A similarly named contact or wrong group got it:** Resend through the right channel and explain the earlier mistake. For private detail, handle the actual channel and people involved; unsend is not proof it went unseen.
- **“Three tomorrow” crosses regions or midnight:** Replace it with a calendar date, explicit time zone, and start time, then read back.
- **The message says “see attached” with no attachment:** Send the real file and state that the earlier message was incomplete.
- **Read without reply is logged as action completed:** Correct it to “read / awaiting answer” and make one specific follow-up for a real deadline.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary, low-sensitivity short text. Delivery, read, unsend, and invitation features vary by platform; check the tool you are actually using. Reading, sight, and language needs may call for plain text, voice, or another accessible format both can use. If you supplement with voice, write back any action-changing conclusion in a readable message. One extra line of context often costs less than making someone guess which “that” you meant.
