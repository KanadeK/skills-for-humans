## 中文

当你做好一份**安全状态可确认**的酱汁、油酸调味汁或炖汁，却看见油和水相分开时，用这份 Skill 先辨别“本来会短暂分层”“只是表面浮油”还是“本应均匀却破乳”，然后决定临用混匀、撇去浮油、按原配方小样补救，或停止。目标是这顿饭能安全、诚实地使用它；不是把所有分层都搅成同一种酱。口味、外观和重新混匀都不能证明储存安全。

### 准备与输入

先停止继续猛加热或添加更多油。找到原配方、包装或实际食材记录：这份原本应是油醋汁、带浮油的炖汁/肉汁、稳定乳化酱，还是含蛋奶或巧克力的特殊酱？确认制作、放置和储存史仍可靠、这顿会怎样使用、有无可安全操作的干净小碗、勺与搅拌器。只在可安全尝味的阶段取样；不知道原料或时间/温度史就按当地食品安全指导停止。

这份 Skill 不修复生蛋或未熟食材的安全问题，不将罐藏、发酵或自制浸油食品改成可常温保存品。明显凝固的蛋奶颗粒、焦味或霉变也不是“多搅几下”的油水分层。

### 执行

1. **辨认原本应有什么结构。** 看配方及未分离时的目标：普通油醋汁可能只需在取用前短暂分散；汤、炖汁或肉汁上浮的游离油可能本来就不应被强行打入水相；原应顺滑的稳定乳化酱若出现油层和颗粒，才需要判断是否破乳。没有配方或目标、分不清机制时，不发明通用修复剂。
2. **先隔离小样。** 用干净工具取少量到小碗，原份保持不动。观察轻搅后是再次变成预期状态、很快复分层，还是仍有颗粒、油圈或焦味。别从外观推断食品一定安全；安全史仍由原料和处理记录决定。
3. **若是本来短暂混合的油醋汁：** 在原料和保存史均可靠时，按容器安全条件搅匀或摇匀这次要用的一小份，立即用于原定食物。它再次分层可以是正常的；不用为了永不分层持续加酸、油或陌生乳化剂。与 H038 制作流程不同，这里只处理已做成品临上桌的状态。
4. **若是炖汁或肉汁的表面浮油：** 配方与目标允许时，小心用勺从表面撇去不想要的多余游离油，再看下面的汁是否仍可按用途使用。不要把浮油强力打回去仅为制造均匀外观。若底下味道或结构另有问题，按那一项另行判断。
5. **若原本应稳定乳化却破了：** 停止继续加油或升温，查这份**具体配方**是否给出可执行的复乳化方法和温度边界。只有方法与现有材料、安全状态都相符，才在小样按其顺序试，再核对目标质地与味道。对简单、配方支持的冷油水酱，水相与油相可按其做法边搅边缓慢结合；这不自动适用于蛋黄、奶油、巧克力或其他热酱。无可靠方法、过热后蛋奶凝结或小样失败时停下，换酱或改用安全且愿意吃的部分。
6. **决定是否扩大与保存。** 只有小样按该酱的目标确实可用、没有改变安全条件、用餐者接受时才处理这顿需要的量。任何剩余按实际成分和当地食品安全指导冷却、储存或丢弃；重新乳化不延长保质期。无法确认安全、没有合适方法或无人愿意吃时，停止补救并丢弃。

### 成功条件

你能说清看到的是什么分层、原酱预期是什么结构，且这顿有一份仍安全并适合实际用途的酱，或明确选择换酱/停止。短暂重混、撇油和破乳后的配方专用补救分别记录；不把其中一种当所有分离的答案。

### 常见报错与补救

- **油醋汁过会儿又分层就不停加配料：** 在原料与保存史可靠时，只临用混合这次份量；短暂分散无需升级成永久乳化。
- **把炖汁表面油层打进整锅：** 先对照配方用途，适合撇油时只取去表面多余部分；别为外观把油变成新的口感问题。
- **蛋奶或巧克力酱破乳后用油醋汁的摇瓶法：** 停止；它们的结构和温度边界不同，只用该配方可靠的修复流程或放弃。
- **小样短暂顺滑就认定整锅和隔夜剩余都没问题：** 只按目标上桌状态验收这顿份量；储存仍看原料与时间/温度史。
- **需要生蛋、陌生乳化剂或长时间室温试验才能补救：** 停止当前方案，选适用的安全替代或丢弃；不要为了不浪费扩大风险。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里只处理普通食物的**质量判断**，不靠闻、看、尝或搅拌证明食品安全。不同配方的油、水、酸、蛋白、糖和温度决定能否重新混合。握力不足时用稳定小碗和适用搅拌器或请可信的人协助，不要求摇晃装满热液的玻璃瓶。撇油会改变味道和份量，临用混匀不保证久放，停下可能需要换酱；这些都比错误的万能修复更诚实。

## English

Use this Skill when a **known-safe finished** sauce, oil-and-acid dressing, or stew liquid shows a separate oil layer. Decide whether this is expected short-lived mixing, unwanted surface fat, or a sauce that should have stayed emulsified. Then remix for immediate use, skim, try a recipe-specific repair on a small sample, or stop. The aim is an honest safe use for this meal, not to stir every separation into one type of sauce. Appearance, flavour, and re-mixing do not establish storage safety.

### Preparation and inputs

Stop hard heating or adding more oil. Find the original recipe, packaging, or ingredient record. Was this meant to be vinaigrette, stew or gravy with surface fat, a stable emulsified sauce, or an egg-, dairy-, or chocolate-based specialty? Confirm reliable preparation, holding, and storage history, the intended use at this meal, and a clean small bowl, spoon, and suitable stirrer. Taste only at a safe stage. If ingredients or time and temperature history are unknown, stop under local food-safety guidance.

This Skill cannot repair the safety of raw egg or undercooked ingredients, and does not turn canned, fermented, or homemade infused-oil food into shelf-stable food. Obvious cooked egg or dairy particles, burnt flavour, or mould are not an oil-and-water split fixed by more stirring.

### Execution

1. **Identify the intended structure.** Check the recipe and former target. An ordinary vinaigrette may only need brief dispersion before use. Free fat on soup, stew, or gravy may be better removed than forced into the liquid. A sauce meant to remain smooth but now showing oil and particles may have broken. Without a recipe or target, do not invent a universal repair ingredient.
2. **Isolate a small trial.** Take a little into a clean bowl and leave the original alone. Observe whether gentle mixing returns it to the expected state, whether it quickly separates again, or whether grains, an oil ring, or burnt flavour remain. Do not infer food safety from appearance; ingredient and handling records still control that decision.
3. **For an intentionally short-lived oil-and-acid dressing:** If ingredients and handling remain sound, stir or shake only this meal's serving under safe container conditions, then use it promptly on the intended food. Re-separation can be normal. Do not keep adding acid, oil, or an unknown emulsifier to force permanence. H038 covers making a fresh dressing; this branch handles an already made serving.
4. **For surface fat on stew or gravy:** If the recipe and intended use permit, carefully spoon off unwanted free fat and check whether the liquid below works. Do not force fat back in solely for an even appearance. Judge any separate problem with flavour or thickness on its own.
5. **For a sauce intended to stay emulsified:** Stop adding oil or raising heat. Look for an executable repair and temperature limit for **that exact recipe**. Only when it fits the ingredients and safe conditions should you trial its sequence in a small sample, then check the intended texture and flavour. A simple cold oil-and-water sauce may, where its method permits, be combined by slowly adding oil to its watery base while whisking; that does not apply automatically to egg, dairy, chocolate, or another hot sauce. Stop and replace the sauce or use only a safe acceptable part if there is no reliable method, heat has curdled egg or dairy, or the trial fails.
6. **Decide whether to scale and store.** Handle only the amount needed now after the sample genuinely meets that sauce's target, safety conditions are unchanged, and diners accept it. Cool, store, or discard any remainder according to its actual ingredients and local guidance; renewed emulsification does not extend shelf life. If safety is unclear, no suitable method exists, or nobody wants it, stop and discard.

### Success

You can name the observed split and the intended structure. This meal has a still-safe sauce fit for its actual use, or you explicitly choose a replacement or stop. Record brief re-mixing, skimming, recipe-specific repair, and stopping as different decisions; none is every sauce's answer.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Vinaigrette re-separates and you keep adding ingredients:** If ingredients and handling remain sound, mix only the serving just before use. Temporary dispersion need not become a permanent emulsion.
- **You beat surface fat back into the whole stew:** Check the recipe's purpose first. When skimming fits, remove only unwanted top fat rather than creating another mouthfeel problem.
- **You shake a split egg, dairy, or chocolate sauce like vinaigrette:** Stop. Its structure and temperature limits differ; use only a reliable method for that sauce or abandon it.
- **A smooth moment in the trial becomes a claim that the pot and overnight leftovers are fine:** Judge today's serving at its intended state. Storage still depends on ingredients and time and temperature history.
- **Repair would need raw egg, an unknown emulsifier, or long room-temperature trials:** Stop this route. Choose a safe suitable replacement or discard rather than widening the risk to avoid waste.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is a **quality decision** for ordinary food. Smell, sight, taste, and whisking cannot prove safety. Oil, water, acid, protein, sugar, and temperature determine what can be recombined in a specific recipe. If grip is limited, use a stable small bowl and suitable mixer or trusted help; do not shake a full hot glass jar. Skimming changes flavour and amount, brief remixing may not last, and stopping may require another sauce. Those are honest outcomes.
