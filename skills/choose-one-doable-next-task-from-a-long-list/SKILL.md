## 中文

当你面对过长的普通非紧急待办清单，**还没决定此刻做哪一件**时，加载这份 Skill。结果是按实际截止、影响、依赖、时间和可用材料选出**一个当前可执行任务**，并说明没选的事项如何保留；随后真正动手归 H261。这里不替人判断医疗、法律、财务或紧急安全事项，也不把“最容易”自动等于“最重要”。

### 准备与输入

看清当下可用的时间段、地点、能量/可及条件和你有权使用的工具。清单里明显属于危机或专业高风险事项的，转相应专业渠道；本篇只选普通任务。先从今天或近期真正会受影响的项目取少量候选，不必把几百行重新排完。对写着“生活”“工作”这类大标题，先保留原条目但找其能交付的一小件，不拿空泛名词参与排序。

### 执行

1. **筛硬约束。** 先标实际已知的截止、预约、别人正等待你给输入的项目和拖延会让选择消失的项目。来源不明的“今天必须做”先核，而不是让红色字体替你决定。
2. **把不能此刻做的移到等待。** 对每个靠前候选问：现在有材料、许可、地点和人吗？需要别人先回复的记“待答”，需要到指定时间/场地的记“届时”，不把它们当此刻可执行；必要时“向 X 请求 Y”可单独成为一个现在能做的普通动作。
3. **在可做项里比一轮。** 对两三个候选看真实影响、截止余量、是否解除后续依赖，以及是否装得进当前时间和能力条件。你不用精确预测分钟；只问“这段时间能留下什么可见进度”。不要靠任务看起来令人愉快就忽略真正有时间后果的事。
4. **选一项并写清选择理由。** 写“现在先做 A，因为 B 的确认要等回复、C 的截止较后；我眼前有 A 的材料，能做到 X 这个小停点”。若两个任务难分，选对别人等待影响更大的；仍相同则选能实际开始的一件，不无限造排序表。
5. **保留余项的返回点。** 给未选且有期限的项目留真实下次查看时间或提醒，来源和状态保持可找回。清单不必清空。把已选任务交给 H261 的启动流程或你自己的下一步动作；本篇以决定结束，不把“选好了”说成“做完了”。

### 完成、常见问题与补救

你指出了一个此刻具备条件的普通任务、选择依据和可见小停点，并让更紧迫但暂不可做的事项留在可信等待/提醒处。只给所有行贴 A/B/C 却仍不知道现在做哪件，不算完成。

- **所有任务都被标“紧急”：** 核真实外部截止与后果；没有证据的紧急感不自动排第一。
- **排第一的任务缺关键输入：** 记待答，选一个可做项；或把一次明确求取作为当前动作。
- **只挑最轻松的避开有后果任务：** 再比较截止、他人依赖和当前窗口，解释自己的取舍。
- **清单大到无法全看：** 先看近期会受影响的少量项目，保留其余原位，别在整理系统里耗尽可用时间。
- **时间/体力或可及条件变化：** 重新核此刻能做什么，不把原排序当不可修改的命令。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

日历、纸清单或可访问任务应用都能装下这次选择。期限、影响和身体条件由你当前事实决定；本篇不宣称一套万能优先级公式。某些事项会暂时留着，但留下实际可追的状态比把所有任务塞进“现在”更诚实。

## English

Load this Skill when an overlong list of ordinary nonurgent tasks is in front of you and **you have not chosen what to do now**. Use actual deadlines, effects, dependencies, time, and available materials to choose **one currently doable task**, while leaving the rest findable. Starting that chosen task belongs to H261. This does not decide medical, legal, financial, or urgent safety matters, and “easiest” is not automatically “most important.”

### Preparation and inputs

Note the current time window, place, energy and access conditions, and tools you may use. Route obvious crises or specialised high-risk matters to appropriate support; this Skill selects ordinary tasks only. Pull a small candidate set from items truly affected today or soon rather than re-ranking hundreds of lines. For vague headings like “life” or “work,” retain the original but identify a smaller deliverable before comparing.

### Execution

1. **Mark hard constraints.** Notice verified deadlines, appointments, items where another person waits for your input, and items whose options expire with delay. Check the source of a “must do today” claim rather than letting red text make the decision.
2. **Move what cannot happen now to waiting.** For each leading candidate ask whether materials, permission, place, and people are available. Label an item needing a reply “waiting” and one tied to a later place or time “then.” A clear “ask X for Y” may itself be a doable ordinary action now.
3. **Compare only doable items once.** Among two or three candidates, consider real impact, time left, whether the item unlocks later work, and whether it fits your present window and abilities. Do not demand minute-perfect forecasts; ask what visible progress can fit. A pleasant task should not silently displace one with real time consequences.
4. **Pick one and write the reason.** For example: “A now because B needs a reply and C is due later; I have A's inputs and can reach small stop X.” If two remain close, prefer the one blocking another person. If still equal, pick one you can actually begin instead of producing another ranking table.
5. **Leave a return point for the rest.** Give unchosen timed items a genuine next check or reminder, keeping source and status findable. The list need not be empty. Pass the chosen task to H261's starting procedure or your own next action. This Skill ends with a decision, not a claim that work is done.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

You named one ordinary task you can do now, a reason and visible small stop, while more time-sensitive blocked items remain in a trustworthy waiting or reminder place. Giving every row an A/B/C grade without knowing what to do now is not completion.

- **Everything is labelled urgent:** Verify external deadlines and consequences. Unevidenced urgency does not automatically win.
- **The top task lacks an essential input:** Mark it waiting and choose a doable one, or make a clear request the current action.
- **You pick only the easiest item to avoid a consequential one:** Compare deadline, other people's dependencies, and the present window again and explain the tradeoff.
- **The list is too large to read fully:** Inspect a few items affected soon and keep the rest in place instead of spending the whole window redesigning the list.
- **Time, energy, or accessibility conditions change:** Recheck what is doable now rather than treating an old order as a command.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

A calendar, paper list, or accessible task app can support this choice. Deadlines, impact, and physical conditions come from your current facts. No universal priority formula is promised. Some items remain, but a real traceable state is more honest than putting every task in “now.”
