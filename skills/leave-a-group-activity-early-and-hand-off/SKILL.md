## 中文

当你正在参加普通的游戏、聚餐、出游或其他自愿集体活动，却需要在约好的结束前离开时，加载这份 Skill。目标是**把别人正在依赖你的部分交代清楚，然后明确退出**。你不必证明离开的理由足够“正当”，也不应悄悄把未完成的共同任务留给别人。

这不是工作值班、正式志愿岗位、儿童看护、付费合同、紧急撤离或受到威胁时的退出流程。那些情形按真实责任或求助渠道处理。若你必须立刻离开以保护自身安全，先离开安全地点，能安全联系时再做必要通知。

### 准备与输入

花一分钟看三件事：你是否持有钥匙、票、车辆、共同用品或预订信息；你是否答应了接送、付款、保管、带队或某个环节；谁需要知道你走了才能调整。没有这些依赖时，简短告诉相关人就够，不用召开退场会议。

### 执行

1. **说出决定与时间。** 找到负责协调或会直接受影响的人，说：“我需要现在/在 [时间] 离开。我先把 [具体事项] 交接好。”你可以只说“我需要走了”，不需要公开健康、家庭或情绪细节。
2. **列出真正不能随你消失的依赖。** 共用物品交还或明确谁接手；钥匙、预订、票和地址把必要信息交给有权使用的人；你答应的接送、食物或活动环节要说明还能否履行。不要把个人账号、私人密码或他人的隐私当作交接材料。
3. **请求接手，未答应就标缺口。** “车钥匙我交给小林；回程还有两人需要另找交通。你能负责联系他们吗？”只有对方明确答应，才算交接。没有接手者时，及时告诉受影响者这个部分取消或待重排，不把一个点头表情当成完整安排。
4. **处理共同费用和物品。** 查清已发生的订单或分摊约定，向实际承担的人说明哪一部分需要后来核对；不要在离场口头保证退款，也不要让别人为你新花的钱自动买单。把借来的东西还给原主或约好可执行的归还方式。
5. **确认离开方式。** 检查自己的交通、方向和必要随身物；若原本共用交通，告知其他乘客或司机变更，别让人以为你还在车上或会回来接送。离开时给相关人一句“我现在走了，已交接 [事项]；未解决的是 [事项]，下一步由 [人/时间] 确认”。

### 完成条件

相关人知道你已退出；你手中的共同物品和仍影响别人的承诺已有明确接手、取消或待处理状态；没人需要靠猜测你是否还在场。对方不喜欢这个决定，不等于你没有完成交接。若情况迫使你先走，之后能安全联系时补发必要事实。

### 常见报错与补救

- **你没有承担任何任务：** 告诉会找你的人“我先走了”，确认自己的物品和交通，然后离开；不要制造虚假的交接清单。
- **没人愿意接手：** 直说该环节暂停或取消，让实际受影响者重新安排；不能因为你问过一次就把责任静默转给别人。
- **临走时有人要求解释私人原因：** 重复你会离开的事实和必要交接；可以不披露原因。若对方不接受，也不要用虚构承诺换取放行。
- **已经离开才想起共用物品或承诺：** 尽快直接联系能处理的人，说明物品位置、当前状态和你能采取的补救；不要让群聊猜是谁拿走了钥匙。
- **你的退出改变人数或预订：** 提醒协调者重新核对容量、座位、票或订单；实际更改以运营方条款为准。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这是一场自愿、普通的集体活动，你有权决定自己是否继续参加；你也要诚实交代自己控制的共同依赖。不同关系对告别方式的期待不同，按实际影响选择当面、电话或文字通知。有人可能失望，活动可能需重排；不评价他们或自己的品格。你的退出不是整场活动的绩效考核。

## English

Load this Skill when you are already at an ordinary game, meal, trip, or other voluntary group activity and need to leave before its agreed end. Your goal is to **hand over what others currently depend on, then make your exit clear**. You do not have to prove that your reason is “good enough,” but should not silently leave an unfinished shared task with someone else.

This is not the exit process for a work shift, formal volunteer duty, child supervision, paid contract, emergency evacuation, or a threatening situation. Follow the real responsibility or support route in those cases. If you must leave immediately for your safety, get somewhere safe first and give necessary notice when contact is safe.

### Preparation and inputs

Take a minute to check three things: whether you hold a key, ticket, vehicle, shared supply, or booking detail; whether you promised a ride, payment, custody, leadership, or part of the activity; and who must know you left in order to adapt. If none applies, a brief notice to the relevant people is enough; there is no need for an exit meeting.

### Execution

1. **State the decision and time.** Find the organizer or directly affected person and say, “I need to leave now/at [time]. I will first hand over [specific item].” “I need to go” is enough; you need not disclose health, family, or emotional details to the group.
2. **List the dependencies that cannot vanish with you.** Return or explicitly transfer shared items. Give a key, booking, ticket, or address detail to someone entitled to use it. Explain whether you can still deliver a promised ride, food, or activity segment. Do not pass on personal account access, private passwords, or another person's private information as handoff material.
3. **Ask for a handoff; mark a gap until accepted.** “I gave the car key to Lin. Two people still need a ride back. Can you contact them?” A task is handed over only when the other person agrees. If nobody takes it, tell affected people that part is cancelled or needs replanning; a reaction icon is not a complete arrangement.
4. **Deal with shared cost and belongings.** Check actual orders or cost-sharing agreements and tell the people who bear a cost what must be checked later. Do not promise a refund while leaving or assume others will pay a new cost for you. Return borrowed items or agree on a workable return method with their owner.
5. **Confirm your route out.** Check your own transport, direction, and essentials. If transport was shared, tell the other passengers or driver what changed; do not leave people believing you are still aboard or will return for them. On exit, say, “I am leaving now. [Item] was handed over. [Item] is unresolved; [person/time] will confirm next.”

### Success

Relevant people know you have left. Shared items and promises you controlled have an accepted handoff, an explicit cancellation, or a visible pending state. Nobody has to guess whether you are still there. Others may dislike the decision without invalidating the handoff. If circumstances require you to go first, send essential facts afterward when contact is safe.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You had no assigned task:** Tell anyone who would look for you, check your things and transport, then leave. Do not invent a handoff list.
- **Nobody accepts the handoff:** Say the segment is paused or cancelled so the people affected can replan. Asking once does not silently transfer responsibility.
- **Someone demands a private reason at the door:** Repeat the fact of departure and necessary handoff. You can keep the reason private; do not make a false promise to gain permission to leave.
- **You remember a shared item or promise only after leaving:** Contact someone who can act as soon as practical. State the item's location, current status, and what repair you can offer; do not leave the group guessing who took the key.
- **Your departure changes the count or booking:** Prompt the coordinator to recheck capacity, seats, tickets, or orders. Actual changes follow the operator's terms.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This is a voluntary ordinary group activity. You decide whether to keep participating, while honestly accounting for shared dependencies you control. Relationships differ in how a goodbye is expected; use in-person, phone, or text notice according to the real impact. Someone may be disappointed and the event may need replanning. Neither their character nor yours needs a rating. Your exit is not the event's performance review.
