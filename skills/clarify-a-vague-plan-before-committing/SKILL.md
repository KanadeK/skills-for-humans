## 中文

你和别人只说了“改天见”“下周一起去吧”或类似片段，尚不清楚**究竟邀请参加什么、何时何地、以什么方式参与**，还不能负责任地回答“去”或“已约好”时，用这份 Skill。产物是一个对方能接受或拒绝的具体提议，或者一张仍未定的关键问题清单。它停在**澄清与提出可答复版本**；H182 才处理接受后双方对同一版本的确认。

### 准备与输入

回看消息或回想原话，分两列写“已经明确说过”和“我自己补上的猜测”。至少核对活动、具体日期/当地时间范围、地点或线上入口、谁参加，以及会改变可行性的费用、时长、交通或可及限制。不是所有小细节都要先定；优先问那些不清楚就无法选择、出门或预订的条件。若多个参与者在不同城市，具体时区归专门核对流程，不能把“晚上八点”当全球相同。

### 执行

1. **找出最先挡住决定的空白。** “那家店”可能有三处分店；“下周”有七天；“一起”不说明是否还有别人。选一至三个关键未知，不用一口气让对方填写活动策划表。把自己猜的答案保留为提议，不冒充对方已承诺。
2. **用一条短消息提出可核对版本。** 例如：“你说下周喝咖啡，是想周三傍晚在〔店名/地址〕见面，还是还没定时间地点？我周三六点后可以。” 若活动本身也没定，先问活动是什么；必要时给一个你真的愿意的选项，但不要替对方选择。
3. **把参与方式和限制补到刚好能答复。** 是现场还是线上、每人自行到达还是一起走、是否有票或预约、预计持续多久？仅问会改变参与选择或实际到达的项目。某场馆营业、费用或可进入性若是关键，用本次活动的正式信息核实，不把对方的印象当服务承诺。
4. **读回对方实际回答。** 对方若给出具体版本，简短复述日期、时间、明确地点/入口或线上方式、参与者与仍需决定的关键项，并询问是否与你理解一致。答复若是“看看”“可能”或只回答了地点，就把其余保持开放；不要把一个表情或你发出的消息当双方已同意。
5. **给开放项设下一步，别先承担成本。** 若暂时只是想法，说“那先记为想法，等〔关键条件〕确定再决定”；需要订票或占位时，先说明真实截止和由谁去核实，未经同意不代对方付款或加日历。清楚的提议形成后，每个人再分别决定接受、拒绝或提出新条件。

### 成功条件

你能够清楚说出当前版本是**仅有想法 / 可答复的提议 / 仍有列明问题**，而不是凭语气把它升级为已确认约定。若形成提议，对方能判断活动、时间、地点或参与方式和重要限制；若尚未形成，知道是谁要回答哪个问题、何时再看。完成澄清不等于任何人已接受。

### 常见报错与补救

- **把“下周有空”当作同意周三赴约：** 把可用时间与参加承诺分开，再问活动和具体版本。
- **“那家店门口”有两个入口或多家分店：** 补全店名、地址和会合入口；未确定就标待定。
- **一条消息问了十几件事，对方只回“行”：** 缩回到阻止决定的两三项，逐项核对“行”究竟指什么。
- **不同人回复了不同版本：** 把目前各版本并列，提出一个需要每人确认的提议；不替未发声的人答应。
- **活动快开始但仍无回复：** 按未确认处理，及时说明自己的安排决定；不依据猜测出发或让别人替你买票。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

适用于普通自愿小型约见，不处理大型多人排期、已确定安排的变更或紧急情况。文化和关系决定询问语气，但不能改变“没有说过的条件仍是未知”。文字不便可当面复述或用双方可访问的方式核对；视觉/听觉需要可用文字记录或可信协助。多发一条澄清消息可能让计划暂时没有那么浪漫，却能减少两个人到不同门口寻找“那家店”的时间。

## English

Use this Skill when you and someone else have only said “let's meet sometime” or “we should go next week,” and **the activity, time, place, or way to join is too vague for an honest yes or a confirmed plan**. Finish with a proposal they can accept or decline, or a short list of questions still open. This Skill stops at clarification and an answerable version. H182 handles accepting and mutually confirming that version.

### Preparation and inputs

Look at the messages or recall the actual words. Separate “explicitly said” from “I filled this in myself.” Check activity, calendar date and local time range, place or online entry, attendees, and any cost, duration, travel, or access condition that changes feasibility. Every small detail need not be settled; ask first about things without which you cannot decide, reach the place, or book. For people in different cities, verify the actual time zone through the dedicated flow. “Eight tonight” is not one universal instant.

### Execution

1. **Find the first decision-blocking gaps.** “That café” may have several branches; “next week” has seven days; “together” does not name everyone. Select one to three critical unknowns rather than asking for an event plan. Keep your inferred answers as proposals, not claims that someone agreed.
2. **Send one checkable version.** For example: “When you said coffee next week, did you mean Wednesday evening at [café and address], or are time and place still open? I can do after six on Wednesday.” If the activity itself is unknown, ask what it is first. You may offer one option you genuinely want, but do not select on their behalf.
3. **Add only participation details that change an answer.** Is it in person or online, independent travel or together, ticketed or reserved, and roughly how long? Ask only what changes attendance or actual arrival. For a venue's hours, charges, or access that matters, verify this event's official information rather than treating someone's impression as a promise from the service.
4. **Read back the actual reply.** If they give a concrete version, restate the calendar date, time, named place and entrance or online method, attendees, and any key open item. Ask if that is what they meant. “Maybe,” “we'll see,” or an answer only about location leaves the other parts open. An emoji or your outgoing message is not mutual agreement.
5. **Give open items a next action without incurring cost.** If it is still an idea, say “Let's keep it as an idea until [condition] is known.” For tickets or reservations, state a real cutoff and who will check it; do not pay or add someone to a calendar without agreement. After the proposal is clear, each person can accept, decline, or offer different terms.

### Success

You can identify the current state as **an idea only / an answerable proposal / named unanswered questions**, rather than upgrading it to a confirmed appointment from tone alone. An answerable proposal says what, when, where or how to join, and any important constraint. Otherwise, the owner and next check for each critical question are clear. Clarification alone does not mean anyone accepted.

### Common errors and recovery

- **“Free next week” is treated as agreement to Wednesday:** Separate availability from attendance and ask for the activity and exact version.
- **“At that café” has two doors or several branches:** Add full venue, address, and meeting entrance; otherwise mark it open.
- **One message asks fifteen questions and receives “sure”:** Reduce to the two or three blockers and ask what the “sure” referred to.
- **Different people reply with different versions:** Put the versions side by side and propose one for each person to confirm. Do not speak for silent attendees.
- **The event is near but no answer arrives:** Treat it as unconfirmed, communicate your own decision in time, and do not set out or allow someone to buy a ticket for you on a guess.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers a small voluntary ordinary meeting, not large-group scheduling, changes to an already settled plan, or an emergency. Relationship and culture change how you ask, but unsaid details remain unknown. If text is hard to use, read back in person or through a channel both can access; a written note or trusted help may support sight or hearing needs. One more clarifying message may make the plan less poetic, but saves a search for “that café” at two different entrances.
