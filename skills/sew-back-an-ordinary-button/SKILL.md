## 中文

当你的一件**普通、非防护用途**衣物掉了一颗功能纽扣，而布面、扣眼和其余闭合结构都完好时，用这份 Skill 把纽扣缝回可正常扣合的位置。结果是闭合时不绷、不脱，线结稳固；颜色完全相同是可选项。扣眼破裂、布料撕开、铆钉/按扣或承重附件需要别的修复，不能靠多缠几圈线伪装完成。

### 准备与输入

找回原纽扣或衣服附带的备用扣。若没有，量一量现有扣与扣眼，选能正常通过且不会轻易滑出的相近尺寸；看它是有两/四个贯通孔的平扣，还是背面有一只环的带柄扣。找原线孔，或把两片衣襟按实际穿着位置叠好，通过扣眼确定新位置；参考相邻纽扣的间距和方向，不按衣服性别预设左右。检查布料是否已被拉薄或撕裂。准备能顺畅通过扣孔与布料的手缝针、合适强度的线、剪刀和可稳定放针的明亮桌面；需要时用顶针或让可信的人协助穿针。

若这颗扣子属于防护衣物、救生/攀爬装备、婴幼儿易吞小部件，或布面/扣眼已经破损，先停止本流程并找对应制造商或专业维修。普通衣物也不应在仍穿在身上时下针。

### 执行

1. **先确定位置和扣型。** 把衣物平放，把两片衣料照真正扣合的样子重叠，轻轻标记扣眼对到的底层位置；若旧线孔可靠，可与之核对。拿纽扣实际试穿扣眼，太小会滑脱，太大可能扯坏扣眼。位置确定后再穿针，避免把错误位置缝得很牢。
2. **准备线与起点。** 按手中线和布料选择能用手控制的长度，穿针后可把线对折并在线尾打结；从衣物背面把针带到标记处。检查针没有弯曲、断尖，手指不放在针将穿出的背面。小心保管未用的针和断线。
3. **平扣走贯通孔。** 把两孔或四孔扣居中放好，让针从布背面穿上一个孔、从对应另一个孔回到背面，沿同一对孔重复形成几道均匀固定线；四孔扣再处理另一对。不要把布料拉皱。若衣料较厚、扣合需要空隙，可按纽扣结构用可取出的细间隔物在扣面下留一点活动空间，缝好后取出，再用线轻绕扣底的线束固定；薄衬衣不必强行做很长的扣柄。
4. **带柄扣走背后小环。** 从布背面到正面，穿过纽扣背面的环，再返回布背面，重复到扣子坐稳。不要假装带柄扣的装饰面有贯通孔，也不要从一个点猛拉得布面起皱。
5. **在背面收结并试用。** 在线从布背面出来时打牢结、剪去多余线，把针立刻放回安全位置。将衣物平放后轻扣、解扣几次：纽扣应通过扣眼、不意外弹开，布面不过度鼓起；轻轻拉动扣子，确认线结没有松脱。若扣眼被拉扯或位置不对，先拆这一小处重定位，不继续缝更多扣去掩盖。

### 成功条件

纽扣的尺寸与位置适合原扣眼，穿孔扣或带柄扣按实际结构固定，背面结头稳固，衣物正常扣解时不绷、不滑脱，也没有新增破洞或遗留针。手头没有合适纽扣、针线或布面已损伤时，停止并安排可靠修复同样是完成判断。

### 常见报错与补救

- **扣子大小不合，缝好仍会滑出或扯扣眼：** 拆下换与扣眼相容的尺寸，不用更多线圈补尺寸。
- **对着旧孔缝完，衣襟闭合歪斜：** 先以真实扣合状态重新定位；旧孔可能已被拉伸，不能单独作证。
- **线绷太紧，布面皱或厚衣料扣不上：** 检查是否需给平扣留适度空隙；必要时重缝，不靠强拉扣眼。
- **线结松或纽扣晃：** 在布面仍完好时拆除松线、重新用合适线固定；不要用胶水堵住扣眼。
- **针尖、断针或扣子去向不明：** 停止穿用并找回锐物或小件；没确认无残留前不交给别人穿。
- **布面或扣眼裂了：** 停止加线，转衣物开线/小破口处理或专业维修；纽扣本身不承重修布。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里只处理普通非安全关键衣物和能用针线复位的纽扣。不同厚度、织法与纽扣背面结构改变针、线和活动空间；以原衣结构和可实际扣合为准。看不清小孔时使用明亮灯、放大镜或可信帮助；手部力量不足时用顶针和稳定桌面，不在身上缝。备用扣可能与旧扣略有色差，但功能应真实可靠。

## English

Use this Skill when a functional button has come off an **ordinary, non-protective garment** and its fabric, buttonhole, and other closure parts remain intact. Sew a button back where it fastens normally. The result is a closure that does not pull or pop open, with a secure thread finish. A perfect colour match is optional. A torn buttonhole, ripped fabric, rivet, snap, or load-bearing attachment needs a different repair; extra thread cannot disguise that gap.

### Preparation and inputs

Find the original button or a spare supplied with the garment. Otherwise compare an existing button and the buttonhole, choosing a similar size that passes through without slipping out too easily. Identify a flat two- or four-hole sew-through button versus one with a loop on its back. Locate old thread holes, or overlap the garment as worn and use the buttonhole to find the correct position; compare neighbouring buttons rather than assuming a side from gender. Check for thinned or torn fabric. Have a hand needle that passes through the holes and fabric without force, suitable thread, scissors, and a steady well-lit table. Use a thimble or trusted help with threading if useful.

Stop this general workflow for protective clothing, lifesaving or climbing gear, infant-accessible small parts, or already damaged fabric or buttonholes. Seek the maker or appropriate professional repair. Do not sew even an ordinary garment while it is being worn.

### Execution

1. **Choose the place and button type first.** Lay the garment flat, overlap it in its real closed position, and mark where the buttonhole meets the lower layer; compare reliable old holes if present. Try the button through the hole. Too small may slip; too large can strain it. Thread the needle after positioning so an incorrect location does not become a very secure error.
2. **Prepare thread and start.** Choose a length you can control with this fabric and thread. Thread the needle; you may double the thread and knot its end. Bring it from the garment's wrong side to the mark. Check the needle has no bend or broken point, and keep fingers away from where it emerges. Account for unused needles and broken thread.
3. **For a flat sew-through button, use its holes.** Centre it, pass the needle up through one hole and down another, and make several even securing passes through that pair. On a four-hole button, work the other pair too. Do not pucker the fabric. If thicker fabric needs room to close, use a removable slim spacer under the button as its structure permits. Remove it after stitching and wrap the thread bundle beneath the button to support that space. A thin shirt does not need an oversized shank.
4. **For a shank button, use the back loop.** Pass from the fabric's wrong side through the loop and back to the wrong side several times until it sits securely. Do not hunt for holes through its decorative face or pull so hard that the fabric gathers at one point.
5. **Knot on the back and test.** Tie off securely on the wrong side, trim excess thread, and return the needle to a safe place immediately. Button and unbutton the garment gently a few times. It should pass through, stay closed, and leave the fabric reasonably flat. Give the button a light pull to check the knot. If it pulls the buttonhole or sits in the wrong place, undo this small repair and reposition before adding more stitches.

### Success

Button size and placement fit the original buttonhole. Its actual hole or shank structure is sewn securely, the back knot holds, and normal fastening does not pull, slip, or create a new hole. No needle remains in the garment. With no suitable button, thread, or intact fabric, stopping for a reliable repair is also a complete decision.

### Common errors and recovery

- **A mismatched button still slips or strains the hole:** Remove it and choose a compatible size. More thread cannot change button diameter.
- **Old stitch holes lead to a crooked closure:** Reposition against the garment as actually buttoned. Old holes may have stretched.
- **Tight thread puckers fabric or prevents closing over thickness:** Check whether a flat button needs modest space. Resew if needed; do not force the buttonhole.
- **The knot loosens or the button wobbles:** If the fabric is still sound, remove loose thread and resew with suitable thread. Do not fill the buttonhole with glue.
- **A needle tip, broken needle, or button is unaccounted for:** Stop wearing or handing over the garment until the sharp or small item is found.
- **Fabric or the buttonhole tears:** Stop adding thread and use a small-tear repair or professional help. The button cannot bear the fabric's repair load.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary, non-safety-critical clothing buttons that can be restored with a needle and thread. Fabric thickness, weave, and button-back structure affect needle, thread, and required clearance; use the original construction and actual fastening as the checks. For tiny holes, use bright light, magnification, or trusted help. For limited hand strength, use a thimble and stable work surface rather than sewing on the body. A spare button may differ slightly in colour, but the closure should genuinely work.
