## 中文

当你刚学会或以为学会一项普通概念，想**实际讲给一位愿意听的人**来检查自己能否清楚解释时，加载这份 Skill。目标是用自己的话说明条件、给一个可回来源核对的例子，并识别尚不懂的地方；不是让对方替你背答案，也不是一次发言就证明长期掌握。多人学习会归 H259，隔时独立练习归 H257。

### 准备与输入

选一个能在几分钟讲完的具体问题，例如“一种方法在什么条件下适用”，而非整章或整门课。准备可靠教材/课堂材料的可定位位置和一个**不含私人或受保护大段内容**的小例子。先问对方是否愿意听几分钟，以及其希望的语言、文字/语音或可及形式。医疗、法律、财务等高风险判断不以这份普通学习 Skill 对外提供建议。

### 执行

1. **先说边界和结论。** 用一句话说明“我在解释 X，它在条件 Y 下能做什么；超出 Y 我还不知道”。别先报一串术语，让听者猜为什么重要。
2. **用自己的话拆成两三步。** 说出关键条件、动作和结果之间的关系，不照读原文。需要图、实物或文字时，用对方实际可访问的形式；解释不因声音大就更正确。
3. **跑一个可核的小例子。** 从来源给定的普通情况或自己构造但可按来源检查的例子，指明输入、为什么选这一步、可观察结果和一个不适用边界。若是自造例子，核它没有违背教材条件，不把方便讲的比喻当事实。
4. **让听者指出一处不清楚的地方。** 可以问“哪一步需要我再解释？”或请其说说遇到不同条件会先查什么。对方不愿被测试时尊重其意愿；你也可请其指出术语或跳步，不把听者答错全怪对方。
5. **现场核一处疑点并留下未知。** 听者提出的问题若你不能从来源或现有理解回答，直接说“这点我还没核”，记下问题与原文位置，之后查资料或问教师。能改清楚的地方用自己的话再解释一次，并确认对方是否更明白；仍不明白就停止这轮，不编一个自信但错的解释。

### 完成、常见问题与补救

你已向真实愿意听的人讲过一个窄概念，例子能回来源核对，并留下对方指出的疑点或一项明确未知。对方必须完全学会不是你的完成条件；你讲得流利也不能替代事实核查。

- **例子只换了原文名词却没讲条件：** 加上何时适用、为何这一步和何时不适用。
- **听者提出你不会的问题：** 标未知并查来源；不要为了保持讲者形象当场编答案。
- **对方只是点头：** 问一处具体的清晰度或边界问题，不把礼貌点头当理解证明。
- **对方没有时间或不愿听：** 尊重拒绝并另约愿意的人；自录练习可作准备，但不算已“讲给别人”。
- **文字、听力或语言不匹配：** 换双方认可的文字、语音、图示或辅助形式，让核对例子仍可访问。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

听者无需成为考官，也不承担核实所有知识的义务。你对来源和未核事项负责；解释形式可因语言或可及需求变化。把内容讲通一次可能暴露真正缺口，也可能发现自己能在这个例子里讲清楚；两者都不自动等于长期保持或专业资格。

## English

Load this Skill when you recently learned, or think you learned, one ordinary idea and want to **explain it to a willing person** to check your own clarity. Explain the conditions in your words, use one example traceable to a source, and identify what you still cannot explain. The listener need not memorise an answer, and one talk is not proof of lasting mastery. Running a group session belongs to H259; later independent recall to H257.

### Preparation and inputs

Choose one question narrow enough for a few minutes, such as “when does this method apply?” rather than a whole chapter. Have a locatable trustworthy course source and a small example **without private material or large protected excerpts**. Ask whether the person has a few minutes and which language, text, speech, or accessible form works. Do not use this ordinary study Skill to give high-stakes medical, legal, or financial advice.

### Execution

1. **State scope and point first.** Say in one sentence, “I'm explaining X; under condition Y it does Z; beyond Y I do not yet know.” Do not lead with a list of terms and make the listener guess why they matter.
2. **Explain in two or three steps of your own.** Link conditions, action, and result instead of reading the source aloud. Use text, a diagram, or an object when that is accessible to the listener. Speaking louder is not a method of becoming more correct.
3. **Work one checkable small example.** Use an ordinary source example or one you create that can be checked against the source. Name inputs, why you choose a step, an observable result, and one condition where it would not apply. For your own example, check that it respects the source rather than treating a convenient analogy as a fact.
4. **Invite one clarity check.** Ask, “Which step should I explain again?” or invite the listener to say what they would check first under a changed condition. Respect a person who does not want to be tested. They can point out a term or jump; a listener's wrong answer is not automatically their fault.
5. **Check one doubt and keep unknowns visible.** If a question cannot be answered from the source or your current understanding, say, “I have not checked that,” note the question and source location, and look it up later or ask a teacher. Explain a correctable point again in your words and ask whether it is clearer. If still unclear, stop the round instead of inventing a confident but wrong explanation.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

You explained one narrow idea to a real willing person, used an example traceable to a source, and kept a doubt the listener raised or one explicit unknown. The listener becoming fully proficient is not required; smooth speaking does not replace factual checks.

- **The example swaps words but omits conditions:** Add when it applies, why that step, and when it does not.
- **A listener asks what you do not know:** Mark it unknown and check a source instead of inventing an answer to preserve authority.
- **They only nod:** Ask one specific clarity or boundary question; polite nodding is not proof of understanding.
- **They have no time or decline:** Respect the refusal and find a willing person later. Recording yourself can prepare you but does not count as explaining to someone else.
- **Text, hearing, or language needs differ:** Switch to mutually acceptable text, speech, diagram, or assistive form while keeping the example checkable.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

The listener is not an examiner and need not verify every claim. You remain responsible for the source and unresolved points. Format can change with language and access needs. Explaining may expose a real gap or show that you can explain this example now. Neither automatically proves lasting retention or professional qualification.
