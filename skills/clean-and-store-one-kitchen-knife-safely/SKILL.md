## 中文

当你用完一把**普通厨房刀**，需要让它恢复可正常用于下一次食物准备的清洁状态并安全归位时，用这份 Skill 一把一把处理。目标是刀身和手柄无可见食物/洗剂残留、刀已干燥、锋刃被合适位置保护；手不用在看不见刀的水槽里寻找它。磨刀、修柄和专业食安消毒不在这次日常收尾内。

### 准备与输入

确认刀没有松动手柄、崩刃或裂缝，查看制造商对刀身、木/合成手柄、洗剂、浸水和干燥的说明。准备洁净软海绵/布、标签适用的手洗餐具洗剂、可安全接触的清洁水、干净干布和既有刀架、护套或抽屉隔格。先清开水槽边与归位路线，一次只拿一把；不要把刀放在满是泡沫的盆底，也不与普通餐具叠成一堆。

儿童、宠物或旁人可能突然伸手到洗区时先腾出受控空间。刀柄松、刀尖断、刀口有不明损伤，先停止将它当可用刀，按制造商/专业维修安排。

### 执行

1. **让锋刃始终看得见。** 握住干燥稳定的刀柄，把刀放在可见、稳固的位置，刀刃朝远离手与他人的方向；不要在水下盲摸刀口。食物残渣明显时，先在制造商允许的水与软工具下处理，不用手指沿锋刃摩擦来证明它干净。
2. **分面清洗刀身与手柄。** 按刀与洗剂说明，用洁净软洗具及适用的温和洗剂清洗刀身两侧、刀背附近及食物接触处，手指与锋刃保持距离；清理手柄可见污物，不把材质禁止浸水的木柄泡在盆里。不要用磨损刀身的硬刷或未获制造商允许的强清洁品。
3. **漂净并立即干燥。** 在可控制水流与刀姿势的位置漂去洗剂，继续让整片锋刃可见。把刀放在稳固干净位置，用干净布逐面吸干刀身与手柄，手不跨过刀口；湿布换掉。对木柄或易留水的连接处按制造商要求确认干燥，不把湿刀藏进护套。
4. **检查后有保护地归位。** 看刀身和手柄有无剩余食物、油膜、泡沫、裂痕或松动。无问题时放到已有刀架、适合的护套或隔开的抽屉位，确保下一位取用的人不会先抓到裸露刃；不要松散丢进餐叉堆里。若这把刀刚处理生肉禽海鲜，继续遵守当地食品接触器具的清洁/必要额外处理规则，不用“看起来亮了”证明已安全用于即食食物。

### 成功条件

一把完整刀具按其材质和制造商说明洗净、漂净、干燥并以刃受保护的方式归位；过程没有盲摸锋刃或把它藏在洗涤水里。若刀具损坏、无可安全归位位置或需要专业食安处理，暂停再次使用并交给可靠来源同样是完成判断。

### 常见报错与补救

- **刀沉在泡泡盆里：** 暂停其他手洗，不伸手盲捞；待水清晰可见后从可见位置安全取出，再一把一把处理。
- **用手指沿刀口擦：** 停止，改握手柄并用适用软洗具处理平面，手始终避开锋边。
- **木柄或接缝仍湿就套护套：** 取出按制造商要求晾/擦到适用干燥状态，再归位，别把水封在里面。
- **干净刀与叉勺松散混放：** 改到刀架、护套或合适隔格，不让别人翻抽屉时碰裸刃。
- **柄松、刀尖缺或刃有裂：** 标记为暂不可用，查制造商/专业维修；洗干净不能恢复结构安全。
- **刀刚接触生食就拿去切即食食物：** 停下，按当地食品安全的生熟分开与清洁要求处理，别用亮面替代处理记录。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里只处理普通家用厨房刀的日常取洗收，不教持刀切割、磨刃、拆柄或特殊消毒。不同品牌、钢材和木/塑柄对洗碗机与浸水的许可不同，以准确产品说明为准。握力或视力受限时使用稳定光线、少量水、合适护刃位置或可信协助；无法保证刀口可见就停止。单把处理比把刀丢进大盆多一道动作，但可避免隐藏锋刃。

## English

Use this Skill after using **one ordinary kitchen knife** to return it to normal clean condition for later food preparation and store it safely. Work one knife at a time. The result is no visible food or soap on blade and handle, a dry knife, and a protected edge. You should not have to search for a hidden blade below dish suds. Sharpening, handle repair, and professional food-safety sanitizing are outside this ordinary closing task.

### Preparation and inputs

Check that handle, tip, and blade are intact. Read the maker's guidance for blade and wood or synthetic handle, detergent, immersion, and drying. Have a clean soft sponge or cloth, labelled hand-dishwashing soap, usable water, a clean dry cloth, and an existing block, sheath, or separated drawer space. Clear the sink edge and route to storage. Hold only one knife. Do not submerge it in an opaque sudsy basin or stack it with ordinary dishes.

Set up controlled space first if children, pets, or others might suddenly reach into the area. Stop treating a knife with a loose handle, broken tip, or uncertain blade damage as ready for use and ask its maker or a repair professional.

### Execution

1. **Keep the edge visible.** Hold a dry steady handle and place the knife where the blade can be seen, with the edge away from hands and others. Do not feel blindly underwater for the cutting edge. Remove visible food with maker-approved water and a soft tool, not fingers rubbed along the edge.
2. **Wash blade faces and handle separately.** Under knife and soap guidance, use a clean soft tool and suitable mild detergent on both flat faces, near the spine, and other food-contact areas. Keep fingers away from the edge. Clean visible handle soil without soaking wood that its maker says must stay out of water. Do not use an abrasive brush or strong unapproved cleaner.
3. **Rinse and dry promptly.** Rinse detergent with controlled water flow while the whole edge stays visible. Set the knife on a stable clean spot. Use a clean cloth to absorb moisture from each blade face and handle without reaching across the edge; replace a wet cloth. Confirm dryness at a wooden handle or joint under maker guidance. A damp knife does not go straight into a sheath.
4. **Inspect, then return with edge protection.** Check blade and handle for food, grease film, suds, cracks, or looseness. If sound, place it in an existing block, fitting sheath, or separated drawer holder so the next person does not first touch bare steel. Do not throw it loose among forks. If the knife touched raw meat, poultry, or seafood, follow local food-contact cleaning and any needed additional treatment before ready-to-eat use. A shiny surface is not that proof.

### Success

One intact knife is washed, rinsed, dried under material and maker instructions and stored with its edge protected. Nobody searched for it by touch or hid it under wash water. If the knife is damaged, no safe storage is available, or specialist food-contact treatment is needed, holding it from reuse for the right source is also complete.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The knife has disappeared into a sudsy basin:** Stop other hand washing. Do not reach blindly. Make the location visible before safely removing it, then handle one knife at a time.
- **Fingers wipe along the sharp edge:** Stop and use a suitable soft tool on the flat faces while holding the handle, with hands clear of the edge.
- **A damp wood handle or joint goes into a sheath:** Remove and dry as its maker directs before storage; do not seal moisture in.
- **The clean knife lies loose among spoons:** Move it to a block, sheath, or fitting divider so another person cannot contact a bare edge while searching.
- **Handle is loose or tip or blade is cracked:** Mark it unavailable and seek maker or professional repair. Washing does not restore structural safety.
- **A raw-food knife is taken straight to ready-to-eat food:** Stop and follow local separation and cleaning requirements; a shine is not a handling record.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary home kitchen-knife cleanup, not cutting technique, sharpening, disassembly, or special sanitizing. Brands, steels, and wood or synthetic handles permit different immersion or dishwasher use; exact product guidance controls that choice. For limited grip or sight, use steady lighting, controlled water, protected storage, or trusted help. Stop if the edge cannot stay visible. One-at-a-time cleaning takes an extra motion but removes the hidden-blade hazard.
