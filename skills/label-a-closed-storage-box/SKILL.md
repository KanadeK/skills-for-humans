## 中文

当几个普通收纳盒从外面看都差不多，每次想找一件东西都得逐个开盖时，加载这份 Skill。你只为**一个有权检查的盒子**制作足以回答“我要找的东西可能在这里吗”的外部信息，并在关盖后测试。它不负责决定物品去留、搬家打包、食品保存、重要证件档案或给整个房间安装索引系统。

### 准备与输入

先写一件最近真想从盒中找回的东西或用途，并确认谁拥有盒内物、谁有权看内容。准备现有纸片、笔、可移除标签，或你已经会用的触觉/音频标记；没有专用标签机也能做。盒子太重、太高或压在不稳的堆里时不要独自搬开；先选能安全取到的盒子或请有权协助的人处理。

外部标记会被别人看见。若物品归属、隐私或安全要求使内容不宜写在外面，先与有权的人确认可公开到什么程度；可以写中性的用途与盒主，再把必要的细节保留在自己的私密记录里。没有权限就不打开、不猜内容。

### 执行

1. **核实当前内容。** 打开你有权检查且可安全取到的盒子，观察主要用途、明显区分于旁边盒子的内容，以及是否有不能公开的细节。只凭旧标签或去年拍的照片不能证明现在仍是那些东西；无法查看就标“未核实”，不要给它写确定的内容。
2. **写能做决定的短标签。** 用“用途/大类 + 一项区分线索 + 必要的归属信息”表达，而不是只写“杂物”。例如“画画备用纸与胶带 · 轻材料 · 我的”；具体私人内容不要贴在外面。标签不需要抄完盒内每件物品。
3. **放在拿盒前能遇到的位置。** 把标记放在盒子实际朝向你的那一面或盖子上能看到/摸到的地方，不挡住开合、把手或原包装上的重要说明。若盒子换方向摆，确认标记仍可读；用大字、对比、触觉、盲文或音频方式时，以实际使用者能辨认为准，不只用颜色。
4. **关盖做一次选择测试。** 不看盒内，拿最近的真实查找问题问：“这个盒子值得打开吗？”能从外部信息作出合理的“是 / 不是 / 还不知道”，并区分它与最相似的另一盒，才算有效。“还不知道”意味着需核对，不是随便猜一盒。
5. **内容变化时改标签。** 取走或加入会改变查找结论的东西后，更新外部信息；旧标签不再真实时先遮掉或撤掉，必要时标“待复核”。不要让盒子内容和标签各自拥有一套互不通知的版本。

### 完成与停止

你或获准使用的人能在不开盒时判断它是否值得为一个实际目标打开，或诚实得知内容未核实。标签可辨认、与现状相符，没有暴露不该公开的信息。若内容无法安全查看、权限未明或标记会泄露私人信息，停止写确定标签，保留“待复核”与下一步；不要为完成计数擅自拆箱。

### 常见报错与补救

- **所有盒子都写“杂物”：** 加上真实用途或能区分相邻盒的一项线索；若确实不知道内容，就写未核实而不是换个好听的词。
- **旧标签比记忆更自信：** 以当前实际内容为准；查不了就暂时撤销确定说法，等能核实时再补。
- **标签贴在看不见的侧面：** 按实际存放朝向移到可见/可触面，关盖后再测试一次。
- **字太小或只有颜色：** 改用较大字、固定位置或可用的触觉/音频方式；不要求使用者为了一个盒子购买设备。
- **做一半被打断：** 留下“已核实内容 / 还没测试外部选择”两种状态；旧标签与新内容冲突时先标待复核，下次从测试继续。

### 假设、替代与已知副作用

盒子透明度、存放高度、共用权限、视觉与触觉习惯不同；信息量以能够决定是否打开为准，不按某种审美或分类体系统一。若盒子用于食品、药物、危险物或专门文书，应遵循该类物品的正式标签与保存要求，本篇普通盒外说明不能代替它们。副作用是内容变化后你需要更新一句话，否则标签会比没有标签更误导。

## English

Load this Skill when several ordinary storage boxes look alike from the outside and finding one thing means opening every lid. Make the outside of **one box you are allowed to inspect** informative enough to answer “Could the thing I need be in here?”, then test it with the lid closed. This does not decide what to keep, pack a move, store food, manage important identity papers, or install a whole-room indexing system.

### Preparation and inputs

Name one thing you recently tried to retrieve from the box, or the job it serves. Confirm who owns its contents and who may inspect them. Use paper and a pen, an existing removable label, or an accessible tactile/audio method you already know; no label printer is required. If a box is too heavy, high, or under an unstable stack, do not move it alone. Choose a safely reachable box or ask someone entitled to help.

An outside label may be seen by others. If ownership, privacy, or safety makes detailed contents unsuitable for display, agree with the person entitled to decide how much may be shown. A neutral use and owner may be enough; keep necessary detail in your own private record. Without permission, do not open the box or guess what it holds.

### Execution

1. **Verify what is there now.** Open a box you may inspect and can reach safely. Note its main use, a clue that distinguishes it from nearby boxes, and anything that must stay private. An old label or last year's photo does not prove current contents. If you cannot inspect it, mark “unverified” rather than asserting a contents list.
2. **Write a short decision label.** Use “use/category + one distinguishing clue + needed owner information,” not only “miscellaneous.” For example, “spare drawing paper and tape · light materials · mine.” Keep private details off the outside. You do not need to copy out every item in the box.
3. **Put it where you meet it before opening.** Place the cue on the side that faces you in storage or an accessible part of the lid. Do not cover the opening, handle, or important original instructions. If the box is turned, check that the cue still works. Large print, contrast, tactile, braille, or audio cues should suit the actual reader; colour alone is not enough.
4. **Test a closed-lid decision.** Without looking inside, ask a real retrieval question: “Is this box worth opening?” The outside information should support “yes / no / still unknown” and distinguish this box from its most similar neighbour. “Unknown” calls for checking; it is not a reason to guess a random box.
5. **Update when contents change.** If adding or removing something changes the retrieval decision, revise the outside cue. Cover or remove an old assertion when it is no longer true, and mark “check again” if needed. A box and its label should not run separate, unannounced versions.

### Success and stop conditions

You or another permitted reader can decide whether to open this box for one actual goal without first lifting the lid, or can honestly tell that contents are unverified. The cue is accessible, current, and does not disclose what should remain private. If inspection is unsafe, permission is unclear, or a label would expose private contents, stop short of a definite label and leave “check again” with a next action. Do not open a box without authority to reach a count.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Every box says “miscellaneous”:** Add its real use or one clue that distinguishes its neighbour. If contents really are unknown, say unverified rather than choosing a prettier word.
- **The old label is more confident than memory:** Trust current observed contents. If you cannot inspect them, withdraw the definite claim until you can.
- **The cue is on a hidden side:** Move it to the visible or touchable face for the actual storage orientation, then repeat the closed-lid test.
- **The print is tiny or colour-only:** Use larger writing, a consistent position, or an available tactile/audio method. No one needs to buy a device for this box.
- **You are interrupted halfway through:** Keep “contents verified / outside-choice test still pending” as separate states. If the old label conflicts with what you saw, mark it for recheck and resume with the test.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Transparency, shelf height, sharing permissions, sight, and touch differ. Use only enough information to decide whether to open, not one required aesthetic or taxonomy. Boxes for food, medicines, hazardous material, or special records require their own formal labels and storage rules; this ordinary outside cue cannot replace those. When contents change, you will need to update a sentence. A stale label can mislead more than no label.
