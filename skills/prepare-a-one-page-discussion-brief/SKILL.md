## 中文

你负责一次普通工作、学习或志愿小组的讨论，已知道会议要进行，但参与者还不知道**要讨论什么、是否要决定、需要先看什么**时，用这份 Skill。目标是做一页可读的会前要点，让人带着同一问题进场；不是提前替大家作决定，也不是会后会议纪要。正式治理会议的通知、表决和记录规则应按组织制度执行。

### 准备与输入

找出召集人说过的真实目的、参与者、日期、时长、地点或线上方式，以及谁有权作本次决定。把已有事实材料和仍缺的材料分开；只用你获准分享的版本，不把私有文件链接发给无权查看的人。如果你不是召集人或决策者，先确认自己可以拟议程，不能擅自宣称群体将表决某件事。

### 执行

1. **用一句话写预期结果。** 是让大家知道一条信息、比较两个方案、取得建议，还是由有权者作决定？一页顶部写“这次结束时希望得到……”；若没有真实结果，就先向召集人问清是否需要开会，不用靠堆议题制造必要性。
2. **每项只写可处理的问题。** 挑最影响结果的两三项；每项标“告知 / 讨论 / 需决定”、要回答的具体问题、现有选项或限制，以及谁负责给出信息或决定。把“聊一下进展”改为“确认下周需要哪两项材料”；但未经授权不要编一个已定结论。
3. **配上刚好够用的输入。** 对每项给一个可访问的材料名称或短链接、需要提前看的部分，以及尚缺谁的事实。材料较长时写清页码或关键段，不让一页要点变成几十页附件。缺材料会影响决定时，标“本次仅讨论/等待输入后再决定”，不假装万事俱备。
4. **按时长删减。** 把每项的估计讨论时间与总时长对照，留一点结尾确认下一步的时间。装不进的一项延期或改为会前文字收集；不要让“一页”通过小字号偷偷塞进六场会议。需要别人事先准备时，在合适时间发送，并确认收件人有权限打开材料。
5. **发前从参与者视角检查。** 读者能从这一页知道何时何地/如何加入、讨论结果、每项问题、需要带的材料、谁来决定或仍待确认吗？标题和正文用可读文字，不把全部要点藏在图片。若议程后来改变，发一个指向新版本的简短更新，旧版本不自动消失。

### 成功条件

参与者能从同一页知道本次要达到的结果、每项是告知/讨论/决定、所需信息与谁提供、现实时间范围和必要的会前准备；缺失的决策权限或材料被明确标记并交给负责人。发送议程不等于参与者已读、会议已开或决定已作出；会中的真实结果另由 H245 记录。

### 常见报错与补救

- **标题写“例会”，正文只有五个名词：** 为每项补一个要回答的问题和期待的结果。
- **一页排满十几项而会议只有半小时：** 合并、延期或改用会前文字，留下实际可讨论的少数项。
- **材料链接打不开或权限不足：** 修正共享权限或提供可访问版本；未解决时标材料缺失，不要求参与者凭空决定。
- **想决定，却有权者不在场：** 改成本次收集事实/建议，或请召集人安排有权者参加，不冒充群体可作决定。
- **发送后目标发生变化：** 明确发新版本与变化点，避免两份议程各自成为“最新版”。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

适用于小型、普通协作讨论，不代替法定会议通知、董事会程序、行业决策或专业判断。UMN Extension 的议程指南强调先定目的、所需信息、决策方式和时间；这里将其收束成可读的一页，不把其公共会议规则移植到每个小组。没有共享文档可用一页纸或普通文字消息；视觉或数字渠道受限时，可提前提供双方能读的版本。多做一页要点，可能提前发现这次其实不能决定任何事；那是有用的发现。

## English

Use this Skill when you help prepare an ordinary work, study, or volunteer discussion but attendees do not yet know **what will be discussed, whether a decision is needed, or what to read first**. Make one readable pre-meeting page so people arrive with the same questions. This does not decide for them or record the meeting afterward. Formal governance notice, voting, and minutes follow the organization's actual rules.

### Preparation and inputs

Find the convener's real purpose, participants, date, duration, place or online mode, and who has authority to decide. Separate available source material from missing input. Share only a version you may share; do not send private file links to people without access. If you are neither convener nor decision-maker, confirm you may draft the agenda rather than announcing that the group will vote.

### Execution

1. **Write the intended result in one sentence.** Will people receive information, compare options, offer advice, or make a decision through an authorized person? Put “By the end, we aim to...” at the top. If no real result is known, ask the convener whether a meeting is needed instead of piling on topics to justify it.
2. **Make each item answerable.** Select the two or three items most relevant to that result. Label each “inform / discuss / decide,” its concrete question, known choices or limits, and who supplies facts or makes the decision. Replace “talk about progress” with a question such as “Which two materials are needed next week?” Do not invent a settled outcome without authority.
3. **Add just enough input.** Give an accessible document name or short link, the part to read in advance, and who still owes a missing fact. For long material, name a page or section rather than making one page point at dozens of pages. If missing material blocks a decision, mark “discussion only / decide after input” instead of pretending ready.
4. **Cut to the available time.** Compare each item's expected discussion time with the meeting's duration and leave a little space at the end to confirm next actions. Defer an item or collect it in writing beforehand rather than hiding six meetings in tiny type on one page. Send it early enough for any required preparation and check that recipients can actually open the material.
5. **Read it from an attendee's side before sending.** Can they see when and how to join, the desired result, each question, needed material, and who decides or what remains open? Use readable text rather than placing everything inside an image. If the agenda changes later, send a short update pointing to the new version; the old one will not vanish on its own.

### Success

From the same page, attendees can identify the meeting result, whether each item is for information, discussion, or decision, the needed inputs and their owners, realistic time, and preparation. Missing authority or material is visible with an owner. Sending an agenda is not proof that it was read, that the meeting happened, or that a decision was made. H245 records the real discussion outcome.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The title says “weekly meeting” and the body has only five nouns:** Add one answerable question and intended result to each item.
- **A half-hour meeting contains fifteen topics:** Combine, defer, or collect in writing until only a few discussable items remain.
- **A file link fails or permissions exclude attendees:** Fix access or provide an accessible version. Until then, mark input missing rather than ask people to decide blindly.
- **A decision is wanted but the authorized person is absent:** Gather facts or advice this time, or ask the convener to include the decision-maker; do not claim the group has authority it lacks.
- **The goal changes after distribution:** Send a clear revised version and change note so two agendas do not both look current.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is for small ordinary collaboration, not statutory meeting notice, board procedure, industry decision-making, or professional judgment. UMN Extension's agenda guide starts from purpose, needed information, decision process, and time; this Skill condenses those questions to a readable page without exporting public-meeting rules to every group. One sheet of paper or a plain-text message can replace a shared document. Provide an accessible version ahead of time when sight or digital access is limited. A one-page brief may reveal that no decision is possible yet; that is useful information.
