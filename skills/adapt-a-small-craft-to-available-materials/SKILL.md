## 中文

当你正要做一件**不承重、不防护、不通电**的小型装饰作品，发现原定纸、卡片、连接件或装饰不够时，用这份 Skill按**缺少材料承担的功能**重新设计、试摆并完成一个诚实的新版本。结果可能更小或更简洁，不是把缺料处用危险工具和陌生胶水硬补成原图。某一折法、拼贴法的具体制作属于对应技法任务；本篇解决材料变动后的结构决定。

### 准备与输入

先写原定结果：要自立、要平铺展示，还是只要可看见的一组形状？列出缺少什么、现有多少。只用你有权使用的干净普通纸、薄卡、手能安全处理的拼接材料，以及产品说明允许的常见文具；不拆电器、不碰承重或儿童防护部件、不以食物容器的安全性作艺术推断。若某材料承担安全或结构必需功能而你找不到合适替代，改做纯展示版本或暂停，不声称功能保留。若要用湿胶，按该产品的适用材料、用量和干燥说明处理，不发明通用等待分钟数。

### 执行

1. **把缺料翻译成用途。** 对每种缺口写“它原本负责____”：作为底面、让几片相连、保持形状、承载颜色/文字，还是纯装饰。先保留作品最想表达的一点，其他部分可删。材料名称只是输入，真正决定的是功能。
2. **选一个可行改法。** 面积不足就缩小版面或减少面数；连接材料不足就把设计改为可折叠、自立或暂时无胶试摆的结构，前提是用现有材料能够稳定放置；颜色或花纹不足就改配色或留白。不能安全完成某项关键功能，就放弃该功能或暂停。不要用加热、锋利刀具、未知溶剂或额外购买来假装“家里现成”。
3. **先做可逆小测试。** 在桌上摆出缩小布局，轻折或临时定位一小部分，确认底边接触、连接位置、文字可读或目标效果确实存在。先不把所有材料永久黏上。若试摆一碰就散，回到上一步减少跨度、面数或重量；试摆仍不能成立就明确停止。
4. **按新版本制作。** 只在小测试过关后，用你会安全操作的普通方式完成可保留的部分。每做一个关键连接或支撑点就检查一次；若实际材料变形或不粘，不继续堆材料掩盖失效。湿材料按原说明干燥期间保留空间，不把“看起来差不多”当已固定。
5. **按新目标验收。** 写下“原计划____；缺口是____；我改成____；现在能____，不能____”。让作品在预定位置实际站住、平放或呈现你改后的目标；若不成立，只保留试验记录并暂停，不把未完成品计作成功。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你能说出缺料的**功能**、新方案牺牲了什么，并有一件经实际摆放/展示检查的小型低风险作品。没有对应功能、只有换了材料名字，不算改方案。

- **只缺装饰却开始重做全部结构：** 留住已稳定的主体，改配色、面积或留白即可。
- **缩小后仍站不住：** 重新看底面、跨度和重心；改成平放展示或暂停，不给轻型纸件加重物冒充自立。
- **连接方式不确定：** 先试一小块兼容性与稳定性；产品说明不支持就撤回该连接，不混用未知胶与溶剂。
- **现有材料来源不明或带别人的受保护图样：** 不用作外显内容，换自己的空白材料或暂停；本篇不处理版权结论。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

本篇只处理普通小型非安全关键手作。实际材质、湿度、工具和行动能力会改变可行结构；用大纸样、可触摸的布局或可信协助检查也可以，但你仍须确认结果。作品可能比想象的小、颜色少或只能平放；这些是明确的新约束，不是偷偷把原方案称为已完成。剩余材料按其说明收好；不把一次手作扩成房间整理。

## English

Use this Skill when you are about to make a **small decorative object that bears no load, provides no protection, and uses no electricity**, but discover you lack enough of the planned paper, card, joining material, or decoration. Redesign by the **function the missing material served**, test a layout, and complete an honest new version. It may be smaller or simpler. Do not force the original design with hazardous tools or unfamiliar adhesive. A specific fold or collage method belongs to its own technique task; this Skill decides the structure after supplies change.

### Preparation and inputs

Write the intended result first: should it stand, lie flat for display, or simply show a group of shapes? List what is missing and how much remains. Use only clean ordinary paper, light card, hand-safe joining materials, and familiar stationery allowed by its instructions. Do not dismantle electrical gear, create load-bearing or child-protection parts, or infer a food container's safety from its appearance. If a missing material provided an essential safety or structural function with no suitable replacement, choose a display-only version or pause; do not claim that function remains. For wet adhesive, follow its actual material, amount, and drying instructions rather than inventing one universal waiting time.

### Execution

1. **Translate the shortage into a job.** For each missing item write “this was meant to ____”: form a base, connect pieces, hold shape, carry colour or text, or decorate. Keep the one point you most want to express and allow other parts to go. The material name is an input; its job drives the choice.
2. **Choose one feasible redesign.** If surface area is short, reduce size or panel count. If a join is missing, change the design to a fold, self-standing arrangement, or temporary no-glue layout only if available materials can actually stay stable. If colour or pattern is short, change palette or leave space blank. Drop an essential function or pause when it cannot be made safely. Do not heat, use sharp blades, mix unknown solvents, or buy extra materials and call them “already available.”
3. **Run a reversible small test.** Lay out the smaller design on a table and lightly fold or position one part. Check base contact, joining position, text readability, or the visual effect you chose. Do not permanently attach every piece yet. If a touch scatters the layout, reduce span, panel count, or weight and test again. If it still cannot work, stop explicitly.
4. **Make the new version.** Only after the small test works, complete the parts you can keep using an ordinary method you know how to perform safely. Check each key join or support as you make it. If the real material warps or fails to adhere, do not pile on material to hide failure. Leave drying space for wet materials as their labels direct; “looks close enough” does not mean fixed.
5. **Check against the new goal.** Write “planned ____; shortage ____; redesigned as ____; now it can ____ but cannot ____.” Actually test whether it stands, lies flat, or shows your revised intended point in its planned place. If not, retain the experiment note and pause rather than count an unfinished object as success.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You can name the missing material's **function**, the tradeoff of the redesign, and a small low-risk object tested in its actual display position. Merely renaming a replacement material without checking the function is not a redesign.

- **Only decoration is short but you restart the whole structure:** Keep the stable base and change palette, covered area, or empty space.
- **A smaller version still falls:** Recheck base, span, and balance. Display it flat or pause rather than weight down a light paper object and call it self-standing.
- **The join is uncertain:** Test a small compatible piece and its stability first. If product instructions do not support it, abandon that join instead of mixing unknown glues and solvents.
- **Available material has uncertain origin or another person's protected imagery:** Do not expose it in your work. Use blank material of your own or pause; this Skill offers no copyright verdict.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Only ordinary, small, non-safety-critical handcrafts are in scope. Real material, humidity, tools, and mobility can change which structure works. Large paper mock-ups, tactile layouts, or trusted help may aid a check, but you still verify the result. The object may be smaller, use less colour, or only lie flat. Those are declared new limits, not a hidden claim that the original plan was completed. Put remaining materials away by their instructions; this is not a whole-room organisation task.
