## 中文

当你从安全状态可确认的液体开始，按一份**明确使用淀粉浆**的普通配方做小量热酱汁时，用这份 Skill。关键顺序是淀粉先在配方指定的冷液中分散，再进入热底液并按配方加热到目标稠度。已经做坏的“太稀酱汁”归成品补救，不要用本篇替它编历史。

### 准备与输入

看完整配方，确认底液、淀粉品种、冷液种类与比例、加热阶段、目标用途和食品安全检查。不同淀粉或含酸、糖、乳品的底液可能有不同表现，不把某食谱比例当万能数。准备小锅、洁净小碗、量具与搅拌器，确认热锅稳定、把手安全。原料保存史不明、需煮熟的成分未有可信完成方法，或设备不能安全加热时先停。

### 执行

1. **量取本次小份。** 按可信配方选所需的底液、淀粉和冷液，保持单位一致，核对锅容量和最后要盛入的容器。若配方只写“适量粉”，先找能实际执行的来源，不用精确小数伪装经验。
2. **先在冷液里消掉干粉团。** 将指定淀粉分次加入配方所需的冷水或其他冷液，搅成均匀可流动的浆。检查碗底没有明显干粉；不把干淀粉直接撒入热锅，然后指望它自己开会。
3. **按配方加热底液。** 在稳定锅具中让底液到达该做法要求的热状态，防止液面过高或喷溅。若底液含有需要彻底烹调的食材，先按该食品说明完成安全加热；未经安全阶段不试吃。
4. **边搅边逐步加入浆。** 再搅匀会沉下去的淀粉浆，按配方缓慢倒入热液，持续用适合的工具搅动，观察整锅是否均匀变稠。一次不要加超出配方的粉；有些稠度变化需要继续按配方加热才能显现。
5. **用用途检查完成。** 到配方要求的加热和观察点，取少量让其冷到可安全接触或试味，检查是否能轻薄挂在目标食物上或达到这份做法规定的稠度；这不是所有酱汁都要“粘住勺背”。按产品及当地指导确认需要烹调的食材已安全完成，再停热盛出。
6. **决定现用还是安全保存。** 小量酱汁按这顿用途现用；若确有剩余，按其实际成分和当地指导及时冷却、冷藏或丢弃。淀粉让它变稠，不让它变成常温罐头。

### 成功条件

你得到一份按可靠配方制作、没有明显干粉团、达到所需用途稠度且食材安全状态可确认的热酱汁；或者因配方、原料或安全加热条件缺失而明确停止。

### 常见报错与补救

- **粉直接倒进热锅结块：** 停止继续加粉；只在安全可处理时按该配方的补救方法处理当前小批，下一次先冷液分散，不把新粉团叠上去。
- **刚加浆还稀就继续猛加：** 先按配方完成所需加热与搅拌，再判断；不能用超量干粉抢时间。
- **浓得不能用于原用途：** 若仍安全且配方允许，少量加入兼容液体并重新检查；已发生明显失败的成品可转相应补救流程。
- **底液有未熟动物性食材：** 不靠稠度判断熟度，停止试味并按该食物的可信安全说明完成或放弃。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这只处理有可信配方的小量普通淀粉热酱汁，不教医疗膳食、罐藏、发酵或用增稠掩盖不安全食物。底液、淀粉、锅具与地区食品安全规则影响步骤；依手中配方。若站立、搅拌或读量具困难，缩小批量、选稳定锅具、大字量具或可信协助，不能安全控制热液就停。会留下一个碗和一把搅拌器要洗，稠度也可能随冷却变化。

## English

Use this Skill to make one small ordinary hot sauce **from a trusted recipe that calls for a starch slurry**, starting with ingredients of known condition. Disperse the specified starch in cold recipe liquid before adding it to a hot base, then heat as directed to the target texture. Rescue of a sauce that has already failed is a different task, not a reason to invent this sauce's history.

### Preparation and inputs

Read the full recipe for base liquid, starch, cold liquid and ratio, heating stage, intended use, and food-safety checks. Different starches and acidic, sweet, or dairy bases can behave differently; one recipe's ratios are not universal. Have a small pan, clean bowl, measures, and stirrer. Ensure the hot pan is stable and its handle safe. Stop if ingredient history, a safe method for a cook-required food, or heating equipment is missing.

### Execution

1. **Measure this small batch.** Use the trusted recipe's base, starch, and cold liquid in consistent units. Check pan capacity and the vessel that will receive the sauce. If a source says only “some powder,” find an executable recipe rather than clothing a guess in decimals.
2. **Disperse dry starch in cold liquid first.** Add the named starch to the specified cold water or other liquid in a small bowl and mix to a smooth pourable slurry. Check that dry powder is not sitting at the bottom. Do not sprinkle dry starch into a hot pan and expect it to organize itself.
3. **Heat the base as directed.** Bring it to the recipe's required state in a stable pan without overfilling or splashing. If any ingredient must be cooked thoroughly, complete its food-specific safe heating path. Do not taste before a safe stage.
4. **Add the slurry gradually while stirring.** Remix any settled starch, pour it into the hot liquid at the recipe's pace, and stir with suitable tools until thickening is even. Do not exceed the recipe's starch simply because the first seconds look thin; its process may need more heating to develop texture.
5. **Check against the intended use.** At the specified heating and observation point, take a little and let it cool enough to touch or taste safely. Ask whether it coats the target food lightly or meets this recipe's texture; not every sauce must cling to a spoon in the same way. Confirm any cook-required food is safely done under product and local guidance, then turn off heat and serve.
6. **Use now or store honestly.** Use the small sauce for this meal. If any remains, follow its actual ingredients and local guidance for prompt cooling, refrigeration, or disposal. Starch thickens sauce; it does not make a shelf-stable canned food.

### Success

You have a hot sauce made by its trusted method, free of obvious dry-starch lumps, at the texture needed for its use, with food safety conditions established. A clear stop for missing recipe, ingredient, or safe heating evidence is also complete.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Dry starch is dumped into the hot pan and clumps:** Stop adding powder. Handle the small batch under a recipe-supported recovery only when safe; next time disperse in cold liquid first rather than layering on more lumps.
- **You add more as soon as the slurry enters:** Complete the recipe's heating and stirring stage before judging; excess dry starch cannot buy back time.
- **The sauce is too thick for its job:** If safe and the recipe permits, add a little compatible liquid and recheck. A substantial failure belongs in the separate recovery workflow.
- **The base contains undercooked animal food:** Thickness is not doneness. Stop tasting and follow the food's supported safety process or abandon the batch.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers a small ordinary starch-thickened hot sauce with a trusted recipe. It does not teach medical diets, canning, fermentation, or thickening unsafe food into acceptability. Base liquid, starch, pan, and local rules change the steps; use the recipe at hand. If standing, stirring, or reading measures is difficult, use a smaller batch, stable pan, clear markings, or trusted help. Stop if hot liquid cannot be controlled. A bowl and stirrer need washing, and texture may change as the sauce cools.
