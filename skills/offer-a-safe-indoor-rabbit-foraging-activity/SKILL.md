## 中文

当你的一只**按原方案进食和排便正常的室内兔**，在已检查安全的活动区可做一次简单自愿觅食时，加载这份 Skill。结果是用原配额中的草/食物布置一个可进入、可退出的安全纸盒/玩具，兔可自行探索或拒绝，结束后受损材料被收起。它不以零食加量换参与，不替代兔同伴或日常空间。兔突然不吃或无新粪便，立即找兽医，不拿玩具测病。

### 准备与输入

先读原兽医/主人计划中的可用草、食物份额、材料限制和同伴使用方式；从**本次原配额**取少量，不另加陌生植物/甜食。选干净、无订书钉、胶带、墨重涂层、线绳或尖角的兔适用纸盒/纸筒，开口足以安全进出且不易坍塌；材料不明就用原有批准玩具。人全程可看见，不把它放到无人看守空间。

### 执行

1. **先核兔与空间。** 兔正常吃草、有新粪、活动区没有可咬电线/危险植物；异常食欲排便是立即兽医出口。保持原水、厕所和躲藏入口开放，不把玩具挡住必需资源。
2. **把计划份额藏得可取得。** 在纸盒里用一小把原草或原有饲料制造轻微寻找动作，别把食物封进只能靠撕碎危险材料才拿到的结构；多兔按原计划提供足够地点，避免一只独占所有食物。
3. **让兔自愿接近并观察。** 把玩具放在熟悉空间，后退让兔选择；不摇晃盒子吓它、不把兔强塞进去。看是否有啃下大块不宜吞的材料、被卡住或同伴争抢，发生就取走，恢复原来可安全取食的位置。
4. **结束与收尾。** 本次短时段结束或兔离开后收走松脱纸片、受损纸盒和剩下会腐坏的食物；干净未受损的获准玩具按计划保留。记是否参与和材料反应，未玩也不算“失败的兔”。

### 完成、常见问题与恢复

有一个用原份额、原安全区布置的可自愿参与活动，兔能退出，异常材料在监督下被撤，后续空间恢复；拒绝也被如实记录。把兔关进只一出口盒子、无人监督，不算安全觅食。

- **兔不理玩具：** 不强迫，收好并保持原草可达。
- **兔要吞大块胶纸/线绳：** 立即撤该玩具，换获准材料；已吞或异常找兽医。
- **同伴争抢：** 分散多个获准取食点或回原配额方法，不用更多食物“平息”。
- **盒子摇晃/塌陷：** 撤下换稳固、双出口合用材料。
- **视觉/行动受限：** 低位开放布局与可信协助监督，无法看护时不开展本次活动。

### 假设与边界

兔需要觅食和探索机会，但具体玩具与原配额由这只兔的计划控制；纸盒只是可替换例子。自愿参与不代表健康诊断、训练成功或兔同伴可被人替代。

## English

Load this Skill when a **known indoor rabbit is eating and passing droppings normally under its plan** and a safe checked activity space allows a short voluntary foraging activity. Finish with a clean safe box/toy holding hay or food from the existing ration, free choice to explore or decline, and damaged material removed afterwards. Do not increase treats to buy participation or replace rabbit companionship or daily space. For sudden no eating or new droppings, call a vet immediately instead of testing with a toy.

### Preparation and inputs

Read the owner/vet plan for approved hay, food ration, materials, and companion access. Take a little **from the existing ration**, not an extra unknown plant or sweet. Choose clean rabbit-suitable cardboard/tube without staples, tape, heavy ink coating, strings, or sharp corners, with safe access and exit and no easy collapse. Use an already approved toy when material is uncertain. Remain able to watch throughout; do not leave it in an unobserved area.

### Execution

1. **Check rabbit and space first.** Rabbit eats hay and has new droppings, with no reachable power wire or unsafe plant. Appetite or droppings change is an immediate vet exit. Keep usual water, toilet, and hide routes open rather than blocking essentials with a toy.
2. **Hide only a planned share where reachable.** Put a little approved hay or usual feed in a box for easy searching. Do not seal food behind material the rabbit must dangerously shred to reach. For several rabbits use enough plan-approved sites so one cannot monopolise the entire portion.
3. **Let the rabbit choose and watch.** Place toy in the familiar area and step back. Do not shake it to frighten or force the rabbit inside. Watch for large unsafe pieces being chewed, trapping, or companion competition; remove the toy and restore ordinary food access if those occur.
4. **End and reset.** When the short activity ends or rabbit leaves, remove loose paper, damaged box and perishable remaining food. An approved intact toy may stay under the plan. Record whether it chose to interact and how material held up; declining is not a failed rabbit.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

An activity uses the original food share in the existing safe space with free entry/exit, unsafe material removed under supervision, and the area reset; refusal is recorded honestly. Closing a rabbit in a one-exit box and leaving it unsupervised is not safe foraging.

- **Rabbit ignores it:** Do not force; put it away and keep normal hay accessible.
- **Rabbit may swallow tape, string, or large unsafe pieces:** Remove toy at once, use approved material, and seek a vet if swallowed or abnormal.
- **Companions compete:** Spread approved access points or return to ordinary ration placement, not extra food as a fix.
- **Box rocks or collapses:** Remove it for a stable suitable two-exit option.
- **Sight or movement is limited:** Low open layout and trusted supervision can help; no observer means no activity today.

### Assumptions and limits

Rabbits benefit from exploration and foraging, but their actual toy and ration follow the existing plan. Cardboard is only a replaceable example. Voluntary play is not health diagnosis, training success, or a substitute for rabbit companions.
