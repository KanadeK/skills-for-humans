## 中文

当你洗完一轮餐具，厨房水槽、沥水位和洗具上还有普通水滴、食物屑或洗剂残留，想把空间交给下一轮正常清洗时，用这份 Skill 处理**可接近的表面**。目标是已洗净餐具有去向，水槽与沥水位按材质擦净、无积水，洗具能干燥；这不是拆管道、清洁垃圾处理器内部或解决倒灌的流程。若这里接触过生肉禽汁，普通擦拭还不能代替适用的额外食品安全处理。

### 准备与输入

确认水槽正常排水、材质和制造商护理说明可查，沥水架/垫的材料与可拆方式已知。回忆刚才清洗的是普通餐具还是曾有生肉、禽、海鲜汁进入水槽；确认没有碎玻璃、刀具或尖锐物藏在水里。备好与材质相容且按产品标签使用的清洁品、干净软布或海绵、漂洗用的洁净水和让洗具晾干的位置。清洁品不要混合，尤其不把漂白剂与酸或其他清洁品凑成“加强版”。

水槽排水明显变慢、积水不退、倒灌、管件漏水、材料破损或特殊污染时先停，按制造商/所在地维修或食安指导处理；这份 Skill 不拆存水弯，也不往排水口倒强力疏通剂。

### 执行

1. **先把干净餐具和待洗物分开。** 已干燥且可用的餐具归其位置；还湿的继续留在干净、留有空隙的沥水架，别因要“清空架子”就湿叠进柜。将可见的普通食物屑移到适用垃圾位置，尖刀、碎片或不明锐物按相应任务安全移出，不伸手在浑水里盲找。
2. **只处理可接近的碎屑与滤网。** 若准确水槽结构允许取出普通可拆滤篮，先稳妥取下、清掉可见残渣并按说明洗净；不把手伸进排水孔，不拆固定件或研磨设备。没有滤篮或物体掉到无法看见的深处，就停下并找适用维修，不用长工具试探。
3. **按表面材质擦洗。** 先在不显眼处确认产品与水槽/沥水材料相容，用软布或适用软洗具清理盆面、边缘、可接近的水龙头把手外表面和沥水架/垫上可见的普通残留。按制造商与产品标签控制用量、力度与停留方式；不默认钢丝球、研磨粉、酸或含氯用品对不锈钢、涂层和复合材料都安全。刷具换清洁面，不把擦过脏排水区的布拿去擦净餐具。
4. **按标签去残留、干燥。** 用洁净水按产品要求漂掉清洁品，再用干净干布或制造商许可的方式处理水迹；沥水架须留有空气流通的位置。海绵、洗布和软刷按其说明洗净后展开晾干或投入适用洗衣流程，不把湿海绵关在不通风容器里。检查可见角落仍无食物屑、粘膜或积水。
5. **生食汁接触时另作额外处理。** 若这次水槽内侧触过生肉禽或其汁液，先完成上面清洁，再按所在地权威指导选择**明确适用于该厨房表面**且与水槽材质相容的产品，严格服从标签的接触时间、是否需再漂洗与干燥要求。没有合适产品或指引时，暂不把水槽当作可接触即食食物/干净食具的区域，先取得可靠方法；不自行猜漂白剂配比或混加清洁品。
6. **给下一轮一个真实可用状态。** 正常排水、可接近盆面及沥水位无普通残留，清洁品已去除，洗具和未归位餐具各有能干燥的位置，就结束。仍有积水、异味来自内部、滤篮破损或产品不适用时，留下明确停用/待修标记，不把“擦了一圈”当成排水修好了。

### 成功条件

普通使用后的水槽与沥水区按材质清洁、去残留并可干燥，已洗净餐具和洗具有清楚去向；上次接触生食汁的区域完成了适用的额外步骤或明确保持隔离。排水异常或无法核实材质/产品时停止普通复位，不能把未完成处当可用。

### 常见报错与补救

- **为了清空沥水架，把湿餐具塞入柜：** 让餐具继续在干净位置干燥；架子可以分批清理，不靠湿叠解决排队。
- **水槽里藏刀或碎玻璃就伸手捞：** 停止盲摸，先让危险物可见，再按刀具/破损处理安全移出。
- **滤篮下面有杂物，想拆开管道：** 到可拆滤篮为止；深处和堵塞转制造商或维修渠道。
- **不锈钢/涂层被粗糙刷具划伤：** 停止摩擦，查准确材质说明，不用更强酸/碱“修划痕”。
- **生肉汁接触后只用水冲一遍：** 停止将该区域交给即食物或干净食具，先按当地食安及产品标签完成适用处理。
- **清洁品残留或湿海绵被密闭收起：** 依据产品说明复漂、留洗具展开干燥；不能以香味判断已清洁。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这只处理**正常排水的家用厨房水槽与沥水区**可见、可接近表面。不同不锈钢、陶瓷、复合、涂层水槽和塑料架允许的产品不同，准确制造商说明优先；食安额外处理也要按所在地与食品接触用途核对。看不清小屑或弯腰不便时用好光线、可及位置和可信协助，不能安全看清锐物就停。可能需要等餐具、架和海绵自然干燥，复位不是瞬间把所有水分消灭。

## English

Use this Skill after a dishwashing round when the **accessible kitchen sink, draining area, and washing tools** still have ordinary drips, food bits, or soap residue. Return the space to a condition usable for the next normal wash. Clean dishes need a destination; sink and rack surfaces need compatible cleaning and drying; tools need air. This does not open plumbing, clean inside a garbage disposer, or solve backflow. If raw meat or poultry juice contacted the sink, ordinary wiping is not the whole food-safety response.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm normal drainage and identify the sink and drying-rack materials and maker care. Recall whether the last wash involved ordinary dishes or raw meat, poultry, or seafood juices in the basin. Ensure no broken glass, knife, or other sharp object hides underwater. Have material-compatible cleaner under its product label, a clean soft cloth or sponge, clean rinse water, and a place for washing tools to dry. Do not mix cleaners, especially bleach with acids or other products, to make a supposed “stronger” formula.

Stop ordinary reset for slow drainage, water that will not leave, backflow, leaking pipes, broken material, or unusual contamination. Use maker, local repair, or food-safety guidance. This Skill does not open the trap or pour strong drain cleaner into it.

### Execution

1. **Separate clean dishes from pending work.** Put away dishes already dry and usable. Leave damp ones with space on a clean rack rather than stacking them wet just to empty it. Move visible ordinary scraps to the appropriate waste route. Handle a sharp knife, glass fragment, or unknown point by its own safe route; do not feel for it blindly in cloudy water.
2. **Limit debris removal to accessible parts.** If the exact sink permits an ordinary removable strainer basket, lift it steadily, clear visible scraps, wash it as directed, and replace it. Do not reach into the drain or take apart fixed parts or grinding equipment. Stop and seek appropriate service when an object is out of sight or too deep for this basket path.
3. **Wash by actual material.** Trial cleaner out of sight as the sink or rack maker requires. Use a soft compatible cloth or tool on basin faces, rim, accessible faucet-handle exterior, and ordinary residue on the rack or mat. Follow maker and cleaner label for dose, pressure, and dwell. Do not assume steel wool, abrasive powder, acid, or chlorine product suits stainless steel, coatings, and composite alike. Change tool faces rather than wiping clean dishes with cloth from the dirty drain area.
4. **Remove product and allow drying.** Rinse cleaner under its label with clean water. Treat water marks with a clean dry cloth or maker-approved method. Leave the rack exposed to air. Clean and spread sponges, cloths, and soft brushes to dry or send them to an allowed laundry route; do not seal a wet sponge in a closed container. Check visible corners for scraps, film, or standing water.
5. **Apply any raw-juice response separately.** If raw meat or poultry or its juice contacted the inner sink, clean first, then choose a product explicitly suitable for **that kitchen surface** and material under local authoritative guidance. Follow label contact time, rinsing, and drying. Without an approved product or method, keep the area from ready-to-eat food or clean-utensil contact until reliable guidance is available. Do not guess a bleach ratio or mix cleaning products.
6. **Leave a genuinely usable next round.** Normal drainage, no ordinary residue on accessible basin and rack areas, cleaner removed, and clear drying places for tools and dishes complete this reset. For standing water, internal odour, broken strainer, or product uncertainty, leave an explicit out-of-use or service note. A wipe does not repair a drain.

### Success

The normally used sink and drying area are cleaned under material guidance, free of product residue, and able to dry, with destinations for dishes and tools. A raw-juice-contact area has received its supported further treatment or remains explicitly isolated. Stop when drainage, material, or product conditions cannot be confirmed rather than presenting an unfinished area as ready.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Wet dishes go into the cupboard so the rack looks empty:** Let them dry on a clean surface. Clear the rack in stages instead of solving a queue by wet stacking.
- **A hidden blade or glass piece is sought by hand:** Stop blind searching. Make it visible, then use the sharp-object route to remove it.
- **Debris below the basket prompts pipe disassembly:** Stop at the removable basket. Deep objects and clogs belong with the maker or repair service.
- **Rough tools scratch stainless steel or coating:** Stop friction and read exact material guidance. Stronger acids or alkalis do not undo a scratch.
- **Raw-meat juice is followed by one water rinse:** Keep ready-to-eat food and clean tools away until local food-safety and product directions are completed.
- **Cleaner film remains or a wet sponge is sealed away:** Rinse again as the product directs and spread washing tools to dry; fragrance is not a cleanliness test.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers **visible accessible surfaces of a normally draining household kitchen sink and rack**. Stainless steel, ceramic, composite, coated sinks, and plastic racks accept different products; exact maker guidance controls. Further food-safety treatment follows local authority and the intended food-contact use. Use good lighting, accessible placement, or trusted help when seeing crumbs or bending is hard; stop when sharps cannot be seen safely. Dishes, rack, and sponge may need real drying time. Resetting does not evaporate all water instantly.
