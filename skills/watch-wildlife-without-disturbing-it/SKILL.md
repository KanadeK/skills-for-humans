## 中文

当你在管理方允许的公园或自然教育区域想观察普通野生动物时，用这份 Skill 从**原地、按本地规定的距离**看一小段时间，记下实见行为或诚实的“未见”，然后离开。结果不要求拍到近照；追逐、喂食、触摸或用叫声引动物出来都不是观察的“增强功能”。植物观察另有任务，危险动物遭遇与野外生存不在这里。

### 准备与输入

先查场地管理方当日开放区域、针对当地动物的最小观看距离、宠物/食物和摄影规则；不同公园的距离要求不同，不从别处搬数字。选有明确路标、能保持通道畅通且容易退出的公众区域，不蹲守巢穴、幼崽或警示区。带的食物收好，不给动物看/闻得到的诱饵。望远镜或相机长焦只是可选，肉眼观察也成立；不使用无人机、叫声播放、闪光灯或诱捕。同行人知道这次只看、不接触。若管理方警告某区域有危险动物或关闭，改地点，不靠本篇处理遭遇。

### 执行

1. **先定短时停点。** 在允许的位置停下，写大致日期、地点和“今天看____区域约一小段时间”；不必承诺看到某物种，也不公开稀有物种或巢穴的精确位置。
2. **先看动物是否受你影响。** 不向它靠近、挡住它的路径或围成一圈。依当地规则保持距离；若动物因为你转身、逃离、警戒或朝你走来，停止当前观察，沿允许路线增加距离并按场地工作人员指示处理。别为更近的镜头再上前。
3. **记录自然发生的事。** 如果从原位看得到，用一两句描述你确实看到的外形/动作及所处环境，例如“鸟在树上停后飞走”；不凭模糊轮廓猜物种、病况或幼崽被遗弃。看不到就写“这个时段未见”，仍保留时间与位置，不制造一次目击。
4. **让现场保持原状。** 不投喂、呼唤、触碰、捡蛋/羽毛或移动树枝；食物、垃圾和宠物按本地规则管好。同行者想走近时说明观察边界，并选择离开，不需要与他人争执距离数字。
5. **按边界收工。** 到时间、动物靠近、路面/天气变化或管理方要求时离开，带走自己的物品。记录“看见/未见/因影响动物而停止”；后者是及时退出，不冒充一次无干扰观察成功。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你能指出在哪里和何时从允许位置观察，**见到的实际行为或未见事实**，且没有追逐、投喂、触摸或引诱动物。即使没有目击，也可以完成一次有界、无打扰的尝试；动物明显因你改变行为时要退出。

- **目标动物躲起来：** 留下“未见”并换日，不追进草丛或用食物引出。
- **有人靠得过近或动物向你接近：** 自己按安全开放路线拉开距离/离开，必要时请工作人员处理，不伸手拍/喂。
- **摄影规则不明：** 先不拍，向管理方确认；即使允许拍照，也不拍巢穴位置或用闪光逼出反应。
- **你无法判断距离是否合规：** 退远、问管理方或停止，不用通用“几步”代替本地规定。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

仅适用于能从公共维护区域远观的普通自然观察，不教授大型动物遭遇、受伤动物救助、巢穴处理或动物疾病判断。地方、季节和动物不同，距离与区域限制由管理方决定。视力或听力不便时可用正式观景点、可访问的说明或可信同伴描述，并注明观察来源；不用冒险走近。照片和位置记录可能暴露敏感栖息地，只留必要的个人笔记。

## English

Use this Skill when you want to watch ordinary wildlife in a park or nature education area that permits visitors. Stay **in one allowed place at the local required distance** for a short observation, note real behaviour or an honest “none seen,” and leave. A close-up photo is not required. Following, feeding, touching, or playing calls to draw an animal out are not upgrades to observation. Plant observation is separate; dangerous encounters and wilderness survival are outside this Skill.

### Preparation and inputs

Check today's open areas, local minimum viewing distances for relevant animals, and pet, food, and photo rules from the site manager. Distances vary by park; do not import another park's number. Choose a signed, easy-exit public area where you will not block others. Do not camp beside a nest, young, or warning zone. Secure your food and bring no bait. Binoculars or a long lens are optional; eyes alone work. No drone, call playback, flash, or trap. Tell companions this is watching, not contact. If the manager warns of dangerous wildlife or closes an area, choose elsewhere; this Skill does not handle an encounter.

### Execution

1. **Set a short stopping point.** From the allowed place, note date, general location, and that you will watch this area briefly. Do not promise a species sighting or publish exact locations of rare wildlife or nests.
2. **Check whether you affect the animal.** Do not approach, block its route, or surround it. Keep local required distance. If it turns, flees, becomes alert because of you, or approaches you, end this view, increase distance by the allowed route, and follow site staff guidance. Do not step closer for a better frame.
3. **Record what occurred naturally.** If visible, write one or two sentences about appearance or action and surroundings, such as “a bird paused in a tree, then flew.” Do not diagnose species, illness, or abandoned young from a vague glimpse. If nothing appears, write “none seen in this period” with time and place rather than invent a sighting.
4. **Leave the setting as it was.** Do not feed, call, touch, collect eggs or feathers, or move branches. Manage food, waste, and pets by local rules. If a companion wants to approach, state the boundary and leave if needed; no debate over a universal distance is required.
5. **Close on the boundary.** Leave at your time, if wildlife approaches, conditions change, or staff ask. Take your belongings. Record “seen / none seen / stopped because I affected wildlife.” The last is a timely stop, not a claim of undisturbed watching.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You can say where and when you watched from an allowed place, **what behaviour you actually saw or that you saw none**, and that you did not chase, feed, touch, or attract an animal. No sighting can still be a bounded, nondisturbing attempt. Leave if the animal clearly changes behaviour in response to you.

- **The animal hides:** Write “none seen” and return another day; do not pursue it into vegetation or bait it.
- **Another person gets too close or wildlife approaches you:** Increase your own distance along a safe open route or leave; ask staff if needed. Do not reach for a photo or feeding.
- **Photography rules are unclear:** Do not photograph until the manager confirms. Even where allowed, avoid revealing nests or using flash to provoke a reaction.
- **You cannot judge the required distance:** Step back, ask the manager, or stop; generic “a few steps” is no local rule.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Only ordinary distant viewing from a public maintained area is in scope—not large-animal encounters, injured-animal rescue, nest handling, or disease assessment. Place, season, and species change distance and area limits; follow the manager. For limited sight or hearing, use an official viewpoint, accessible descriptions, or trusted help and label what you observed versus heard described. Moving closer is not an access alternative. Photos and locations can expose sensitive habitat; keep only needed personal notes.
