## 中文

当你只有几分钟、乐器在手边，却不知道从哪里开始时，用这份 Skill 完成一小段**可检查的练习**。结果不是“整首会了”，而是一次起点尝试、针对一个问题的调整、一次末次尝试，以及如实写下的变化。录音回听与节奏分析归另一任务；这里用当场能听见或看见的证据。

### 准备与输入

选你已知基本安全操作的乐器，找允许练习、音量不打扰他人的地方。材料可以是自创的几个音、老师允许的片段或你有权使用的谱，不需购买或复制付费课程。只选**一个观察点**，例如音符顺序、换音前的停顿或某个音是否清楚；别同时追求速度、音色和整首完成。写下“片段—观察点—停点”。若乐器故障、材料不可用或环境不允许发声，先解决条件或改约时间；想象中的练习不计完成。

### 执行

1. **划出小片段。** 缩到本轮能重复的长度，例如四个音或一次换指。写出末次检查的具体问题：“缓慢弹这四个音，到第三个音前还会停吗？”5–15 分钟是可用窗口，不是练满配额。
2. **做起点尝试。** 以舒服、可控的速度演奏一次，只记录所选观察点上你确实听到或看到的事，如“第三个音前停了”。“很烂”不是证据。听觉不便可选手指位置、按键顺序等可见动作；必须判断声音时请可信的人协助，不声称听见了未听到的声音。
3. **只改一个条件。** 对卡住的最小部分放慢并试几次；必要时减少音符或先只做动作，再接回原片段。每次核对同一观察点。若错误原样重复，改变分段或速度，不靠提高音量或加力赌成功。
4. **做末次检查。** 再演奏原定片段一次，对照同一观察点写一行“起点 → 改法 → 末次结果”。仍卡住就如实写“较慢时能接上，原速度仍停”，并记下次从哪开始。可检查不等于掌握，更不等于可演出。
5. **按实际状态收工。** 时间到、注意力散了或已检查过目标，就结束并按乐器说明收好。若疼痛、麻木、眩晕或出现异常刺耳、失控的声音，立即停下；这里不判断损伤，也不教带故障继续练。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你能指出一个具体片段、同一观察点上的起点与末次结果，以及下一轮入口。“末次仍没做好”可以是诚实的结果；只有流逝的时间不能冒充练习。

- **片段太长，总从开头重来：** 缩到出错前后几个音或一个换指，再比较起点和末次。
- **越练越快，听不出改变：** 放慢到能辨认观察点的速度；不设所有乐器通用的节拍数。
- **中途被打断：** 记下最后一个可重复片段和观察点；下次重新做起点尝试，不把两段拼成连续练习。
- **材料或乐器不可用：** 停在准备阶段，换有权使用的自创小片段或另约时间；不偷用付费谱，不拆修乐器。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

你已知道乐器的基本安全操作；技法教学、身体不适和维修交合适的老师或专业人员。环境、音量规则、听觉、视力和手部能力会改变观察方式，可用可见动作、可读标记或可信协助，不强制录音或看谱。没有进步也会留下一行真实记录；它是下次入口，不是成绩单。

## English

Use this Skill when you have a few minutes and an instrument nearby but no clear starting point. Complete **one small, checkable practice pass**: a baseline attempt, one adjustment in response to a problem, a final attempt, and an honest note of what changed. This is not mastery of a whole piece. Recording playback and rhythm analysis are separate tasks; this pass uses what you can observe in the moment.

### Preparation and inputs

Use an instrument whose basic safe operation you know, where playing is allowed and your volume suits people nearby. Choose a few notes you made, a passage your teacher permits, or a score you may lawfully use. You need not buy or copy a paid lesson. Choose **one observation point**: note order, a pause during a change, or clarity of one sound. Do not chase speed, tone, and a whole-piece finish at once. Write “passage—observation—stopping point.” If the instrument malfunctions, material is unavailable, or the setting rules out sound, fix that condition or reschedule; imagined playing is not a completed pass.

### Execution

1. **Mark a small passage.** Reduce it to something you can repeat now, such as four notes or one finger change. Write a concrete final-check question: “Playing these four notes slowly, do I still stop before the third?” Five to fifteen minutes is a useful window, not a quota to fill.
2. **Make a baseline attempt.** Play once at a comfortable, controllable pace. Record only what you really hear or see at the chosen observation point, such as “paused before note three.” “Terrible” is not evidence. If hearing is limited, choose finger position, key order, or another visible action; ask a trusted person to help where sound must be judged. Do not claim to hear what you cannot hear.
3. **Change one condition.** Slow the smallest difficult part and try it a few times. If needed, use fewer notes or rehearse the movement, then reconnect the original passage. Check the same observation each time. If the error merely repeats, change the segment or speed instead of adding force or volume.
4. **Make a final check.** Play the original small passage again. Compare the same observation point and write “baseline → change → final result.” If you still pause, say so: “connects slowly; still pauses at original speed.” Note where to begin next time. A checkable pass does not prove mastery or performance readiness.
5. **Finish at the real boundary.** Stop when time, attention, or the check is finished, and put the instrument away as instructed. Stop immediately for pain, numbness, dizziness, or unexpectedly harsh or uncontrolled sound. This Skill does not diagnose injury or teach playing through a malfunction.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You can name the small passage, baseline and final results for the same observation point, and the next entry point. “Still not right” is an honest result; elapsed minutes alone do not establish practice.

- **The passage is too long and you keep restarting:** Reduce it to a few notes around the snag or one finger change, then compare a new baseline and final attempt.
- **You speed up and cannot hear a change:** Slow to a pace at which your observation is clear. There is no universal metronome number for every instrument here.
- **You are interrupted:** Note the last repeatable segment and observation. Make a fresh baseline next time; do not combine two interrupted attempts into one continuous pass.
- **Material or instrument is unavailable:** Stop at preparation. Choose a self-made passage you may use or reschedule. Do not take someone's paid score or dismantle the instrument.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

You already know the instrument's basic safe operation. Technique instruction, bodily symptoms, and repairs belong with an appropriate teacher or professional. Place, volume rules, hearing, vision, and hand movement can change the observation method: use visible movements, readable marks, or trusted help. Recording and score reading are not compulsory. Even without improvement, one honest note gives you a place to start next time; it is not a grade.
