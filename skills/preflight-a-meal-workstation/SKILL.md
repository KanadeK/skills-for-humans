## 中文

当一顿普通家常饭的菜单与做法已经选定，你准备开始切配或开火，却还没实际确认工具与材料都在手边时，用这份 Skill 做一次**开工预检**。它的结果是可开始的就位工作台，或一份明确缺件与停止决定；它不替你发明菜谱、修理设备或执行烹调。

### 准备与输入

把完整做法放在可读位置，确认实际要做的份量、各步需要的原料与包装信息、锅/模具/刀/量具、可用台面、水和清洁条件、盛出或按计划留存的容器。确认哪些步骤会涉及生动物性食品与即食食品；它们需要分开的器具和操作安排。设备按钮或附件不确定时，查手中准确型号说明书；无法确认能否安全使用就先不用它。

### 执行

1. **从第一步读到最后一步。** 写出每步必需的东西，包括中途会用的勺、计时方式、装盘容器和已计划的安全留存容器。把“有最好”和“没有就做不了”分开，别只检查前五分钟。
2. **按真实状态摆原料。** 核对名称、需要的量、开封/保存说明、可用性和应先处理的部分；未知日期、异常包装或无法确认保存史的食品先退出本次菜单，不用“反正会煮”覆盖疑点。需要解冻的东西还要能按该食品的安全方法赶上。
3. **试摆器具与工作面。** 锅能安全放上实际可用的火口，配方要求的附件或模具存在，切配与盛装处可清洁、可触及、不被无关物品挡住。确认电器或燃气设备处于通常可用状态；发现损坏、异味或不懂的设置时停用并按制造商或专业途径处理，不自行维修。
4. **给生食和即食食物留开独立路径。** 至少知道哪些工具/台面将接触生食、哪些接触即食食物，以及如何避免相互污染；安排无法成立时先补齐安全操作区，不边切边临时决定。
5. **做一次空手彩排。** 顺着做法指一遍“从哪里取 → 用哪个器具 → 放到哪里 → 用什么检查完成”。手够不到、容器装不下、通道被挡或时间不足时，在开工前换可行的做法、取得适用物品、缩小菜单或停止。
6. **留下开工信号。** 必需物在可用位置，关键食品状态可确认，清洁/分区和最后盛装都有路径，才开始实际操作。还缺一件关键东西时写明它及下一步，不把开火当成寻找工具的计时器。

### 成功条件

你无需离开正在进行的危险步骤寻找必需原料、工具或容器；工作面可用，食品与器具关键状态已确认，第一步和最后一步都能指出物品位置。若关键条件缺失，明确暂缓或改做法，也是完成预检。

### 常见报错与补救

- **锅已经热了才发现没有量具或盛装容器：** 暂停进入下一步，在安全时关闭热源；重新按完整做法核对后再开始，不在高温旁翻箱倒柜。
- **同一工具必须碰生食又碰即食食品：** 先建立可执行的分开或按当地指导清洁的方案；无法做到就改菜单或停止。
- **附件看似相同却不确定能否装在机器上：** 查准确型号的说明，不靠“差不多能卡住”使用。
- **工作面或身体能力不支持动作：** 清出可触及的稳定台面、减少步骤或请可信的人协助；不能安全完成的做法不因食材已拿出来就必须继续。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

各家厨房、身体能力、预算、设备和食品标签不同；本 Skill 不给燃气、电气维修或危险刀工指示。视觉/听觉/握力/站立不便时使用放大清单、视觉或震动计时、坐姿可及位置与适用帮手。替代食材与有限设备的菜单重排属于相邻任务；此处只要确定自己到底能否安全开工。可能多洗一个准备容器，但通常能少一次开火后的找东西。

## English

Use this Skill when an ordinary meal and its method are chosen, but you have not confirmed that the ingredients, tools, and work space are actually ready before cutting or heat begins. The result is a **ready workstation** or a clear missing-item stop. It does not invent a recipe, repair an appliance, or perform the cooking.

### Preparation and inputs

Keep the complete method readable. Confirm amounts, food and package information needed at each step, pan or tin, knife and measures, available surface, water and cleaning conditions, and containers for serving or planned storage. Notice which steps involve raw animal food and ready-to-eat food; they need separate tools or a verified separation process. If an appliance setting or attachment is unclear, consult the exact model's directions. Do not use uncertain equipment as an experiment.

### Execution

1. **Read from first step through the last.** List essentials for each step, including a spoon needed later, a timer, serving dish, and any container for food you deliberately plan to keep. Separate helpful extras from items without which the method cannot run. The first five minutes are not the full recipe.
2. **Set out food in its real state.** Check identity, amount, opening and storage directions, usability, and items that need earlier preparation. Exclude food with an unknown date, damaged package, or unconfirmed storage history instead of assuming cooking will erase the uncertainty. Frozen food also needs a safe usable thawing or cook-from-frozen route in time.
3. **Dry-fit tools and space.** Check that the pan safely fits an available heat source, any specified attachment or tin exists, and a clean reachable preparation and serving surface is not blocked. Appliances should be in their normal usable condition. Stop using one with damage, an unusual smell, or an unclear setting, and use the maker or professional route rather than attempting a repair.
4. **Reserve separate paths for raw and ready-to-eat food.** Know which tools and surfaces will touch raw food, which will touch food eaten without further cooking, and how contact will be prevented. If the layout cannot work, set up a safe division before cutting rather than deciding mid-step.
5. **Rehearse with your hands empty.** Point through the method: “take it from here → use this tool → put it there → check completion this way.” If an item is unreachable, a container is too small, the route is blocked, or time is insufficient, change to a workable method, obtain a suitable item, reduce the menu, or stop before starting.
6. **Give yourself a start signal.** Begin physical preparation only when essentials are accessible, critical food and tool conditions are confirmed, and cleaning, separation, and final serving all have a route. Write a missing requirement and next action rather than using the lit burner as a tool-finding timer.

### Success

You will not need to leave a hazardous step to search for an essential ingredient, tool, or container. The surface works, key food and equipment conditions are known, and you can point to the first and last step's supplies. Deferring or changing the method when a critical condition fails is also a completed preflight.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The pan is hot before you find the measure or serving dish:** Stop before the next step and turn off heat when safe. Check the full method before restarting; do not rummage beside an active burner.
- **One tool must touch raw and ready-to-eat food:** Establish real separation or a locally supported cleaning process first. If that cannot be done, change the menu or stop.
- **An attachment looks similar but its appliance fit is uncertain:** Read the directions for the exact model. “It seems to clip in” is not compatibility evidence.
- **The surface or your physical capacity does not support an action:** Clear a stable reachable space, remove steps, or use trusted help. Food already placed on the counter does not oblige you to continue an unsafe method.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Kitchens, physical abilities, budgets, equipment, and labels differ. This Skill does not teach gas or electrical repair or hazardous knife work. Use enlarged lists, visual or vibrating timers, seated reachable work, and appropriate help when vision, hearing, grip, or standing is a barrier. Ingredient substitution and menu redesign for limited equipment are separate tasks. Here you decide whether you can safely begin. You may wash one more preparation container and avoid one mid-cook search.
