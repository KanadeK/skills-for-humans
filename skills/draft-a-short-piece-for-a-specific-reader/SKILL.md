## 中文

当你想写一小段**自己的文字**，主题已大致确定，却只有想法、笔记或一个写不下去的开头时，用这份 Skill 写出有开头、主体和有意停点的完整初稿。可以是随笔、说明或小故事；文体是输入，不按文体复制多套流程。这里不代写法律/医疗/财务说明，不引用未经许可的大段作品，也不把未经核实的事实直接对外发布。

### 准备与输入

先写一句给自己看的任务句：“给谁看、希望对方看完知道/感到/能做什么、这次不写什么。”决定一个现实可完成的篇幅或时长，选一个能保存草稿的纸页或文件。若会公开，确认你有权使用里面的人名、照片、引文与素材；不确定的先用自己的观察和语言，不借用受保护角色、他人私密故事或整段原文。视觉、打字或手写不便时用本人可控制的语音输入/大字工具或可信协助，不要求某种软件。

### 执行

1. **把大题缩成一次可结束的文本。** 用一句话写这篇只回答/展示的一件事，再列最多几个确实服务它的材料；其他有趣分支先记在旁边，不让短稿变成某主题全史。
2. **为读者挑一个可进入的开头。** 从具体时刻、问题或事实进入，让读者知道自己为何要继续；不必先写完美标题、宏大背景或公开承诺。读者需要的上下文放正文，不指望他们读心。
3. **安排一个中段和终点。** 中段让一件事发生、解释或推进；结尾给出这段的变化、判断或下一步，而不是突然断在“还有很多可说”。说明文可用“问题—关键依据—现在怎么办”，叙事可用“起点—变化—停点”，按实际目的调整，不当成固定公式。
4. **连续写完可读的一版。** 先把开头、中段、结尾都写出，再回头改句子；卡在一个词或事实时作清楚的“待核”记号，并继续有依据的部分。带待核事实的草稿不能直接对外当成事实，先查可靠来源或删去该句。
5. **做一次完整性检查。** 从头读到尾，不看自己的提纲，问：目标读者能知道谁/什么、为什么与结尾发生了什么吗？缺一段就补最短必要内容；超出范围的材料移出本文。完成状态是“有全文可修”，不是“每句都好”。

### 成功条件、常见问题与补救

你有一篇围绕单一目的、读者可进入且有结束点的原创短稿；不确定的事实/许可被标记并暂不发布。下一步可进入结构修订，初稿不需要以发表级完美为门槛。

- **一页全是背景，正文还没开始：** 删到读者确实需要的几句，把主要动作或问题提前。
- **写第一句写了半小时：** 暂用直接说明“这篇要谈什么”的普通句子，先把结尾写出来，之后再挑开头。
- **材料太多：** 只留下服务任务句的部分，其余放到另一个题目记录，不给本文塞脚注仓库。
- **时间用完且没有结尾：** 写出当前能诚实停在何处的短结尾；若无法成为全文，就保存为未完成草稿，不宣称完成。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这处理普通低风险短篇写作，不承诺获奖、考试分数或读者喜欢。读者可以是自己、同意阅读的朋友或公开读者，选择会改变解释量与隐私边界；不要为假想“所有人”写到失去具体对象。纸、手机或电脑都可用，核心动作不依赖 AI。现实副作用是有一份需要修订而非需要被崇拜的初稿。

## English

Use this Skill when you want to write a short piece **in your own words** but have only an idea, notes, or an opening you cannot get past. Produce a complete first draft with a beginning, middle, and deliberate end. It may be a personal piece, explanation, or small story. Genre changes the inputs, not the need for a title matrix. This does not draft legal, medical, or financial advice, copy long unlicensed passages, or publish unchecked facts as true.

### Preparation and inputs

Write a private task sentence: “Who is this for, what should they know, feel, or be able to do afterward, and what is outside this piece?” Choose a realistic length or time and paper or a basic editor where you can keep the draft. For public work, check your right to names, images, quotes, and source material. If uncertain, use your own observation and prose rather than a protected character, another person's private story, or an upstream paragraph. Use controllable dictation, large type, or trusted help for vision, typing, or handwriting needs; no specific software is required.

### Execution

1. **Shrink the broad subject to a finishable text.** State the one question or experience this piece covers and list only a few materials that serve it. Park interesting branches nearby rather than turn a short draft into the entire history of a topic.
2. **Give this reader an entry.** Start at a concrete moment, question, or fact so they know why to continue. A perfect title, giant background, or public promise can wait. Put necessary context in the text rather than expect mind-reading.
3. **Plan a middle and an end.** Let something happen, be explained, or advance in the middle. End with a change, judgement, or next step rather than cut off at “there is much more.” An explanation might move from problem to evidence to action; a story from start to change to stop. Adjust to your actual purpose, not a compulsory formula.
4. **Write one readable version through.** Get opening, middle, and end onto the page before polishing every sentence. If one word or factual claim stalls you, mark it clearly for verification and continue with what you can support. Do not publish a draft with an unchecked factual claim; verify it from a reliable source or remove it.
5. **Check completeness once.** Read top to bottom without your outline. Can the intended reader tell who or what matters, why, and what the ending changed? Add the shortest missing passage and remove material outside scope. “A whole text ready to revise” is the finish, not “every sentence is excellent.”

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You have an original short draft serving one purpose, giving this reader an entry and a real end. Uncertain facts and permissions are marked and held from publication. Structural revision can begin; publication-grade perfection is not the first-draft gate.

- **A page of background precedes the piece:** Keep only context the reader needs and move the main action or question forward.
- **The first sentence takes half an hour:** Use a plain statement of the topic, write the ending, and reconsider the opener afterward.
- **Too much material arrives:** Keep what serves the task sentence and record the rest as another possible piece rather than a footnote warehouse.
- **Time ends with no ending:** Write an honest short stop where the piece now stands. If it cannot be a whole text, save it as unfinished rather than claim a completed draft.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is ordinary low-stakes short writing, without promises of prizes, grades, or audience approval. The reader may be you, a friend who agreed to read, or the public; that choice changes context and privacy. Writing for “everyone” can erase the actual reader. Paper, phone, and computer all work without AI. The side effect is a draft that needs revision rather than admiration.
