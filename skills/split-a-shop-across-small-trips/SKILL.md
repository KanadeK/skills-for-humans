## 中文

当你已有未来几天要买的普通食品或日用品，但**一趟无法安全带回，或家中冷藏空间无法一次接住**时，用这份 Skill 决定哪天买哪一部分。它安排的是几次真实采购机会之间的分配，不设计单趟跨店路线，也不替每项计算包装用量。若一次采购本来可行，不必给购物清单安装日程管理器。

### 准备与输入

拿出已核实的需求清单，给每项写“最早需要”和“最迟可买”时间、预计占用的重量/体积，以及标签要求的未开封和开封后储存条件。写下未来确实能去店或可接收商品的时间，而非日历上看似空白的格子；也写出每趟你能稳定携带的负担、真实冷藏空间、预算和可能的交通费用。看不清标签或不确定家中库存时，先放大、询问或核实，不把未知商品分配到行程里。

### 执行

1. **只规划到下一次可靠机会。** 先圈出最近两三次你实际上能完成采购的日期；若只有一次，就按一次来。列出这些日期之前必须满足的用途，其他需求留在后续而不是伪装成今天的负担。
2. **给每件商品标记等待能力。** 能按标签在现有条件下放到后几天的，可在合适的一趟集中补；需要低温、但冷藏位不足的，只有在你确实能按要求带回、放下并使用时才排入当次。没有可靠冷藏时，把需冷藏品从“买来放几天”栏删掉，改选合格常温形式或安排到能立即安全处理的时间；多跑一次不会制造冰箱。
3. **把日期和负重一起排。** 从最早要用的项目开始放进最早可行的一趟，逐趟合计**整件商品**的重量与体积，保留必要的手和空间。不要把一大包拆成日历上的三份，除非现场确实能分别购买。要买的具体包装量再按实际用途核对。
4. **检查每趟是否真的成立。** 同一天去店与返家时间、开门时段、你的体力、交通费、商品包装和家中可用空间都需相容。需要两趟但你只可能去一趟，方案就不成立；可改商品形式、选择可行的取得方式、减少这段时间的需求，或诚实延期。跨店顺序由单趟路线任务处理。
5. **写好错过一趟的退路。** 对下一趟的关键项目标出“若该趟取消，之前可用什么、哪件先延后、何时重新判断”。不要预先把需要冷藏的商品买来等待一个可能取消的计划。普通存货不足且没有安全替代时，明确更改这几天的用途或餐食。
6. **输出小清单。** 每个真实日期只列这次要买的东西、预计负重、必须满足的冷藏/接收条件、总预算，以及一次取消后的下一步。出门前用当日信息复核，不把上周的营业时间当成永久 API。

### 成功条件

你有一组按真实日期排列、每趟都带得回且有地方放的有限清单；每项在需要前到手，关键商品在错过一趟时也有明确的延期或替代决定。若这些条件不能同时成立，输出“当前计划不可行”并删改需求，同样完成本次判断。

### 常见报错与补救

- **第二趟只是日历空格：** 先确认你有时间、交通和体力，再把商品分给它；否则减少承诺的覆盖天数或改取得方式。
- **每趟都轻，但冷藏总量放不下：** 按每次到家后的真实剩余空间重新排；能常温保存的合格形式可替代，不能把需冷藏品留在台面上等下一顿。
- **包装不能按你写的份数拆卖：** 用实际可买整包重算，或换规格；纸上分装不减轻从店到家的重量。
- **一次小采购临时取消：** 先保住当前已有的安全可用物品，按退路删改用途和下次清单；不要把缺的一件假定为已经在家。
- **多次交通费用或等待超出预算：** 比较你当前能确认的总费用，改用可核实的取得方式或缩小这段计划；低单次负重不保证低总成本。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这适用于普通采购，不提供医疗饮食、过敏替代或食物保存时长的通用数值。店铺、交通、个人体力、冷藏设备与标签随地区和家庭而变；用你手中的商品说明及当地食品安全指导。无法多次外出时，可信协助或实际可用的配送可以作为取得方式，但需先核对费用、交付位置和接收条件。结果可能是更频繁出门、更少浪费，或承认某段需求暂时无法满足；不能把任何一种写成必然。

## English

Use this Skill when you know what ordinary groceries or household supplies you need over the next few days, but **one trip is too much to carry safely or your available cold storage cannot take the whole shop at once**. It allocates purchases across actual future opportunities. It does not design the stops within one trip or calculate the right pack size for each item. If one shop works, your list does not need a calendar subscription.

### Preparation and inputs

Start with a checked list. For each item, note when it is first needed and the latest useful purchase time, its approximate packed weight and size, and label instructions before and after opening. Mark occasions when you can truly visit a shop or receive goods, rather than empty-looking calendar squares. Record what you can steadily carry on each trip, real cold-storage space, your budget, and any travel costs. If a label is unreadable or existing stock is uncertain, enlarge it, ask, or check first; do not assign unknown items to a trip.

### Execution

1. **Plan only through the next reliable opportunities.** Mark the next two or three dates when you can actually shop. If there is just one, plan for one. List the uses that must be met before those dates; later needs are not automatically today's load.
2. **Mark how long each item can wait.** Products you can keep under their labels in your real conditions may go on a suitable later trip. A product needing cold storage belongs on a trip only when you can bring it home, fit it in, and use it as required. With no reliable cooling, remove chilled food from any “buy now, hold for days” slot. Choose a valid shelf-stable form or a time when you can handle it safely right away. Extra trips do not create a refrigerator.
3. **Allocate dates and loads together.** Start with the earliest required use and place each item on the earliest workable trip. Total the weight and size of **whole products as sold**, leaving room and hands for the journey. A large pack cannot be divided into three calendar entries unless the shop really sells it separately. Confirm its actual pack amount against the intended uses.
4. **Check each trip is real.** Shop and return time, opening hours, your capacity, travel cost, actual packs, and space at home must agree. If the plan needs two trips and you can make only one, change the product form, choose a feasible way to obtain it, reduce what this period must cover, or defer. Plan stops within a trip in the separate route workflow.
5. **Give a missed trip a fallback.** For critical items on the next visit, note what usable stock would remain, what use you would postpone, and when you would decide again if that visit is cancelled. Do not buy refrigerated food early just to wait for an uncertain plan. If stock and safe alternatives both fail, revise the meal or other intended use for those days.
6. **Write short trip lists.** For each real date, list only its items, expected load, required cooling or receipt condition, total budget, and the next move if it is cancelled. Recheck current information before leaving; last week's shop hours are not a permanent API.

### Success

You have dated, limited lists that you can carry and have room to store. Each item can arrive before it is needed, and a missed trip has an explicit deferral or replacement decision for critical uses. If these conditions cannot all hold, “this plan is not currently feasible” with a revised need is also a complete answer.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The second trip is only an empty calendar slot:** Confirm time, transport, and capacity before assigning purchases to it. Otherwise cover fewer days or change how you obtain the goods.
- **Each trip is light, but the fridge is full overall:** Reallocate against actual space after every arrival. A valid shelf-stable form may work; chilled food cannot wait on a counter for the next meal.
- **The pack cannot be sold in the portions on your list:** Recalculate with whole packs actually sold, or choose another size. Dividing it on paper does not reduce the load home.
- **A small trip is cancelled:** Use confirmed safe stock first, then revise the intended uses and next list. Do not count a missing item as already at home.
- **Repeated travel or waiting exceeds the budget:** Compare total costs you can confirm now, use another verified way to obtain the items, or reduce this planning period. A lighter single trip is not necessarily cheaper overall.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary shopping, not medical diets, allergy substitutions, or universal food-storage times. Shops, transit, personal capacity, cooling equipment, and labels vary by place and household. Follow the particular product and local food-safety guidance. If repeated trips are inaccessible, trusted help or an actually available delivery service may be a way to obtain goods after checking its cost, handoff point, and receipt conditions. You may go out more often, waste less, or find that a need cannot yet be met. None is guaranteed.
