## 中文

当普通少油浅锅煎炒的一批食物**到了配方应翻动的观察点却黏住锅面**，而当前没有烟、明火、失控热油或设备异常时，加载这份 Skill。目标是区分“还没到自然松脱时机”与“正在焦深或会撕坏食物/锅面”，轻柔放开或安全停下。本篇是已发生的黏附恢复；正常观察翻动仍由 H033 已提交的 `turn-and-monitor-a-saute-pan` 负责，明火立即退出日常流程。

### 准备与输入

先核食物配方是否本来要求一面静置、锅具表面是涂层、不锈钢、铸铁还是其他，以及本锅说明允许的铲具。看食物接触面颜色、锅里油是否冒烟、锅柄是否稳、自己能否安全触到火力开关。已有焦味、烟、异常飞溅或油火时，先关热并远离危险，按适用安全指导处理，不把“再铲一下”当最后机会。动物性食品仍需其独立安全熟度证据，不能因表面黏住就端走。

### 执行

1. **先停止用力拉扯。** 在配方允许的翻动点，用兼容锅面的工具从一小处轻试；如果食物表面尚未形成可松动的接触层而没有焦深，就让其再受控接触一小段并持续看守。不要反复猛刮涂层或从食物最薄处硬拽。
2. **用表面状态决定等还是停。** 颜色正按配方发展、无焦味和烟且锅稳定，可小幅等候再试；表面快速变黑、焦味出现或油开始冒烟，立即安全降热/关热，让锅留在原位。不要往有热油的锅里突然泼水，也不端热锅追求“离火快一点”。
3. **只在松动时轻取。** 找到食物能释放的边缘，用适合该锅的铲具稳稳托起；若只释放一部分，先处理可安全取出的部分，其余停热后按锅具说明待温度下降再处置。不要用金属尖器去撬不适合金属的涂层，也不要为了外形完整继续过热。
4. **核食品完成而非只核松脱。** 已释放食物如果尚未达到配方的食物特定完成条件，仍需在安全、合适的锅和温度下继续；肉禽海鲜等需核相应安全熟度。黏锅本身不能证明“差不多熟”，撕裂也不能证明不能吃。
5. **下一批才调整原因。** 这批安全停止后，按具体锅具/配方反思是否预热不足或过高、油量不符、食物太湿或翻得过早；不同锅面的制造商建议不同。只在下一批冷静重设，不在冒烟锅中加大量油或试不熟悉的去黏技巧。

### 完成、常见问题与补救

食物已在不强刮锅面的情况下松脱并继续按食物条件完成，或你已停止热源、保留可安全取出的部分并让其余待锅冷却；火、烟、伤人的飞溅立即退出。食物是否可食用和锅具是否受损须分别判断，不用“铲下来”替代两者。

- **刚下锅就急着翻：** 先看本食物的配方是否需要初期接触，不用不停试铲制造破碎。
- **表面越来越黑仍不松：** 关热，不以更长高温换自然松脱；后续质量与安全另核。
- **锅具涂层被刮出划痕：** 停止硬刮并按该锅制造商说明评估是否继续使用，不在本篇修涂层。
- **食物裂开但中心未熟：** 继续用适用方法核安全熟度，不能因碎了就缩短必要加热。
- **站立、握力或视觉条件难以轻试：** 用稳妥可握的锅面兼容工具、减小下批负载或请现场协助；无法安全控热就先关热。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

只处理普通浅锅少油、可持续看守的食物黏附，不处理深油炸、糖浆、专业高温锅法或锅具维修。铸铁与不锈钢可能需要不同的预热和油量，不能把某一品牌分钟数作为通用修复。停下后这批食物可能不够完整；保住热源控制与锅面比把每块食物完整撬起更重要。

## English

Load this Skill when food in an ordinary shallow, low-oil sauté **sticks at the recipe's intended turning check**, with no smoke, flame, uncontrolled hot oil, or equipment fault. Distinguish “not ready to release yet” from scorching or damage, then release gently or stop safely. This is recovery after adhesion has begun; H033's pending `turn-and-monitor-a-saute-pan` covers ordinary turning. Fire exits this everyday flow immediately.

### Preparation and inputs

Check whether the recipe calls for one face to rest, what the pan surface is, and which utensil its maker allows. Observe colour where food meets the pan, oil smoke, handle stability, and safe access to heat control. For scorched odour, smoke, abnormal splatter, or oil fire, turn heat off when safe and leave the hazard under the applicable safety guidance. “One more scrape” is not a recovery plan. Animal foods still need their own safe-doneness evidence; adhesion does not make them ready to serve.

### Execution

1. **Stop forceful pulling.** At an allowed turning point, test one small edge with a pan-compatible utensil. If the contact surface has not yet developed a releasable layer and is not getting too dark, allow a short controlled contact while attending it. Do not repeatedly scrape a coating or drag a thin food section until it tears.
2. **Choose waiting or stopping by surface evidence.** If colour is developing as the recipe expects with no scorched smell or smoke and a stable pan, wait a little and test again. If the surface blackens fast, smells scorched, or oil smokes, lower or stop heat safely and leave the pan in place. Do not throw water into hot oil or carry a hot pan around to make it “leave heat faster.”
3. **Lift only as it releases.** From a loose edge, support the food with a suitable spatula. If only part releases, take what can be removed safely; turn heat off and handle the rest under cookware guidance once it cools. Do not pry a non-metal-safe coating with pointed metal, or overheat the food for appearance.
4. **Check cooking, not only release.** Food that came free but has not met its recipe-specific endpoint still needs safe continued cooking with suitable equipment. Meat, poultry, seafood, and similar foods need the applicable safe-doneness check. Sticking does not prove “almost cooked,” and tearing does not prove the food is unusable.
5. **Change setup for the next batch.** Once this batch is safely stopped, use the actual cookware and recipe to consider inadequate or excessive preheat, oil amount, wet food, or early turning. Makers differ by surface. Reset for a later batch, not by pouring extra oil into a smoking pan or improvising unfamiliar release tricks.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

Food released without forceful pan scraping and was finished against food-specific conditions, or you stopped heat, kept any safely removable part, and let the rest wait for a cooler pan. Flame, smoke, or harmful splatter exits this flow. Edibility and pan condition need separate checks; “I scraped it free” proves neither.

- **You turn immediately after loading:** Check whether this recipe needs initial contact rather than repeatedly testing and tearing.
- **The face grows black while still stuck:** Turn off heat; do not trade longer high heat for a hoped-for natural release. Assess later quality and safety separately.
- **You scratched a coated surface:** Stop hard scraping and follow the exact cookware maker's guidance on further use; this Skill does not repair a coating.
- **The food tears but its centre is underdone:** Continue under an applicable method and check safe doneness. Damage to shape does not shorten needed cooking.
- **Grip, standing, or vision limits make a gentle test hard:** Use a steadier pan-safe tool, reduce the next batch, or get in-person help. If you cannot control heat safely, turn it off first.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers attended, ordinary shallow low-oil cooking, not deep frying, sugar syrup, extreme-heat professional methods, or cookware repair. Cast iron and stainless steel may call for different heat and oil, so no maker's exact minutes become a universal fix. The batch may lose some shape. Keeping heat and pan under control matters more than prying every piece intact.
