## 中文

当你的一份普通蛋糕已经**按原配方确认烤熟、状态安全**，但仍粘在完整烤模里，轻轻倒扣也不出来时，用这份 Skill 找到安全可试的松边动作，并在失败时改为模内分份。目标是拿到能吃的蛋糕，同时保住手、模具和台面；完整外形只是可选加分项。这里不处理未熟中心或损坏的涂层。

### 准备与输入

找原配方的熟度与脱模/冷却顺序，确认中心已熟、放置史可靠；查看模具是否不粘涂层、是否有中心筒、边缘哪些地方可接触。准备稳定干净的台面、大于模具的干净托盘或冷却架、柔性刮刀/尼龙工具，以及处理仍温热模具所需的干燥隔热用品。若模具破裂、涂层剥落或蛋糕接触到不洁碎片，停止使用受影响部分，别为上桌把异物混进食物。

这份 Skill 不教用刀尖硬撬不粘涂层、猛烈摔模、用沸水蒸汽熏模或反复高温烤模。缺少安全取出的条件时，可以改变呈现，不欠模具一个完整圆环。

### 执行

1. **先排除“其实还没烤熟”。** 按原配方检查中心和厚处的完成状态；湿生面糊不能试吃，也不能靠脱模变熟。熟度不确定就停止这条取出流程，按配方和当地食品安全指导处理。
2. **按配方给它合适的冷却时间。** 让模具稳放，按这份蛋糕的说明决定何时尝试脱模；不要因为着急在烫手状态抓模，也别假定每种蛋糕都应完全冷透才放。配方没写时，先等到能够安全握住和操作，再进入小动作。
3. **只松可安全触及的粘点。** 用适合模具材质的柔性刮刀或尼龙工具，沿可见边缘和有中心筒时的内缘轻轻走一圈。只向蛋糕与模具之间的缝隙工作，不向涂层刮刻；若工具卡住或需要明显蛮力，就停。金属刀具尤其不适合不粘涂层。
4. **一次受控倒扣。** 把干净托盘或架盖在模具上，稳稳扶住两者一起翻转，并尽量保持靠近台面。轻轻敲或晃到可感到蛋糕松动；没有松动就放回正面，而不是抬高、猛摔或让热模滑脱。若蛋糕已裂，先接住能安全取出的部分。
5. **决定取出还是改呈现。** 边缘可见仍有粘点时，可重新放稳，只对那处再试一次柔性松边。仍抵抗就停止强脱模；待安全拿取后，用不伤模具的工具从模内取小份到干净碗或托盘，明确告诉吃的人形状变了。若取出后已经碎裂，按 H047 的“已熟成品结构失败”判断呈现；不继续用模具承担修蛋糕的成本。
6. **收尾。** 检查取出的食物无包装、涂层或器具碎片，按蛋糕和实际添加品的食品安全条件上桌或保存。等模具适合清洗时按生产商说明清理，不用尖锐工具刮涂层来追最后一块蛋糕。

### 成功条件

你安全取出可食用的完整蛋糕或部分蛋糕，或安全地从模内分份；模具和手没有因强行脱模受损，熟度、接触史和剩余去向可说清。蛋糕不完整不等于这次判断失败。

### 常见报错与补救

- **倒扣几次不出就猛敲：** 放回稳定台面，检查可见粘点和配方冷却时机；再试柔性松边一次，仍不动就模内分份。
- **用金属刀插进不粘涂层：** 立刻停手，换模具允许的柔性工具；若涂层已剥落，隔离受影响食物，不靠挑走肉眼可见的一片就宣称无异物。
- **热模从手里滑落或盘子太小：** 先放稳并换大于模具的托盘，等能安全抓握再做一次受控翻转；别在空中接蛋糕。
- **一松边蛋糕就碎：** 接住安全部分，改小份上桌；完整外形不再是必须完成的任务。
- **中心仍湿或放置史不明：** 停止脱模补救，先按食品安全和原配方处理；装饰不能补上未完成的烘烤。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里只处理已烤熟、模具完整的普通家用烤蛋糕；不同配方、模具材质和涂层有不同冷却与工具要求，以手里的说明为准。握力或翻模受限时请可信的人协助，或在模内安全分份，不要求单人举起沉重热模。少量粘连可能留下碎屑和额外清洗，分份可能改变端上桌的外观与数量；不为保住外形损坏器具。

## English

Use this Skill when an ordinary cake is **fully baked under its recipe and safely handled** but remains stuck in an intact pan after a gentle attempt to invert it. Find a safe release step, then switch to portions served from the pan if it resists. The result is edible cake with hands, pan, and counter intact. A perfect silhouette is optional. This does not treat an underbaked centre or damaged coating.

### Preparation and inputs

Find the original recipe's doneness, cooling, and release order. Confirm a cooked centre and reliable holding history. Note whether the pan has a nonstick coating, a centre tube, and accessible sticking points. Set up a stable clean counter, a clean tray or rack larger than the pan, a flexible spatula or nylon tool suitable for its surface, and dry heat protection if the pan is still warm. If the pan is broken, its coating is peeling, or cake has touched dirty fragments, stop using affected food rather than mixing foreign matter into a serving.

This Skill does not teach prying a nonstick surface with a knife point, slamming the pan, steaming it over boiling water, or repeatedly heating it hard. When safe release is unavailable, change the serving form; you do not owe the pan a whole ring.

### Execution

1. **Exclude underbaking first.** Use the original recipe's cues for the centre and thick parts. Do not taste wet raw batter or assume unmoulding will cook it. If doneness is uncertain, stop this release path and use the recipe and local food-safety guidance.
2. **Give it the recipe's cooling interval.** Rest the pan on a stable surface and follow this cake's instructions for when to release. Do not grab a scalding pan from impatience or assume all cakes must be completely cold. If no timing is given, wait until you can handle it safely before small release steps.
3. **Loosen only accessible sticking points.** Use a flexible spatula or nylon tool allowed by the pan material. Gently trace visible outer edges and the inner edge of a centre tube where present. Work only in the seam between cake and pan, without gouging a coating. Stop if the tool jams or requires force. Metal blades are especially unsuitable for nonstick coatings.
4. **Make one controlled inversion.** Put the clean tray or rack over the pan, hold the two firmly, and turn them together close to the counter. Tap or jiggle gently until the cake can be felt releasing. If nothing moves, return it upright instead of raising it high, slamming it, or risking a slipping hot pan. Catch safely releasable parts if the cake has cracked.
5. **Choose release or a new presentation.** If a visible spot still sticks, set the pan down and make one more gentle pass there. If it still resists, stop forced release. Once safe to handle, use a pan-safe tool to lift small portions into clean bowls or onto a tray, and tell diners that the shape changed. If the removed cake is broken, H047 covers presentation after release. Do not make the pan pay for the cake's appearance.
6. **Finish.** Check portions for paper, coating, or utensil fragments, then serve or store under the cake's and any added ingredient's actual food-safety conditions. When the pan is ready to wash, follow its maker's care guidance instead of scraping its coating for the last crumb.

### Success

You have safely removed an edible whole or part cake, or safely served it from the pan. Neither hands nor pan were damaged by forced release, and you can account for doneness, contact history, and leftovers. An incomplete cake is not automatically a failed decision.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Repeated inversion becomes harder banging:** Put the pan on a stable counter. Check visible sticking points and the recipe's cooling stage; make one more gentle flexible pass, then portion from the pan if it will not release.
- **A metal knife goes into nonstick coating:** Stop and switch to a permitted flexible tool. If coating has peeled, isolate affected food; removing one visible flake does not prove there are no fragments.
- **A hot pan slips or the receiving plate is too small:** Park it, choose a tray larger than the pan, and wait for a safe grip before one controlled turn. Do not catch a cake in midair.
- **The cake breaks as the edge loosens:** Catch safe parts and serve smaller portions. Whole shape is no longer required.
- **The centre is wet or holding history unknown:** Stop release recovery and follow food safety and the original recipe first; decoration cannot finish baking.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This applies to fully baked ordinary home cake in an intact pan. Recipes, pan materials, and coatings have different cooling and tool rules; follow the ones in your hands. If lifting or turning is difficult, ask a trusted person to help or portion safely from the pan rather than lifting a heavy hot vessel alone. Sticking may leave crumbs and more washing; portions may change presentation and count. Do not damage equipment to preserve a silhouette.
