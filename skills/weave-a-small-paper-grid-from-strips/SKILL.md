## 中文

当你有少量可自由使用的普通纸，想实际试一次**纸条上下交错**而非折一个立体支架或把纸片贴成拼贴时，加载这份 Skill。结果是一小张能拿起查看、横竖纸条确实互相穿插的原创网格；颜色和图案由你决定，不复制博物馆作品、受保护纹样或付费模板。危险刀具、热胶、喷胶和承重用途不在本篇。

### 准备与输入

准备一张做底的纸与若干宽度大致相容的自有纸条；可用现成预切条，或在安全稳定桌面用普通剪刀裁，握持不便时请可信的人预切。底纸要留出边框，横向条需能穿过底纸的平行开口并有可固定的端部。先用废纸试一条；太薄易撕、太厚难弯就换纸，不用更锋利刀具强行处理。

### 执行

1. **做能连在一起的底纸“经条”。** 将底纸对折，画几条从折边向外、彼此不交叉的线，每条都在外边缘前停住；用普通安全剪刀沿线剪开后展开。保留下来的外框把这些平行条连住。若不便剪，可用已安全预切的底纸；不能让切口一路剪到外边使底纸散开。
2. **先穿第一条“纬条”。** 将一条横向纸条依次从底纸平行条上面、下面、上面、下面经过，轻轻拉平，留住两端。纸若明显拉扯起毛，放松并调整条宽，不靠蛮力通关。
3. **第二条反向开始。** 下一条先从下面、上面交替，使相邻行互相错开；逐行往下排，随时用手或平尺轻轻并齐，不把纸条推得起褶。你可用自己的颜色序列形成原创图案，但完成判断靠**交错与可拿起**，不是像某张参考图。
4. **按材料许可固定端部。** 把横条两端折回或用少量符合纸材的胶/胶带固定在背面；若用胶，按产品说明等干，别把整张纸浸湿。拿起一角轻试，松条就局部重穿/重固定，不覆盖整面强粘。

### 完成、常见问题与补救

底纸外框仍完整，几行纸条相邻起点交替，横竖在多个位置真正穿插，端部固定后能轻拿起而不散。只把几条纸平铺相交、没有上下穿过，不算织成网格。

- **切口剪穿到底纸边：** 换一张底纸，留框再剪；不要靠更多胶假装经条连着。
- **每行都从同一面起：** 逐行核上/下交替，需要时抽一条重穿，别把错误锁进胶里。
- **纸条太软、太窄或易破：** 改用较结实的自有纸或更宽预切条；小网格比密网格更易起步。
- **胶让纸起皱：** 按产品减少用量或改可移除纸胶带，平放待干；不加热烘干。
- **手眼协调不便：** 用对比色、宽条、可访问照明、预切材料或现场协助；仍由你确认上/下结构。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这是一张低风险纸艺练习，不教刀具、文化特定纹样再现或有载荷的织物制作。普通剪刀应在合适空间使用，儿童按实际监护要求。纸的厚薄、颜色和尺寸影响操作，不能把博物馆活动的精确英寸写成唯一规格。成品可能有边缘不齐，但交错结构和端部稳定可核。

## English

Load this Skill when you have ordinary paper you may use and want to try **over-under interlacing**, rather than folding a self-standing form or gluing a collage. Finish with a small original grid that can be lifted and shows actual crossing strips. Choose your own colours and pattern; do not copy a museum artwork, protected cultural motif, or paid template. Sharp blades, hot or spray glue, and load-bearing use are outside this Skill.

### Preparation and inputs

Use one owned backing sheet and a few roughly compatible strips. Pre-cut strips work, or use ordinary scissors at a stable table. Ask trusted help to pre-cut when grip is difficult. Leave a border around the backing so horizontal strips can pass through parallel openings with ends left for fastening. Test one strip on scrap. Change paper if too thin tears or too stiff will not bend instead of reaching for a sharper blade.

### Execution

1. **Make connected backing strips.** Fold the backing in half and draw a few parallel non-crossing lines from the fold, stopping before the outside edge. Cut along them with ordinary safety scissors and unfold. The outer border keeps the parallel warp strips joined. Use a safely pre-cut backing if cutting is inaccessible. Slits running through the edge make a loose pile, not a connected grid.
2. **Weave the first cross strip.** Pass one horizontal strip over, under, over, under the backing strips in turn. Ease it flat with ends still available. If it snags or fuzzes, loosen it and adjust width instead of pulling hard.
3. **Reverse the next row.** Begin the next strip under, over, under so neighbouring rows offset. Continue a few rows, easing them close with fingers or a straightedge without creasing. Your own colour order can form an original design, while **real interlacing and liftability** decide completion, not resemblance to a reference image.
4. **Secure ends by material directions.** Fold ends back or use a small amount of paper-compatible glue or tape on the reverse. For glue, follow its drying directions instead of soaking the sheet. Lift a corner gently; reweave or refasten a loose strip locally rather than covering the face with extra glue.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

The backing border remains connected, adjacent rows start on alternating faces, strips interlace at several crossings, and the dry secured piece can be lifted gently without falling apart. Laying strips across one another without over-under movement is not a weave.

- **A slit cuts through the outer edge:** Restart a backing with an intact border instead of gluing disconnected warp strips into an apparent sheet.
- **Every row starts on the same side:** Check alternate over-under starts; pull and reweave a row before sealing the error with glue.
- **Strips are too soft, narrow, or fragile:** Use sturdier owned paper or wider pre-cut strips. A small coarse grid is a valid first result.
- **Glue wrinkles the paper:** Use less under its directions or paper-compatible tape, and let it dry flat without heat.
- **Hand-eye coordination is difficult:** Use contrast, wide strips, accessible light, pre-cut pieces, or in-person help while checking the crossing structure yourself.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is a low-risk paper exercise, not blade instruction, reproduction of a specific culture's design, or load-bearing textile work. Use ordinary scissors in suitable space and actual supervision for children. Paper weight, colour, and size change the process; a museum activity's inches are not a required universal spec. Edges may look uneven while the weave remains checkable and stable.
