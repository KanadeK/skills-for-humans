## 中文

你已经接下一项普通工作、学习或志愿协作任务，实际推进时被缺少输入、权限、互相冲突的要求或可观察错误卡住，**仍由你负责**，需要他人提供信息或决定时，用这份 Skill。目标是发出对方能处理的求助，并保持真实进度和下一次核对点。它不把卡住隐瞒到截止日，也不把求助自动变成 H247 式责任交接。

### 准备与输入

对照原约定的产物、边界和截止，写下最后一次实际完成并核实的状态。把“看到的现象”与“我猜的原因”分开，保留必要的错误信息或材料位置；不公开密码、私人文件或无权分享的内容。判断这是否真的挡住交付，还是可先完成不依赖它的部分。行业安全、医疗、财务、法律或危险操作问题交专业负责人，别靠普通协作技巧越权解决。

### 执行

1. **把卡点缩成一句可检查事实。** 例如“我已完成前两栏，第三栏所需的最新名册还未由指定负责人提供”，而不是“这个任务好难”。说清卡的是哪项产物、从何时开始、你最后一次看见的状态；无法核实原因时用“可能”，不把猜测写成根因。
2. **列有限的已试动作。** 写你已按授权尝试的两三项、各自结果和为何仍不能继续；如果没有安全可试的动作，就直说。不要为了显得努力无限重复同一失败，也不要未经授权改权限、删材料或临时换真源。给能复现问题的人一个获准查看的路径或简短证据。
3. **说明真实影响和仍可做的部分。** 标出原截止是否受影响、现在能独立完成什么、若不处理会停在哪一步。估计不是承诺；写“若名册周三前到，可继续检查”比“保证周四全部交”诚实。让负责人能看出紧迫程度，不用夸大成危机。
4. **向有权处理的人问一个具体决定。** 是要他提供最新材料、确认哪一个版本为准、批准范围变更，还是指定有权限的人？给少数真实可行的选项和你需要回复的时点。普通同伴也许能解释，但不能替组织授权或让你接下专业风险。
5. **记录答复，再验证是否真正解开。** 请求已发送、对方答应处理、材料实际到手、可继续完成分别标状态。到约定时点仍没回复，按正常负责人/协调渠道跟进一次并更新进度；对方说“已经修好”后，亲自核对关键输入或步骤是否可用，再把阻塞改为已解除。若决定改变原任务，读回新范围和截止。

### 成功条件

相关负责人拿到可检查的卡点、已试动作、对产物/截止的影响和一个可回答的请求；你的任务状态是“仍受阻/有答复待核/已核实可继续”中的真实一种，下一核对点明确。发出求助不等于问题已解决、产物已交付或责任已转走。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只说“卡住了”，没人知道该回什么：** 补具体位置、最后成功状态和需要的决定/材料。
- **猜测原因被写成事实：** 把观察和推断拆开，提供对方能核对的证据。
- **为了赶进度自行绕过权限：** 停止越权动作，记录依赖并问有权者走正式流程。
- **明知影响截止却仍汇报“正常”：** 更正受影响范围与最早的可核对下一点，不给假承诺。
- **对方说“应该好了”就标已解除：** 用真实材料或流程复查；复查失败时保留受阻状态。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

适用于低风险普通协作，任务仍由你负责，不裁定公司人事、法律或专业行业问题。Atlassian 的状态报告指南强调及早暴露阻塞与下一行动，英国项目指南区分记录问题、估计影响和由合适层级作决定；这里只借鉴信息结构，不移植其大型项目审批机制。不能用同一协作工具时，可用双方可访问的短文字或当面读回，但关键状态仍留可追踪记录。诚实报告卡点可能让进度看起来变慢，却避免后来出现更难解释的假完成。

## English

Use this Skill when you have accepted an ordinary work, study, or volunteer task and progress is blocked by missing input, permission, conflicting requirements, or an observed error. **You still own the task**, but need someone's information or decision. Send a request they can act on while keeping progress and the next check honest. Do not hide the blocker until the deadline or treat asking for help as a transfer of responsibility under H247.

### Preparation and inputs

Read the agreed output, scope, and deadline and note the last state you actually completed and verified. Separate what you saw from what you think caused it. Keep relevant error details or material location without exposing passwords, private files, or content you may not share. Decide whether this blocks delivery or whether independent work can continue. Industry safety, medical, financial, legal, or hazardous operations go to their qualified owner, not a routine collaboration workaround.

### Execution

1. **Reduce the blocker to one checkable fact.** “I completed the first two columns, but the designated owner has not provided the current list needed for column three” is more useful than “This task is hard.” Name the output affected, since when, and the last observed state. Label an unverified cause as a possibility, not a root cause.
2. **List bounded attempts.** Give two or three authorized things you tried, their results, and why they did not unblock it. If nothing can be tried safely, say so. Do not repeat the same failure indefinitely or change permission, delete materials, or swap the source of truth without authority. Give a permitted path or short evidence to someone who can verify it.
3. **State the real impact and what can still move.** Say which part of the original deadline is at risk, what independent work remains possible, and where progress stops without an answer. An estimate is not a promise. “If the list arrives by Wednesday, I can continue checking” is more honest than “I guarantee full delivery Thursday.” Show urgency without inventing a crisis.
4. **Ask one specific decision of the right person.** Do you need the latest material, a choice of authoritative version, permission for a scope change, or someone with the required access? Offer only a few viable options and a genuine response point. A peer may explain, but cannot grant organizational authority or make you own specialized risk.
5. **Record the answer, then verify the unblock.** Sent request, promise to act, material actually received, and resumed work are different states. If no reply arrives by the agreed point, follow up once through the normal owner or coordination route and update status. After “fixed,” check the actual input or step before marking the blocker removed. Read back changed scope or deadline.

### Success

The relevant owner has a checkable blocker, attempts, impact on output or deadline, and an answerable request. Your state is honestly “blocked / response received, verification pending / verified able to continue,” with a next check point. Asking for help is not solving the problem, delivering the output, or transferring ownership.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Only “I'm stuck” was sent:** Add the exact point, last success, and the decision or input needed.
- **A guessed cause is recorded as fact:** Separate observation from inference and give checkable evidence.
- **You bypass access controls to keep moving:** Stop the unauthorized route and use the authorized owner's process.
- **A deadline is at risk but your report says “on track”:** Correct the affected scope and next realistic check rather than making a false promise.
- **“Should be fixed” becomes “unblocked”:** Test the real material or step. If it fails, keep blocked state.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers low-risk ordinary collaboration while you retain the task, not personnel, legal, or specialized professional decisions. Atlassian's status-report guide calls for visible blockers and next steps; UK project guidance separates recording issues, assessing impact, and decisions by the proper authority. These are information patterns, not imported large-project approval rules. If a shared tool is inaccessible, use short accessible text or a spoken readback, keeping the critical state traceable. Honest blockers may make progress look slower, but prevent harder-to-explain false completion later.
