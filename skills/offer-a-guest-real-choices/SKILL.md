## 中文

当普通来访已经开始，你要提供饮水、点心、座位、活动或某件可用物品，而不想让来客为了礼貌被迫接受时，加载这份 Skill。目标是给出**真实存在、对方可以拒绝**的选择，然后只执行被接受且你有权提供的部分。来访前确认需求属于另一任务；本篇不判断过敏安全、不做营养建议、不替同住者许可他们的物品或空间。

### 准备与输入

先确认你手边实际有哪几种可提供的选项、食品/设备的真实安全与使用条件、是否属于你能决定开放的物品或区域，以及来客已经表达的限制或偏好。没有查清食品成分、保存史或特殊限制时，不把它包装成“肯定没问题”。没有可用选择时可以坦白，招待不是一场必须上菜的考试。

若同住者或场地对某个房间、物品有使用规定，先按实际权限处理。看见空椅子或打开的柜门不等于获得了替别人授权的能力。

### 执行

1. **说出少量真的能提供的选项。** 例如“我有水和茶，你想要哪种？都不用也可以。”若是空间或活动，可说“你可以坐这里，或者我们在桌边聊；你更方便哪种？”不要提出库存里没有的选项，也不假装必须接受其中之一。
2. **把拒绝当成完整答复。** 来客说“不用”“现在不想吃”或选择安静坐着，就确认一次“好”，继续正常交流。不要追问饮食或身体原因，也不通过第二次、第三次换说法要求同意。
3. **有条件的选择先核实条件。** 来客提到食物限制而你无法可靠核对成分、交叉接触或保存情况时，不承诺该食品安全；可以不提供这项、让对方选择自己确定合适的方案，或只继续非饮食活动。设备或座位对对方不方便时，问需要怎样调整，不推断其身体原因。
4. **使用空间和物品时说明边界。** 只开放你有权决定且适合此次使用的区域与物品，讲清需要先问的部分。例如“洗手间在右边，书房先不要进入”。别人拥有的物品先问物主；来客提出需要时，回答真实可提供范围。
5. **按被选中的内容执行并读回。** 只准备对方接受的饮品/活动，按实际标签和安全要求处理；若选择后来变了，重新确认，不把先前的“好”当成整晚许可。多人有不同选择时逐个确认，不要求少数人统一。

### 完成条件

来客已听到可提供的实际选项和可以拒绝的出口；你只做了对方选择且你有权、安全地提供的事。拒绝不需要解释，也不被记成待修复错误。若某个选项无法可靠提供，你明确停止了它，并让来访继续在可行范围内进行。

### 常见报错与补救

- **“就吃一点”说出口了：** 收口为“抱歉，不用也完全可以”，随后不要再推同一项；无需让对方安慰你。
- **菜单写得比冰箱里的东西多：** 把选项缩回已确认、可实际提供的部分，不临时承诺采购或烹饪。
- **食品限制说得不完整：** 不按猜测替对方判断；只问这次需要避免什么安排，不能验证安全就不提供该食品。
- **座位或物品属于同住者：** 先问物主；没有许可就提供你可控制的其他选择。
- **来客先答应后来改变主意：** 接受新选择或停止，不把先前同意当成永久授权。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这是普通、自愿的待客互动；饮食、鞋、座位、寒暄和是否参与活动没有统一文化默认值。听觉、视觉、语言或疲劳可能使长菜单难以回答，用短问题、指向可选位置或对方可用的沟通方式即可。客人不愿选择时，你可以让活动继续，而非制造一个必须选的界面。具体食品安全、设备说明和场地规定仍要按真实条件核对。

副作用是你可能多剩一份点心、少得到一次夸奖。一次能轻松说“不”的来访仍然成立。

## English

Load this Skill during an ordinary visit when you want to offer water, a snack, a seat, an activity, or use of an item without making politeness feel like compulsory acceptance. Offer **real options with an easy no**, then provide only what the guest accepts and you may actually offer. Confirming needs before the visit is a separate task. This Skill does not certify allergy safety, prescribe a diet, or grant permission over another resident's property or space.

### Preparation and inputs

Check which options you actually have, the real safety and use conditions for food or equipment, whether you may offer the item or area, and any limit or preference the guest has already stated. Do not present food with unknown ingredients, storage history, or relevant restrictions as “definitely fine.” If you have no suitable option, say so. Hosting is not an exam requiring a dish.

Follow any actual co-resident or venue rule for a room or item. An empty chair or open cupboard does not let you authorize someone else's property.

### Execution

1. **Offer a few things you can really provide.** “I have water and tea. Would you like either? Neither is fine.” For space or activity: “We can sit here or talk at the table. Which is easier?” Do not offer absent items or pretend one choice is required.
2. **Treat refusal as a complete answer.** If the guest says “no thanks,” “not hungry,” or prefers to sit quietly, say “okay” once and continue the visit. Do not ask for a medical or personal explanation or repeat the same request in new wording until they agree.
3. **Check conditions before a conditional offer.** If a guest mentions a food restriction and you cannot reliably verify ingredients, cross-contact, or storage, do not promise the food is safe. Leave it out, let the guest choose an option they know works, or continue without food. If a seat or device does not work for them, ask what adjustment would help rather than inferring a bodily reason.
4. **State boundaries for spaces and objects.** Offer only areas and items you may control and that work for this use. Say which places need asking first: “The toilet is on the right; please leave the study closed.” Ask the owner before lending someone else's property. Give the real scope when the guest asks for something.
5. **Do the chosen thing and keep checking consent.** Prepare only an accepted drink or activity, following its actual label and safety requirements. Confirm if the choice changes; an earlier “yes” is not permission for the whole evening. Ask several guests individually when their choices differ.

### Success

The guest heard real available options and a genuine way to decline. You did only what they chose and what you could provide with authority and suitable safety. A no needs no explanation and is not an error to repair. If one offer cannot be provided reliably, you stopped that offer and let the visit continue within feasible bounds.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You say “just have a little”:** Correct it with “Sorry, no need at all,” and do not push the same item again. The guest need not reassure you.
- **Your menu exceeds what is in the cupboard:** Return to verified available options. Do not promise a surprise shop or cooking job.
- **A food restriction is incomplete:** Do not guess for the guest. Ask only what arrangement must be avoided this time; when you cannot verify safety, do not serve that food.
- **The seat or item belongs to a co-resident:** Ask its owner first; without permission, offer something you control.
- **The guest changes their mind:** Accept the new choice or stop. Earlier consent is not permanent authorization.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This is ordinary voluntary hosting. Food, shoes, seating, small talk, and activities have no universal cultural default. Hearing, vision, language, or fatigue may make a long menu difficult; use a brief question, point to real options, or communicate in a way the guest can use. If they do not want to choose, the visit can continue without a forced menu. Check objective food, equipment, and venue conditions from actual labels, instructions, and applicable rules.

You may have an extra snack and one fewer compliment. A visit where someone can comfortably say no still works.
