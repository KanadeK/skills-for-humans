## 中文

当一次普通短时来访已开始约定，但饮食、进入路线、声音/光线、活动方式或时间边界会影响这次安排，而你还不知道对方的实际需要时，加载这份 Skill。目标是**提前取得可执行的条件或明确保留未知**，不是询问诊断、收入、宗教或要求对方解释私人原因。把家准备好属于下一步；本篇不提供过敏治疗、营养建议或保证某道食物绝对安全。

### 准备与输入

先写出已知的来访时间、地点、人数、目的、你打算提供的座位/饮水/餐食等，以及你真正能改变的条件。只问会改变本次安排的问题。若没有餐食，就无需发一份饮食史问卷；若入口有台阶或家有宠物，应该在决定场地之前说清这些事实。

选对方方便回复的短信、电话、语音或当面方式。多人来访时，每个人的需要由本人或其认可的联系人表达；一位来客不能自动替所有人确认。说清对方可只给出所需安排，不必说明为什么。

### 执行

1. **先给已知事实，再问少量可回答的问题。** 例如：“周六 15:00–17:00 到我家坐一会儿。我家入口有两级台阶，也有猫；目前能提供水和茶，不准备正餐。进入、声音/宠物、饮食或时间方面，有什么需要我提前调整的吗？只说安排即可，不用解释原因。”根据实际情况删掉无关项。
2. **把答复译成动作，不贴身份标签。** “需要无台阶路线”是场地条件，“不能接触某食物”是提供食品的限制，“希望 16:30 离开”是时间边界。不要猜测背后的身体情况或文化习俗，也不要把普通偏好写成医疗结论。
3. **核对自己能否做到。** 对每项明确回答“可以按这样安排 / 我做不到但可以改用…… / 我还需确认”。饮食限制若不能可靠满足，就不要承诺自制餐食安全；可以商量不提供食物、由对方选择或改地点。不能用一句“我会小心”替代具体能力。
4. **回传一份短确认。** 写出这次已确认的时间、地点、可提供条件和对方提出的安排；把未答或未落实的部分列为待确认。对方不回复时，不把沉默记成“没有需要”；只在你能保证的已知条件内准备，关键条件缺失就及时改约或换地点。
5. **把结果交给实际准备。** 只把已确认的条件用于入口、座位、环境和物品安排。若人数、时间或需求后来改变，更新对应部分并再次告知，不让旧消息成为永远有效的承诺。

### 完成条件

你已向对方发出足以回答的简短问题，并清楚记录“已确认 / 我能提供 / 待确认或不能提供”。如果对方回复，你已回传实际可行的安排；如果未回复，未知被保留并有下一次确认或改约动作。完成不要求对方必须讲私人原因或一定接受邀请。

### 常见报错与补救

- **问题像一份完整问卷：** 只保留会改变这次访问的条件，并允许对方说“没别的需要”。
- **从外表、语言或身份猜需求：** 改问可执行安排；不要替对方定义身体、文化或喜好。
- **对方没回复：** 用约定渠道再给一次简短确认机会；关键进入或食品条件仍未知时，不做无依据承诺。
- **对方提的条件你做不到：** 尽早说清，提出你确实能提供的时间、地点或活动替代，让对方决定是否可行。
- **多人需求互相冲突：** 逐项确认哪些条件可以同时满足；不能满足就改活动或分开安排，不要求其中一人默默承受。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里假设一次自愿、普通、可调整的到访；饮食、鞋、宠物、社交距离、安静程度和结束时间不因地区或身份自动确定。对方可能更愿意用电话或一句话而非文字表格，保持提问渠道可及。只记录完成安排所需的信息，不在公共群传播敏感细节。具体食品安全和场地无障碍能力必须由真实条件及适用资料确认。

副作用是你可能需要换一个地点，或少准备一道不必要的菜。这比到门口才发现不可进入更可控。

## English

Load this Skill when an ordinary short visit is being agreed but food, entrance access, sound/light, activity, or timing could change the plan and you do not know this guest's actual needs. Your result is **practical conditions confirmed in advance, or clearly unresolved**. You are not asking for diagnoses, income, religion, or a private explanation. Preparing the home comes next. This Skill gives no allergy treatment, nutrition advice, or guarantee that a dish is absolutely safe.

### Preparation and inputs

Note the time, place, headcount, purpose, seating, water or food you plan to offer, and what you can genuinely change. Ask only questions that affect this visit. Without a meal, you do not need a food-history questionnaire. If the entrance has steps or you have a pet, disclose those real conditions before settling the venue.

Use a text, call, voice message, or in-person channel the guest can answer. With several visitors, each person's need comes from them or a contact they accept; one guest cannot automatically confirm for everyone. Say they may name the arrangement they need without explaining why.

### Execution

1. **Give known facts, then a few answerable questions.** For example: “Come by Saturday 3–5 pm. My entrance has two steps and I have a cat. I can offer water and tea, but I am not planning a meal. Is there anything about access, sound/pets, food, or timing I should change for you? You can just name the arrangement; no reason needed.” Remove categories irrelevant to this visit.
2. **Turn a reply into an action, not a label.** “I need a step-free route” is a venue condition; “I cannot have this food” limits what you offer; “I need to leave at 4:30” sets the time. Do not guess a medical condition or cultural practice behind it, or turn an ordinary preference into a diagnosis.
3. **Check what you can actually meet.** For each point say “I can arrange that / I cannot, but here is an option / I need to check.” If you cannot reliably meet a food restriction, do not promise that home-cooked food is safe; agree on no food, food the guest chooses, or another location. “I'll be careful” is not a concrete capability.
4. **Send one short confirmation back.** State the confirmed time, place, what you can provide, and the requested arrangements. Mark anything unanswered or not yet secured as pending. No reply does not mean no needs; prepare only known conditions you can guarantee, and change time or venue promptly if a critical condition remains unknown.
5. **Hand the result to actual preparation.** Use confirmed conditions for entrance, seat, environment, and supplies. If headcount, time, or needs change later, update the affected part and tell the guest; an old message is not a permanent promise.

### Success

You sent a short question the guest can answer and have a clear list of “confirmed / I can provide / still unknown or unavailable.” If they replied, you returned a feasible plan. If they did not, unknowns remain visible with a next check or change-of-plan action. Completion does not require a private explanation or acceptance of your invitation.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The request reads like a full survey:** Keep only conditions that change this visit, and leave room for “nothing else needed.”
- **You infer needs from appearance, language, or identity:** Ask for the practical arrangement instead of defining someone else's body, culture, or taste.
- **The guest has not replied:** Offer one concise follow-up on the agreed channel. Do not promise an entry or food condition you cannot confirm.
- **You cannot meet a requested condition:** Say so early and offer a time, place, or activity you can actually support, leaving the decision to the guest.
- **Several guests' needs conflict:** Check what can coexist. If it cannot, change the activity or arrange separate visits rather than making one person silently absorb the conflict.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This assumes a voluntary, ordinary visit that can still be adjusted. Food, shoes, pets, personal space, noise, and end time are not set automatically by region or identity. A call or one sentence may be more accessible than a form. Record only what is needed to arrange the visit; do not broadcast sensitive details in a group chat. Verify objective food safety and venue access from actual conditions and applicable sources.

You may need a different venue or one fewer unnecessary dish. Both are easier to manage than discovering at the door that entry does not work.
