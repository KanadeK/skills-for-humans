## 中文

当你在买两件**同用途、固体装在液体中的普通包装食品**，而你的这次用法会倒掉包装液时，加载这份 Skill。例如两罐都要沥干后加入同一道普通菜。目标是比较**每相同重量沥干固体的当前价格**，而不是把水、盐水或糖液也算成你要买的固体。食品是否适用、质量、日期和安全状态先按各自条件核对；本 Skill 不给医疗饮食或食品安全结论。

### 准备与输入

确认两件食品能用于同一用途，并且你的实际做法都不使用包装液。逐件读取确切商品、现价、总净重和明确标出的“沥干重 / drained weight”及其单位；不要从罐头体积或固体看起来占几成猜数字。若只用一件商品的汤汁、另一件倒掉，它们不在同一比较口径。两件均无可靠沥干重时，这份购前比较无法完成。

### 执行步骤

1. **先确定你买的那部分。** 对每个候选写“固体要用，包装液倒掉”。如果菜谱或用途会用到液体，先停止此流程，改按真正使用的内容重新定义比较；沥干重不是万能的“更真实”价格。
2. **找到两种重量。** 标签的净重通常描述包装内食物总量；沥干重描述排去包装液后的固体重量。两者不能混填。只用商品包装或对应型号的可靠资料给出的沥干重，不借别的品牌或旧包装数据。
3. **统一沥干重单位。** 把两件都写成克或千克，再用“当前整包售价 ÷ 沥干固体重量 × 共同重量”。例：A 净重 400 克、沥干重 240 克、现价 3.60，则每 100 克沥干固体为 1.50；B 净重 300 克、沥干重 250 克、现价 3.50，则为 1.40。按这次用途，B 虽总净重较小，却每 100 克固体少付 0.10。
4. **核对货架单位价的分母。** 货架若已给每 100 克或每千克，先确认它按**沥干重还是总净重**，不要把不同分母的数字直接排队。不清楚时用可核实的包装数字自行计算，并把货架价标为“口径未确认”。
5. **保留整包现实。** 记下每件沥干固体总量、整包应付额和单位价。B 的沥干固体较多或单价较低，不自动说明你用得完、放得下或必须买它；额外购买量另行判断。

### 完成与停止

你能说出两件食品在**同样倒掉包装液**的用法下，各自每 100 克或每千克沥干固体多少钱，并保留整包价，就完成了。任一件缺可靠沥干重、用途会用液体、商品规格不等价或售价不能确认时，输出“暂不可比”，不要把总净重冒充沥干重。

### 常见报错与补救

- **较重的罐头看起来更划算：** 重读沥干重；总净重可能包含你会倒掉的液体。
- **只找到一侧的沥干重：** 不用另一侧总净重替代；找同型号可核实说明或换成资料齐全的候选。
- **货架单价与手算不符：** 先核对货架按总净重还是沥干重、是否对应同规格及当前价格；仍冲突时停用该单位价并请店员核对。
- **决定把液体也用于料理：** 此次结果作废，重新定义使用量；别在付款前后随意切换分母来证明原决定正确。
- **沥干固体仍有不可用的部分：** 沥干重不自动等于可食用或符合用途的重量；停止这个数值结论，先解决商品规格与实际使用问题。

### 假设、替代与副作用

这里仅处理购物前可读的标签重量，不要求打开包装、亲自沥干或尝食。标签制度随地区与食品种类而变；英国资料说明沥干重这种字段的含义，不保证你所在地每件商品都有。看不清时放大照片、用读屏或请可信的人读出完整“净重/沥干重”，用纸笔或计算器换算；缺数字就停。现实副作用是你可能发现一罐较重的是液体，而你的饭并不需要那部分重量。

## English

Load this Skill when you are choosing between two **suitable ordinary solid foods packed in liquid** and your intended use discards the packing liquid from both. Two cans that will both be drained for the same ordinary dish are one example. Compare the **current price per equal weight of drained solids**, rather than treating water, brine, or syrup as solid food you intended to buy. Check suitability, quality, dates, and food condition separately. This Skill does not give medical-diet or food-safety conclusions.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm that both foods serve the same purpose and that you will discard the packing liquid from each. For the exact items, read the current price, total net weight, and clearly labelled drained weight with units. Do not infer drained weight from can size or the visible fraction occupied by solids. If you use the liquid from one product but discard it from the other, these are different comparison bases. If you cannot obtain reliable drained weights for both, this pre-purchase comparison cannot finish.

### Execution steps

1. **Define the part you will use.** Write “use the solids, discard the packing liquid” for each candidate. If your recipe or task uses the liquid, stop this procedure and define the real quantity you use instead. Drained weight is not a universal “truer” price.
2. **Locate both weights.** Net weight normally describes the contents as packed; drained weight describes solids after the packing liquid is removed. Do not interchange them. Use only the exact pack's label or reliable model-specific information, not another brand or last year's pack.
3. **Put drained weights on one scale.** Express both in grams or kilograms. Use “current full-pack price ÷ drained solid weight × common weight.” For example, A has 400 g net, 240 g drained, and costs 3.60: that is 1.50 per 100 g drained. B has 300 g net, 250 g drained, and costs 3.50: that is 1.40 per 100 g drained. For this use, B has a smaller total net weight yet costs 0.10 less per 100 g of solids.
4. **Check the shelf unit-price denominator.** If a shelf label offers a price per 100 g or kilogram, establish whether it uses **drained or total net weight**. Do not rank numbers with different denominators. If the basis is unclear, calculate from the confirmed pack details and mark the shelf figure “basis unconfirmed.”
5. **Keep the whole-pack reality.** Note each pack's drained solid amount, amount payable, and drained unit price. A lower unit price or more solids does not prove you can use or store the extra, or that you must buy it. Decide quantity separately.

### Success and stop

You can state each food's cost per 100 g or kilogram of drained solids for the **same discard-the-liquid use**, while keeping the whole-pack price visible. If either drained weight, intended use, product equivalence, or current price cannot be confirmed, output “not comparable yet.” Do not pass total net weight off as drained weight.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The heavier can appears cheaper:** Read its drained weight; total net weight may include liquid you would discard.
- **Only one candidate has a drained weight:** Do not fill the other denominator with total net weight. Find reliable information for that exact pack or choose a candidate with complete figures.
- **Shelf and calculation disagree:** Check whether the shelf uses total or drained weight, the matching pack size, and the current price. If unresolved, stop using the displayed unit figure and ask staff to confirm it.
- **You decide to use the liquid after all:** This result no longer applies. Redefine the quantity you use; do not switch denominators mid-purchase to defend an earlier choice.
- **Some drained solids are still unusable:** Drained weight is not proof of edible or purpose-suitable quantity. Stop this numerical conclusion and settle the product-specification and use question first.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This only uses pre-purchase label quantities; you do not need to open, drain, or taste a pack. Labelling rules vary by place and food type. UK guidance explains the drained-weight field but does not promise it appears on every local product. Enlarge a photo, use screen reading, or ask a trusted person to read the full net and drained values; use paper or a calculator for the arithmetic. Stop when a decisive number is unavailable. A heavier can may owe some of its weight to liquid your meal does not need.
