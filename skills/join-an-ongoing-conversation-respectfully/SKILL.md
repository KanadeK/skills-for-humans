## 中文

你来到普通聚会、小组或休息区，别人已经在谈话，你想参与但不确定这段话是否对你开放时，用这份 Skill。结果是**在合适时机作一次可被接受或拒绝的加入尝试**，或者判断此刻不加入并转向别的活动。它不教你从表情猜心思，也不保证对方欢迎你；私人谈话和敏感内容应先尊重边界。

### 准备与输入

看看场合是公开讨论、闲聊，还是两个人正在低声处理自己的事；关注他们实际说了什么、是否请其他人参与、轮流发言是否开放。不要只用眼神、身体姿势或听力推断意图：有人看不见、听不清、正专注，或文化习惯不同。若不知道谈话是不是公开的，可以在有空档时简短问，而不是站在旁边偷听到足够私人细节再插话。

### 执行

1. **先判断这是你的活动范围吗。** 小组公开话题、主持人邀请众人发言或闲聊正向周围开放，可以准备加入。明显的私事、争执、保密工作或一对一交接则不要因为你恰好路过就坐进来；另找允许的活动或等他们结束。
2. **等一个不会截断别人意思的空档。** 对方说完一句、主持人开放发言或几人自然换话题时，再靠近到正常交谈距离。若空间拥挤、需要轮候或会议有正式发言规则，按现场规则走；不越过别人、抢走麦克风或堵住通道。
3. **用短句说明想加入。** 可说“你们在聊这个活动吗？我可以听一会儿吗？”或者在公开讨论中接一句与当前话题直接相关的话。若你有重要信息但当前不便插入，说“方便时我有一条关于〔话题〕的信息”即可，不必先讲完整故事。等待他们实际给出空间，而不是把沉默当邀请。
4. **进入后先接住原话题。** 听一轮，确认自己理解正在谈什么；发言简短，别把他人话题改成自己的介绍、连续问题或长建议。需要澄清一个具体信息时可另用提问流程；你不必靠一次发言证明自己值得留下。
5. **没有空间就体面退出。** 若对方说这是私下谈话、没有回应、时间不合适或你听不清上下文，说“没关系，打扰了”并离开或等别的机会。被允许旁听不等于马上轮到你发言；后来被明确邀请，再按新状态参与。

### 成功条件

你判断了谈话是否适合加入，选择了不打断的时机，用可拒绝的方式提出或作出一次与原话题有关的参与；若没有加入许可，你没有继续占用谈话空间。成功是尊重边界并完成自己的动作，不是让群体改变话题、同意你的观点或保证下次邀请你。

### 常见报错与补救

- **听到几个词就以为知道全部背景：** 先听一轮或在空档问话题，不对未听完的故事下结论。
- **两人低声说私事，你站着等机会插话：** 走开或等待另一段公开活动，不把私事当互动入口。
- **一句“我也觉得”后马上讲很长的自己：** 回到原话题，给其他人回应空间；自我介绍可在被邀请时简短做。
- **无人回答“我能加入吗”：** 保留“未获邀请”状态，别通过不断靠近或反复提问逼出同意。
- **轮流发言的场合看不出空档：** 用举手、主持人点名、文字聊天等该场合实际允许的方式参与。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

本 Skill 适用于普通自愿谈话，不处理保密会议、冲突危机或需要强行打断的安全事件。加入信号随文化、听力、视觉、神经类型和场合变化；不能把目光接触、笑容或停顿单独当同意。难以听清可在获准参与后请求一句话摘要，或选该场合允许的文字方式；不能靠近可用可及座位或请主持人说明发言方式。不加入是正常结果，不是待修复的人格错误。

## English

Use this Skill when you arrive at an ordinary gathering, group, or break area where people are already talking and are unsure whether you may join. Finish with **one respectful, optional attempt at a natural opening**, or decide not to join and move to another activity. This does not read minds from faces or guarantee a welcome. Private and sensitive exchanges keep their own boundaries.

### Preparation and inputs

Notice whether the setting is an open discussion, casual talk, or two people quietly handling their own matter. Listen for what is actually said, an invitation to others, and whether turn-taking is open. Do not infer everything from eye contact, body position, or hearing: people may have different sensory access, be focused, or follow different customs. If openness is unclear, ask briefly at a pause rather than standing nearby to gather private detail.

### Execution

1. **Decide whether the exchange is within your activity.** A public group topic, a facilitator's invitation, or casual talk opening to others may be joinable. Obvious private matters, an argument, confidential work, or a one-to-one handover are not yours just because you walked past. Choose another permitted activity or wait until they finish.
2. **Wait for a gap that does not cut off a point.** Approach at a sentence end, an open floor, or a natural topic change and keep a normal conversational distance. In a crowded space or a meeting with formal turns, follow the actual rule; do not step over people, seize a microphone, or block a path.
3. **State the wish to join briefly.** “Are you discussing this activity? May I listen for a moment?” works, or make one directly relevant comment in an open discussion. If you have useful information but this is not the moment, “When there is a gap, I have one detail about [topic]” is enough. Wait for actual room to join; silence is not an invitation.
4. **Carry the existing topic first.** Listen for one round and check your understanding. Keep the first contribution short rather than replacing the topic with your biography, a stream of questions, or a long solution. Use a separate question flow for a real information gap. One turn need not prove your worthiness to remain.
5. **Step away when there is no opening.** If someone says it is private, does not respond, the timing is wrong, or the context is unclear, say “No problem—sorry to interrupt” and leave or wait for another chance. Permission to listen does not grant an immediate speaking turn. Join later if you are actually invited.

### Success

You assessed whether the talk was open, chose a non-interrupting moment, and made one declineable request or contribution related to its topic. Without room to join, you did not keep occupying the exchange. Success is your respectful action, not making the group change topic, agree with you, or invite you next time.

### Common errors and recovery

- **A few words sound like the whole story:** Listen for a round or ask at a gap what the topic is; do not judge a conversation you have not heard.
- **Two people quietly discuss private matters and you wait to insert yourself:** Step away or use another open activity; their private issue is not a conversational opening.
- **“I agree” becomes a long story about yourself:** Return to the original topic and leave room for others. Introduce yourself briefly if invited.
- **No one answers “May I join?”:** Keep it “not invited” rather than moving closer or asking repeatedly to force a yes.
- **A structured discussion has no obvious gap:** Use a raised hand, facilitator's call, text chat, or whatever turn method that setting actually allows.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is for ordinary voluntary talk, not confidential meetings, conflict crises, or safety events needing urgent interruption. Joining cues vary by culture, hearing, sight, neurotype, and setting. Eye contact, a smile, or a pause alone is not consent. If hearing is hard, ask for a one-sentence recap after being welcomed, or use a permitted text option. If approaching is difficult, use accessible seating or ask the facilitator about taking a turn. Not joining is a normal outcome, not a personality defect needing repair.
