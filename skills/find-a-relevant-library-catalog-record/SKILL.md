## 中文

当你知道要找一本书、某个主题的入门资料或一种格式，却被图书馆目录里同名、不同版本和不同馆点弄晕时，用这份 Skill 找出**一条与你需求相符的馆藏记录及它的当前状态**。结果是可复查的书目线索；目录写“馆藏”不等于你已拿到实物，也不等于可以在线阅读全文。

### 准备与输入

先写你真正需要的要素：题名/作者（若已知）、主题、语言、载体（纸书/音频/电子等）以及你能去的馆点或可接受的访问方式。打开该馆的官方网站或馆内目录终端；各馆界面、分类与登录规则不同，别用第三方网页的“有货”替代馆方状态。能匿名查就先查；若某信息必须登录，依馆方正式流程办理或请馆员协助，不把账号和密码写进搜索笔记。本篇不替代借阅、预约或版权权限判断。

### 执行

1. **用最可靠的词起搜。** 已知确切书名/作者，就先用它；只有主题时，用两三个能说明需求的关键词，不把一句很长的自然语言原封不动塞进去。结果太多，按格式、语言或馆点缩窄；结果为零，去掉过窄条件或换同义主题词再试。
2. **打开候选的完整记录。** 对照题名、作者、出版/版次、格式和简介或主题描述，问“这条真的回答我的需求吗？”同名电影、另一本书或不同语种版本不能只凭封面认定相同。若你要精确版次，用 ISBN/年份等馆方可见字段核对。
3. **单独看获取信息。** 记录馆点、馆藏位置、索书号（如有）、可用/借出/仅馆内使用/需请求等实际状态，以及查看时间。各馆字段不同；若页面只有“馆藏”但没展示在架状态，就写“状态未确认”，不要默认为立即可拿。
4. **留下最短可复查线索。** 用本地便签写“馆名—题名/作者—格式/版次—馆点—索书号或记录链接—状态/时间”，不抄别人的借阅账号。若没找到合用记录，写“在这个馆目录用过哪些词与过滤条件，未找到合用结果”；这只证明本次检索没有命中，不证明世界上没有这份资料。
5. **选下一条实际路径。** 记录显示开放书架可用时，下一步按馆点和索书号找实物；显示借出、异馆、闭架或电子权限时，查馆方服务说明或问馆员。这里停在确定路径，不擅自提交预约或声称资料已可用。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你有一条符合需求且能回到馆方页面的记录，并知道它的格式、目标馆点/访问方式与状态；或者留下有界检索未命中的真实记录和向馆员描述问题的材料。“搜索出现一个封面”还没有完成匹配和状态核对。

- **同名太多：** 加作者、出版年、ISBN 或格式逐项排除，别只认第一张封面。
- **主题词搜不到：** 换一个常见同义词、上位词或更少过滤条件；仍无结果再问馆员，不硬说馆里没有。
- **页面说“可用”却在别的馆点：** 记真实馆点，不按你所在分馆想象它就在眼前。
- **目录不可用或视觉/语言界面难操作：** 用馆内终端、放大/读屏或直接向馆员描述需求；不编造一条你没核实的记录。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

馆藏状态会变化，截图和笔记不是保留承诺；出发或到馆前再查。不同地区的公共图书馆可能使用不同分类号、开放书架、闭架和电子授权，流程以本馆为准。可用馆员、电话或适合的辅助技术替代自己操作屏幕，仍要核实记录是否回答原需求。检索本身不产生借阅权、账号或复制许可。

## English

Use this Skill when you know a title, need introductory material on a topic, or need a certain format but the library catalog shows namesakes, editions, and branches. Find **one record that truly fits your need and its current access status**. The result is a checkable catalog lead. “Held by the library” does not mean the physical item is in your hand or the whole text is online.

### Preparation and inputs

Write the real constraints: title or author if known, subject, language, format (print, audio, digital, and so on), and a branch you can reach or access method you accept. Use that public library's official site or in-library terminal. Catalog interfaces, classification, and sign-in rules vary; do not substitute a third-party “in stock” label for the library's status. Search anonymously if possible. If a detail requires sign-in, follow the library's official route or ask staff rather than put credentials in a search note. Borrowing, holds, and copyright permission are separate tasks.

### Execution

1. **Start with reliable terms.** If you know exact title and author, use them first. For a topic, choose two or three terms that state your need instead of pasting a long sentence. Narrow abundant results by format, language, or branch; for zero results, remove an overstrict filter or try a related term.
2. **Open the full candidate record.** Compare title, author, publication or edition, format, and description or subjects. Does it answer your need? A namesake film, another book, or another language is not the same item because the cover looks familiar. Use visible ISBN or year when a precise edition matters.
3. **Read access separately.** Note branch, collection location, call number if shown, actual available/checked-out/in-library-only/request status, and the time checked. Fields vary. If the page says only “held” with no shelf status, write “status unconfirmed,” not “ready now.”
4. **Leave a checkable lead.** In a local note keep “library—title/author—format/edition—branch—call number or record link—status/time.” Do not copy another person's library account. If nothing fits, note which terms and filters you tried at this library. That is a bounded failed search, not proof the material does not exist anywhere.
5. **Choose the next real route.** For an available open-shelf copy, use branch and call number to find it. For checked-out, other-branch, closed-stack, or digital-access results, check that library's service instructions or ask a librarian. Stop here before placing a hold or claiming the material is already available to you.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You have a relevant record you can reopen on the library site, with format, branch or access method, and status; or an honest bounded no-match record useful to a librarian. A cover in search results alone is not a match and access check.

- **Many items share a title:** Add author, year, ISBN, or format one at a time instead of taking the first cover.
- **Topic words find nothing:** Try a common synonym, broader term, or fewer filters. Ask staff if still empty; do not declare the library has nothing.
- **“Available” is at another branch:** Note the actual branch, not an imagined shelf at your current one.
- **Catalog is down or its language/visual interface is difficult:** Use a terminal, magnification or screen reader, or describe the need to staff. Do not invent a record you did not check.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Availability changes. A screenshot or note is no reservation; check again before travelling or on arrival. Public libraries in different regions use different call numbers, open or closed shelves, and digital rights. Follow the actual library. Staff, phone help, or suitable assistive technology can replace solo screen use while you still verify fit. Searching grants no loan, account, or copying permission.
