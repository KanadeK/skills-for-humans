## 中文

当你**正在听一场公开讲座**，对讲者刚说的一点有真实疑问，而主办方开放现场或线上问答时，用这份 Skill 把疑问压成一个具体、可回答的问题，按主持人的渠道提交。完成不取决于讲者必须回答或同意你；你的真实动作是守规则地提出并记录结果。只是听讲做笔记是另一任务。

### 准备与输入

从你刚听到的观点、例子或术语中选一个不清楚的点，先确认讲者后来没有解答。听主办方是否允许随时提问、统一最后 Q&A、举手、排麦、纸条还是平台 Q&A 栏；聊天框不一定是提问栏。留意时间和是否限制问题数量。不要在公开场合透露自己的凭据、病史、财务细节或他人的私人资料；这些不是让讲者现场处理的普通问题。若需要字幕、翻译或另一种提交方式，可向主办方询问正式可及通道。

### 执行

1. **写一句上下文和一句问题。** 例如“您刚才用____说明____。这个结论在____情况下还成立吗？”只问一个判断或解释；不是借麦克风讲自己的长故事，也不要求讲者代你作专业诊断/法律决定。
2. **检查问题是否真能回答。** 去掉重复的背景和第二、第三问，确认你能指出它来自讲座的哪一处。若是讲者已在投影片写明的信息，先核对，再问你实际没懂的关系。
3. **等主办方开口。** 到指定时间举手、排队、用纸条或在官方 Q&A 栏提交；先遵守主持人给的顺序、麦克风与发言时限。没有开放环节就不打断演讲；可保留问题用于主办方公开提供的后续渠道，但本篇尚未现场提问成功。
4. **被点到时简短说完。** 对着麦克风或按线上方式清楚念出一句上下文和一个问题，必要时请主持人重复你的问题以便全场听见。若你没被点到或线上问题没被选中，记录“已提交、未被选中”，不要说讲者回避了你。
5. **记录真实回应。** 有答案就用自己的话记讲者怎么说，以及仍不确定的点；回答部分不清楚可在规则允许、还有时间时追问一处，不能就留待查证。没有答案也如实留问题，不逼对方给保证。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你在主办方允许的方式下提出一个指向讲座内容的具体问题，并记录“已答/部分答/未选中”的真实状态。回答质量与讲者态度不受你控制；只在纸上写好却没有机会提交，是已准备而非已提问。

- **准备的问题在讲座后段被答了：** 划掉或改成仍未知的一处，不重复占问答时间。
- **问题太长或有三个问号：** 留讲座上下文一句、核心疑问一句，其余留作个人笔记。
- **没有麦克风或线上通道不清：** 按主持人指示找可及方式；不能就保持问题待问，不大声插话。
- **主持人提前结束 Q&A：** 尊重时间，记录未提交；如主办方提供后续正式渠道再用，不假称现场问过。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

公开讲座不一定允许提问，线上问答是否公开可见也因平台与主办方而异。你可以用纸条、文字 Q&A、翻译/辅助服务代替口头发问，但选择实际允许的方式。提问时别人可能听到你的声音或看到你的名字，先考虑你愿意公开多少。不同意见可以用问题表达，仍需尊重主持、讲者和其他观众的时间。

## English

Use this Skill when you are **attending a public talk**, have a real uncertainty about something just said, and the organiser opens in-person or online Q&A. Reduce it to one answerable question and submit through the moderator's route. Completion does not require the speaker to answer or agree. Your controllable action is to ask within the rules and record the actual outcome. Listening and note-taking alone are another task.

### Preparation and inputs

Choose one unclear claim, example, or term you just heard and check whether the speaker answers it later. Listen for whether questions are allowed during the talk, at the end, by hand, microphone queue, paper, or platform Q&A. A chat box is not always the Q&A channel. Note time and any question limit. Do not reveal credentials, private medical or financial details, or someone else's personal information to the room; a speaker should not handle those as ordinary public questions. Ask the organiser about captions, interpretation, or another approved submission route if needed.

### Execution

1. **Write one context sentence and one question.** For example, “You used ____ to explain ____. Would that still apply when ____?” Ask for one explanation or condition. Do not take a microphone for a long personal story or demand a professional diagnosis or legal decision.
2. **Check that it can be answered.** Remove extra background and second or third questions. Be able to point to the passage of the talk that prompted it. If a slide already states the fact, ask about the relation you still do not understand instead.
3. **Wait for the organiser's opening.** Raise a hand, queue, hand over a note, or submit in the official Q&A at the specified time. Follow moderator order, microphone, and time limits. With no open Q&A, do not interrupt. You may save a question for a formal later channel, but you have not completed a live question yet.
4. **Ask briefly if chosen.** State one context and one question clearly through microphone or approved online route. If needed, ask the moderator to repeat it so others can hear. If your raised hand or submitted online question is not selected, record “submitted, not selected,” not “speaker avoided me.”
5. **Record the real response.** If answered, note in your own words what the speaker said and what remains uncertain. If partially answered and rules allow time, ask one brief clarification. Otherwise leave it for later checking. Do not demand a guarantee where none was given.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You submitted one concrete talk-related question by an allowed method and recorded its real state: answered, partly answered, or not selected. You do not control the response or the speaker's view. A question written on paper but never offered is prepared, not asked.

- **The speaker answers it later in the talk:** Drop it or narrow to a remaining uncertainty instead of taking Q&A time to repeat it.
- **The question is long with three question marks:** Keep one context sentence and one core question; retain the rest in private notes.
- **The microphone or online route is unclear:** Ask staff or the moderator for an accessible approved route. If none works, leave it open instead of shouting into the talk.
- **The moderator closes Q&A early:** Respect the limit, mark unsubmitted, and use a later official channel if offered. Do not claim you asked live.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

A public talk may offer no Q&A, and online questions may be visible only to organisers or to everyone. Use an actually approved paper note, Q&A text, interpretation, or access service instead of speech where relevant. Others may hear your voice or see your name; decide how much of yourself to expose before asking. A disagreement can be expressed as a question while respecting moderators, speaker, and other listeners' time.
