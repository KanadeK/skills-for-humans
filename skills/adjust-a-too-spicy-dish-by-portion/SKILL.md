## 中文

当一道**仍可确认安全**的普通家常成品比预期更辣，某些人不愿直接吃原份量时，用这份 Skill 先决定要调整谁的那一份。目标是得到有人愿意吃的安全份量、温和搭配，或诚实停止；不承诺靠糖、酸、奶把辣味成分神奇消除。若有人出现需要医疗处理的反应，这份口味补救立即停止。

### 准备与输入

确认菜是什么、辣味大概来自可见辣椒块还是已混进的调味、食物是否已经按适用规则完成烹调并处在安全处理状态。问清这顿谁愿意吃原辣度、谁想少辣，以及是否有他们确实能吃的不辣主食、熟蔬菜、未调味同类底料或适用配食。准备干净小勺、单独小碗和足够容量的锅或盛装容器；若新增食物会产生剩余，先确认实际可用的冷藏和容器。过敏替代、医疗饮食和不明时间/温度史不在这里试调。

### 执行

1. **先把热源和问题分开。** 若菜还在收汁或持续变浓，安全时先降低或停止加热；不要让相同辣味进入更少体积。仅在食品可安全尝的阶段，搅匀后舀一小勺、降温再判断；未熟食材不能靠试吃定位辣度。
2. **先确定谁的份需要改。** 想吃原版的人可保留原份；为怕辣的人只取一小份到干净碗里试。不要用整锅做无法撤回的第一轮实验，也不要在别人餐盘里悄悄加配料。
3. **能移除的先试物理移除。** 如果独立的辣椒片、辣油层或调味包仍可在不破坏食物安全的条件下取出，先从测试份移除。辣味已经扩散时，这一步不会让它自动变不辣，仍需后面的实际试吃决定。
4. **按结构降低每口强度。** 对汤、炖菜等可混合的测试份，分次加入兼容且本身安全可吃的未调味底料或熟食主体，再搅匀并按实际食材要求处理温度。对不能再混合的成品，改为更小的辣菜份，配不辣且适合当餐的主食或配菜。搭配改变每口的体验，不能保证原菜内的辣味成分消失。
5. **每次只做一个小调整并再判断。** 在安全可尝的阶段用干净勺检查测试份，问目标用餐者能否接受，而不是按你的耐辣程度代答。糖、酸或乳制品只在菜式和人都适合时作少量风味配合；它们不构成“中和成功”证书，也可能带来新的限制或过敏原。
6. **决定原锅和新增份量的去向。** 测试份可接受且锅/冷藏容量足够，才考虑按相同方法调整更多；若需要无限加量才能勉强吃，就停止。仍安全但太辣的原菜可在愿意吃的人同意下作为少量调味配角，或放弃食用。新增的熟食按当地食品安全指导及时冷却、保存或丢弃。

### 成功条件

需要少辣的人有一份**自己确认可接受**且安全的食物或明确的替代餐；原菜是否保留、每份实际辣度与新增食物的去向都说得清。没有可行的温和底料、容量或安全保存条件时，停止补救也是完成判断。

### 常见报错与补救

- **整锅直接加糖或酸想“灭火”：** 先停在小测试份；强烈甜酸可能只是改变味道，不证明辣味去除。
- **只加水导致菜被稀释但仍难吃：** 先确认这道菜需要的是未调味主体、分小份搭配，还是干脆换餐；不为了体积而牺牲可食性。
- **试一口后认为所有人都能吃：** 由实际要吃的人决定接受程度；另留原版或提供温和替代，避免强迫。
- **修复生成超出锅和冰箱容量的份量：** 不继续扩锅；只处理可立即吃或能按适用规则安全保存的小量，其余选择停止。
- **食物安全史不清：** 停止一切调味补救，按当地食品安全指导处理。辣度问题不能覆盖变温或未熟问题。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

辣感是因人和菜而异的口味，不提供统一“辣度单位”或健康建议。乳制品、酸、糖和主食在不同文化与过敏规则下并非人人适用；以真实用餐者与原料信息为准。视觉、嗅觉或味觉不能可靠判断时，可用已知加入量、可见辣料和可信的人在安全阶段协助，但不把对方的口味当你的必须。增加主体可能提高总份量与储存负担，少量搭配可能改变菜单，都是合理的后果。

## English

Use this Skill when an ordinary finished dish is **still known to be safe** but hotter than expected, so some diners will not want their original serving. First decide whose portion needs changing. The result is an acceptable safe portion, a compatible mild pairing, or an honest stop. Sugar, acid, and dairy are not magical neutralizers. Stop this flavour task if anyone has a reaction requiring medical help.

### Preparation and inputs

Identify the dish, whether the heat comes from removable chilli pieces or mixed-in seasoning, and whether cooking and handling remain safe under applicable guidance. Ask who wants the original heat and who wants less. Find mild staples, cooked vegetables, unseasoned matching base, or sides those particular people can actually eat. Have a clean spoon, separate small bowl, and enough pan or serving capacity. Before making more food, check real cold storage and containers for any extra. Allergy substitution, medical diets, and unknown time or temperature history are outside this adjustment.

### Execution

1. **Stop making the problem stronger.** If the dish is still reducing, lower or stop heat when safe so the same seasoning does not enter less volume. At a stage safe to taste, stir evenly, take a clean spoonful, cool it, and assess. Do not sample undercooked food to diagnose heat.
2. **Choose the portion to change.** Keep the original for people who want it. Put only a small amount for heat-sensitive diners in a clean bowl for the first trial. Do not make the whole pot your irreversible experiment or alter someone else's plate without telling them.
3. **Remove what is physically removable.** If discrete chilli pieces, a separate oil layer, or a seasoning packet can be taken out without creating another safety problem, remove them from the test portion. If heat has spread through the dish, this does not make it mild by itself; the later tasting decision still matters.
4. **Reduce intensity per bite by the dish's structure.** In a mixable soup or stew sample, add compatible, safely edible unseasoned base or cooked bulk in stages, mixing and returning it to a safe serving state for its actual ingredients. For a finished item that cannot be remixed, serve a smaller spicy amount with a suitable mild staple or side. Pairing can change each bite; it does not prove spicy compounds vanished from the original dish.
5. **Change one small thing, then check again.** Taste safely with a clean spoon and let the intended diner decide whether the sample works; your own tolerance is not their vote. Sugar, acid, or dairy may be small flavour additions only when the dish and diner permit them. None certifies “neutralization,” and additions may introduce restrictions or allergens.
6. **Account for the original and the extra volume.** Only if the sample works and pan and cold-storage capacity exist should you adjust more food. Stop if the amount needed becomes unreasonable. Safe but still-hot original food may be offered as a small optional accent to someone who wants it, or not eaten. Handle added cooked food promptly under local cooling, storage, or disposal guidance.

### Success

People asking for less heat have a portion **they confirm they can accept** that remains safe, or an explicit alternative meal. You can explain what happened to the original dish and any new servings. With no suitable mild base, capacity, or safe storage, stopping the adjustment is also complete.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You add sugar or acid to the whole pot to “put out the fire”:** Return to a small sample. Strong sweet or sour flavour may distract but does not prove heat has gone.
- **Water increases volume but the dish remains unpleasant:** Decide whether this structure needs mild compatible bulk, smaller portions with a side, or another meal. Volume alone is not success.
- **One person tastes it and decides everyone can eat it:** Ask the diners who will actually eat it. Keep the original for those who want it or offer a mild alternative without pressure.
- **The fix creates more food than pan and fridge can hold:** Stop expanding the batch. Handle only what can be eaten now or stored safely under applicable rules; discard or stop the rest.
- **Food history is uncertain:** Stop flavour recovery and follow local food-safety guidance. A spice problem cannot erase unsafe handling or undercooking.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Heat preference differs by person and dish. This Skill provides no universal spice scale or health advice. Dairy, acid, sugar, and staples may not fit someone's culture, allergies, or food needs; use actual diner and ingredient information. If sight, smell, or taste is unavailable, rely on known amounts, visible seasoning, and trusted help at a safe tasting stage without adopting someone else's tolerance as mandatory. Added bulk creates storage work, and a smaller pairing may change the menu. Both are honest consequences.
