## 中文

当你在普通对话里按某种理解回复、答应或行动，随后发现可能误会了对方的口头或文字表达时，加载这份 Skill。目标是**核对对方实际意思，并修正自己的回应与相关安排**；不是证明对方表达不清，也不要求对方解释私人动机或接受你的补救。发错收件人、单纯抢话和持续强迫各有不同边界，不在这里用同一套话术覆盖。

### 准备与输入

先找出对方实际说/写的那一句、你当时理解成什么、你已经向谁说了什么或做了什么，以及哪些后续动作还可以暂停。能从原消息核对就核对，不截取一半句子证明自己有理。口头表达记不准时承认记忆不完整，别给对方强加一句他未说过的原话。

### 执行

1. **暂停基于旧理解的下一步。** 若你正在按那个解释安排时间、转告别人或取用物品，先停在可逆的地方。问题仍在时，不继续扩大错误承诺。
2. **说出自己的理解并开放修正。** “我刚把你说的周五理解成这周五，并已经回复可以；我可能听错了。你实际是指哪一天？”比“你明明说了这周五”更能得到信息。只问会改变实际安排的部分，不借机会审问动机。
3. **先听完正确意思。** 让对方按自己的话解释，必要时用一句“所以你是指下周五，对吗？”核对。对方说的事实和你的推测分开记；对方没有回答时标未知，不让你提出的答案自动成为定案。
4. **修正自己的回应。** “我之前答应的是这周五。现在明白是下周五，我需要重新看安排，稍后给你准确答复。”能履行就明确新动作；不能履行就及时说清，不用对方给你找借口。
5. **只通知受影响的人。** 若你的旧理解已经传给了相关同伴，给这些人简短更新必要的事实与下一步，不截图传播对方私人谈话。没有实际受影响者，就不需要公开解释一次普通误会。

### 完成条件

你已把“原话 / 我的旧理解 / 对方确认的意思 / 我现在的回应”分开；能改的行动已改，不能立即决定的部分明确待确认。若对方不回复，你停止依据猜测继续行动并留一个合适的再问时机。对方必须认可你、原谅你或给你完整私人背景，不是完成条件。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只凭语气推断“他肯定是在生气”：** 回到可核对的内容与具体问题，不把情绪猜测当事实。
- **问题写成“你是那个意思，对吧？”：** 给开放回答空间，允许对方说“不是”或提出第三种解释。
- **解释自己的理由比听对方还长：** 先停，等实际意思说完，再说明受影响的安排。
- **旧信息已告诉很多人：** 只对受影响的人更正，不用更大的群聊重播私人原文。
- **已经做了无法撤回的小动作：** 诚实说明发生了什么和你仍能做的修正，不承诺已恢复成从未发生。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

口头停顿、文字语气、日期格式和文化表达都可能不同，不能靠某种语言或身份决定对方“真正想说什么”。听觉、语言处理或阅读条件使核对困难时，可请对方换成双方可用的文字、语音或更具体的时间/动作描述。这里只处理普通可协商的误会；欺骗、骚扰、威胁或危机不在范围内。

副作用是你可能要收回一句过快的“没问题”。有依据的新版答复通常比坚持旧版省事。

- Original synthesis。把自己的解释当作待核假设，依据这次当事人的实际澄清修正行动，不把推测或原谅当事实。

## English

Load this Skill when you replied, agreed, or acted on one reading of an ordinary spoken or written message and then realize you may have misunderstood it. **Check what the other person actually meant and correct your response and affected plan.** This is not proof that they communicated badly, and it does not require private motives or acceptance of your repair. A wrong recipient, an interruption alone, or sustained coercion follows a different boundary.

### Preparation and inputs

Find the actual words, what you understood, what you already said or did, and which next actions can still pause. Read the full message when available instead of extracting half a sentence as evidence that you were right. If you cannot remember spoken wording, acknowledge that rather than attributing words they may never have said.

### Execution

1. **Pause the next step built on the old reading.** If you are scheduling, relaying, or using an item based on it, stop at a reversible point. Do not expand a doubtful commitment while the key fact is unresolved.
2. **State your interpretation as a check.** “I took your ‘Friday’ to mean this Friday and replied that I could do it. I may have that wrong. Which Friday did you mean?” This is more likely to uncover the fact than “You clearly said this Friday.” Ask only what changes the plan, not for a private motive.
3. **Hear the correction through.** Let them explain in their own words. Confirm with one line when needed: “So you mean next Friday, right?” Keep their stated fact separate from your inference. Without an answer, leave it unknown rather than treating your suggested meaning as confirmed.
4. **Correct your own response.** “I agreed thinking it was this Friday. Now I understand it is next Friday; I need to check before I can confirm.” State a new action you can do or say promptly when you cannot. Do not ask them to invent an excuse for you.
5. **Tell only affected people.** If you passed the old reading to collaborators, give those people a short update with the necessary fact and next step. Do not forward screenshots of someone else's private conversation. If nobody else was affected, there is no need for a public explanation of an ordinary misunderstanding.

### Success

You can separate “original words / my old reading / their confirmed meaning / my current response.” Reversible actions are corrected, and anything not ready for decision is marked pending. Without a reply, you stopped acting on guesswork and left a reasonable way to ask again. Their approval, forgiveness, or full personal backstory is not a completion requirement.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Tone alone becomes “they must be angry”:** Return to checkable content and one concrete question. A guess about feeling is not a fact.
- **Your question says “You meant X, right?” with no real alternative:** Make room for “no” and a third interpretation.
- **Your reasons take longer than their explanation:** Stop and hear the actual meaning before describing the affected arrangement.
- **You told many people the old version:** Correct only those affected instead of replaying private wording to a larger group.
- **You already took a small irreversible action:** Say honestly what happened and what you can still repair; do not promise it is as if nothing happened.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Pauses, text tone, date formats, and cultural expressions vary; language or identity cannot decide another person's intended meaning. If hearing, language processing, or reading access makes a check difficult, ask for text, voice, or a more specific date/action in a channel both can use. This covers ordinary negotiable misunderstandings, not deception, harassment, threats, or crisis.

You may have to retract a quick “no problem.” An evidenced new answer is usually easier to maintain than insisting on the old one.

### Sources

- Original synthesis. Treat your interpretation as a hypothesis and correct action from the person's actual clarification rather than treating guesses or forgiveness as facts.
