## 中文

当你要结束一小段绘画、纸艺、手写或其他普通个人创作，而作品还没到“可随手塞进抽屉”的状态时，用这份 Skill **保护当前状态、收好材料、留下下一步**。结果不是创作突然完成，而是你下次能找到它、知道哪里停下，也没有把湿作品、散落工具或未保存文件丢给未来的自己。

### 准备与输入

先确认作品归你处理，材料是普通、低风险的纸、笔、胶、颜料或受控数字文件。含危险化学品、动力工具、热源或需要特殊处置的材料按其说明与场所要求处理，本篇不替代。找一个能放作品且不会挡通行、被压坏或被儿童/宠物误碰的地点；湿、带胶或需固化的材料先看产品说明，不用这里的通用时间猜“已经干了”。手机或电脑作品则确认你能用原应用正常保存和重新找到文件；不需要上传或购买云服务。备一张纸或本地便签，准备写一条准确记录。

### 执行

1. **定格当前事实。** 用一句话写“现在已完成____，还缺____”。若作品已完成，也明确写“已完成；只待____（如装框或给朋友看）”，不把收工误记为成品验收。需要记位置时用自己的简短标记，不拍入别人的私人资料。
2. **先保护作品。** 纸张或小件放到有足够支撑的干净位置，不压在其他物品下面。湿、黏或仍在固化的作品按该材料说明保持所需空间与通风，暂时别封袋、堆叠或接触别的成品；不确定材料状态就标“未干/待检查”。数字文件用原应用保存，查看文件名和位置，必要时重新打开确认目前版本确实在，不以屏幕仍亮着当作已保存。
3. **把材料恢复到可控状态。** 依产品标签盖好普通颜料/胶/笔、把纸片和可复用材料分开放回可找的容器；普通工具按原说明放好。清理当前使用的台面，但不要为了收工改用陌生溶剂、拆设备或摸有风险的锐器。若物品仍在晾干区，在不挡路的位置给它标注“别碰”和下次检查条件，不提前打包。
4. **留下一个能开始的动作。** 写“下次先____，条件是____”，例如“胶完全按说明干后，先检查边缘有没有翘起”；数字作品可写“打开____文件，先改第二段的顺序”。动作要具体到拿起哪一件或检查哪一点，而不只是“继续努力”。
5. **收尾检查。** 能指给自己看作品在哪里、材料在哪里、什么尚未完成、下次第一步是什么；最后看一遍位置是否安全。若作品无法安全存放或材料状态不明，先隔离在合适位置并寻求产品说明/有经验者帮助，不宣称已经妥善收工。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你能找回当前版本，现场的普通材料处于可控状态，且有一条带条件的下一步。即使作品未完成，也可以完成一次收工；若湿件被密封、文件未保存或下次入口只有“再说”，仍需补救。

- **作品还湿但桌面马上要腾出：** 找到允许暂放且不会被碰到的位置，按材料说明继续等待；没有安全位置就暂停搬动并协调空间，不强行封袋。
- **材料混在一起，下次认不出：** 只把本次用到的材料按用途放回并做简短标记，不展开整个房间的大整理。
- **文件名有多个“最终版”：** 在原应用里确认当前文件名、位置和可重新打开的内容，记下它；本篇不替你决定删除旧版本。
- **中途必须离开：** 至少先处理会泄漏、弄脏或丢失状态的部分，并写“尚未安全收工”；回来再做完整检查，不把匆忙离开说成完成。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这只覆盖普通小作品的日常收工，不涉及文物级保存、危险材料处理、复杂文件备份或作品版权判断。材料、气候和说明书不同，干燥与封存条件以实际产品为准。行动、视觉或手部不便时可用易够到的安全位置、大字标签、语音便签或可信协助；你仍要能确认作品状态和下一步。作品会继续占空间，标签和记录也需要保留到下一次。

## English

Use this Skill when ending a short drawing, paper make, hand-written piece, or other ordinary personal creative session while the work is not ready to be tossed into a drawer. **Preserve the current state, control the materials, and leave a next step.** The result is not sudden completion of the artwork. You can find it and know where you stopped without leaving a wet piece, loose tools, or unsaved file for your future self.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm the work is yours to handle and the materials are ordinary low-risk paper, pens, adhesive, paint, or a controlled digital file. Follow product and site instructions for hazardous chemicals, power tools, heat, or material needing special disposal; this Skill does not replace them. Find a place where the work will not block passage, be crushed, or be reached by children or pets. For wet, glued, or curing materials, check the actual product instructions; do not guess a universal drying time here. For a phone or computer work, confirm you can save with the original app and find the file again. No upload or paid cloud service is required. Have paper or a local note ready.

### Execution

1. **Record the current fact.** Write “finished so far: ____; still needs: ____.” If the work itself is complete, say “complete; only ____ remains,” perhaps framing or showing it. Ending a session is not a claim that the artwork passed a final review. Use your own short location label if needed, without capturing other people's private information.
2. **Protect the work first.** Put paper or a small object on a clean supported surface without weight on top. For wet, tacky, or curing work, follow that material's instructions for space and ventilation; do not bag, stack, or touch it to finished work yet. If uncertain, label it “not dry / check later.” Save a digital file in its original app, check name and location, and reopen if needed to verify this version exists. A lit screen alone is not a saved file.
3. **Control the materials.** Close ordinary paint, glue, and pens according to their labels, and return paper scraps and reusable materials to findable containers. Put ordinary tools away as instructed. Clear the current work surface without reaching for an unfamiliar solvent, dismantling equipment, or handling risky blades. If something remains in a drying area, mark “do not touch” and the condition for checking it next time, away from traffic; do not pack it early.
4. **Leave a first action.** Write “next time, first ____ once ____,” such as “after the adhesive has dried as instructed, check for lifted edges.” For a digital piece: “open ____ file and first reorder paragraph two.” Name an object or check, not merely “keep working.”
5. **Check the close.** You can point to the work, materials, unfinished part, and next first step. Look once more at whether the location is safe. If you cannot store the piece safely or do not know the material's state, isolate it appropriately and consult its instructions or an experienced person. Do not claim a secure close.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You can find the current version, ordinary materials are controlled, and one conditional next action remains. The artwork can be unfinished while this close succeeds. A sealed wet object, unsaved file, or note saying only “later” still needs repair.

- **The work is wet but you need the table:** Find an allowed protected place and wait as the material directs. With no safe place, pause moving it and arrange space; do not bag it to clear the table.
- **Materials are mixed and unrecognisable:** Return only what you used this time by purpose and add a short label; do not begin a whole-room reorganisation.
- **Several files are called “final”:** In the original app, confirm the current filename, location, and reopenable contents, then note it. Deleting old versions is not this task.
- **You must leave suddenly:** First deal with what could leak, stain, or lose state and write “not safely closed yet.” Return for the full check; a rushed exit is not completion.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary small personal work, not conservation of historic objects, hazardous-material handling, complex backups, or copyright decisions. Materials, climate, and labels vary, so drying and storage follow the actual product. For mobility, sight, or hand-access needs, choose an easy-to-reach safe place, large labels, voice notes, or trusted help; you still need to verify state and next action. The work occupies space, and its label or note needs to survive until the next session.
