## 中文

当你还没选定具体商品，却需要知道一处普通收纳位、家具摆放区或低处通道**到底有多少可用空间**时，加载这份 Skill。结果是一张别人也能复核的尺寸记录，不是“某商品一定放得下”的购买判定；后者还需要准确商品与包装尺寸。不要为测量爬不稳的梯子、移动重物或碰触电气、燃气和结构部件。

### 准备与输入

选定一个具体位置与用途，例如“桌下放一个普通收纳箱”。准备卷尺或直尺、纸笔；纸上画粗略顶视图，标出入口、墙、地面、障碍物和你能安全接触的边界。约定宽、高、深各从哪里量到哪里，并在纸上写明使用厘米、毫米或英寸。卷尺两面单位不同，就先选一面，不让数字脱离单位独自旅行。

### 执行

1. **先定可用边界。** 测真正能放物品的内侧空间，而非墙外沿或柜门外框。标出踢脚线、把手、斜顶、固定支架、散热器等会缩小空间的障碍；只记录位置，不移动或拆卸它们。
2. **把卷尺放直再读。** 零点贴齐起点，卷尺沿要测的方向保持平直，不走对角捷径。读刻线并连单位写在相应箭头旁；若卷尺末端弯钩松坏、起点无法贴齐或读数难辨，就换直尺、请人协助或把该项标为未确认，不编精度。
3. **同一方向量不止一个点。** 对不规则的宽度量近端、中间、远端；高度量左右或有障碍的位置；深度量从入口到最先挡住物品的地方。每个读数标注位置，保留最窄或最低的限制点，不取一个好看的平均数。
4. **标记进入和使用条件。** 若物品须经过门洞或绕开固定障碍，量你能安全接近的净开口并标在图上；门、抽屉或人的通行需要的空间也画出来。高处、封闭管线附近或无法进入的段落写“未量”，不靠目测补齐。
5. **复测限制点。** 换一遍起止方向，重读最小那一处，并确认图上的箭头、数字和单位相符。两次读数不同就查零点、卷尺是否斜放与是否量到了同一边界；未解决的差异保留为范围或未确认。
6. **交付可复用的记录。** 写位置、测量日期、每个方向的起止点与单位、多点读数、限制点、障碍和未量之处。可附照片，但照片比例不能代替带单位的数字。之后选商品时再拿准确型号及包装资料比较，不在这一步先宣布通过。

### 成功条件

不在场的人只看你的草图或标注照片，就能知道哪处量了什么、用什么单位、最小可用宽高深在哪里、哪些条件仍未知。这张记录可以用于筛选候选；它不是搬运、安装或最终适配保证。

### 常见报错、停止与补救

- **只写“80 × 60”，没有方向和单位：** 补上宽/高/深、起止点和 cm、mm 或 in；不能补证据的旧数字重测。
- **卷尺斜着跨过空间：** 重新沿同一方向平直量；斜线长度不能充当净宽。
- **上宽下窄或有凸出物：** 保留各点读数和最小限制点，不把较大的那次当唯一尺寸。
- **起点、弯钩或刻度看不清：** 用另一把可信的尺、清晰近照或同伴读回；无法复测就标未确认。
- **必须爬高、搬重物或拆东西才能量：** 在安全可达处停止，记录未量段，向有能力且有权限的人求助。不要为了填满表格冒险。

### 假设、可及替代与现实副作用

你对该位置有测量许可，并能安全到达至少部分边界。轮椅、站立耐受、握力或视觉条件不应阻止记录：可坐姿量可达部位、用大字或语音记录数值、请可信的人读回并注明代量位置。无法确认的点保持未知。地区常用单位不同，先保留原始读数；后续商品比较时统一单位，不在这里用假精确数填空。

副作用是一张草图和几个诚实的空格。卷尺负责长度，方向仍要你标。

## English

Load this Skill before choosing a specific product when you need to know the **usable space** in an ordinary storage spot, furniture area, or safely reachable passage. The result is a measurement record someone else can check. It is not a verdict that a particular product fits; that also needs the exact item and package dimensions. Do not climb an unstable ladder, move heavy objects, or touch electrical, gas, or structural parts to fill the record.

### Preparation and inputs

Choose one place and purpose, such as an ordinary storage box under a desk. Bring a tape or ruler and a way to make notes. Sketch a simple top view showing the entry, walls, floor, obstacles, and boundaries you can safely reach. Decide where width, height, and depth start and end. Label your chosen unit—centimeters, millimeters, or inches—on the sketch. If the tape shows two systems, use one side consistently. A bare number cannot explain itself later.

### Execution

1. **Define usable edges.** Measure the inside space where an object could actually go, not an outside frame. Mark skirting boards, handles, sloped ceilings, fixed brackets, radiators, and other projections that reduce it. Record where they are; do not move or dismantle them.
2. **Align the tape before reading.** Place zero at the start and hold the tape straight along the direction being measured, not diagonally. Read the tick mark and write its unit next to the corresponding arrow. If the hook is damaged, zero cannot be aligned, or marks are unreadable, use a ruler or helper or mark the value unconfirmed rather than inventing precision.
3. **Take more than one reading per direction.** For an uneven width, check near, middle, and far points. Check height at both sides or where an obstacle hangs down. Measure depth to the first fixed obstruction. Label each location and keep the narrowest or lowest limiting point; do not average it away.
4. **Mark entry and use space.** If an item must pass through an opening or around a fixed obstacle, record the clear opening you can reach safely. Draw room needed for a door, drawer, or person to pass. Mark high, hidden, or inaccessible sections “not measured”; a glance is not a measurement.
5. **Recheck the limit.** Measure the smallest point again from the other direction, then match the arrow, number, and unit on the drawing. If readings differ, check zero, tape alignment, and whether both readings used the same edges. Leave an unresolved difference as a range or unconfirmed value.
6. **Deliver a reusable record.** Include place, date, each direction's start and end points and units, repeated readings, limiting point, obstacles, and unmeasured areas. Photos may help, but image scale does not replace numbers with units. Later, compare this record with exact item and package information; do not declare a fit here.

### Success

Someone who was not present can use your sketch or annotated photo to tell what was measured, in which unit, where usable width/height/depth are smallest, and what remains unknown. The record can screen candidates. It does not guarantee delivery, installation, or final fit.

### Common errors, stop points, and recovery

- **You wrote “80 × 60” without directions or units:** Add width/height/depth, start and end points, and cm, mm, or in. Remeasure old numbers if their evidence is gone.
- **The tape crosses the space diagonally:** Align it with the intended direction and remeasure. A diagonal is not clear width.
- **The top is wider than the bottom, or something projects:** Keep each reading and the limiting point instead of choosing the largest number.
- **Zero, hook, or marks cannot be read reliably:** Use another sound measuring tool, a clear close-up, or a companion who reads the mark back. If you cannot recheck, label it unconfirmed.
- **Measurement needs climbing, moving heavy objects, or dismantling:** Stop at the safe reachable boundary, record the gap, and seek someone able and permitted to help. A complete-looking form is not worth a fall.

### Assumptions, accessible alternatives, and side effects

You have permission to measure the place and can reach at least some edges safely. Mobility, standing tolerance, grip, or vision need not block the record: measure reachable parts while seated, use large-print or spoken notes, or have a trusted helper read values back and note where they measured. Unknown areas stay unknown. Regional units differ; retain original readings and convert only when comparing a product later. Do not fill gaps with false precision.

The side effect is a sketch with some honest blanks. The tape supplies length; you still label the direction.
