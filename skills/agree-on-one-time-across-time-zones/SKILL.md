## 中文

当你和另一个地区的人约普通通话、线上活动或到访时间，发现“周五十点”可能在两人日历里变成不同日期时加载。目标是**为同一瞬间写出双方各自的当地日期、钟点和清楚的时区地点，并取得对方复述确认**。这里不替人发日历邀请、不协调多人可用时段，也不假定全世界看的是你此刻的时钟。

### 准备与输入

确定活动是以哪个地点的时间为准，例如场所所在城市或双方约定的组织者城市；写出完整日期、星期和当地钟点。确认参加者在**活动当天**将在哪里，不只问他们今天在哪。用支持城市/地区时区和活动日期的日历或时钟查询；不要只记“比我快八小时”或只写 CST/EST 等缩写。时区及夏令时间规则可能被主管机构更改，临近活动可再次核对。

### 执行

1. **设一个唯一锚点。** 先写“活动发生在__年__月__日__时，按__城市/地区时区”，例如以场所所在地为准；跨午夜时仍保留锚点的完整日期。若只说“我这边下午”，先补城市和日期，不进入下一步猜偏移。
2. **按活动那天转换。** 在日历的时区选择器或可靠的地点时区工具中选双方活动当天所在的城市/地区，输入锚点日期和时刻，读出对方当地的**日期、星期、时间**。不要拿现在两地的时差直接加减到数月后的日期；尤其临近夏令时间切换或政府规则变化时，重看工具显示。
3. **处理不明确的钟点。** 若工具提示某地的时刻在换钟日不存在或重复、两款工具对远期日期显示不一致，暂停定案，换一个明确时段或向双方可用的当前日历核对。用 24 小时制或明确上午/下午，避免 12 点午/夜和跨日误读。
4. **在同一条消息写两边。** 模板：“按__城市，__年__月__日（周__）__时；你在__城市看到的是__年__月__日（周__）__时。我们约的是同一场__，请回一句你那边日历显示的日期和时间。” 只写时区缩写或一枚日历截图都不够；截图可能显示的是发送者当前本地设置。
5. **以双方回读为准。** 对方复述的日期/钟点若与预期不同，重新确认他们活动当天位置和时区设置，再转换。确认同一瞬间后才由实际组织者手动创建含正确事件时区的日历邀请；收到邀请仍要看自己显示的本地日期。出行地点或时区规则变化时，更新并重新确认受影响的表示。

### 完成与停止

你和对方各有一条**完整的当地日期、钟点和地点时区**，双方复述指向同一瞬间，并知道谁发邀请/何时复看，才算时间表达完成。**转换正确不等于对方有空、已接受邀请或活动已经发生。** 地点未知、钟点在换钟日含糊、日历显示冲突时先不要把时间标“已确认”。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只写 CST 或“东部时间”：** 补具体城市/地区和活动日期；缩写可能有多种含义或季节变化。
- **对方周六，你这里周五：** 两边都写完整日期和星期，不把“同一天”当默认条件。
- **现在的时差记得很熟：** 用活动日重新查，不按今天的夏令时偏移推算未来。
- **手机旅行后自动换时区：** 看事件锚点/创建时区和这台设备当前显示时区，必要时让对方回读当地时间。
- **邀请已发但两人理解不一致：** 暂停按旧时间执行，改正唯一事件并发正式更新，取得双方再次确认。

### 假设、替代与副作用

适用于自愿普通预约的时间表达，不提供某地区法律时间解释或跨国资格判断。时区规则、设备数据库和日历设置会变；远期活动在靠近当天时复核一次。视觉、听觉、语言或设备不便时，可用双方能读回的文字/电话与可信时区工具，不要求同一个 App。副作用是消息会比“明天十点”长，但不会让两人各自准时错过对方。

## English

Load this Skill when you and someone in another region plan an ordinary call, online activity or visit and “Friday at ten” could land on different calendar dates. Your result is **one instant written with each person's local date, clock time and named time-zone location, then read back by the other person**. This does not send calendar invitations, survey a whole group's availability or assume everyone sees your current clock.

### Preparation and inputs

Choose the place whose time anchors the event, such as the venue's city or an agreed organiser city, with full date, weekday and clock time. Check where each participant will be **on the event date**, not only where they are today. Use a calendar or clock that understands location-based zones and the actual event date. A memorised “eight hours ahead” or CST/EST alone is not enough. Governments can change zone or daylight-saving rules; recheck nearer the event.

### Execution

1. **Set one unique anchor.** Write “event on [year, month, day] at [time] in the [city/region] time zone.” Keep the full date even when another participant's date rolls over midnight. For “my afternoon,” first ask which city and date rather than guessing an offset.
2. **Convert for the event day.** In a calendar's zone selector or reliable location-aware clock, select both cities or regions for the date when the activity happens. Enter the anchored date and time, then read the other person's local **date, weekday and time**. Do not add today's time difference to a date months away. Look again around daylight-saving transitions or announced rule changes.
3. **Handle ambiguous clock times.** If a tool says a local time does not exist or occurs twice on a clock-change day, or two tools disagree for a distant date, pause commitment. Choose an unambiguous time or verify with a current calendar both can use. Use a 24-hour clock or explicit a.m. or p.m. so noon, midnight and day rollover are clear.
4. **Write both local versions together.** Template: “In [anchor city], [date and weekday] at [time]; for you in [city], [date and weekday] at [time]. It is the same [activity]. Please reply with the date and time your calendar shows.” A zone abbreviation or screenshot alone may reflect only the sender's current device settings.
5. **Use both people's readback.** If their date or clock differs, verify where they will be that day and their calendar zone, then convert again. Once you agree on one instant, the organiser can manually create an event with the right event zone. The recipient still checks their local date. For changed travel location or zone rules, update and reconfirm the affected wording.

### Success and stop

You and the other person each have a **complete local date, time and location-based zone**, your readbacks identify the same instant, and you know who will send an invite and when to recheck. **A correct conversion is not availability, acceptance or attendance.** For an unknown location, ambiguous clock-change hour or conflicting calendars, leave the time unconfirmed.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Only CST or “Eastern time” appears:** Add a specific city or region and event date; abbreviations can be ambiguous or seasonal.
- **Saturday there is Friday here:** Write both full dates and weekdays; a shared calendar day is not assumed.
- **You know today's offset by heart:** Check the event date again rather than projecting today's daylight-saving difference.
- **A phone changes zones while travelling:** Inspect the event anchor and creation zone alongside the device's displayed zone, and ask for the other person's local readback.
- **An invite was sent but interpretations conflict:** Pause the old time, correct the one organiser event, send a formal update and get both sides' confirmation again.

### Assumptions, alternatives and side effects

This is for voluntary ordinary scheduling, not legal interpretation of regional time or international eligibility. Rules, device databases and calendar settings can change; recheck a distant event closer to its date. For sight, hearing, language or device needs, use readable text or a phone readback and a trusted zone-aware tool without requiring the same app. The message is longer than “tomorrow at ten,” but it prevents two people from each arriving punctually at different instants.
