## 中文

当普通交流中你忘了对方希望使用的名字或称呼、叫错了，或不确定怎么念时，加载这份 Skill。目标是**诚实确认并按对方给出的形式继续交流**，不是证明自己记忆力好，也不要求对方透露法定姓名、解释称呼理由或原谅一次口误。本篇不处理冒名顶替、故意骚扰或身份核验。

### 准备与输入

先分清你是完全忘了、只是不确定发音，还是刚收到纠正。选择一个不会打断重要发言的短时机，尽量避免把个人称呼变成当众测验。若交流当前不需要叫名字，你也可以先用自然、不冒犯的无名表达，稍后再问；但不要靠猜一个名字让错误继续扩散。

### 执行

1. **不知道就直问一次。** “抱歉，我一下想不起该怎么称呼你了，可以再告诉我吗？”若只是不确定发音，就问“这个名字你平时怎么念？”不要求对方报身份证上的名字；对方给出想用的形式即可。
2. **若刚叫错，简短修正。** “抱歉，我叫错了。谢谢告诉我，我会用‘X’。”随后立即使用对方给出的称呼，不把道歉延长成请对方安慰你的演讲。
3. **核对听到的内容。** 在合适时轻声复述一次，“是 X，对吗？”若对方改用昵称或不同写法，以其当前说明为准。看不清名字牌、听不清或有读写限制时，用双方都可用的文字、读音或简短重复方式确认。
4. **把注意力还给原来的谈话。** 确认后继续正在讨论的事情，不接连问“你是不是生气了？”或解释自己最近多累。称呼是为了让交流继续，不是新增一场关于你记忆表现的会议。
5. **暂时得不到答案就不猜。** 对方不方便说、现场太吵或未回复时，先用尊重的中性方式继续，记录自己待确认的问题；不要让旁人推测其私人身份来填空。下次得到回答后再更新。

### 完成条件

你已确认并使用对方目前愿意的名字/称呼，或在无法确认时停止猜测并使用合适的无名表达，留下可再次确认的时机。一次叫错已被简短承认和更正；完成不要求对方夸你真诚、分享私人背景或当场原谅。

### 常见报错与补救

- **猜一个相似名字赌运气：** 停止猜，直接询问；错两次比问一次更难收尾。
- **道歉越说越长：** 缩成“抱歉，我会改用 X”，然后让对方继续说。
- **按名字牌或记录坚持旧叫法：** 若对方现在给了新形式，按其当前意愿使用；记录可能过时。
- **没听清，又怕再问：** 再确认一次发音或写法，比长期叫错更尊重实际信息。
- **对方没有回答：** 不追问法定身份；用中性称呼和上下文继续，并选择以后合适的确认时机。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

姓名、昵称、发音和称谓习惯会随个人和情境变化；语言、地区、外表或过去记录不能替对方决定现在的称呼。听觉、阅读、记忆或语言条件不方便时，可请求对方打字、慢说、用自己习惯的称呼，或只用指向具体人的简短句子。不要把私人称呼写进公共名单，除非相关人确实同意。

现实副作用是半分钟诚实提问，以及少一段假装认识多年的表演。谈话通常还能继续。

## English

Load this Skill when you forget the name or form of address someone wants used in an ordinary conversation, use the wrong one, or are unsure how to pronounce it. Your result is **an honest check and a conversation that continues with the form they give**. It is not a memory test and does not require a legal name, an explanation, or forgiveness for a slip. This does not handle impersonation, intentional harassment, or identity verification.

### Preparation and inputs

Separate a complete memory lapse from uncertain pronunciation or a correction you just received. Choose a short moment that does not cut off an important point, and avoid turning someone's name into a public quiz. If the current sentence needs no name, use a natural respectful phrasing and ask later. Do not keep guessing a name and spreading the mistake.

### Execution

1. **Ask once when you do not know.** “Sorry, I have lost the name you prefer. Could you tell me again?” If it is pronunciation only, ask “How do you usually say your name?” You do not need a name from legal documents; the form they want used here is enough.
2. **Correct a wrong name briefly.** “Sorry, I got that wrong. Thanks for telling me; I'll use X.” Then use the form they supplied. Do not turn an apology into a speech asking them to comfort you.
3. **Check what you heard.** Repeat once when appropriate: “X, right?” If they give a nickname or a different spelling, follow their current direction. For a hard-to-read badge, noise, or reading/hearing access needs, confirm by text, pronunciation, or one short repetition in a channel both can use.
4. **Return attention to the conversation.** After checking, continue the original topic rather than repeatedly asking whether they are upset or explaining your week. A name helps communication continue; it does not schedule a new meeting about your memory performance.
5. **Do not guess when an answer is unavailable.** If they cannot answer now, the room is too loud, or a message has no reply, continue with a respectful neutral phrasing and note that you need to check later. Do not ask bystanders to invent private identity details to fill the gap.

### Success

You confirmed and used the person's current preferred name or address, or stopped guessing and used an appropriate name-free phrasing until you can ask again. A wrong name was briefly acknowledged and corrected. Completion does not require praise for honesty, a private background explanation, or immediate forgiveness.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You gamble on a similar name:** Stop guessing and ask. Two wrong guesses are harder to repair than one question.
- **The apology keeps growing:** Reduce it to “Sorry, I'll use X” and let the other person continue.
- **You insist on an old badge or record:** Follow the person's current direction; records can be out of date.
- **You did not hear clearly and fear asking again:** Confirm pronunciation or spelling once more rather than using the wrong form for months.
- **They do not answer:** Do not demand a legal identity. Continue with neutral phrasing and pick a suitable later moment to ask.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Names, nicknames, pronunciations, and forms of address vary by person and situation. Language, region, appearance, and old records cannot decide another person's current choice. If hearing, reading, memory, or language access is difficult, ask them to type, speak slowly, use their familiar form, or use a clear name-free reference. Do not add private name information to a public list without agreement.

The side effect is half a minute of honesty and one less performance of remembering someone for years. The conversation can still continue.
