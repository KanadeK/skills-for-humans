## 中文

当你负责的一只普通室内猫的砂盆已有排泄物，或按既定护理需要整盆更换时，加载这份 Skill。结果是**已用物被收集、砂盆重新可用、清理者完成手部卫生**；不凭猫砂味道推断猫健康。

### 准备与输入

先看自己是否适合承担这项工作。美国 CDC 建议孕期或免疫功能较弱的人尽量由家中其他人清理猫砂；若无法转交，应向自己的医疗人员询问适用防护。本 Skill 不替代个人医学建议。准备专用工具、可封的废物袋、补充猫砂、当地垃圾收集说明与洗手设施；不要在食物处理台面清洗猫砂盆。

### 执行

1. **确认猫已离开操作区。** 不让猫、儿童或其他人踩进正清理的废物；保留一处猫可用的替代选择时再做长时间整盆清洗。
2. **清除日常排泄物。** 用专用铲轻收可见粪便和结团/受污猫砂，放入可封容器；按本地规则投入指定垃圾，不假设可冲马桶或进厨余。遇到异常血迹、持续腹泻或猫排尿困难等情况，及时联系兽医，不把清盆当成治疗。
3. **按需要补砂。** 根据这只猫已接受的砂种和实际深度补充，不一次更换所有条件。Cats Protection 建议定期把整盆砂倒掉并用热肥皂水清洗；具体频率按猫、砂种和兽医要求调整。
4. **整盆清洗时分开路线。** 倒掉旧砂后在适合的非食品区域用热水与温和肥皂清洗、冲净、干燥，再放回猫能到达的位置。不要把漂白剂与其他清洁剂混用，不凭强香味认定更安全；产品标签若要求特殊做法，先核对。
5. **封袋、清场、洗手。** 把废物按实际投放规则交出或安全暂存，清洁专用工具，最后用肥皂和水洗手。CDC 提醒接触砂盆后立即洗手；一次性手套不能代替这一步。

### 成功条件

已用物被装好并有实际去向，砂盆放回可达位置、砂量适合这只猫、工具与工作区清理，双手已洗。若清理者属于更高风险人群、缺卫生条件或出现需兽医判断的异常，先交接/求助，不把“简单清完”当作安全证明。

### 常见报错与补救

- **当地不收某种猫砂：** 留在密封袋中查本地说明，不倒进排水或随意堆肥。
- **洗净后猫拒用：** 检查砂种、残留气味、位置是否突然改变；持续异常请教兽医或行为人员。
- **清洁剂闻起来很重：** 按产品与盆器说明冲洗、晾干；不要再叠加另一种化学品“中和”。
- **孕期或免疫功能较弱者被临时分配：** 优先请他人接手并咨询医疗人员的个人防护建议。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这是已由你负责的普通室内猫砂盆日常卫生，不教疾病诊断、处理人体暴露或配制消毒液。行动或握力受限时用轻型专用工具、可及清理位置或请合适的人代劳；猫仍需及时得到可用设施。废物袋、工具清洁和洗手都是现实收尾，不由自动校验代做。

## English

Load this Skill when a used litter tray for an ordinary indoor cat in your care has waste, or a full change is due under its routine. Finish with **waste contained, the tray usable again, and the handler's hands washed**. Litter odour is not a diagnosis of the cat.

### Preparation and inputs

Check whether you should handle this task. The US CDC advises that a pregnant person or someone with a weakened immune system preferably have another household member clean the tray; if that is not possible, ask your healthcare professional about personal precautions. This Skill does not replace individual medical advice. Prepare dedicated tools, a sealable waste bag, replacement litter, actual local disposal rules, and handwashing. Do not wash a litter tray on a food-preparation surface.

### Execution

1. **Keep the cat out of the work area.** Prevent the cat, children, and others stepping into waste during cleaning. Provide another usable cat option when a full wash will take time.
2. **Remove routine waste.** Scoop visible feces and clumped/soiled litter gently into a sealable container. Use the actual local waste route; do not assume flushing or food-scrap collection accepts litter. Contact a veterinarian promptly for blood, persistent diarrhea, or trouble urinating rather than treating cleaning as care for illness.
3. **Restore accepted litter.** Add the type and depth this cat accepts without changing every condition at once. Cats Protection recommends regularly emptying all litter and washing the tray with warm soapy water; adjust frequency to the cat, litter type, and veterinary plan.
4. **Use a separate full-wash route.** When due, empty old litter, wash in a suitable nonfood area with warm water and mild soap, rinse, dry, and return the tray to a reachable spot. Never mix bleach with other cleaners or assume a strong scent means safer. Check any product-specific directions first.
5. **Seal, clear and wash hands.** Dispose of or safely hold the bag under real local rules, clean dedicated tools, and wash hands with soap and water afterward. CDC says wash immediately after handling a litter box; disposable gloves do not replace it.

### Success

Waste is contained with a real route, the tray is back within cat reach with accepted litter, tools and area are clean, and hands have been washed. If the handler is at higher risk, hygiene facilities are missing, or the cat has signs needing veterinary assessment, delegate or seek help rather than claiming routine cleanup proves safety.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Local collection will not accept this litter:** Keep it sealed while verifying local instructions; do not pour it down a drain or assume it is compostable.
- **The cat rejects a washed tray:** Recheck litter, lingering cleaner smell, and unexpected location changes. Persistent issues need veterinary or qualified behavior advice.
- **Cleaner smell is strong:** Rinse and dry under product and tray directions. Do not add another chemical to “neutralize” it.
- **A pregnant or immunocompromised person gets assigned at the last minute:** Prefer another handler and ask their healthcare professional about individual precautions.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This is routine hygiene for a tray you are responsible for, not veterinary diagnosis, treatment of human exposure, or a disinfectant recipe. Use light dedicated tools, an accessible cleaning area, or another suitable handler if movement or grip is limited; the cat still needs a timely usable tray. The waste bag, tool cleaning, and handwashing are physical end states, not automatic test results.
