## 中文

当你被邀请加入一局普通桌游、纸牌或社区游戏桌，想参加却没玩过这款时，用这份 Skill **确认规则、轮次和退出点后真正参与一轮**。成功不是赢、不是记住整本规则，而是你知道什么时候轮到自己、这一轮怎样结束，以及如果时间/感官负担不合适怎样告知和离开。借用物清点和场地清理归另一个收工任务。

### 准备与输入

先看主办方/游戏拥有者是否欢迎新手、适合你可用时间、人数和年龄/场地规则；公共活动可能需报名、限人或有摄影提示，不因“drop-in”一词就不核。只选普通非赌博游戏，不进行真钱下注或不透明收费。找到这**一版**的规则纸、盒内说明或主持人正式讲解；同名游戏可能有不同版本/玩法。告诉其他人你能玩多久、是否需要大字/口述/不靠颜色辨识的提示，以及你可能在某轮后离开；不用解释私人原因。

### 执行

1. **把规则缩成四问。** 开局前问：目标是什么？轮到我时允许做什么？行动如何传给下个人？这一轮/全局何时结束？如有多个模式，大家先选同一个版本，不把玩家各自记忆混成一套。
2. **约定本次停点。** 说“我可以玩到____时间/这一轮结束，之后想休息或离开”，问若有人提前走该怎么处理剩余回合/手牌/计分；先有一个大家接受的简短办法。规则不清或游戏明显超过你的时间，就选更短一局或只旁观，不硬坐下才问退出。
3. **做第一个合法动作。** 看一位愿意示范的人怎样开始，再在自己轮到时按该版规则操作；没听懂就说“我想确认这步可不可以____”。轮外等待、轮内动作和胜负判断分开，不因为新手慢就靠旁人替你偷偷下决定。
4. **遇争议先停牌/停子。** 比对当前版本规则，仍不清就请主持人/大家按开局约定决定这一局的处理，不把一次小争议升级成人身评价。拿错组件、漏一回合时修正能修的部分，无法修就如实继续/重开，不暗改别人分数。
5. **按约定结束或退出。** 玩到该轮终点可结算并把结果交给大家；若需早退，在自己回合前或合适停点明说“我现在要走”，交代手牌/组件和计分如何由主持人处理。即使无法等到自然停点，你也可离开，不需被游戏扣住；只要不悄悄拿走公共组件。下一步是按场地规则归还和清点，不在本篇复制整场清理。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你参加了至少一个真实回合/一轮，在开局时知道目标、轮次和终点；玩完或早退时其他人知道你的游戏状态和组件如何处理。输赢和表现不决定完成。只读了教程却没加入一回合，是准备而非参与。

- **规则讲太快：** 请人示范你下一轮的一个动作，必要时看这版说明；仍不懂可旁观，不猜着操作。
- **大家玩的规则不一致：** 暂停并统一用盒内本版或开局同意的家规，不半局突然改目标。
- **时长超过预计：** 在可用停点说明离开并交接，下一次选短局；不要因礼貌错过必要安排。
- **视觉/手部/听觉条件不适合：** 请求大字、口述、可触摸提示或经同意的代拿组件；没有可行方式就换游戏或停止。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

桌游/纸牌版本与家规差异大，这份 Skill不重写任何游戏的正式规则。某些公共活动有年龄、报名、图片使用或借物要求，按主办方当期说明办理。参与应在自愿、低压力、无真钱赌博的条件下进行；别人也有权拒绝一项家规。你可能只学会一轮，游戏仍能作为一次完整休闲体验。

## English

Use this Skill when invited to an ordinary board, card, or community game table that you have not played before. **Agree on the rules, turns, and exit before joining one real round.** Success is not winning or memorising a rulebook. You know when your turn begins, how this round ends, and how to say you need to stop because time or sensory load no longer works. Borrowed-piece inventory and room cleanup are another closing task.

### Preparation and inputs

Check whether the host welcomes beginners and whether the game's time, player count, age, and venue rules fit. A public drop-in may still have capacity or photography conditions; check the event notice. Choose an ordinary non-gambling game, without real-money betting or unclear charges. Find **this edition's** rule sheet, box instructions, or a host's approved explanation. Namesake versions and modes can differ. Tell others how long you can play and whether you need large print, spoken or non-colour cues, and that you may leave after a round. No private explanation is required.

### Execution

1. **Reduce the rules to four questions.** Before starting ask: what is the objective; what may I do on my turn; how does a turn pass; when does this round or game end? For several modes, agree on one edition or variant rather than combine memories of different rules.
2. **Agree on today's stop.** Say “I can play until ____ time or this round ends, then I may rest or leave.” Ask how an early departure would handle remaining turns, cards, and score, and agree on a simple approach. If rules or duration do not fit, choose a shorter game or watch rather than sit down before asking about an exit.
3. **Make one allowed move.** Watch a willing person demonstrate a start. On your turn, act under this version's rule. For uncertainty say “May I do ____ here?” Waiting, your move, and deciding a winner are separate. Beginner slowness need not mean someone secretly makes choices for you.
4. **Pause a rule dispute.** Read this edition's rule or ask the host and group to apply their agreed house rule for this game. Do not turn a minor dispute into a judgment of a person. Correct a misplaced piece or skipped turn if possible; if not, agree to continue or restart openly rather than change another player's score quietly.
5. **Finish or leave explicitly.** At the agreed end, settle the round and hand over the result. For early departure, say before your turn or at a suitable pause “I need to go,” and hand cards, pieces, or score handling to the host as agreed. You may still leave if you cannot wait for a natural break; a game is not a contract to stay. Do not quietly take shared pieces away. Follow the separate venue return and inventory process afterward.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You joined at least one real turn or round knowing the objective, turn flow, and end, and other players know what happens to your state and pieces when you finish or leave. Winning and skill level do not determine success. Reading instructions without joining a turn is preparation, not participation.

- **Rules are too fast:** Ask for one demonstration of your next move and inspect this edition's sheet. Watch instead if it remains unclear.
- **Players use conflicting rules:** Pause and use the current box rules or house rules accepted at the start; do not change the goal mid-game unannounced.
- **The game lasts longer than expected:** State your departure and hand over state at a workable pause, choosing a shorter game next time. Courtesy need not override an essential commitment.
- **Sight, hand, or hearing access does not fit:** Request large print, spoken or tactile cues, or consent-based piece handling. Change game or stop if none works.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Game editions and house rules differ; this Skill does not recreate any game's official rules. Public events may set age, booking, image-use, and borrowed-material terms, so check the actual organiser. Join voluntarily in a low-pressure setting without real-money gambling. Others may decline a house rule too. Learning just one turn can still make a complete leisure experience.
