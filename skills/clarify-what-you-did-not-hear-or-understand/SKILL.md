## 中文

普通谈话里，你刚才**没有听清一段话**，或字词都听到了却**不明白它在此处是什么意思**，需要在答应或行动前弄明白时，用这份 Skill。目标是请对方补上具体缺口、用你可用的方式核对意思；若仍无法核实，就保留“未知”，不点头伪装已懂。它不处理医疗、法律、财务或紧急指令，也不代替就全新信息提出问题的 H193。

### 准备与输入

在自己还记得上下文时，先区分卡在哪里：声音被噪声盖住、某个名字/数字没听到、字词听到了但含义或下一步不明。留住确定的部分，不必把整段话都判为没听见。想清楚是否会据此去某地、在某时行动或替人做事；会影响行动的细节需要核实，普通闲聊可选择继续听而不逐字盘问。

### 执行

1. **及时指出具体缺口。** 可以说“前半句我听到了，最后的地点没听清，能再说一次吗？”或“我听到你说‘照旧’，但这次指的是原来的时间还是地点？” 把需要补的部分说清，比连续“什么？”更容易让对方帮上忙，也不要求你解释自己的身体状况。
2. **按问题换方式。** 没听到声音，可请对方重说、放慢一点、换到较安静且允许停留的位置，或写下名字和数字；听到字词但不懂，可请对方换一种说法、给一个与此事有关的例子或指出下一步。单纯加大音量未必能解决词义问题。对方也许有自己的沟通需要，选双方可用的方法。
3. **读回关键意思。** 用自己的话简短复述：“所以我们是周三 15:00 在北门见，还是周四？”让对方纠正。名字、日期、价格或地址等行动要用的信息可写下再核对；自动字幕、听写或别人转述只是辅助，关键处仍需原说话者或正式资料确认。
4. **仍不明白就停在未知。** 两次尝试后依然不清楚，可以说“我现在还不能确认这个安排，麻烦稍后用文字发给我/换个时间再核对。” 不能确定就暂缓依赖它的动作，不假装同意或给别人传一个可能错的版本。普通聊天可礼貌结束这段；重要后续给出下一次核对方式。

### 成功条件

具体缺口已由说话者补足，你能读回实际意思并知道下一步；或者你明确告诉对方仍有哪一处未知，安排可用的后续，不基于猜测行动。对方愿意重复或改说法是协作的一部分，但不要求一次就完美。听不清不是对话中的道德故障。

### 常见报错与补救

- **把没听见的数字猜成最像的那个：** 请对方写下或逐位确认，再据此安排时间或地点。
- **只说“什么？”对方整段从头再讲仍听不清：** 指出最后三词、名字或地点等真正缺口，并换安静处/文字。
- **听到了术语却只让对方更大声重复：** 改问“这在我们这件事里具体要做什么？”或请换说法。
- **自动字幕给出一句很确定的错话：** 将字幕当线索，针对关键内容向原说话者读回核对。
- **仍不明白却点头接受任务：** 及时撤回“我懂了”的假状态，说明尚未确认的部分和下一次核对方式。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

本 Skill 面向普通沟通，不诊断听力、语言或认知情况，也不让人必须公开诊断才可请求重复。RNID 建议重说、换词、书写和减少背景噪声作为可选办法，具体偏好因人而异。无法使用纸笔可用双方认可的设备文字；不方便移位时可改说话方向或另约可听清的环境。多花一次读回时间，通常比把未确认的版本转给第三人更可靠。

## English

Use this Skill in an ordinary conversation when you **missed part of what was said** or heard the words but **do not understand what they mean here**, especially before you agree or act. Ask for the specific missing part and check the meaning in a format you can use. If it stays unclear, keep it unknown rather than nodding as if you understood. This is not for medical, legal, financial, or emergency instructions, or a question about wholly new information under H193.

### Preparation and inputs

While the context is fresh, identify the type of gap: noise covered the sound, a name or number was missed, or the words came through but the meaning or next action did not. Keep what you did understand instead of declaring the entire exchange missing. If you will travel, turn up at a time, or do something for someone based on it, verify that detail. Casual talk need not become a word-by-word interrogation.

### Execution

1. **Name the gap promptly.** “I got the first part but missed the place at the end—could you repeat it?” or “I heard ‘as usual’; do you mean the original time or the original location this time?” A specific gap is easier to repair than repeated “What?” You do not have to explain a health condition to request clarity.
2. **Switch method to fit the problem.** For missing sound, ask for a repeat, slightly slower speech, a quieter permitted place, or writing of a name or number. For words heard without clear meaning, ask for another phrasing, one relevant example, or the actual next action. Louder repetition alone may not solve meaning. The other person may have communication needs too; choose a method both can use.
3. **Read back the critical meaning.** Briefly say what you understood: “So is it Wednesday at 15:00 at the north entrance, or Thursday?” Write and verify a name, date, price, or address before using it. Automated captions, dictation, or someone else's retelling can help, but check an action-changing detail with the speaker or an official source.
4. **Keep unresolved detail unresolved.** If two attempts still do not clarify it, say “I can't confirm this arrangement yet; could you send it in writing later or check it with me at another time?” Pause the action that depends on it; do not pretend to agree or pass on a guessed version. A casual exchange can end politely; an important follow-up needs a way and time to check again.

### Success

The speaker filled the specific gap, you can read back the real meaning and know the next step, or you clearly named what remains unknown and arranged a usable follow-up without acting on a guess. Repeating or rephrasing is shared work, but the first try need not be perfect. Missing words is not a moral failure in conversation.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You guess the number that sounded closest:** Ask for it in writing or digit by digit before scheduling around it.
- **Only “What?” causes the whole sentence to be repeated, still unclear:** Name the last few words, name, or place you missed, then switch to a quieter place or text.
- **You heard a term but only ask for it louder:** Ask what it means for this specific task or for different wording.
- **Automated captions offer a confident wrong sentence:** Use them as a clue, then read the critical content back to the speaker.
- **You nod and accept a task while still unsure:** Correct that false “I understand,” state the unresolved part, and set a follow-up method.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is ordinary communication, not a diagnosis of hearing, language, or cognition. No one must disclose a diagnosis to request repetition. RNID offers repetition, rephrasing, writing, and less background noise as options, with preferences varying by person. If pen and paper are inaccessible, use device text both people accept. If you cannot move, change speaking position or agree on a later setting. One extra readback is more reliable than forwarding an unverified version to a third person.
