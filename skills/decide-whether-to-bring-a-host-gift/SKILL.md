## 中文

当你要去别人家参加一次普通、自愿的小型来访，正在犹豫是否“应该带点什么”时，加载这份 Skill。结果可以是带、什么也不带，或先问主人；没有哪种关系、节日、年龄或语言自动要求礼物。本篇决定**这次是否适合增加一件物品**，不提供品牌、实时价格、过敏处理或礼物交换礼仪大全。

### 准备与输入

看这次邀请原文或双方实际说过的话：主人是否明确说“不用带”、这次是聊天、共同用餐还是物品交接、是否已有分工。再看自己的可承受花费、时间、携带能力，以及候选物品会不会给主人增加保存、照料、清洁或回礼压力。食物、香味物品、植物和需要空间的东西只有在主人确实愿意且具体条件清楚时才考虑；不要凭对方身份猜口味或身体情况。

### 执行

1. **先尊重已经给出的信息。** 主人说“别带东西”就把不带当成有效且通常最省事的决定；已经明确分工带某项，就按约定核对数量与交接方式，不另外叠加惊喜物品。若没有线索，不把社交焦虑当作购买清单。
2. **问一个轻量问题，或决定不问。** 若礼物是否会帮忙确实影响你，可说“这次有什么带过去会方便？没有也完全没关系。”提问不是要求主人给你布置任务。时间太近、联系不便或对方没回复时，仍可以选择不带。
3. **只有在有意义且可负担时才选带。** 物品应适合这次场合、不会要求主人马上使用、收纳或回礼，并在你愿意花费和能携带的范围内。若只能靠猜过敏、饮食、宠物或空间条件证明它合适，就停在“不带/先问”。礼物不该靠风险不明的食品或设备完成。
4. **把带来的东西作为可拒绝的提议。** 到场可说“带了这个；如果你用不上或不方便收，真的可以不收。”主人接受后再放到其指定位置；对方拒绝就收回，不反复劝。你带来的食物不自动变成今天的菜单，也不自动需要当场打开。
5. **让决定有一个收尾。** 不带时直接赴约并按原约定参与；带时记住是谁提出、是否接受及还需带回什么。不要因为花了钱就要求主人表现出喜欢或答应下次互赠。

### 完成条件

出门前你已根据实际邀请、主人答复或明确未知，作出“带 / 不带 / 待问后决定”的选择；它符合你能承受的花费与携带条件。若带了，对方能自由接受或拒绝，物品不被强加为保存任务。主人必须高兴或回礼不是完成条件。

### 常见报错与补救

- **主人说不用带，你仍准备一大份：** 停止追加；按对方说的空手赴约，比考验其储物空间更尊重信息。
- **来不及问且不知道需求：** 不带也是完整决定。不要在路上按“看起来贵”临时购买。
- **食品、香味或植物可能造成限制：** 未确认具体条件就不选它；不把“天然”“大家都喜欢”当安全或合适证明。
- **你无法承担花费或搬运：** 不为赴约透支或受伤；真诚参加已约定活动即可。
- **对方拒收：** 说“好，我带回去”，停止说服；不要让礼物成为礼貌考试。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

礼物习惯因个人、家庭和场合而异，不能从地区、文化或关系标签推出一个统一答案。行动能力、视觉、金钱和时间限制都可以让“不带”成为最佳可执行版本。若是有明确主办方规则的场地，以该次邀请和主办方信息为准；食品、植物或设备的具体安全条件要另行核对。

你可能得到一次没有物品交换的愉快来访，也可能多带回一件未被接受的东西。这两种结果都比强迫主人给购物决定打分清楚。

## English

Load this Skill before an ordinary voluntary small visit to someone's home when you are wondering whether you “should bring something.” The result may be bring, bring nothing, or ask first. No relationship, holiday, age, or language automatically requires a gift. This decides **whether one more object fits this visit**, not a brand, live price, allergy response, or universal gift-etiquette guide.

### Preparation and inputs

Read what this invitation actually says and what the host has told you. Did they say “no need to bring anything”? Is this a chat, shared meal, or quick handoff with an agreed contribution? Check the cost and time you accept, what you can carry, and whether a candidate would give the host storage, care, cleaning, or return-gift work. Consider food, scented objects, plants, or bulky items only when the host actually wants them and relevant conditions are known. Do not guess tastes or bodily needs from identity.

### Execution

1. **Use information already given.** If the host said “don't bring anything,” treating that as a valid answer is usually the simplest choice. If you agreed to bring one particular item, check its amount and handoff rather than adding surprise extras. Without a clue, social anxiety is not a shopping list.
2. **Ask lightly, or choose not to ask.** If the answer would help, say “Is there anything useful for me to bring this time? Nothing is fine too.” The question is not a request for the host to assign you work. If timing or contact makes asking difficult or there is no reply, bringing nothing is still an available choice.
3. **Bring only something useful and manageable.** An item should fit this occasion without requiring immediate use, storage, or a reciprocal gift and stay within what you can afford and carry. If you must guess about allergies, diets, pets, or space to justify it, choose “nothing” or “ask first.” Do not complete a gift plan with food or equipment whose suitability is unknown.
4. **Offer it with a real no.** On arrival, “I brought this; if it is inconvenient or not useful, it is absolutely fine not to take it.” Put it where the host chooses only after acceptance. If they decline, take it back without arguing. Food you bring does not automatically become today's menu or need to be opened now.
5. **Close the decision.** If you bring nothing, arrive and take part in the agreed visit. If you bring something, note whether it was accepted and what you may need to take home. Paying for it does not require the host to like it visibly or promise another exchange.

### Success

Before leaving, you chose “bring / no gift / ask then decide” from the actual invitation, host response, or an honestly unknown preference, within your cost and carrying limits. If you brought something, the host could accept or refuse freely without inheriting an unwanted storage task. Their happiness or reciprocal gift is not a completion criterion.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The host said no gifts and you are assembling a large offering:** Stop adding things. Arriving empty-handed respects the information better than testing their cupboard space.
- **There is no time to ask and you do not know a need:** No gift is a complete decision. Do not buy something en route merely because it looks expensive.
- **Food, scent, or plants may conflict with a restriction:** Without the specific condition confirmed, do not choose them. “Natural” or “everyone likes it” is not evidence of safety or suitability.
- **You cannot afford or carry it comfortably:** Do not spend beyond your limit or risk an injury to attend. Take part in the planned visit instead.
- **The host declines:** Say “Okay, I'll take it back,” and stop persuading. A gift is not an etiquette exam.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Gift habits differ by person, household, and occasion. Region, culture, or relationship labels do not yield one required answer. Mobility, vision, money, and time may make no gift the best workable option. For a venue with explicit host rules, follow this invitation and organizer's actual information. Verify any specific food, plant, or equipment condition separately.

The visit may be enjoyable without an object changing hands, or you may carry an unaccepted item home. Both are clearer than requiring the host to grade your purchase.
