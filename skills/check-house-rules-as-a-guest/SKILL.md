## 中文

当你去别人家参加一次普通、自愿的来访，刚进门还不知道鞋、包、座位、洗手间、房间或物品的规则时，加载这份 Skill。目标是从**这位主人和这个家**取得可执行边界，再据此行动；不是猜测某个地区、语言或身份的人都遵守同一套习俗。主人没有义务开放所有房间或借出物品，你也不必为无法满足的身体条件沉默。

### 准备与输入

看清自己已知道的邀请范围与主人在门口给的说明，想一想这次真正需要哪些信息：能在哪里放外套和包、鞋怎么处理、坐哪里、需要时怎样去洗手间、能否碰某件东西。只问当前会改变行动的几项，不把做客变成对主人家的现场审计。

若鞋或入口安排与你的行走稳定、感官或其他实际需要冲突，直接说明**需要什么安排**，不必解释诊断。主人可能有自己的地板、宠物、同住者或私人物品边界；双方都应有机会提出能做到的替代。

### 执行

1. **在门口停一拍，问当前规则。** “鞋要放哪里？包可以放这儿吗？”主人已主动说明的就按说明做，不必重复考试。看见别人脱鞋也不能代替这次主人给你的规则。
2. **确认这次会用的空间。** “我们坐哪儿？如果要用洗手间，怎么走？”注意主人指出的可用区域和关闭的门；不把开放的客厅推断成整屋参观许可。遇到宠物或共居空间的限制时按实际安排询问。
3. **要用新物品或区域时再问一次。** 想移动椅子、开柜、使用插座、拿食物或进入另一房间，先说具体动作并等主人答复。“欢迎来”不等于对每件物品的永久授权；同住者的物品尤其要问有权决定的人。
4. **说出实际做不到的条件。** 若需要保留鞋、坐在较稳的位置、避开宠物或不走某段楼梯，简短说明需求并问可行替代。主人不能提供时，可以缩小活动范围、改到别处或结束来访，不靠忍受风险证明礼貌。
5. **读回并守住边界。** 用一句话确认“包放这里、书房不进、洗手间在右侧”。规则或活动变化时再核对；误碰了不该动的东西就停下、道歉、按主人意愿复原，不把一次误会升级为偷偷摸索。

### 完成条件

你知道这次来访实际可进入、可使用和需要先问的范围，并按确认过的规则行动；遇到与你的必要条件不相容的安排，你已提出替代或停止。完成不要求主人同意开放私人空间，也不要求你猜对一个未说出口的家规。

### 常见报错与补救

- **按某个文化印象自动脱鞋或不脱：** 回到这位主人的说明；如果鞋涉及行动需要，直接商量现实替代。
- **主人说“随便”，你仍不知道能去哪：** 问一两个具体动作，例如“洗手间是那边吗？这个房间先不进，对吗？”
- **把别人家的物品当成公共用品：** 放下并先问物主；使用权限不能由物品摆在桌上推断。
- **不小心进入私人区域：** 立即退回，说明是误会并尊重主人后续指示，不继续探索。
- **规则与你的身体条件冲突：** 不勉强执行或要求主人放弃边界；提出可行场地、座位或时间替代。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

鞋、门、宠物、食物和物品使用没有全球统一的家庭默认值。语言、听觉、视力或社交负荷使当场提问困难时，可以用短信、简短手势配合明确指向，或请主人把两三项关键规则写下来。你只需确认这次会用的部分，不收集不相关的私人信息。若出现威胁或其他非普通情形，停止这份做客流程并使用实际可用的安全支持。

现实副作用是多问两句、少一次把私人房间认成捷径的机会。礼貌可以是可验证的，而不必是猜谜。

## English

Load this Skill at the start of an ordinary voluntary visit to someone's home when you do not yet know the rules for shoes, bags, seating, toilet access, rooms, or objects. Get workable boundaries from **this host in this home** before acting; do not guess one custom from region, language, or identity. The host need not open every room or lend an object, and you need not silently accept an arrangement that fails a real access need.

### Preparation and inputs

Use what the invitation and the host at the door have actually said. Identify only the facts that change your next actions: where your coat or bag goes, what to do with shoes, where to sit, how to reach a toilet if needed, and whether you may handle a particular item. This is not an audit of someone's whole home.

If an entry or shoe rule conflicts with stability, sensory, or another practical need, state **what arrangement you need**, not a diagnosis. The host may have floor, pet, co-resident, or private-property boundaries too. Both sides need a chance to offer a workable alternative.

### Execution

1. **Pause briefly at the door and ask the current rule.** “Where should my shoes go? Is this a good place for my bag?” Follow directions already given without making the host repeat a test. Copying another guest's shoes is not proof of the host's rule for you.
2. **Confirm the spaces this visit uses.** “Where shall we sit? How do I reach the toilet if I need it?” Note the areas the host opens and doors they keep closed. An open living room does not grant a tour of the entire home. Ask about pet or shared-space conditions when relevant.
3. **Ask again for a new item or area.** Before moving a chair, opening a cupboard, using an outlet, taking food, or entering another room, name the action and wait for a reply. “Welcome” is not permanent permission for every object. Ask someone authorized to decide about a co-resident's belongings.
4. **Say when a condition does not work for you.** If you need to keep shoes on, use a steadier seat, avoid a pet, or avoid stairs, say so briefly and ask for a workable route. If it cannot be offered, shrink the activity area, move the visit, or leave. Enduring a risk is not proof of politeness.
5. **Repeat back the boundary and follow it.** “Bag here, study closed, toilet on the right.” Check again if the activity changes. If you touch something you should not, stop, apologize, and restore it as the host wishes rather than exploring quietly.

### Success

You know what you may enter or use during this visit and what requires asking, and you follow those confirmed limits. If a necessary condition is incompatible, you have proposed an alternative or stopped. Completion does not require the host to open private rooms or you to guess an unstated house rule.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You put shoes on or off from a cultural guess:** Return to this host's direction. Discuss a real alternative if footwear affects access.
- **The host says “make yourself at home” but you still do not know where to go:** Ask about one or two specific actions, such as the toilet or a closed room.
- **You treat a visible object as shared property:** Put it down and ask its owner. Its location on a table does not prove use permission.
- **You enter a private space by mistake:** Step back, explain briefly, and follow the host's direction without further exploring.
- **A house rule conflicts with a physical need:** Do not force yourself through it or demand the host surrender their boundary. Offer a feasible venue, seat, or timing alternative.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Shoes, doors, pets, food, and object use have no worldwide household default. If language, hearing, sight, or social load makes live questions hard, use a text, brief gesture plus a clear point, or ask the host to write the few key rules. Confirm only what this visit uses; do not collect unrelated private information. Stop this ordinary guest flow if threats or another non-ordinary situation arise and use actual available safety support.

The side effect is two extra questions and one fewer chance of treating a private room as a shortcut. Politeness can be checked instead of guessed.
