## 中文

当普通烹饪已经结束、热源关闭且锅**安全冷却后仍有硬焦结**，用这份 Skill 判断黑色的是可去除食物，还是锅面已有损伤，再决定一次制造商支持的清理或停用求助。它不处理正在冒烟、起火、失控热油的现场；遇这些情况先离开清洁任务，按当地消防/紧急指导行动。锅具清洁不等于保证食物或锅一定能挽回。

### 准备与输入

确认锅已离火并按制造商说明冷到可安全触碰；不往高温锅里倒冷水。识别实际材质、表面涂层、品牌或型号，读该锅对焦结、浸泡、清洁剂、刮片和干燥的说明。准备足够光线、适用海绵或厂家允许的工具。若发现珐琅崩口、涂层起片、裂缝、底部严重变形、把手松动或材质不能辨认，先停止作为食品接触锅使用并问制造商/专业人员；不凭“黑色都一样”继续刷。

### 执行

1. **先把剩余食物与锅面分开观察。** 安全移出未粘牢食物；看硬黑层是否在表面、边缘能否用该锅许可工具轻松动，还是能看见下面的珐琅/涂层本身脱落。拍照记位置与锅具型号，别用尖锐金属工具刮一大片来“确定”。
2. **按材料做一个小范围、可停止的清理尝试。** 对确认完好且厂家允许的锅，选**这款锅**说明中的温和方案：某些珐琅或不粘锅允许冷却后温和洗剂与适度浸泡，养护铸铁可能要求刮片、短时加水后彻底干燥/养护，不锈钢可能允许其专用非磨蚀用品。只在说明允许时使用水或再次加热；不把这些示例做法的时间、温度、比例跨锅复制。
3. **看食物是否松动、表面是否完整。** 轻试一小块，若焦结松脱且底面无新的崩口、起片或明显变形，才按同一受支持方法扩大；难以移动、需要越来越大力、出现表面碎屑或工具划伤时立即停止。不同材料的变色不自动等于损坏，也不自动等于安全可继续用。
4. **洗净并做使用决定。** 清理成功时按这款锅说明去除洗剂、漂净、彻底干燥并完成适用养护，再核对食品接触面完整。若该制造商对已观察到的损伤要求停用（例如某些珐琅崩露金属），遵守其说明；其他涂层划痕不在这里判定健康危害，保留记录并向厂家确认适用性。
5. **给未解决的锅明确去向。** 一次受支持的温和尝试仍无法清理、无法确认表面或有结构损坏时，暂停再次烹饪，咨询制造商/专业建议或按当地方式更换与回收。不要把酸、漂白剂、氨、不明清洁粉混在一起，也不让家庭清洁升级为强力化学或电气/燃气维修。

### 成功条件、常见问题与补救

你能说明焦结与锅面损伤的观察依据，完成一次该锅说明允许的小范围试验，并取得“洗净且完整可用 / 停用待确认 / 更换”的结果；不以把焦层硬刮掉作为唯一成功标准。

- **锅仍热就想冲水：** 停下并按制造商说明冷却，避免烫伤和材料热冲击。
- **焦层没动、海绵越来越硬：** 停止增加力度，查本型号顽固污渍指导或向厂家求助。
- **珐琅看到崩口和裸金属：** 停止使用，按该制造商的停用/售后指导处理，不用调味油遮住破口。
- **不粘锅有划痕：** 不做普遍毒性断言；停下可能继续磨损的清洁方法，读准确系列说明并向厂家确认是否应更换。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

只处理安全冷却的普通锅具，不负责正在发生的火灾、罐藏锅、压力锅内部、食物安全认证或设备维修。制造商和系列的涂层差异会改变清洁上限；“用小苏打煮所有锅”不是可靠通用规则。握力受限时在稳定水槽做小范围温和动作并请可信的人搬重锅；视力不足时用光线和放大照片，未知崩口不用裸指去摸。锅若停用，会临时减少可用器具。

## English

Use this Skill after ordinary cooking has ended, heat is off, and a pan has **safely cooled with hard burnt-on food still attached**. Decide whether black material is removable food or possible surface damage, then try one maker-supported cleaning method or stop. This does not handle active smoke, fire, or uncontrolled hot oil; leave cleaning and follow local fire or emergency guidance then. Cleaning cannot guarantee the food or pan is recoverable.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm the pan is off heat and cool enough to handle under its maker directions. Do not pour cold water into a hot pan. Identify its actual body and cooking surface, brand, or model and read directions for char, soaking, product, scraper, and drying. Have good light and an allowed sponge or tool. Stop food-contact use and ask the maker or a professional if enamel has chipped, coating flakes, the pan cracks or warps badly, a handle loosens, or the material cannot be identified. “All black marks are the same” is not a cleaning rule.

### Execution

1. **Observe food and surface separately.** Remove loose food safely. Look whether the hard black layer sits on top and an allowed tool can lift an edge, or enamel or coating itself appears to come away. Photograph the location and model; do not scrape a large patch with sharp metal merely to find out.
2. **Try one small, stoppable method for this material.** For an intact pan whose maker permits it, select a mild approach from **that exact product's** care directions. Some enamel or nonstick cookware permits mild soap and bounded soaking after cooling; maintained cast iron may use a scraper, limited water heat, then thorough drying and care; stainless may permit its nonabrasive specialty product. Use water or renewed heat only where authorised. Do not transfer example minutes, temperatures, or ratios across pans.
3. **Check both loosening and surface integrity.** Gently trial one patch. Expand the same supported method only if food loosens without new chips, flakes, or obvious warping underneath. Stop when it takes increasing force, surface debris appears, or a tool scratches the pan. Colour change varies by material; it is neither automatic proof of damage nor a universal all-clear.
4. **Clean and decide on use.** If successful, remove product, rinse, dry fully and perform only the applicable care in this pan's directions. Inspect the food-contact surface. Follow a maker stop-use instruction for observed damage, such as certain enamel chipped to bare metal. Do not make universal health claims from other coating scratches; keep evidence and ask that maker about continued use.
5. **Give an unresolved pan a destination.** If one mild supported attempt fails, the surface cannot be assessed, or structure is damaged, pause further cooking and ask the maker or a professional, or replace and recycle under local rules. Do not mix acids, bleach, ammonia, or mystery powders, or turn cleaning into harsh chemical or gas/electrical repair.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You can explain the observed difference between char and pan damage, have made one small maker-supported attempt, and reached “clean and intact,” “hold pending advice,” or “replace.” Forcing off every black patch is not the only successful outcome.

- **The pan is still hot and you want to add water:** Stop and cool it under maker directions to avoid burns and thermal shock.
- **Char does not move and scrubbing gets harsher:** Stop increasing force and seek this model's stubborn-residue guidance or maker help.
- **Enamel has a chip to bare metal:** Stop use and follow this maker's stop-use or support guidance; oiling over the gap does not repair it.
- **A nonstick pan has scratches:** Do not claim universal toxicity. Stop abrasive cleaning and ask the maker about that collection's continued use or replacement.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers cooled ordinary cookware, not an active fire, canning vessel, pressure-cooker internals, food-safety certification, or appliance repair. Coatings differ by maker and collection, changing the cleaning limit. Boiling baking soda in every pan is not a universal method. For limited grip, work gently in a stable sink and ask trusted help for a heavy pan. Use bright light or magnified photos when needed rather than feeling an unknown chip with bare fingers. A held pan reduces available cookware for now.
