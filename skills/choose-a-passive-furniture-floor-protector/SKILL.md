## 中文

当普通家具的脚、底座或小型摆件要接触已知表面，而你想用**不承担承重稳定、防倾倒或防护作用的被动保护垫**减少日常划痕时加载。结果是选择与实际表面和接触点相容的垫，或明确暂不贴。这里不修家具腿、不让不稳的物品变安全，也不教移动重家具。

### 准备与输入

确认表面种类与厂商护理要求、家具脚底实际形状/尺寸、物件会固定放置还是日常轻微移动、现有垫是否已经磨薄或积沙。查看候选保护垫的适用表面、接触尺寸、固定方式和更换说明。租住或共用地面如有特定要求先核对；不把某种地板品牌的推荐当所有材质的规则。

### 执行

1. **先分辨保护与结构功能。** 只有为普通刮擦接触而设的被动垫才进本流程。若该脚本来不稳、部件断裂、家具需要防倾倒件、脚轮/防滑装置承担安全功能，停止并按准确厂家或专业路线处理，不能用软垫掩盖。
2. **匹配地面和动作。** 按实际地面厂商说明判断能否使用 felt 等材质，以及物件是否会在表面轻微移动。厂商说明未知或接触后可能有粘胶/染色问题时先试查资料或问供应方，不凭“软的总没事”。
3. **匹配整个脚底。** 选择能覆盖接触点、不会明显伸出刮到旁边、也不会小到让硬边直接压地的形状和尺寸。多个脚需要各自有一致的可用接触；不以叠几层垫来调整严重歪斜或承担结构支撑。
4. **核对安装可行性。** 自粘、套入或其他形式按其说明选；需要钉入、钻孔、化学粘接或把重物危险翻转才可装的产品不因名称叫“脚垫”就进入这份低风险 Skill。
5. **写选择或停点。** 记“家具接触点 → 地面与厂家依据 → 垫的材质/尺寸/安装方式 → 尚需确认”。找不到两端都支持的选择就先保持现状并保护地面使用方式，不宣称某垫万能。

### 完成与停止

你有一个基于家具脚、地面说明和垫产品资料的普通被动保护选择，或清楚知道为何暂不安装。选择不证明垫能防滑、防倾倒、修断腿或允许拖动重家具；这些条件存在时停用这条路径。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只按颜色/价格选了很小一片：** 重新看实际脚底和垫覆盖范围，避免硬边仍直接接地。
- **地板种类不确定：** 查准确地面材料与护理说明，未知就不保证某材质适用。
- **旧垫磨薄或夹着砂粒：** 先记录需要清洁/更换，不能靠多贴一层盖住磨损与脏物。
- **家具已经晃或腿裂：** 退出脚垫选择，找合适维修或停用路径；脚垫不是结构垫片。
- **需要把重物翻过来才贴得到：** 暂不安装，向有能力的人或服务方核对安全办法。

### 假设、替代与副作用

只处理普通表面防刮的消耗性保护物；地区、地板涂层和品牌不同，没有通用“所有地面都用 felt”的保证。行动、视力或握持不便时可请可信者测量和读回资料，不需要本人抬家具。副作用是“随便贴一块”先变成一项有依据的选择。

## English

Load this Skill when an ordinary furniture foot, base or small object will touch a known surface and you want a **passive pad that is not relied on for structural support, anti-tip protection or safety** to reduce everyday scratching. Finish with a pad supported by the actual surface and contact details, or a clear decision not to attach one yet. This does not repair a leg, make an unstable object safe or teach heavy furniture moving.

### Preparation and inputs

Identify the surface material and maker care guidance, the real shape and size of the furniture contact, whether the object stays still or moves lightly in normal use, and whether an existing pad is thin or gritty. Read the proposed protector's surface suitability, contact size, attachment and replacement guidance. Check local rental or shared-space requirements when applicable. One flooring maker's recommendation is not a rule for every material.

### Execution

1. **Separate surface protection from structure.** Only passive pads for ordinary scratch contact enter this flow. If a leg is unstable or broken, furniture needs an anti-tip device, or a caster or anti-slip part carries a safety function, stop and use the exact maker or qualified route. A soft pad cannot hide those failures.
2. **Match floor and movement.** Use the real floor maker's guidance on whether felt or another material is suitable and whether light movement is expected. If its care advice is unknown or adhesive or colour transfer may matter, find the actual information or ask the supplier. “Soft” alone is not proof.
3. **Match the whole contact.** Choose a shape and size that covers the foot without an edge protruding to scrape nearby material or leaving hard furniture edges against the floor. Each of several feet needs a usable contact. Do not stack pads to correct major wobble or act as structural support.
4. **Check whether attachment is feasible.** Select self-stick, slip-on or another form under its own instructions. A product requiring nails, drilling, chemical bonding or a dangerous flip of heavy furniture does not become low-risk merely because it is called a floor pad.
5. **Record choice or stop.** Write “contact → floor and maker evidence → pad material/size/attachment → remaining unknown.” If no choice is supported on both sides, leave installation pending and avoid claiming a universal pad.

### Success and stop

You have an ordinary passive protection choice grounded in the foot, floor advice and pad product, or a clear reason to defer. It does not prove slip prevention, anti-tip safety, repair of a broken leg or permission to drag heavy furniture. Exit this route when any of those conditions matter.

### Common errors and recovery

- **A tiny pad was chosen for colour or price:** Recheck the real contact and coverage so a hard edge does not touch the floor.
- **The floor material is unknown:** Find the accurate material and care guide; do not promise a pad is suitable yet.
- **The old pad is thin or full of grit:** Plan cleaning or replacement. Another layer does not remove dirt or restore worn contact.
- **The item wobbles or a leg is cracked:** Leave pad selection for a real repair or stop-use route. A pad is not a structural shim.
- **Access requires flipping a heavy item:** Do not install yet. Ask capable help or the service provider for a safe method.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is consumable ordinary scratch protection. Region, floor coating and brands differ, so no promise says all surfaces should use felt. A trusted person can measure and read details when mobility, sight or grip is limited; you need not lift furniture. “Stick anything underneath” becomes a supported choice.
