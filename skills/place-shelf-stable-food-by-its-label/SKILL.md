## 中文

当你要为**未开封**的普通罐装食品、干货或其他包装说明允许常温保存的食品安排储位，而现有橱柜可能潮湿、受热、混放清洁剂或难以取用时，加载这份 Skill。它要产出一处符合**这件食品包装要求**的位置，而不是给所有食品套一个“放柜里就行”的默认值。一次购物袋入户的整体流程已有[买菜回家后收纳](../put-away-groceries/SKILL.md)；开封后的要求、容器选择和日期轮换分别由其他任务处理。

### 准备与输入

逐件看包装是否完整、是否已开封、储存方式、任何温度/避光/防潮或开封后说明。字太小时拍清晰照片放大、使用已有读屏方式，或请可信的人读出原文；不要把“看不清”改写成“没有限制”。若要求冷藏/冷冻，或已经开封而标签改要求，不把它列为常温候选。

列出可用的架层、柜子或抽屉，检查实际有无漏水、潮气、明显热源、清洁剂和虫害迹象，以及你能否安全取用。包装破损、受潮或有虫害迹象的食品先隔离待核，不要因换到干柜就认为过去的损害消失。

### 执行

1. **先按标签分流。** 只把明确允许目前状态下常温保存的食品留在本流程。需要冷藏、冷冻、开封后另有要求、或说明互相矛盾的，先按相应标签与当地权威核实，不靠食品类别猜。罐头、谷物和土豆可以是常温食品的例子，但它们不是所有产品的通行证。
2. **验证候选位置。** 找清洁、干燥、不受不适当热源影响、符合该包装其他条件的位置。食物与清洁剂及其他化学品分开；不把食品长期放在地上。一个柜子关得上门，不代表里面没有潮气或温度问题。
3. **检查包装和空间是否相配。** 原包装完整且能满足要求时，保留它和上面的品名、配料、保存说明及日期信息。若包装无法提供适当保护，先按食品与容器的专门说明决定能否换装；不把无标签的罐子变成新的权威来源。重物放在你能安全承载和取用的位置，避免压坏较轻包装。
4. **放好并留可识别入口。** 让品名和关键保存条件能在取用时看见，必要时用纸条或可及标签标出位置。相同食品可以放在一起，但不要把不同批次或开封状态混成无法辨认的一包；使用顺序另按真实日期和状态判断。
5. **对没有合格位置的食品停止宣称已储存。** 换一个实际符合标签的地点；仍找不到时，记下食品和缺少的条件，先不追加同类采购，并向生产商或当地可靠指导确认可行方案。不要用“反正还没开封”覆盖不合适的储存环境。

### 完成条件

每件进入本流程的食品都有一个你能找到、能安全拿取且符合其当前包装要求的常温位置；包装信息可回读，食品与清洁剂分开，储位无已知潮湿或不适当热源。没有合格位置或包装条件不明的食品被单独标为待核，不能在完成清单里打勾。

### 常见报错与补救

- **柜子看着干，底板却潮：** 不继续把干货放进去；找干燥替代位置。已经受潮的包装单独评估，不靠擦干外面恢复可信保存史。
- **只剩水槽下或清洁剂旁的空位：** 找不与化学品混放、且符合标签的另一处。空间不足是结果，不是让食物和洗涤剂同居的理由。
- **包装被打开或说明写“开封后冷藏”：** 停止本未开封流程，按产品当前状态的说明处理；不要沿用开封前储位。
- **看见孔洞、虫害或异常包装：** 隔离受影响食品，按当地可靠食品安全指引决定是否保留；储位清洁和虫害处理交给相应流程。
- **柜子太高或标签看不清：** 换可及架层、用大字/读屏标识或请人协助拿取；不要求爬高、搬重物或凭记忆辨认无标签食品。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

“常温”不是一个全球固定的温度或所有食物的共用保存期限。地区气候、房间湿度、设备与生产商包装不同；以这件食品的标签和适用当地指导为准。本篇只安排空间，不判断日期意义、受潮食品是否安全、开封后容器或化学清洁操作。

副作用是橱柜可能多一张位置记录，少一个“上次买的到底放哪儿”的谜题。若住房条件限制了合格储位，停止追加采购可能比增加收纳盒更诚实。

## English

Load this Skill when **unopened** ordinary canned food, dry goods, or another product labelled for room-temperature storage needs a place, and the available cupboards may be damp, hot, mixed with cleaners, or hard to access. The result is a location that meets **this product's package instructions**, not a universal “put it in a cupboard” rule. [Put Away Groceries](../put-away-groceries/SKILL.md) handles an entire incoming trip. After-opening rules, container choice, and date rotation are separate jobs.

### Preparation and inputs

For each item, check package integrity, whether it has been opened, storage instructions, and any temperature, light, moisture, or after-opening conditions. Enlarge a clear photo, use your existing reading aid, or ask someone you trust to read the wording if the print is small. “I cannot read it” does not mean “there are no restrictions.” Do not treat an item requiring chilling/freezing, or an opened item with changed instructions, as an ambient-storage candidate.

List shelves, cupboards, and drawers you could use. Check for leaks, dampness, inappropriate heat, cleaning chemicals, or signs of pests, and whether you can reach each space safely. Isolate a damaged, damp, or pest-affected package for separate judgment; moving it to a dry cupboard cannot undo earlier damage.

### Execution

1. **Sort by the current label first.** Keep only food clearly allowed at room temperature in its present unopened state. Check the applicable label and local guidance for food that needs chilling/freezing, has changed instructions after opening, or has contradictory wording. Cans, cereals, and potatoes are examples of possible ambient foods, not permission slips for every product.
2. **Verify a candidate location.** Find a clean, dry place without unsuitable heat that meets any other packaging condition. Separate food from cleaners and other chemicals; do not keep it on the floor. A cupboard door that closes does not prove the space is dry or cool enough.
3. **Match package and space.** Keep intact original packaging when it meets the need, along with its product name, ingredients, storage directions, and date information. If packaging cannot protect the food, use the separate food/container guidance before repacking; an unlabelled jar is not a new source of truth. Place heavy items where you can lift them safely without crushing lighter packages.
4. **Leave an identifiable route back.** Make the product name and important storage conditions visible when you retrieve it; add a simple paper or accessible location label if needed. Similar foods may sit together, but do not merge different batches or opening states into an unidentified pack. Use order is decided separately from actual dates and condition.
5. **Stop claiming storage when no location fits.** Look for another place that actually meets the label. If none exists, record the item and missing condition, avoid buying more of the same, and check with the producer or applicable local guidance for a workable option. “Still unopened” does not override an unsuitable environment.

### Success

Every item in this flow has a room-temperature location you can find and use safely that meets its current package requirements. You can reread its information, it is separate from cleaners, and the place has no known dampness or unsuitable heat. An item without a suitable location or clear package conditions remains a separate open question, not a completed checkbox.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The cupboard looks dry but its base is damp:** Do not leave dry goods there. Find a dry alternative. Assess any already damp package separately; wiping its outside cannot restore its storage history.
- **Only space under the sink or beside cleaners remains:** Find another location that is separate from chemicals and meets the label. Lack of space is a result, not a reason to co-store food and detergent.
- **The pack was opened or says “refrigerate after opening”:** Stop this unopened-item flow and follow its current-state instructions. Do not reuse the pre-opening location by habit.
- **You see holes, pests, or unusual packaging:** Isolate affected food and use applicable official food-safety guidance to decide whether to keep it. Cleaning the storage area and dealing with pests follow their own processes.
- **The shelf is too high or the label cannot be read:** Use a reachable shelf, large or screen-readable labels, or help from someone you trust. Climbing, lifting unsafe weights, or guessing an unlabelled product is not required.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

“Room temperature” is not one worldwide number or a common storage lifetime for all foods. Climate, indoor humidity, equipment, and manufacturers' packs differ. Follow this item's label and applicable local guidance. This Skill assigns space; it does not interpret dates, certify damp food, choose after-opening containers, or teach chemical cleaning.

Your cupboard may gain one location note and lose one “where did that thing go?” mystery. If your home cannot provide a suitable storage spot, pausing additional purchases may be more honest than buying another organizer.
