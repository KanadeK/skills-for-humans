## 中文

当你已经选好少量照片，想知道别人实际看到了什么时，用这份 Skill 在**愿意参加的小组**前按顺序展示，并收集一个具体观察。完成不是观众答应喜欢你的照片，而是展示在约定边界内发生，你能分清自己的意图与至少一条真实反馈。选片本身和公开发布是另两个任务。

### 准备与输入

你应已有 2–4 张排好顺序的照片及一句“我想表达____”。选 2–4 位愿意看的人，约定短时间、观看方式与是否可以评论；不把路过者拉成被迫观众。逐张确认你有权向这组人展示，图中的可识别人员、私密地点或敏感细节符合相关人的同意与现场要求；不确定就撤下一张或改用安全图。屏幕分享前关闭通知和其他窗口，只显示选定内容；纸样则遮住背面私人信息。任何人不愿被录音、截图或转发时，不做这些动作。本篇不要求观众把照片带走。

### 执行

1. **说明边界。** 开始时说“我想用几分钟给大家按顺序看这几张，想听你们看到的主题；可以不评论，也请不要转发。”确认大家仍愿意看。有人拒绝就缩小小组或另约，不把沉默当同意。
2. **按原序展示。** 每张留一点安静观看时间，给出必要背景而不先讲尽所有解释。屏幕、投影或纸样都可；确保每位参加者能看清或获得你同意提供的简短口述描述。无法显示某张就跳过并说明，不假装大家已看见。
3. **问一个容易回答的问题。** 例如“看完后，你最先注意到什么变化？”或“哪一张让你理解这组的主题？”让人回答具体观察，不要求“你喜欢吗”式的赞美投票。对方不愿评价就接受；别争辩其观察与原意不同。
4. **分开记意图与反馈。** 写两行：“我原想表达____；有人实际注意到____”。若没人愿说，记录“已展示，无口头反馈”，不编造肯定评价。需要改动时记一处可能调整，不在现场强迫小组替你重排整组。
5. **收起并结束。** 按约定关屏、收回纸样或确认共享已停止；不自动发送文件、留下载权限或上传。感谢参与者并在约定时间结束。后续是否修改或发布，再另行决定。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

至少两位愿意观看的人看过你有权展示的短序列，展示没有越过约定边界；你记录自己的意图及实际反馈状态（包括“无人评论”）。如果没人参加或内容未经许可，本篇尚未完成，不靠自己模拟观众。

- **有人不愿看或中途离开：** 尊重并结束其参与；不足两位时另约，不追着求评价。
- **分享画面露出通知或别的文件：** 立即停止共享，关闭或遮住私人内容，再确认愿意继续才恢复。
- **观众只说“挺好”：** 温和追问一处具体看到的内容；不愿细说就如实记录这条泛泛反馈。
- **反馈与原意相反：** 记录差异，之后自己判断是否改选片，不要求观众收回观察。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

小组规模是本篇的结束边界，不是公开演讲标准；2–4 位愿意的观看者即可，线下或受控线上均可。视力、听力或设备条件不同时可用大图、纸样、口述说明或文字反馈，但每人能理解展示内容才算看过。语言和文化习惯会影响反馈方式，允许不评论。照片、讨论和参与者身份保持在约定范围；若内容涉及他人权利，本篇不提供法律结论。一次展示可能带来不同解释，而非掌声。

## English

Use this Skill when you have chosen a few photos and want to learn what other people actually see. Show the ordered set to a **willing small group** and collect one concrete observation. Success is not making viewers like your work; it is a presentation within agreed boundaries and an honest distinction between your intention and real feedback. Selecting photos and public publishing are separate tasks.

### Preparation and inputs

Have 2–4 photos already ordered and one sentence, “I want to show ____.” Invite 2–4 people who want to look and agree on a short time, viewing method, and whether comments are welcome. Passers-by are not a captive audience. Check each frame is yours to show to this group and that identifiable people, private places, or sensitive details meet relevant consent and site conditions; remove a doubtful frame or use a safe one. Before screen sharing, close notifications and other windows so only selected content is visible. Cover private information on the back of paper proofs. Do not record, screenshot, or forward participants or their comments without agreement. Viewers need not take files away.

### Execution

1. **State the boundary.** Say, “I would like to show these in order for a few minutes and hear what theme you notice. You can decline to comment; please do not forward them.” Confirm people still want to see them. If someone declines, use a smaller willing group or reschedule; silence is not consent.
2. **Show in the chosen order.** Allow a quiet look at each image and give necessary context without explaining every interpretation first. Use a screen, projection, or paper proofs. Make sure each participant can see or receive a short accessible description you agree to provide. If a frame cannot be shown, skip it openly rather than pretend everyone saw it.
3. **Ask one answerable question.** For example, “What change did you notice first?” or “Which frame made the theme clear?” Invite a specific observation, not a vote of praise. Accept that someone may not want to evaluate. Do not argue if their reading differs from your intention.
4. **Record intention and response separately.** Write two lines: “I meant to show ____; someone noticed ____.” If nobody comments, record “shown; no spoken feedback” rather than inventing approval. Note one possible adjustment if useful, without demanding that the group rearrange your set on the spot.
5. **Put it away and finish.** Stop sharing, collect paper proofs, or confirm the shared screen has closed. Do not automatically send files, leave download access, or upload them. Thank participants and finish at the agreed time. Decide later whether to revise or publish.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

At least two willing viewers have seen your short sequence that you may show; the display stayed within agreed boundaries. You recorded your intention and the actual feedback state, including “no comment.” If nobody attends or the content lacks permission, this Skill is incomplete; you cannot simulate an audience yourself.

- **Someone declines or leaves:** Respect that and end their participation. If fewer than two remain, arrange another time instead of pursuing them for feedback.
- **Notifications or other files appear in sharing:** Stop immediately, hide the private content, and resume only if participants still agree.
- **A viewer says only “nice”:** Gently ask for one thing they noticed. If they decline, record the general comment as it is.
- **Feedback conflicts with your intention:** Note the difference and decide later whether the sequence should change. Do not ask the viewer to retract an observation.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Group size is a finish boundary here, not a public-speaking standard. Two to four willing viewers can join in person or through controlled online viewing. Use large images, paper proofs, spoken description, or written feedback for sight, hearing, and device needs; each participant must understand what was shown. Language and culture affect how people respond, and no comment is acceptable. Keep images, discussion, and identities inside the agreed audience. This Skill offers no legal verdict on others' rights. A viewing may yield another interpretation rather than applause.
