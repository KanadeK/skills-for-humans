## 中文

当你已经选好几道普通菜，也知道每道菜可用的做法，却需要在有限火口、锅具和注意力下让它们合理上桌时，用这份 Skill 写**先后顺序卡**。它只在开始烹调前排工作；菜单选择、份量、具体加热技法、烹调中事故另有任务。

### 准备与输入

给每道菜找出可信配方或产品说明，抄下必要的准备、主动加热、可等待阶段、临上桌步骤和适用的熟度检查，不凭菜名猜时间。确认今天的开饭目标、锅/烤箱/火口能否同时使用、容器和食物温度可否按食品及本地指导安全等待，以及你能同时照看的任务数。可用纸卡、可访问的计时器、震动或语音提示；看不清配方或听不到铃声时先换一种可用的提醒方式。

### 执行

1. **把每道菜拆成少数节点。** 记“前置准备 → 加热/等待 → 熟度检查 → 装盘”。标出必须先完成的步骤，例如安全解冻、洗切、预热，以及生食处理与即食食物之间需要的清洁。不要把模糊的“做菜”当成一个十分钟方块。
2. **给节点标资源和注意力。** 每一步占哪个锅、火口或烤箱，需不需要持续看守；同一器具不能在两道菜的同一时段上同时出现。高热、刀具或需即时翻看的动作也不能超过你实际能安全照看的数量。
3. **从开饭时间倒排不易等待的菜。** 用各自可信的时间范围和完成信号，先安排最后必须接近上桌才做的步骤，再把长时间、低注意力且按说明可等待的步骤放前面。允许一道菜先好、另一道后好，前提是先好的有**可验证的安全保温、冷藏或立即食用路径**；室温摆着等不等于保温。
4. **填空档，不叠危险任务。** 在真正空出的时间里安排切配、清洗、摆盘和下一道预备。生动物性食物与即食食物的工具和表面分开或按适用指导彻底处理后再使用。安排必要的手部清洗和短暂停顿，不把所有空档视为可无缝执行。
5. **为每道菜写一个检查点。** 卡上列预计开始、器具、何时检查以及“完成靠什么观察或测量”。定时器提示你去检查，不能替代该食品的熟度证据。需要保持热食或冷食时，先确认设备与方法真能达到当地/商品要求；没有就改顺序，不提前做它。
6. **跑一次冲突检查。** 按时间读整张卡：有没有同一锅双占、两个必须盯住的步骤撞车、未熟食物被安排“保温”、冷食离开可靠低温太久？有冲突先移动可移动步骤、换适用的做法或减少菜数；无法解决就把目标标为不可行并在开火前改菜单。

### 成功条件

你拿到一张从第一步到开饭的可读顺序卡：每道菜的开始、共享器具、检查点和允许等待的条件清楚，没有把同一资源或你的注意力用两次，先完成的食物有真实的安全去向。若时间与设备不可能兼容，明确改菜单或开饭时间也是完成规划。

### 常见报错与补救

- **同一口锅同时写两道菜：** 把其中一道前置或后移，或选已核实适用的另一做法；不能靠“手快”让资源复制。
- **第一道菜早熟却无保温方案：** 调整其开做时间；热得慢慢凉下去不是已确认的保存方式。已经开始烹调时按实际食品安全流程处理。
- **计时结束就宣布熟了：** 把计时器改成提醒检查，按该食品和当地指导核对熟度，不凭颜色或猜测替代。
- **顺序卡一变就整张失效：** 找出从这一节点到上桌的受影响步骤，重新检查共享资源与等待条件；烹调中的中断与事故按相应恢复任务处理。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这适用于普通家常几道菜，不提供万能温度、火候或医疗饮食方案。不同设备、配方、海拔与个人能力改变实际时间；以产品说明、可信做法和当地食品安全指导为准。站立、切配、听铃或同时注意多处有困难时，选择少菜、坐姿准备、视觉/震动提醒或可信协助；计划不能要求你越过身体限制。现实副作用是少一道菜、晚一点开饭，或多一张能避免临场慌乱的卡。

## English

Use this Skill when several ordinary dishes and their usable methods are already chosen, but burners, pans, and attention must be scheduled so the meal can be served. Make an **order-of-work card** before cooking. Menu choice, amounts, heating techniques, and problems after cooking starts belong to separate tasks.

### Preparation and inputs

Use a trustworthy recipe or product direction for each dish. Record required preparation, active heating, waiting stages, final steps, and appropriate doneness checks rather than guessing time from a dish name. Confirm serving time, which pans, burners, and oven can actually run together, whether food can wait under applicable local and product safety conditions, and how many tasks you can watch safely. Use paper or accessible timers with visual, vibration, or speech cues. If print or alarms are inaccessible, choose another usable cue before relying on them.

### Execution

1. **Break dishes into a few nodes.** Write “prerequisites → heat or waiting → doneness check → serve.” Mark true dependencies such as safe thawing, washing and cutting, preheating, and cleaning between raw-food and ready-to-eat work. “Cook the dish” is not a single reliable ten-minute block.
2. **Assign equipment and attention.** Name the pan, burner, or oven for each node and whether it needs continuous observation. One tool cannot be in two simultaneous dishes. Nor can high heat, knives, or frequently checked tasks exceed the amount you can safely watch.
3. **Work backward from serving for dishes that cannot wait.** Use each recipe's credible time range and finish cue. Place last-minute steps near serving, then start long, lower-attention steps earlier where their directions allow waiting. One dish may finish before another only if it has a **verified safe hot-holding, chilling, or immediate-use route**. A room-temperature counter is not hot holding.
4. **Fill real gaps without stacking hazardous work.** Use available time for cutting, washing, plating, and the next dish's preparation. Keep raw animal food and ready-to-eat food tools separate, or clean them under applicable guidance before reuse. Allow for handwashing and short pauses rather than making every gap perfectly full.
5. **Put a check on every dish.** Record planned start, equipment, when to inspect it, and what observation or measurement establishes completion. A timer calls you to check; it does not prove safe doneness. If you need to hold hot or cold food, confirm the equipment and method meet the food and local requirement. Otherwise start that dish later.
6. **Run a conflict check.** Read the whole card in time order. Is one pan double-booked? Do two steps demand full attention at once? Is undercooked food assigned to “warming”? Is cold food outside reliable cooling too long? Move flexible steps, choose another supported method, or reduce dishes. If no safe order exists, mark the plan infeasible and change the menu before heating begins.

### Success

You have a readable card from the first step to serving. Each dish's start, shared equipment, check, and conditions for waiting are explicit. No resource or part of your attention is booked twice, and early-finished food has a real safe destination. Changing the menu or serving time because the inputs cannot coexist is also a complete planning result.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Two dishes occupy one pan at once:** Move one earlier or later, or choose a verified suitable method. Fast hands do not clone equipment.
- **The first dish finishes early without a holding route:** Start it later. Cooling on the counter is not an established storage method. Once cooking has begun, follow the actual food-safety workflow.
- **The timer goes off, so you declare it cooked:** Use the timer to prompt a check. Verify doneness for the food and location instead of relying on colour or a guess.
- **One change invalidates the card:** Recheck downstream steps sharing equipment or waiting conditions. Hand off an actual interruption or failure to the relevant recovery task.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is for a few ordinary home dishes, not universal temperatures, cooking techniques, or medical diets. Appliance, recipe, altitude, and personal ability change time; follow product directions, trusted methods, and local food-safety guidance. If standing, cutting, hearing alarms, or dividing attention is difficult, choose fewer dishes, seated preparation, visual or vibrating cues, or trusted help. The plan must not demand you exceed your body. You may serve later, drop a dish, or gain one card that prevents an avoidable scramble.
