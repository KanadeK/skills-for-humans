## 中文

当你参加一次普通共同用餐，食物快要分到你面前，而你需要确认一道菜、份量或分餐方式时，加载这份 Skill。目标是**清楚表达你能接受的选择或停止取用**，不把主人或同桌人的烹饪能力变成现场考试。主人提前提供选择属于另一任务；本篇不诊断过敏、不制定医疗饮食，也不凭别人一句“应该没事”保证食品安全。

### 准备与输入

先确定这次真正会改变你决定的信息：哪道菜、是否能接受某种食材、想要多少、能否自己盛、是否需要分开取用。普通口味偏好可以简短说；涉及你必须避免的食物或已知限制时，只问能改变取用决定的成分与处理信息，不要求大家听一段私人病史。若此信息无人能可靠确认，你可以不取那道菜。

留意谁实际知道菜或包装的情况：做菜者、拿出包装的人或可读的原标签。旁观者的猜测不能替这份信息。没有安全可用的选择时，把“不吃这一项”视为有效结果，而不是要对方临时重做。

### 执行

1. **在盛到盘里前开口。** “这道里有我需要避开的某样东西吗？”“我先要一小份，可以吗？”或“我能自己盛吗？”问题越靠近这次动作越容易回答。不要等盘子堆满后再让所有人倒推你的需求。
2. **区分偏好与必须确认的限制。** 喜欢清淡、小份或不想喝酒，可以直接说选择，不需辩论理由。若食品成分、交叉接触或保存情况对你有实际安全影响，而知情者不能确认，不把猜测升级为保证；跳过这道或使用你自己能确认的方案。
3. **接受现场真实选项。** 能少盛就少盛；不能分餐时可先不取、换已有可行的菜，或说明你这次不参与这道菜。主人不必因为你开口就能立即改变菜谱，与你同桌的人也不必做同一个选择。
4. **被劝多吃时重申一次。** “谢谢，我这份够了”或“这道我不吃，但我会一起坐着聊”。不反复解释身体或文化原因，也不为了结束劝说吃下你已明确拒绝的东西。
5. **吃饭中决定有变化时及时说。** 已盛的份量吃不完、想先停或发现信息与之前不符，停止继续取用并告诉上菜者要怎么处理你自己的那份。剩余食品的保存和安全判断另按对应流程，不用礼貌代替温度或时间依据。

### 完成条件

你已在相关食物进入盘子前表达需要的成分、份量或分餐信息，并选择“取这一份 / 换现成选项 / 不取”。无法核实的安全条件被保留为未知，没有靠礼貌吞下。继续与人同桌是可选的社交结果，不以主人必须改菜或同桌人理解所有原因作为完成条件。

### 常见报错与补救

- **随口说“什么都行”后发现不行：** 尽快更正为这次可执行的选择；一句过早的客套不剥夺你停止取用的权利。
- **别人替你说“他肯定能吃”：** 由你本人或你认可的联系人确认；不让旁人猜测代替实际限制。
- **成分或处理方式答不清：** 不继续试吃来验证；跳过这道或选有可靠信息的已有食物。
- **一大份已经盛到盘里：** 及时说“我只能吃这么多”，询问主人如何处理未动部分，不要为了证明礼貌强迫自己吃完。
- **拒绝后仍被反复劝：** 重申一次具体选择，然后转回谈话或其他活动；普通共同用餐不要求你参加每道菜。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这份 Skill 假设一次自愿的普通聚餐；宗教、饮食习惯、酒精、份量与上菜方式没有统一文化默认值。听觉、语言、握持或取餐不便时，可先发短消息、指向明确选项，或请你信任的人按你的原话传达。具体过敏、医疗饮食与食品安全要求不能由本篇社交建议替代；必要时遵循你已有的专业安排并停止不明食品。

副作用是你可能少吃一份不想要的东西，饭桌也许不会因此自动陷入沉默。

## English

Load this Skill at an ordinary shared meal just before food is served when you need to check a dish, amount, or serving method. Your result is **a clear choice you can accept, or a stop before taking it**. This is not an on-the-spot exam for the cook. The host's offer is a separate task. This Skill does not diagnose allergies, design a medical diet, or turn someone's “probably fine” into a food-safety guarantee.

### Preparation and inputs

Identify only what changes your decision this time: the dish, an ingredient you may not have, your portion, whether you can serve yourself, or whether a separate serving method matters. Ordinary tastes can be stated briefly. For a food you must avoid or another known limit, ask only for ingredient and handling facts that affect taking it; no one needs a public medical history. If no one can verify the needed facts, you may skip that dish.

Find who actually knows the dish or pack: the cook, the person with its packaging, or a readable original label. A bystander's guess is not the same information. No safe workable option can mean not taking this dish, without demanding an instant remake.

### Execution

1. **Speak before it reaches your plate.** “Does this have an ingredient I need to avoid?” “Could I start with a small portion?” or “May I serve myself?” A question tied to the next action is easier to answer than one after a full plate is served.
2. **Separate preference from a condition you must verify.** Say that you prefer mild food, a small serving, or no alcohol without debating why. If ingredients, cross-contact, or storage affect your safety and the knowledgeable person cannot confirm them, do not promote a guess into assurance. Skip that dish or use an option you can verify yourself.
3. **Use the options actually present.** Take less if possible. If separate serving is not possible, pass, choose another available dish, or say you are not taking this one. Speaking up does not require the host to rewrite the recipe immediately, and others need not make your choice.
4. **Restate once if pressed.** “Thank you, this is enough,” or “I won't have that dish, but I'll stay and talk.” You need not repeatedly explain bodily or cultural reasons or eat what you already declined to end persuasion.
5. **Update a changed decision during the meal.** If you cannot finish, need to stop, or discover facts different from what you were told, stop taking more and ask the server how to handle your portion. Storage and safety of remaining food follow their own process; politeness does not replace time or temperature evidence.

### Success

Before taking the relevant food, you stated the ingredient, portion, or serving fact you needed and chose “take this / choose another available option / pass.” An unverifiable safety condition stayed unknown rather than being swallowed for politeness. Continuing to sit with others is optional; the cook need not remake a dish or understand every private reason for the task to be complete.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You said “anything is fine” and find it is not:** Correct it as soon as possible with a workable choice. One premature pleasantry does not remove your ability to stop.
- **Someone else says “they can definitely eat it”:** Confirm through yourself or a contact you trust to speak for you. A bystander's guess cannot define your actual limit.
- **Ingredients or handling are unclear:** Do not taste-test to find out. Skip it or choose existing food with reliable information.
- **A large portion is already on the plate:** Say what you can eat and ask the host what to do with any untouched part. You need not force yourself to finish as proof of gratitude.
- **You are pressed after declining:** Restate your specific choice once, then return to the conversation or another activity. An ordinary shared meal does not require joining every dish.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This covers a voluntary ordinary meal. Religion, diet, alcohol, portions, and serving style have no universal cultural default. If hearing, language, grip, or serving access is difficult, send a short message first, point to a clear option, or ask someone you trust to repeat your words. Social advice here cannot replace allergy, medical-diet, or food-safety requirements. Follow any existing professional plan and stop on unknown food when needed.

You may eat one less unwanted portion. The table is unlikely to fall silent because of it.
