## 中文

当普通可水洗衣物沾到**来源明确的普通泥土**，尚有泥块或颗粒时，加载这份 Skill。与新鲜饮料要尽早吸去液体不同，这里先让普通泥土自然干，再轻去固体，随后按衣标处理留下的痕迹。洪水、污水、动物排泄物、工业场地或化学污染不是普通泥；衣物若可能受这些污染，停止本篇并按适当卫生/专业指导处理。

### 准备与输入

先读衣标是否可水洗，核面料、颜色、装饰和是否允许轻刷/预处理；贵重、易损、专业护理或标签不可读就暂停。找能承接落下泥土的位置、软而干净的刷具或手能安全操作的钝边、产品标签允许的洗衣剂。先把这件和干净衣物分开，避免泥粒到处转移；确认污物真是普通泥，不从颜色推断“只是土”。

### 执行

1. **先让普通泥自然干。** 放在不会污染食物或其他衣物、能通风且符合衣标的地方等待，不用烘干机或高热“加速”。泥仍湿时不要往纤维里揉，也别把大泥块直接放进整桶衣物。
2. **轻去可松脱的固体。** 泥干后在可收拾的位置轻敲/用软刷把表面颗粒从衣物上带走；从边缘小范围试，若面料起毛、掉色或装饰松动就停。不能把泥刷掉的纤维部分不靠硬刮解决。
3. **核衣物与产品双重相容。** 对留下的普通泥痕，选衣标和产品都允许的液体洗衣剂或预处理品，按产品说明先在隐藏处测损伤/色变，再按其量和方式处理。不要把普通泥等同血、蛋白、油或锈迹，也不自行混漂白剂、溶剂或强酸碱。
4. **按衣标洗并湿态检查。** 与兼容衣物成负载，按衣标和机器/手洗说明清洗；洗后核原泥点位置是否还有颗粒或痕迹。若仍有明显痕迹，先不热干，按允许方式再处理或请专业护理，别靠烘干“定案”。
5. **确认可继续的边界。** 表面固体已去、衣物仍完整、没有颜色变化且污渍状态可核时，才进入后续标签干燥流程；若污物来源后来发现不普通，立即停止混洗/处理并按实际污染类型求助。

### 完成、常见问题与补救

你已在洗前去掉可松脱的普通泥固体，洗后核过原位置而未损伤面料；仍有痕迹则明确标“未去净、先不热干”。泥已经干了却还整块留在衣服上进机器，不算完成洗前处理。

- **泥还湿就使劲揉：** 停止，让普通泥自然干，再只去松动固体。
- **软刷试处起毛/掉色：** 立刻停刷，按衣标/制造商说明或专业护理处理。
- **泥混油、排泄物或未知污染：** 不走普通泥路径，隔离并核适当处理方式。
- **洗后还有印：** 不先烘干；核是残土、染色还是纤维变化，按允许方法再处理。
- **站立、握力或感官条件限制：** 用桌面高度、可握工具或现场协助；不能确定污物来源或面料反应时停。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这是日常路面/普通土地的已知泥污，不是灾后或专业工作污染。不同土壤可能有染色成分，干刷只能去颗粒，不保证留下的印记一次洗净。衣标和清洁品说明决定能否水洗、刷洗与用何种产品；本篇不提供跨面料的温度、浸泡时间或漂白配方。

## English

Load this Skill for **known ordinary mud** on washable clothing when clumps or grains still sit on the fabric. Unlike a fresh drink that should be blotted, ordinary mud is first allowed to dry naturally, then loose solids are lifted before a label-permitted wash. Floodwater, sewage, animal waste, industrial ground, or chemical contamination are not ordinary mud. Stop here and use appropriate health or professional guidance if those may be involved.

### Preparation and inputs

Read the care label for water washing, fabric, colour, decoration, and whether gentle brushing or pretreatment is suitable. Pause for valuable, fragile, professional-care, or unreadable-label items. Have a place to catch fallen soil, a clean soft brush or safely usable blunt edge, and a labelled compatible laundry product. Keep this item apart from clean clothes. Know that the source was ordinary mud rather than inferring “just dirt” from colour.

### Execution

1. **Let ordinary mud dry naturally first.** Place the item where it will not soil food or other clothes and where airflow and garment care permit. Do not use dryer heat to “speed up” this preparation. Do not rub wet mud into fibres or put a large clump into a whole load.
2. **Lift only loose solids gently.** Once dry, gently tap or soft-brush away surface particles where they can be contained. Test a small edge first. Stop for fuzzing, colour loss, or loosening decoration. Hard scraping cannot safely remove what remains inside fibres.
3. **Check both garment and product.** For the remaining ordinary mark, choose liquid laundry product or pretreatment compatible with both labels. Test a hidden place for damage or dye change under product directions, then use its stated amount and method. Do not treat mud as blood, protein, grease, or rust, or improvise bleach, solvents, strong acids, or alkalis.
4. **Wash by label and inspect while damp.** Launder with a compatible load under garment and washer or hand-wash instructions. Check the original place afterward for grit or a remaining mark. With a visible mark, avoid heated drying; repeat only an allowed method or ask qualified care help rather than using the dryer to “settle it.”
5. **Confirm the boundary before drying.** Continue to label drying only when loose solids are gone, the fabric remains intact, colour is unchanged, and the mark's state is checkable. If the source later proves unusual, stop ordinary mixing or treatment and seek the correct contamination route.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

You removed loose ordinary mud before laundering and checked the original place afterward without damaging the fabric. A remaining mark is clearly “not clean yet; no heated drying.” Sending the garment into a washer with dried clumps still attached is not completed pretreatment.

- **You rub wet mud hard:** Stop, let ordinary mud dry naturally, then lift only loose solids.
- **A soft-brush test fuzzes or fades fabric:** Stop brushing and use garment or maker instructions or qualified care.
- **The mud contains oil, waste, or unknown contamination:** Leave the ordinary-mud path, isolate it, and verify appropriate handling.
- **A mark remains after washing:** Avoid heat; check whether it is residual soil, dye, or fibre change before an allowed retry.
- **Standing, grip, or sensory access is limited:** Use a table-height area, easier-grip tool, or in-person help. Stop when source or fabric response cannot be confirmed.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is known mud from an ordinary path or ground, not post-disaster or specialist-work contamination. Soils may contain dyes; dry brushing removes particles but does not promise one-wash stain removal. Garment and cleaner labels determine wash and pretreatment eligibility. No universal temperature, soaking duration, or bleach recipe is supplied.
