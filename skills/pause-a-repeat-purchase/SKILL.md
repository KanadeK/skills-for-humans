## 中文

当你正要为一件普通食品或日用品付款，却想起家里可能有同样或能完成同一用途的东西时，加载这份 Skill。它处理**这一次购买决定**，不负责出门前编采购清单、比较包装量、判断食品是否安全，或替你制定储存方案。

### 准备与输入

先暂时把物品留在购物车，不付款。你需要知道：它要完成什么具体用途；家里、其他常放地点和已下单未收到的物品中有什么可替代；现有物品是否可取用、状态是否已确认；下一次确实会用它的时间。如果是替别人买，先核对对方是否已有安排。不要分享订单里的地址或支付信息来证明一包纸巾的存在。

清单、照片或店内询问都可以帮助你核对；它们可能过时。不能靠记忆确认时，把结论标成“未知”，而不是“应该没有”。食品状态、标签或特殊安全要求不清楚时，先按相应专门信息核实；这份 Skill 不给它们盖安全章。

### 执行

1. **给候选物品写一个工作。** 用一句话说出它将在哪一次具体活动中被用到，例如“周四清洁桌面时替换用完的清洁布”。“打折”“迟早会用”不是使用机会。
2. **核对已经占位的物品。** 看实物、近期照片或可信库存记录；再查自己能查看的未到货订单。比较用途而不只看商品名。已损坏、已指定给别的用途、暂时拿不到的物品要写明原因，不能简单算成空缺，也不能假装可用。
3. **核对时间。** 问自己：在下一次方便购买之前，是否有一个已安排且现有可用物品无法覆盖的用途？取消、推迟或改变的计划不算已发生的需求。对食品，再确认你实际会在家吃的次数；数量与包装另作判断。
4. **输出一个决定。** 有现成可用物品且没有未覆盖用途，就移出本次购物车。确有未覆盖用途，就保留满足该用途的购买决定；包装数量、规格和标签仍需分别核对。库存或使用机会不明且并不急用，就保存物品名称，待查后再决定。确有普通的当下用途却无法核对时，明确记下不确定性，只解决这次用途，之后停止补货。
5. **留一个可执行的收尾。** 若选择暂缓，写下“要查什么、何时查”；若购买，写下第一次要用在何处。没有收尾的“先囤着”会自动增长，但不会自动获得用途。

### 完成条件

付款前，你能对这件物品说出“买 / 不买 / 待核对”，并给出已有可用物品、未到货物品和第一次真实使用机会的依据。你没有把优惠标签当成用途，也没有把未知库存说成零库存。

### 常见报错与补救

- **人在店里，库存只在脑中：** 能联系家里时请对方只确认物品和数量；否则保存候选，非急用就暂缓。照片和清单要看日期。
- **包装或品牌不同：** 先比它是否完成同一工作；规格不合适再查商品说明，不用品牌名宣布“不是重复”。
- **促销倒计时催你付款：** 暂停核对。倒计时只描述卖家的提示，不会替你安排使用；放弃一次可能的优惠也是有效结果。
- **“以后总能用到”：** 写不出第一次使用时间与场景时，改为待核对或不买。需要明确备用物品时，写出它防止哪次实际中断，以及放在哪里。
- **现有物品状态不明：** 不把可疑食品或有安全疑虑的物品当成可用；停止这份普通采购判断，改查相应标签、安全或制造商说明。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这套核对适用于你能决定的普通购买；家庭习惯、补货间隔、价格和取得物品的难度没有统一值。不能站在店里久看、读小字或使用手机时，可以先用纸记下名称，回家查实物；也可以请可信的人描述信息，但由你决定是否购买。为了无障碍或两处实际使用而保留第二件物品，可以是明确用途，不必强迫自己只拥有一件。

可能的副作用是多花几分钟、错过一项优惠，或发现确实需要买。完成目标是做出有依据的决定，不是把购物车清零。

## English

Load this Skill when you are about to pay for an everyday food or household item and suspect that you already own the same thing, or something that does the same job. This is a **decision about this purchase**. It does not build a trip-wide shopping list, choose pack size, certify food safety, or plan storage after you get home.

### Preparation and inputs

Leave the item in the cart without paying yet. Identify its specific job; equivalent items at home, in another usual location, or in orders placed but not delivered; whether those items are accessible and their condition is known; and the next real occasion to use it. If the item is for someone else, check whether they already have a plan. You do not need to share an address or payment details to establish that a packet of tissues exists.

A list, photo, or quick question to someone at home can help, but any of these may be out of date. Mark stock you cannot verify as *unknown*, rather than assuming zero. If food condition, labels, or special safety requirements are uncertain, use the relevant specialist guidance first; this Skill does not certify them.

### Execution

1. **Give the candidate item a job.** Name a specific occasion, such as “replace the worn cleaning cloth when I clean the table on Thursday.” “On sale” and “useful someday” are not occasions.
2. **Check items that already fill that job.** Look at the item, a recent photo, or a reliable stock record; check pending orders you can access. Compare function as well as product name. Record why an owned item is damaged, reserved for a different use, or inaccessible. None of these conditions automatically proves either a gap or a usable spare.
3. **Check the timing.** Before your next practical chance to buy, is there a planned use that your verified usable stock cannot cover? A cancelled, postponed, or vague plan is not a present use. For food, check how many meals you will actually eat at home; choose quantity and pack size separately.
4. **Choose one output.** If usable stock covers every real use, remove this item. If a use remains uncovered, keep the purchase decision that serves it, then check pack size, specifications, and labels separately. If stock or use is unknown and the item is not urgent, save its name and decide after checking. If an ordinary immediate need cannot wait for a check, note the uncertainty, meet this one use, and stop further restocking until you verify.
5. **Leave a next action.** If you defer, write what to check and when. If you buy, name its first use. “Store it for later” can grow inventory without producing an occasion to use it.

### Success

Before payment, you can say “buy / skip / check later” and explain your evidence about usable stock, pending orders, and first real use. A sale label has not become a purpose, and unknown stock has not become zero stock.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You are in the shop; stock is only in your memory:** Ask someone at home to confirm the item and amount if that is practical. Otherwise save the candidate and defer a non-urgent purchase. Check when photos and lists were made.
- **The brand or packaging differs:** Compare the job first. If a specification matters, read the product information; a different brand alone does not make it a new need.
- **A countdown is pushing you to pay:** Pause for the check. The timer is a seller's prompt, not a calendar entry for your use. Missing a possible discount is a valid outcome.
- **“I will use it eventually”:** Without a first time and situation, defer or skip. If you need a spare for a real interruption, name that interruption and where the spare will live.
- **The condition of an owned item is unclear:** Do not count doubtful food or an item with safety concerns as ready to use. Stop this ordinary purchase check and consult the relevant label, safety, or manufacturer guidance.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This check covers ordinary purchases you control. Household routines, restocking intervals, prices, and difficulty obtaining goods vary. If standing in a shop, reading small print, or using a phone is difficult, write down the name and inspect stock at home; a trusted person can describe what they see, while you make the decision. A second item for access needs or two genuinely separate locations can have a real job; you do not have to own exactly one of everything.

You may spend a few minutes, miss a promotion, or discover that buying is justified. The finish line is an evidenced choice, not an empty cart.
