## 中文

当普通自愿的谈话里你说了一个玩笑，却看到不适、听到纠正，或意识到它伤到了人时，加载这份 Skill。目标是**停止继续放大它，承认具体影响，并做你能做到的有限修正**。你不能把对方的原谅、公开评价或心情恢复当作完成条件。本篇只处理一次普通失误；持续骚扰、强迫、歧视攻击、危机或虐待需要其他更合适的责任和支持路径。

### 准备与输入

先确定自己实际说了什么、谁听见了、有没有继续传播或模仿，以及对方已经表示了什么。你不需要在现场调查其私人经历来证明玩笑有问题。若只是自己察觉气氛不对而不确定，可先停止这个方向并简短确认，不把“没人立刻骂我”当成没影响。

### 执行

1. **立刻停下这个笑点。** 不重复、不补一个更大的版本，也不请别人解释为什么“不好笑”。在群聊中停止转发或附和；如果你能管理原消息，是否撤回也要考虑已经看见的人和需要留下的必要说明，不用静默删掉来假装没发生。
2. **说清自己的动作与影响。** “我刚才那个玩笑不合适，让你不舒服了。抱歉，我会停。”若你不确定对方感受，可说“我看到这句可能不合适，我先停下；你不需要现在解释。”不加“但我本来没有恶意”来抵消前半句。
3. **只问有用的下一步。** 若确有一条不实内容被大家听到，可在相关范围内更正；若对方希望结束话题，就停止。不要求对方当场教你一套正确说法、给你打分，或接受一场长篇自我反省演讲。
4. **按听见的人群大小控制补救。** 私下造成的不适，先别把人拉进公开道歉现场；在群里传播的错误，可简短在同一群里止住，不再引用原话做二次传播。涉及别人的私人信息时，先停止分享并按实际隐私边界处理。
5. **把修正落实到后续行为。** 不再对同一人重复这个笑点，也不换个字继续试。对方不回复或明确不想谈，就给空间；如果以后再交流，用平常的尊重而非不断要求“你现在原谅我了吗”。

### 完成条件

你已停止重复或扩散那句玩笑，简短承认自己做了什么和已知影响，并完成你控制范围内必要的更正。对方可能接受、拒绝或不回应；这些都不是你能替他决定的验收结果。若行为超出普通一次失误的范围，本 Skill 停止，不把深层伤害包装成一句轻松道歉即可修复。

### 常见报错与补救

- **“我只是开玩笑”：** 这解释意图，不能取消对方经历。回到停止与具体修正。
- **道歉越说越长，轮到对方安慰你：** 缩成一两句，把继续或不继续的选择交还对方。
- **对方没立刻回应：** 不连发消息索要“没事”；保持停止并给空间。
- **在群里反复引用原话解释：** 改为不重复内容的简短更正，减少再次传播。
- **你发现这是持续的伤害而非一次口误：** 不用本 Skill 给自己盖章；停止行为并寻求适当的责任与支持方式。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这份 Skill 假设普通、非强迫的交流；玩笑、熟悉程度与接受道歉的方式没有统一文化默认值。对方可能需要文字、时间或完全不交流。听觉、语言或网络差异可影响你察觉反馈，用双方可用的渠道简短说明即可。你只控制自己停止、承认和修正的动作，不控制他人的感受。

现实副作用是一个自认为好笑的句子可能从你的常用库存中永久删除。这通常比让别人继续替它承担后果更便宜。

## English

Load this Skill when a joke you made in an ordinary voluntary conversation lands badly, someone corrects you, or you notice that it hurt a person. **Stop amplifying it, acknowledge the specific effect, and make a bounded correction you can control.** Their forgiveness, public rating of you, or restored mood is not a completion condition. This covers one ordinary mistake; persistent harassment, coercion, discriminatory attacks, crisis, or abuse need more appropriate accountability and support.

### Preparation and inputs

Identify what you actually said, who heard it, whether it was repeated or forwarded, and what the affected person has already said. You do not need to investigate private history at the scene to prove the joke was a problem. If you only notice a change in the room and are uncertain, stop that line and check briefly. No immediate objection is not evidence of no effect.

### Execution

1. **Stop the joke now.** Do not repeat it, escalate it, or make someone explain why it was “not funny.” Stop forwarding or joining in online. If you can remove a message, consider who already saw it and what concise correction is needed rather than silently erasing it as if nothing happened.
2. **Name your action and what you know.** “That joke was out of place and made you uncomfortable. I'm sorry; I'll stop.” If you do not know their feeling, say “I can see that may not have worked. I'll stop; you don't need to explain now.” Do not attach “but I meant well” to cancel the first half.
3. **Ask only for a useful next step.** Correct a false statement within the relevant group if one was heard. If the person wants the topic ended, end it. Do not require them to teach you a full replacement script, grade your apology, or listen to a long monologue about your growth.
4. **Match repair to the audience.** Do not turn a private mistake into a public apology event without need. Stop a group-chat error briefly in that group without quoting the joke again for new readers. If another person's private information was involved, stop sharing and follow the actual privacy boundary.
5. **Make later behaviour match the correction.** Do not repeat the joke to the same person or try it with one word changed. Give space when they do not reply or do not want to discuss it. If you meet again, show ordinary respect rather than repeatedly asking whether you are forgiven yet.

### Success

You stopped repeating or spreading the joke, briefly acknowledged your act and known effect, and made necessary corrections within your control. The other person may accept, reject, or not answer; you cannot certify any of those for them. If the behaviour extends beyond one ordinary mistake, stop this Skill rather than claiming that a light apology repaired deep harm.

### Common errors and recovery

- **“It was only a joke”:** That explains intent but cannot erase the other's experience. Return to stopping and concrete correction.
- **The apology grows until they comfort you:** Shorten it to one or two sentences and return the choice to engage or not.
- **No immediate reply:** Do not send repeated messages demanding “it's okay.” Keep the behaviour stopped and give space.
- **You keep quoting the joke in the group to explain it:** Use a brief correction that does not recirculate the wording.
- **You see a continuing harmful pattern, not one slip:** Do not use this Skill as an approval stamp. Stop the behaviour and find suitable accountability and support.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This covers ordinary non-coercive exchange. Humour, closeness, and how someone receives an apology have no universal cultural setting. They may need text, time, or no further discussion. Hearing, language, or network differences can change how you detect feedback; use a channel both of you can access for a short acknowledgment. You control stopping, owning, and correcting your action, not another person's feelings.

One sentence you thought was funny may leave your active repertoire permanently. That is often cheaper than asking someone else to keep carrying its effect.
