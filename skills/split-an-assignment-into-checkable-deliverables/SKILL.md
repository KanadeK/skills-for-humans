## 中文

当你已接下一项普通学习、志愿或协作任务，最终要交什么基本明确，但中间仍是一大团“找资料、做一下、再检查”时加载。目标是把它拆成**有先后、有负责人、能被别人实际查看的中间成果**，并选出可以开始的第一步。这份 Skill 不替代专业行业判断、劳动法律、人事决策或最终验收；一张计划表也不等于任务完成。

### 准备与输入

找到实际任务说明、最终文件/物件/活动的接收人、约定截止和已知限制。用一句话写“到最后，谁会收到什么，能用它做什么”。若产出、边界或验收者仍不明确，先澄清需求，不用拆步骤掩盖根本未知。只处理你有权负责的部分；他人的事项需标负责人，不擅自替其作决定。

### 执行

1. **从最终物倒推必要中间物。** 问“别人要看到什么，才能判断它下一步可继续？” 可能是经确认的输入清单、一个可看版本、收到的反馈记录、修好的最终版本；按这件事的真实流程选，不照抄通用模板。每一项写成可留下的东西，而非仅“研究两小时”。
2. **给每步一个可观察完成点。** 写“交出__，在__位置，供__核对__”，比如一页整理好的可查清单，而不是“准备完成”。若你需要另一个人回复，区分“我已提交”和“对方已确认”，不让一个按钮同时代表两件事。
3. **排真正的依赖和负责人。** 哪项必须先有输入，哪项可独立进行，谁持有文件/物品，哪里等待专业判断？对依赖标“由__提供/待__决定”，不要默默把别人的答复写成已完成。顺序以物品和决定关系为准，不为让表看起来整齐制造十几个微动作。
4. **从最小可检查部分开工。** 选一项现在有输入、能在现实时间内产出有用中间物的步骤；告诉接收人你准备先交什么、何时需要看一眼。做完它后实际放出/交付，再记录反馈，下一步才依新证据调整。
5. **维护一个当前版本。** 新信息改变范围时，在同一清单标已完成、待确认、删除或新增，写清原因；别同时保留两份互相矛盾的“最新计划”。临提交前按任务要求自查属于后续流程，计划自身不能替代读者使用、现场执行或正式验收。

### 完成与停止

你有一份从已知最终产出出发、每步都有可查看东西/完成判据/负责人/依赖、第一步已能启动的短清单，就完成拆分。**规划完成不是工作完成，写“已提交”也不是“已被接受”。** 若任务需要专业安全、财务、医疗或行业判断，你只记录待谁决定，停止替专业人补答案。

### 常见报错与补救

- **清单都是“调查、沟通、推进”：** 给每项加一个别人可见的输出和检查者，没有输出就与相邻步骤合并。
- **步骤细到每次点击、拿笔：** 合并成能形成一个实际中间成果的动作，避免管理清单比任务本身更长。
- **第一步等别人给资料，却没写谁：** 标注输入负责人和等待点，同时做不依赖它的部分。
- **你写“完成”，接收人还没看过：** 分开记录自己完成、已发出和对方确认/需修改。
- **任务范围突然变：** 回到最终产出和边界，只改受影响步骤；确属新授权/专业决定的先问负责人。

### 假设、替代与副作用

适用于普通协作小任务，不是项目管理软件、绩效评估、行业作业或对人事/法律权利的建议。纸张、文档或简单清单都可承载，不要求一款 App。视觉、听觉、语言或行动条件不同，可用可读格式、口头复述或可信协助确认每个输出与接收人。副作用是“今天很忙”可能还要补一个具体成果，但成果本身也更容易被接手。

## English

Load this Skill after accepting an ordinary study, volunteer or collaboration assignment whose final output is basically known, but the middle still sounds like “research, do it, check later.” Turn it into **ordered intermediate things another person can actually inspect**, with owners and a first action you can start. This does not make professional domain, employment-law or personnel decisions or replace final acceptance. A plan is not finished work.

### Preparation and inputs

Find the actual assignment, who will receive the final file, item or activity, its agreed due point and known limits. Write one sentence: “At the end, who receives what and what can they do with it?” If output, boundary or reviewer is still unknown, clarify the assignment first rather than hiding uncertainty in a step list. Plan only what you own; name another person's decision instead of silently making it for them.

### Execution

1. **Work backward from the final thing.** Ask what another person must see to judge that the next part can proceed. It might be a checked input list, a reviewable version, feedback recorded or a corrected final copy. Choose from this assignment's real process, not a universal template. Each step should leave something, not just “research for two hours.”
2. **Give each part an observable done point.** Write “produce __ at __ location for __ to check __,” such as one retrievable page of facts, rather than “preparation complete.” If another person's reply matters, distinguish “I submitted it” from “they confirmed it.” One button should not represent both.
3. **Order real dependencies and owners.** Which item needs an input first, which can proceed separately, who holds the file, and where does a specialist judgment wait? Mark “supplied by __ / decision by __” rather than filling in their answer as complete. Order by the relationship of objects and decisions, not by creating tiny actions to make a neat chart.
4. **Start with the smallest checkable part.** Pick a step whose input exists now and that can leave a useful intermediate object in realistic time. Tell the recipient what you will show first and when you need a look. Actually release that part when done, record feedback, and only then adjust the next step using new evidence.
5. **Keep one current version.** For a changed scope, mark each item done, pending, removed or added in the same list with a reason. Do not keep two contradictory “latest plans.” Final pre-submit review belongs to the later task; the plan cannot stand in for real use, execution or formal acceptance.

### Success and stop

You have a short list grounded in the known final output, where each part has an inspectable thing, done criterion, owner and dependency, and the first part can start. **Planning is not doing, and “sent” is not “accepted.”** For professional safety, financial, medical or domain judgments, record who must decide and stop supplying an answer on their behalf.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Every line says “research, communicate, progress”:** Add a visible output and checker or combine it with an adjacent real step.
- **Every click and pen movement is a step:** Combine actions into one useful intermediate object so managing the list does not exceed doing the task.
- **Step one waits for someone else's input but no one is named:** State the input owner and waiting point and do independent work meanwhile.
- **You mark done before the recipient sees it:** Track your completion, submission and their acceptance or requested change separately.
- **Scope changes suddenly:** Return to final output and boundary and revise only affected steps. Ask the owner about genuinely new authority or specialist decisions.

### Assumptions, alternatives and side effects

This is for ordinary small collaboration, not project-management software, performance review, professional field work or employment or legal rights. Paper, a document or a simple list can carry it; no specific app is needed. For sight, hearing, language or mobility needs, use a readable format, spoken readback or trusted help to confirm outputs and owners. “Busy today” may need one concrete artifact, but the work becomes easier for someone else to pick up.
