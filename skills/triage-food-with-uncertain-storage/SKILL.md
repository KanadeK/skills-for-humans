## 中文

当家里一件普通食品的**身份、标签、开封状态、时间/温度史或包装完整性**有关键缺口，导致你不能判断是否还能按要求保存或以后吃时，加载这份 Skill。目标是找到足以适用这件食品的可靠依据，或停止把它当作可用库存；不是凭气味和勇气颁发安全证书。

### 准备与输入

先让其他人知道这件食品**待核实、先别吃**。能识别为需冷藏/冷冻的食品，在调查期间继续按已知要求保持合适条件，不把它拿到室温台面上慢慢研究；包装渗漏时与其他食品隔开。准备原包装、清晰照片、购买或处理记录、可靠的设备温度记录、以及实际接触过它的人能提供的具体事实。没有的记录就写未知，不补一个好看的日期。

### 执行

1. **说清到底缺哪项证据。** 是无法确认食品本身与适用标签？不知道哪一层封口何时打开？不知道离开要求温度多久？还是包装破损、渗漏？把缺口逐项写下，不把“有点担心”留成无从处理的一团。
2. **先隔离异常，不尝味。** 明显渗漏、鼓胀、严重破损、污染，或已知未满足重要保存要求的食品，不与即食食品混放，也不拿来试吃。按这件食品和当地官方指导处理；不要为了减少浪费把它重新贴成“新鲜”。
3. **只从可追溯事实补缺口。** 找原包装或同一批商品的清晰照片、购买/到家时间、真实开封标记、设备温度或停电记录；必要时请实际处理过食品的人说出具体观察。若要补商品说明，查制造商的**准确产品与包装版本**，再核对所在地食品安全机构。相似品牌、旧配方或别国同名字样不能自动替代。
4. **区分安全关键与品质问题。** 用手中产品和当地规则判断缺失信息是否会影响必要的温度、时间、开封后条件或安全日期。不同地区的日期词义可能不同；“最佳食用”之类品质说明也不能覆盖一段失控的冷藏历史。外观和气味只能提示明显坏了，不能反过来证明没问题。
5. **给出有边界的决定。** 若关键事实已核实且按适用要求仍可处理，把依据记下，转入正常归置/轮换；这不是保证绝对安全。若已有明确违反，或易腐食品所需的关键时间/温度/身份事实查不回，就停止供以后食用，并按当地官方建议隔离、丢弃或取得针对该食品的帮助。资料仍在查时标“待核实、不可食用”，同时持续满足已知必要保存条件；不要把等待无限延长。
6. **把结果留给下次看到的人。** 写清食品、缺口、找到的证据、作出的保留/停止决定及日期。若容器和标签都已无法对应，别让下一位靠冰箱考古重做一次猜测。

### 完成条件

你有一个可复述的结果：**依据哪些真实证据按具体要求继续处理**，或**哪些安全关键条件无法证明，因此停止把它当作可食用库存**。单写“闻着没事”、把今天改成开封日，或移进冰箱后宣布历史已修复，都不算完成。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只有正常颜色和气味：** 它们不能补时间、温度或标签；继续找记录，安全关键缺口无法补时保守停止。
- **原包装丢失：** 查可信的购买记录、准确产品照片或制造商信息；产品身份仍无法对应时，别把其他同款包装的说明硬贴上去。
- **曾在室温放置，后来重新冷藏：** 冷藏不能回写之前的时间史。按实际食品和当地指导判断；不能证明符合条件时不要为了“已经放回去”而保留。
- **停电后只知道冰箱现在冷了：** 当前读数不说明停电期间每件食物的状态。按停电时长、设备记录、门状态、食品类别和当地官方专项指导逐项判断；缺关键事实就停止，别借别国表格的数字。
- **家人给出模糊回忆：** 只记录能说清的具体时间和动作；“应该没多久”仍是未知，不用它给易腐食品颁发通行证。

### 假设、替代与副作用

本 Skill 只处理普通家庭食品，不做医疗饮食、过敏处置、婴幼儿食品、罐藏或发酵。商品、地区和保存方式不同，不能设统一时限。看不清标签时用放大、语音读取或可信协助；没有温度计时也不编数字。停止保存可能造成食物和金钱损失，但不能通过嗅觉证明安全。具体清洁、容器选择、冷藏分区和剩菜处理由相应任务完成。

## English

Load this Skill when a food at home has a critical gap in its **identity, label, opening state, time and temperature history, or package integrity**, so you cannot tell whether it can still be stored as required or used later. Find reliable evidence for this particular food or stop counting it as usable stock. A normal smell is not a safety certificate.

### Preparation and inputs

Tell others this item is **unverified; do not eat it yet**. If it is known to require chilling or freezing, maintain that known condition while you check; do not leave it on a room-temperature counter for a long investigation. Keep leaks away from other food. Gather any original package, clear photo, purchase or handling record, reliable appliance temperature record, and specific facts from people who actually handled it. Write “unknown” where the record is missing rather than filling in an attractive date.

### Execution

1. **Name the missing evidence.** Is the food or applicable label unclear? Which seal broke and when? Is time away from a required temperature unknown? Did a package leak or fail? List the gaps separately so “I feel unsure” becomes something you can actually check.
2. **Isolate exceptions without tasting.** Keep obvious leaks, unexpected swelling, serious damage, contamination, or a known failure of important storage conditions away from ready-to-eat food. Follow guidance for the actual food and location. Do not relabel a questionable item “fresh” to reduce waste.
3. **Recover only traceable facts.** Look for the original pack or a clear photo of the same batch, purchase and arrival times, a real opening mark, and appliance or power records. Ask someone who handled the food for concrete actions and times. For directions, find the maker's **exact product and package version** and your local food authority. A similar brand, older recipe, or the same date word in another country is not an automatic substitute.
4. **Separate safety-critical gaps from quality questions.** Use the item and local rules to decide whether the unknown affects necessary temperature, time, after-opening conditions, or a safety date. Date terms may mean different things across regions. A quality statement cannot erase a period of failed refrigeration. Appearance and smell can flag an obvious problem but cannot prove safety by looking normal.
5. **Make a bounded decision.** If critical facts are confirmed and the applicable conditions can still be followed, record the basis and return to ordinary storage or rotation; this is not a guarantee of absolute safety. If there is a clear failure, or a perishable food's critical identity, time, or temperature facts cannot be recovered, stop keeping it for future eating. Isolate or discard it, or obtain advice for this food under local official guidance. While looking for evidence, mark it “unverified—do not eat” and continue any known required storage; do not let the pending state run forever.
6. **Leave the result for the next person.** Record the item, the gap, actual evidence found, the keep-or-stop decision, and date. If the container can no longer be matched to its label, do not ask the next person to perform refrigerator archaeology to guess again.

### Success

You can explain **which real evidence permits handling this item under its specific requirements**, or **which safety-critical condition cannot be established, so it is no longer treated as edible stock**. “Smells fine,” relabelling today as its opening date, or chilling it now and pretending the past was cold are not completed checks.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Only colour and smell look normal:** They cannot supply the missing time, temperature, or label. Seek records; stop conservatively when a critical safety gap remains.
- **The original package is lost:** Check credible purchase records, an exact product photo, or maker information. If the item still cannot be matched, do not attach another pack's directions by guesswork.
- **It sat at room temperature and was chilled later:** Chilling cannot rewrite the earlier timeline. Follow local guidance for the actual item; do not keep it merely because you put it back.
- **The fridge is cold now after a power outage:** Today's reading does not describe every item during the outage. Use outage duration, appliance records, door state, food type, and local official outage guidance item by item. If a critical fact is missing, stop; do not borrow numbers from another country's chart.
- **Someone offers a vague recollection:** Record only specific times and actions they can actually state. “Probably not long” remains unknown for a perishable item.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary home food, not medical diets, allergy care, infant food, canning, or fermentation. Products, regions, and storage methods differ; no universal safe lifetime is supplied. Enlarge a label, use audio reading, or seek trusted help if print is inaccessible. Without a thermometer, do not invent readings. Stopping storage may cost food and money, but smell cannot prove safety. Cleaning, container selection, refrigerator layout, and leftovers remain separate tasks.
