## 中文

当一件普通小物似乎不在原位，你还没把整屋翻乱时，加载这份 Skill。你会在**自己可处理的固定范围**内按顺序找，并以“找到”或“查过哪些位置、下一步是什么”结束。已经因为找不到物品而即将迟到时，优先处理通知与改期；身份凭据、重要文件、数字资料、疑似被盗或危险物品不靠这份普通搜索流程定案。

### 准备与输入

先说清要找的是哪一件、怎样辨认它，以及**最后一次确定**见到或用到它的地点和动作。把“我通常放在那儿”和“我确实在那里见到”分开。决定本次最多查多久、查哪一间或哪一段移动路线，范围须符合你当下的时间、行动、视力和注意力条件。没有固定分钟数；若继续寻找会耽误真正重要的安排，就把停止点提前。

使用现有照明、放大或触觉方式，必要时请在场的可信者描述你有权查看的位置。只查看你可进入、可安全够到的地方；别为了一个普通小物爬高、拆家具、搬不稳的堆，或擅自翻别人的私人物品。

### 执行

1. **画一个小范围。** 从最后确认位置、下一步走过的路径、物品通常归位处，选本轮最可能的几个点。别把“家里”当成一个搜索点；它太大，无法记录是否查完。
2. **按一处一处查。** 先看物品可能落在表面、边缘或容器口的地方，再查它可能被顺手放进的已知口袋、盒或抽屉。用适合你的看、摸、朗读或光照方法确认**这件物品本身**，不只看“像它的轮廓”。
3. **让已查区域保持已查。** 每查完一处，就在纸上或脑中按固定顺序标“已查”；移动了别的东西就放回原位，避免制造第二件失物。不能记住顺序时用两三行短记录，不需要给全屋建地图。
4. **核对物品有没有被合理移动。** 若其他人可能使用或清理过它，问具体时间和地点，允许对方说不知道；把得到的事实作为新线索，不把猜测变成指责。新线索若超出本轮范围，另记下一轮，不无限延长。
5. **到停止点就收口。** 找到时核对确是目标物，再放进一个你下次能找回的位置。没找到时写下“已查范围、没查范围、最后确认事实、下一步/可用替代或改期”，然后停。未找到不等于永久遗失；重复查同一格也不增加证据。

### 完成与停止

本轮成功是找回并确认目标小物，或在设定范围/时间内完成一次不重复的搜索并留下可续做记录。若发现位置需要危险搬动、未经许可的进入或专门调查，停止普通查找。下一件事若不能没有此物，就用可行替代、延后或通知相关的人，而不是让搜索无限占用时间。

### 常见报错与补救

- **“肯定在那儿”却没有看到：** 只承认最后实际见到的位置；把惯常位置列为候选，不在同一抽屉反复开关。
- **查过的区域又翻了一遍：** 给已查位置写简短记号；如果出现新事实才有理由回头。
- **看不清相似物或暗处：** 改变可用照明、对比、放大或触觉方式，必要时请可信者描述；不要靠猜测宣布找到。
- **有人可能移动了，但说不清：** 记录对方能确认的最后动作，扩大下一轮的一个具体区域，不把全屋再次打散。
- **中途被打断：** 保留已查清单、剩余区域和最后线索；回来从未查处继续，不把暂停当作“又从零开始”。

### 假设、替代与已知副作用

家庭空间、共享权限、视觉、行动方式和时间压力不同。纸笔、记忆中的固定顺序或可访问的语音记录都能标已查区域；使用什么工具由你决定。本篇处理低风险物理小件，不要求购买定位器，也不把每次找不到东西诊断成个人问题。副作用可能只是一个诚实的“本轮未找到”；至少下一轮不必重演同一条路线。

## English

Load this Skill when an ordinary small item seems absent from its usual place and you have not yet turned the whole home upside down. Search **a bounded area you may handle** in order and finish with either the item or a record of places checked and the next action. If the missing item is already making you late, handle notice and rescheduling first. This ordinary search does not settle missing credentials, important papers, digital data, suspected theft, or hazardous items.

### Preparation and inputs

Name the exact item and how to recognize it, plus the place and action where you **last definitely saw or used it**. Separate “where I usually put it” from “where I actually saw it.” Set a time and area limit you can afford now, taking your schedule, mobility, sight, and attention into account. There is no universal number of minutes; shorten the search if it would displace a more important commitment.

Use lighting, magnification, or touch methods you already have. Ask a trusted person present to describe a place you may inspect if that helps. Search only where you are permitted and can safely reach. Do not climb, dismantle furniture, shift an unstable pile, or open another person's private belongings for one ordinary item.

### Execution

1. **Draw a small search area.** From the last confirmed place, the path you took next, and the item's usual home, choose a few most likely spots. “Somewhere at home” is too large to record as checked.
2. **Check one place at a time.** Look first at surfaces, edges, and container openings where it could have landed. Then check known pockets, boxes, or drawers where you might have put it down. Use sight, touch, a reading aid, or light that works for you to confirm **the item itself**, not just a similar outline.
3. **Keep checked places checked.** Mark each finished spot in a fixed order on paper or mentally. Put other things back if you move them so you do not create a second missing item. If the order is hard to remember, use a two- or three-line note rather than mapping the whole house.
4. **Check whether it was reasonably moved.** If someone else may have used or tidied it, ask about a specific time and place and allow “I don't know.” Treat confirmed facts as new clues, not guesses as accusations. Write a clue outside this round's area for a later round rather than extending forever.
5. **Close at the stop point.** If found, verify it is the target and give it a place you can find next time. If not found, record the checked and unchecked areas, last confirmed fact, and next action, alternative, or reschedule, then stop. Not found now is not lost forever; searching the same section again adds no evidence.

### Success and stop conditions

This round succeeds when you verify the small item or complete one non-repeating search within your chosen area/time and leave a resumable record. Stop ordinary searching if the next place requires unsafe lifting, unauthorized access, or specialist investigation. If the next task cannot work without this item, choose a real substitute, delay it, or notify the relevant person rather than allowing an endless search.

### Common errors and recovery

- **“It must be there” without seeing it:** Use the last place you actually saw it as a fact. The habitual home is a candidate; do not keep reopening the same drawer.
- **You repeat a checked area:** Make a short mark for each checked place. Revisit only when a new fact gives a reason.
- **Similar objects or darkness make identification hard:** Change available light, contrast, magnification, or touch method, or ask a trusted person to describe it. Do not claim a guess is a find.
- **Someone may have moved it but cannot say where:** Record their last confirmed action and give one specific new area to the next round, rather than scattering the whole house again.
- **You are interrupted:** Keep checked places, remaining areas, and the last clue. Resume with unchecked spots, not another run from zero.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Homes, sharing permissions, sight, movement, and time pressure vary. Paper, a fixed mental order, or an accessible voice note can mark checked places; use what works for you. This covers low-risk physical small items, requires no tracker purchase, and does not diagnose a person from a missing object. An honest “not found this round” may be the result; at least the next round need not repeat the same route.
