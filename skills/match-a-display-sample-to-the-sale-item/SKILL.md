## 中文

当你摸过、看过或试过店里的普通展示样品，准备买一件货架商品时，先确认**你试的究竟是不是将要买的版本**。样品可能是旧批次、不同面料或高级款；这份 Skill 的结果是建立样品与售卖品的对应关系，不负责测量尺寸是否适合你，也不处理交货后的争议。

### 准备与输入

指出展示样品、货架商品或店员准备写入订单的那一款。记下样品上能找到的品牌、完整型号、颜色/材质/配置，以及你亲自观察到、会影响决定的两三项特征。不要只记录“靠窗那件”；位置可以换，型号不能靠窗景推断。

### 执行

1. **读展示样品的身份。** 查样品本体或展示牌的型号和版本；若没有，问店员这个展示样品对应哪一款。未经允许不拆开、不移动固定展示品。
2. **读实际售卖品的身份。** 对照未开封商品的标签、店内库存记录或将写入的订单行，确认完整型号、颜色、材料和配置。展示牌的价格标签不自动等于旁边每个箱子的身份。
3. **逐项连接。** 请店员指出哪些观察适用于这件售卖品，哪些只是样品的磨损、灯光、摆放或不同选配造成的。材料手感、颜色和机构动作若是关键，问有没有同款样品或准确说明；不把另一个版本的体验当成证据。
4. **留下一条可复述记录。** 写“展示样品〔标识〕→ 售卖品〔型号/版本〕→ 相同点/已声明差异/未确认点”。店内允许时可拍样品标签与待买商品标签，不拍工作人员或其他顾客。
5. **按缺口决定是否暂停。** 确切版本对不上，或关键差异没人能确认，就不要靠“应该差不多”付款。换同款实物、取得制造商资料，或以后再来。

### 成功与停止

你能指出样品和实际售卖品的准确身份，并说清哪些亲眼观察可用于这件商品；若无法对应，清楚停在“未确认”。店员不需要保证两件物品在所有微小感受上完全相同。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只知道系列名：** 找到完整型号与版本；系列内可能存在不同配置。
- **样品磨旧了：** 把磨损与设计特征分开记录；不从磨旧样品推断新品的耐久性，也不反向保证新品手感完全相同。
- **样品上的材料与订单文字不同：** 停止，把两处具体措辞并列，请店员用准确商品资料核对；不要自行挑一个更喜欢的版本当真。
- **中途改了颜色或配置：** 重新核对售卖行与样品，之前的对应关系不自动迁移。

### 假设、替代与副作用

假设你能在付款前看到展示标识或请店员提供准确型号。光线、试用磨损、批次与个人感受会改变体验，现场核对只能说明资料对应，不能保证未来交付或长期性能。看不清标牌、无法接近样品或听不清说明时，请店员提供可读文字、朗读标签或让可信同伴协助；拒绝拍照时用纸记下型号。现实副作用是你可能多问一句“这两个型号真的一样吗”。

## English

Load this Skill after you have looked at, touched, or tried an ordinary store display and are about to buy a shelf item. First establish **whether the item offered is the same variant you tried**. A display may be an older revision, different fabric, or upgraded configuration. Your endpoint is a traceable display-to-sale match, not a measurement of whether the product fits your needs or a complaint after delivery.

### Preparation and inputs

Identify the display and the boxed item or order line you are being offered. Record any brand, full model, colour, material, and configuration shown on the sample. Name two or three features you actually observed that matter to you. “The one near the window” is a location, not a model identifier.

### Execution

1. **Identify the sample.** Read its model and revision on the item or display card. If absent, ask staff which sale model the sample represents. Do not open or move a fixed display without permission.
2. **Identify what will be sold.** Read the unopened item's label, store stock record, or proposed order line for the full model, colour, material, and configuration. A price card near the display does not identify every adjacent box.
3. **Connect each relevant observation.** Ask which of the things you saw apply to the sale item and which reflect display wear, lighting, placement, or optional features. If feel, colour, or movement matters, request a sample of the same variant or its exact documentation. Experience with another variant is not evidence for this one.
4. **Keep a short match record.** Write “display [identifier] → sale item [model/variant] → matching features / stated differences / unknowns.” Photograph both labels if the shop permits it, without photographing staff or other shoppers.
5. **Pause on a decisive gap.** If the variants differ or nobody can confirm a feature you need, do not pay on “probably close enough.” Look for a matching display, exact maker information, or return another day.

### Success and stop

You can identify both the sample and sale item and say which observations apply to this item. If you cannot connect them, stop at “unconfirmed.” Staff need not promise that every tiny sensation will be identical between two units.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You have only a series name:** Find the complete model and variant; one series can contain different configurations.
- **The display is worn:** Record wear separately from design. A worn display does not prove a new unit's durability or guarantee the same feel.
- **Sample material conflicts with order wording:** Pause, place the two exact descriptions side by side, and request documentation for the item being sold. Do not pick the more attractive description as truth.
- **Colour or configuration changes midway:** Match the new sale line to the sample again; the earlier match does not transfer automatically.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This assumes you can see a display identifier or obtain the model from staff before paying. Lighting, wear, batches, and personal perception change the experience. An in-store match is evidence about identity, not a guarantee of later delivery or lifetime performance. If labels are unreadable, the sample is hard to reach, or spoken answers are inaccessible, request readable text, a label read aloud, or help from a trusted companion. Use paper if photos are not allowed. One side effect is asking whether two items that look alike really share a model number.
