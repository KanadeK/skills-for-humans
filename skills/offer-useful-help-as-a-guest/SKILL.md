## 中文

当你在别人家做客，看到主人正在准备或收尾，想帮一点忙却不想擅自打乱他的安排时，加载这份 Skill。目标是提出**一件具体、主人愿意让你做**的小事，完成后交回；主人说不用时，停止帮忙也是成功。这里不教烹饪、清洁剂操作、维修或处理危险器具。

### 准备与输入

先看看主人实际在做什么、你有没有安全可做的小动作、自己的体力/行动条件、是否知道这家的物品与空间规则。不要把别人家厨房的柜子、刀具、热锅或清洁用品当作开放的工具箱。没有明显可做之事时，不必用“帮忙”制造一项工作。

### 执行

1. **问一个具体而可拒绝的提议。** 例如“需要我把这几只空杯放到桌上吗？不需要的话我坐着等。”比“我什么都能干”更容易判断。提议必须在你能安全做、也有权进入的范围内。
2. **等主人指明范围。** 主人同意时，确认拿哪几件、放哪里、何时算做完；不凭自己家习惯重新排他的厨房。若主人说“不用”，答“好”，坐下、给通道让位或继续约定的活动，不再反复争取劳动许可。
3. **只做获准的动作。** 轻拿稳放，遇到热、尖锐、漏液、食物安全或设备异常时停下问主人，不靠猜测继续。任何需要食品处理或清洁化学品的步骤须有适用的具体说明；这份 Skill 不替代它。
4. **完成后交回控制权。** 简短说“杯子放好了，接下来我坐这边可以吗？”不擅自开下一个抽屉或把已摆好的东西按自己审美重排。做错位置时按主人指示复原，不争论哪个布局更高效。
5. **若无法做，就诚实降级。** 体力、握持、视觉、语言或时间不允许时，说明“这件我做不稳，能否换一个小动作？”没有合适动作就不做；陪伴或不给通道添堵也可以让这次来访顺利。

### 完成条件

主人同意的一件小事已按其位置和范围完成并交回，或主人拒绝/你无法安全执行而你已停止干预。没有未经允许移动私人物品，也没有把“帮忙”变成主人需要收拾的新任务。主人必须感谢你不是完成条件。

### 常见报错与补救

- **主人说不用，你仍抢着洗碗：** 停手并接受；拒绝帮助可能是主人已有流程，不是对你价值的评价。
- **你把物品放错柜子：** 简短承认，问原位并按指示复原，不继续自行寻宝。
- **帮忙时发现热锅、刀或不明清洁剂：** 不用勇气代替流程；停下请主人处理或换成普通低风险动作。
- **任务越做越多：** 回到最初同意的边界，完成后再问，不自动升级为厨房项目经理。
- **你做不到却怕显得不礼貌：** 说清能做和不能做，选择站开、坐下或提供其他确实能完成的小帮助。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这是普通、自愿的做客场景；不同家庭对客人动手、厨房进入和收尾礼貌没有统一规则。先询问这位主人，别靠地区或关系标签推断。行动、感官或语言需要不同的沟通方式时，可用指向具体物品、简短文字或当面重复确认；不要求客人以身体风险证明热心。

副作用是你可能真的帮上一个小忙，或在该坐的时候坐住。这两种结果都比擅自重构主人家的抽屉容易维护。

## English

Load this Skill as a guest when the host is preparing or tidying and you want to help a little without disrupting their system. Offer **one specific action the host may decline**, do only the agreed task, and hand control back. If the host says no, stopping is a successful result. This does not teach cooking, cleaning chemicals, repairs, or hazardous equipment use.

### Preparation and inputs

Look at what the host is actually doing, a small action you can safely manage, your own physical access and energy, and the house rules you know. Someone else's cupboards, knives, hot pans, and cleaners are not an open toolbox. If no useful action is visible, you do not need to manufacture work by announcing help.

### Execution

1. **Offer one concrete, declineable action.** “Would it help if I put these empty cups on the table? If not, I can sit here.” That is easier to judge than “I can do anything.” Offer only what you can do safely in a space you may use.
2. **Wait for the scope.** If the host agrees, check which items, destination, and finish point. Do not rearrange their kitchen by your own household habits. If they say “no thanks,” say “okay,” sit, leave the route clear, or continue the agreed activity. Do not repeatedly campaign for a work permit.
3. **Do only the permitted action.** Handle items steadily. Stop and ask when heat, sharp tools, leaks, food safety, or equipment faults appear. Food handling and cleaning chemicals require their actual specific instructions; this Skill cannot replace them.
4. **Return control when done.** “The cups are on the table. Is this a good place for me to sit?” Do not open the next drawer or restyle a set table without asking. If you put something in the wrong place, restore it as directed instead of debating which layout is more efficient.
5. **Downgrade honestly when you cannot do it.** If strength, grip, vision, language, or time makes an action unreliable, say “I cannot do this one steadily. Is there a smaller task?” If none fits, do nothing. Company or simply leaving a clear route may help the visit work.

### Success

One small task the host authorized is finished in the agreed place and handed back, or you stopped when the host declined or you could not do it safely. You did not move private items without permission or create a second cleanup job in the name of help. Gratitude from the host is not required completion evidence.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The host said no, but you start washing dishes:** Stop and accept it. The host may already have a process; refusal is not a rating of your worth.
- **You put things in the wrong cupboard:** Acknowledge it briefly, ask for the right place, and restore them rather than continuing a treasure hunt.
- **You encounter a hot pan, knife, or unknown cleaner:** Do not replace instructions with courage. Stop and let the host handle it or choose an ordinary low-risk action.
- **The task keeps expanding:** Finish the agreed scope and ask again; do not promote yourself to kitchen project manager.
- **You cannot do it but worry that saying so is rude:** State what you can and cannot do. Step aside, sit, or offer a different small action you can actually finish.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This is an ordinary voluntary visit. Households differ about guests working, entering kitchens, and tidying; ask this host rather than inferring from region or relationship. If movement, senses, or language need another communication style, point to specific objects, use short text, or repeat the scope aloud. Physical risk is not proof of generosity.

You may genuinely help with one small task or succeed at staying seated when that is better. Both require less maintenance than redesigning the host's drawers without permission.
