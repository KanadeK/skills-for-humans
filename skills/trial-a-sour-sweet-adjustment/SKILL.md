## 中文

当你做好一道普通家常菜或甜品、**仍可确认安全**，但吃起来明显过酸或过甜时，用这份 Skill 在小样里找可行方向，再决定原份是否值得调整。目标是得到愿意吃且结构可接受的食物，或及时停止；加糖不会移除酸，加酸也不会移除糖。这里不凭味道判断罐藏、发酵、保存或医疗限糖食品是否安全。

### 准备与输入

先停止继续收浓或继续加原调味。确认原菜是什么、过量酸或甜大概来自哪个食材、原菜能否搅匀、当前是否处在按适用规则可安全尝味的阶段，以及最终想给谁吃。备好两个干净小碗、小勺和适合这道菜的未调味主体；需要试对比味道时，另备少量已知且可食用的甜或酸食材。留一份未改的小样作参照，并先看锅、容器和冷藏空间是否能接收新增份量。

如果原料、烹调或放置的安全状态不明，停止试吃和口味补救，按当地食品安全指导处理。婴幼儿食品、医疗饮食、罐藏和发酵配方不在本流程内；酸度或糖度在这些场景可能承担味道以外的作用。

### 执行

1. **确定到底是哪一头过量。** 食物已到安全可尝阶段时搅匀，取一小勺稍微降温后尝；与预期菜式和目标用餐者核对，是酸味压住其他味道、甜味盖过其他味道，还是两者都不合适。不要尝生肉、生蛋或未熟混合物来诊断。
2. **只取能放弃的一小份。** 把测试份放进干净小碗，原菜保持不动；另留一口原味作对照。记录测试份大概多少，以及加入了什么、多少。第一轮实验的失败成本只应是一小碗。
3. **先试兼容的未调味主体。** 对汤、酱、熟果料等可混合成品，在测试份中逐次加入少量本菜适用的未调味底料或熟食主体。它可能降低每口酸或甜的强度，也会改变稠度和总份量；水并非所有菜的合适底料。不能混合的成品则试较小份量搭配合适的无额外酸甜配食。
4. **如仍需调味，只在另一小样试一个方向。** 过酸且菜式允许时，可在新测试份里加极少量合适的甜味食材；过甜且菜式允许时，可在新测试份里加极少量合适的酸味食材。一次只变一个量，搅匀后在安全可尝阶段复尝，并比较原味与仅加主体的样本。互相遮盖味道不等于移除了原有酸或糖；酸还可能改变乳制品、酱汁或甜品的结构。
5. **按味道和结构一起裁决。** 问真正要吃的人是否接受测试份，检查是否变得太稀、结块、失去原菜用途或需要无法保存的大量新食物。只有样本确实可接受、原菜可按同样方式处理且容量够时，才把已记录的方法保守地用于更大一份，并边加边核对，不把小样比例当保证。
6. **交代剩余与停止。** 修不了的原菜若仍安全，可由愿意的人少量搭配别的食物食用；没有愿意吃的人或安全储存条件，就停止并丢弃。新增熟食按当地食品安全指导及时分装、冷却和保存。味道变好不能重置食品的时间和温度记录。

### 成功条件

至少有一份安全、目标用餐者愿意吃且质地仍可用的食物，能说清原菜、新增份量和剩余部分的去向。若小样没有改善或容量不足，及时停止整份调整也是完成判断。

### 常见报错与补救

- **把糖直接倒进整锅酸菜，或把酸直接倒进整锅甜品：** 停下；只在新小样里试一个变量，并保留原味对照。两股强味可以同时存在。
- **味道稍好，酱汁却变稀或乳品结块：** 样本判失败，换相容主体、改成小份搭配或停止；不要为了修酸甜把质地问题搬给下一位。
- **小样有效，但照比例一次加入整锅后又过头：** 只先扩大到一部分，逐次添加并复尝。小样记录是起点，不是整锅配方。
- **味觉不可靠或没有合适食材：** 请可信且会实际吃的人在安全阶段协助比较；仍不能判断时保留原菜、换餐或停止，不凭猜测持续添加。
- **试调拖久了或出现未知安全史：** 结束口味实验，按当地食品安全指导处理，别让可口度充当安全检测仪。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

不同人的酸甜偏好与菜式目标不同，不提供通用中和比例。糖、蜂蜜、柠檬、醋和乳品都可能不适合某些人或菜；只用已知相容且目标用餐者能吃的材料。加主体会增加份量，加酸甜食材也可能改变水分、稠度和香气；这份 Skill 只判断这一次成品的可吃性，不修改食品保存配方。

## English

Use this Skill when an ordinary finished dish or dessert is **still known to be safe** but tastes markedly too sour or too sweet. Find a workable direction in a small sample before deciding whether to adjust the original batch. The result is food someone wants to eat with an acceptable texture, or a timely stop. Sugar does not remove acid; acid does not remove sugar. Taste cannot establish the safety of canned or fermented food, stored food, or a medically restricted diet.

### Preparation and inputs

Stop reducing the dish or adding more of the original seasoning. Identify the dish, the likely source of excess sourness or sweetness, whether it can be mixed, whether it is at a stage safe to taste under applicable guidance, and who will eat it. Set out two clean small bowls and a spoon. Find an unseasoned base suited to this dish; for a comparison trial, have a tiny amount of a known edible sweet or sour ingredient. Keep one unchanged sample for reference. Check pan, container, and cold-storage capacity before creating more food.

If ingredient, cooking, or holding safety is uncertain, stop tasting and follow local food-safety guidance. Infant food, medical diets, canning, and fermentation formulas are outside this process: acid or sugar may have a role beyond flavour in those settings.

### Execution

1. **Identify which end is excessive.** At a stage safe to taste, mix the food, cool a clean spoonful, and compare it with the intended dish and actual diner's preference. Is sourness overwhelming, sweetness overwhelming, or are both unpleasant? Do not sample raw meat, egg, or an undercooked mixture to diagnose flavour.
2. **Take only a sample you can abandon.** Put a small trial portion in a clean bowl, leaving the original unchanged and another mouthful as reference. Note roughly how much you sampled and what and how much you add. A failed first experiment should cost one small bowl.
3. **Try compatible unseasoned bulk first.** For a mixable soup, sauce, or cooked fruit preparation, add a little suitable unseasoned base or safely edible cooked bulk to the sample in stages. This may reduce sour or sweet intensity per bite while changing thickness and total volume; water is not a suitable base for every dish. For finished food that cannot be mixed, try a smaller portion alongside a suitable side without extra sourness or sweetness.
4. **If needed, test one flavour direction in a separate sample.** For a too-sour dish that permits it, try a tiny amount of suitable sweetness in a new trial portion. For a too-sweet dish that permits it, try a tiny amount of suitable acid. Change one amount at a time, mix, taste again only when safe, and compare with the original and bulk-only sample. Masking a taste does not remove its original acid or sugar. Acid can also change the structure of dairy, sauces, or desserts.
5. **Judge flavour and structure together.** Ask the person who will eat it whether the sample works. Check for thinning, curdling, loss of its intended use, or an impractical quantity to store. Only if the sample works, the original can be handled the same way, and capacity exists should you apply the recorded method cautiously to a larger portion. Add in stages and reassess; the sample ratio is no guarantee for a whole batch.
6. **Account for leftovers and stopping.** A safe but unrepaired original may be eaten in a small pairing by someone who wants it. If nobody wants it or safe storage is unavailable, stop and discard it. Portion, cool, and store any extra cooked food promptly under local guidance. Better flavour does not reset the food's time and temperature history.

### Success

You have at least one safe portion the intended diner will eat with usable texture, and a clear destination for the original, added volume, and remainder. If the sample fails or capacity is insufficient, stopping before changing the whole batch is also a completed decision.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Sugar goes into the whole sour pot, or acid into the whole sweet dessert:** Stop. Change one variable in a fresh small sample and retain the original for comparison. Two strong flavours can coexist.
- **Flavour improves, but sauce thins or dairy curdles:** Treat the sample as a failure. Try a compatible base, a smaller pairing, or stop; do not pass a texture problem on to the next diner.
- **A successful sample ratio overshoots in the whole batch:** Scale only to part of the food first, add in stages, and retaste. The sample record is a starting point, not a guaranteed batch recipe.
- **Taste is unreliable or no suitable ingredient is available:** Ask a trusted person who will actually eat it to compare only at a safe stage. If it is still uncertain, retain the original, choose another meal, or stop rather than adding by guesswork.
- **Trials drag on or safety history becomes unknown:** End the flavour experiment and follow local food-safety guidance. Palatability is not a safety test.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Sour and sweet preferences and the intended dish differ, so there is no universal neutralizing ratio. Sugar, honey, lemon, vinegar, and dairy may be unsuitable for some diners or dishes; use only known compatible ingredients the intended diner can eat. Added bulk increases volume, while sweet or sour additions can alter moisture, thickness, and aroma. This Skill judges one finished dish's acceptability; it does not revise a food-preservation formula.
