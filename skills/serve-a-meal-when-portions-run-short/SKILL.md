## 中文

当你的一顿普通家庭餐**已经做好**，端盘前发现可安全提供的份量少于现在要吃的人时，用这份 Skill。目标是让每个人知道实际能得到什么，选择可行的补充或调整；它不能把一锅成品按人数缩放，也不负责开火前的配方换算。若成品本身安全性或熟度不明，先按食品安全流程处理，不把它计入可分配量。

### 准备与输入

确认实际到场人数、已完成每道菜的可食份量、是否有人已被承诺特定一份、何时开饭、手中可立即安全食用或能按可信说明及时做好的备用食物。询问同桌者是否愿意接受较小份量、补充配菜或延后；不要假定所有人需求相同。特殊医疗饮食和严重过敏的替换需既有安全方案，本篇不猜。

### 执行

1. **先把真实份量数清。** 用干净餐具按这餐实际可吃的量估算，不把装饰、未熟部分或准备留下的份额冒充完整一份。把菜先留在适用保温/冷藏条件下；讨论份量时不让易腐食物在台面无安排等待。
2. **区分“每人少一点”和“有人没有”。** 若安全成品足以分出大家接受的较小份量，先说明并均匀装盘；若缩小后仍有人没有可吃的餐食，不把空盘称为平均分配。已答应特定需求的一份，须先与相关人确认能否改动。
3. **核查补充路径。** 先看已经合格的即食食物或安全保存的成品，按包装、保存史和使用条件判断是否现在能用；必要时用一个实际可执行的无加热组合补充。需要现做的，只在有可靠做法、材料、设备、注意力和真实时间时开始，按该食物要求完成烹调。没有这些条件就不以半熟、猜测或猛火换速度。
4. **告诉同桌者实际方案。** 说清现有菜够几份、补充是什么、谁会等多久；让人选择能接受的较小份量、补充、延后或另找餐食。不同意时记录缺口，不替对方宣布“够了”。不把一份包装食物按外观当作所有人的医疗或过敏替代品。
5. **执行并结束。** 用干净餐具分出确认的份量，备用食物按其自身条件准备，上桌时再次确认数量。没有可行补充时，明确承认未满足的份数并改约、请各人自行安排或取消该次供应；及时处理已做食物的剩余部分。

### 成功条件、常见问题与停止补救

每位就餐者能看到一份实际可吃且其愿意接受的安排，或已明确知道当前仍缺多少份并作出下一步决定。若无法安全补足且有人没有可吃的餐食，停止按原计划分餐并说明缺口。已经安全端出的份量不靠隐藏大小差异维持“够吃”的幻觉。

- **计算时把生食或未核实剩菜也算进去：** 立刻从可用份量中移除，按该食物的安全流程另行判断。
- **补充食物需要烹调但来不及：** 告知真实延时，改选已核实可即食的食物，或承认缺口。
- **第一盘盛得太多：** 暂停分配，与同桌者协调尚未盛出的份量；不从别人已接触的盘里回收共享。
- **有人不接受替换：** 尊重其决定，另找可行安排；不能用“只是配菜”抹掉已经承诺的餐食。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里是普通家庭餐，不给人人适用的克数、营养或医疗建议。文化、食量、费用、口味和能否自取餐不同；可用文字、大字或口头说明份量，并安排可信协助分餐。谁不方便等、谁需要独立可吃的一份，要由当事人表达。增加配菜意味着更多准备和可能的剩余食物，按当地食品安全要求处理。

## English

Use this Skill when an ordinary household meal is **already cooked**, but just before serving you find fewer safe portions than people now waiting to eat. The goal is for everyone to know what is actually available and to choose a workable supplement or adjustment. It cannot scale a cooked pot by changing the headcount, and it does not redo recipe calculations from before cooking. If doneness or safety is uncertain, use the food-safety workflow first; do not count that food as available.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm who is actually eating, each finished dish's edible portions, whether a particular portion was promised, the serving time, and backup food that can be eaten safely now or prepared in time under reliable directions. Ask diners whether a smaller share, added side, or delay works for them; do not assume everyone has the same needs. Changes for medical diets or severe allergies need an existing safe plan, not a guess from this Skill.

### Execution

1. **Count real portions.** Use clean serving ware to estimate what people can actually eat at this meal. Decoration, undercooked pieces, and food reserved for another commitment do not become full portions. Keep food under applicable safe hot or cold conditions while deciding; do not leave perishables unplanned on a counter.
2. **Separate smaller shares from missing meals.** If the finished safe food can give everyone an accepted smaller portion, say so and plate evenly. If downsizing still leaves someone without a meal, an empty plate is not equal sharing. Check with anyone promised a particular portion before changing it.
3. **Verify a supplement.** First consider qualified ready-to-eat food or safely stored cooked food; check its pack, holding history, and current use conditions. An actual no-heat combination may fill the gap. Start extra cooking only with reliable directions, ingredients, equipment, attention, and enough real time to meet that food's cooking requirements. Do not trade safety for speed with undercooking, guesses, or excessive heat.
4. **Tell diners the actual plan.** Say how many portions exist, what the supplement is, and how long anyone will wait. Let people choose an acceptable smaller share, supplement, delay, or another meal. If they decline, record the remaining gap rather than declaring success for them. A packaged food is not automatically a suitable medical or allergy substitute.
5. **Serve and finish.** Plate the agreed portions with clean tools, prepare backup food under its own conditions, and check the count again at service. If no supplement works, name the number still missing and reschedule, let people arrange another meal, or cancel this service. Handle any retained cooked food under local food-safety guidance.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

Each diner can see an actual edible arrangement they accept, or knows how many portions are still missing and has made a next-step choice. If no safe supplement can leave everyone with a meal, stop plating the original plan and name the gap. Safe servings do not depend on hiding their size to keep up an appearance of plenty.

- **Raw food or unverified leftovers were counted:** Remove them from available portions now and assess them under their own safety workflow.
- **A supplement needs cooking but time has run out:** State the real delay, choose verified ready-to-eat food, or acknowledge the gap.
- **The first plates were overserved:** Pause and coordinate the food not yet plated. Do not reclaim shared food from plates people have touched.
- **Someone rejects the replacement:** Respect that choice and find another feasible arrangement. Calling it “only a side” does not erase a promised meal.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is an ordinary household meal, without one universal portion weight or medical nutrition advice. Culture, appetite, cost, taste, and ability to self-serve differ. Explain the portion plan in speech, writing, or large print and arrange trusted serving help when useful. People themselves say whether they can wait or need a separate edible option. Extra sides mean more preparation and possibly leftovers, which still need local food-safety handling.
