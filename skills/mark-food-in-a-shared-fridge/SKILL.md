## 中文

当你把自己的普通食品放进多人共用的冰箱，需要让别人知道**归谁、能不能取用**时，加载这份 Skill。完成结果是一条可读的归属与权限信息，或一个大家已同意的专属区域；姓名贴纸不会替食物保冷，也不会延长日期。身体安全、包装、冷藏位置和开封后期限仍按食品标签及相应任务处理。

### 准备与输入

先确认这台冰箱是家庭共用、合租共用，还是学校/单位等有明文规则的公用冰箱。查看是否规定标签内容、清理周期、禁放物和联系方法；机构规则只适用于那台冰箱。家庭没有约定时，和同住者商量用姓名/昵称标签、分区，或两者结合，并明确“共享”意味着谁可以取、是否要先问。没有同意之前，你的食品默认是私人物品，别把沉默当成共享许可。

拿到食品原包装，确认食物名称、开封状态、真实放入日期和需要保留的保存说明。选择能在潮湿环境里看清、但不会遮住原标签的纸条、容器标签或可及标识。公开空间不必写住址、完整电话或其他不必要的个人资料；若场地要求联系信息，核对规定并选择你愿意提供的联系渠道。

### 执行

1. **先让食物符合冷藏条件。** 按原标签要求及时冷藏，盖好或封好，防止生食滴漏到即食食品。归属标记不替代温度、容器或生熟分区；不能安全放进冰箱时先处理保存问题，不靠贴名字宣布完成。
2. **写出最小可辨认信息。** 标上同住者能识别的姓名、昵称或已约定代号，食品名称及实际放入日期。已经开封且知道开封日时可写明，但不捏造不知道的日期；原包装的使用日期、批次和储存说明仍应可回读。
3. **单独写权限。** 私人物品写“本人使用 / 请先问”；双方同意共享的写“共享”及必要边界，例如“大家可用，剩最后一份先问”。“共享”需要真实约定，不因放在公共架层就自动成立。若机构规定不能共享或有特定格式，按当地规则填写。
4. **放到约定的位置。** 已同意分区的按分区摆放；用标签的让字朝向可见处。无论谁的区域，都不堵通风、让门关不严，或把生食放到可能滴落在即食食品上的地方。空间安排与安全分区不能由所有权标签改写。
5. **读回并留维护入口。** 从别人的视角检查能否判断“是什么、谁的、可否取、何时放入”。标签脱落、食品转移容器或权限改变时更新受影响的那一件；若新室友尚未知道规则，用一句话告知规则在哪里，而不是要求他们猜颜色代码。

### 完成条件

你放入的食品有可辨认的归属与取用权限，或位于双方已同意、含义明确的区域；食品身份和原有保存信息没有被遮掉，冷藏条件仍独立符合要求。别人的同意没有被你代签。若共享规则暂未达成，标为私人并用不妨碍食品安全的方式单独放置，继续协商。

### 常见报错与补救

- **“放中间就是共享”：** 位置不能代替同意；补一条明确的私有/共享标识或共同规则。
- **纸条只写人名，盒里是什么无人知道：** 加食品名称并保留原标签信息；给安全日期另作真实核对。
- **标签盖住原厂保存说明：** 移动标签，不用自写日期覆盖生产商原文。
- **冰箱表面湿，贴纸掉了：** 换能固定在容器或袋上的标签，或使用大家认可的分区；没有可靠标识时不要假装权限已传达。
- **室友对共享范围理解不同：** 暂按私有处理，约定哪些食品、哪些人和什么情况下可用；不能靠“反正会补”解决未同意的取用。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

家庭、合租和学校/单位冰箱的管理权不同。Weill Cornell Housing 把分区或共同选择的标签列为可行方式；NYU 一处公用冰箱要求姓名和日期，但那是该场地自己的规则，不能推广到所有住所。视觉、阅读或握笔不便时可以用大字、触觉或可读屏清单配合固定位置，只要同用冰箱的人能理解且不遮住食品安全信息。

现实副作用是多一张标签或一个约定；较少的一种副作用是别人误以为你的早餐已获公开许可。

## English

Load this Skill when you put your own ordinary food into a refrigerator used by several people and need them to know **whose it is and whether they may use it**. The result is readable ownership and permission information, or a clearly agreed personal zone. A name label does not chill food or extend its date. Follow the actual food label and separate storage tasks for safety, packaging, position, and after-opening limits.

### Preparation and inputs

Find out whether this is a household, roommate, school, or workplace refrigerator. Check any local rules for label fields, clean-outs, prohibited items, and contact methods; an institution's rule applies to its own fridge. If a household has no system, discuss name or alias labels, zones, or both, and what “shared” lets each person take without asking. Until people agree, treat your food as private. Silence is not permission to share it.

Keep the original package available and identify the food, opening state, actual placement date, and storage directions that must remain visible. Choose paper, a container tag, or an accessible marker that remains readable in a damp fridge without covering the manufacturer's label. A communal space does not require your address or full phone number unless its actual policy requires a contact route; check that rule and choose a contact method you are willing to provide.

### Execution

1. **Meet the food's cold-storage requirements first.** Chill it promptly under its label, cover or seal it, and prevent raw juices dripping onto ready-to-eat food. Ownership marks do not replace temperature, a suitable container, or raw-food separation. If it cannot be stored safely, solve that problem before calling a name label completion.
2. **Write the smallest identifying set.** Add a name, alias, or agreed code others can recognize, the food name, and its actual placement date. Add a known opening date if relevant, but never invent a missing one. Keep the original use date, batch, and storage information readable.
3. **Mark permission separately.** Say “private / ask first” for private food. Use “shared” only after agreement, with any needed boundary such as “anyone may use; ask before the last portion.” A common shelf does not grant consent. Follow an institution's specific format or no-sharing rule when it applies.
4. **Use the agreed location.** Place food in a mutually understood zone, or face the label outward. Whatever the owner, do not block cold airflow, stop the door closing, or put raw food where it could drip onto ready-to-eat food. Ownership cannot override physical safety zoning.
5. **Read it back and keep it current.** From another resident's perspective, check whether “what, whose, may I use it, when placed” is clear. Update the affected item when a label falls off, food changes container, or permission changes. Show a new roommate where the rule is rather than expecting them to decode colours.

### Success

Your item has identifiable ownership and use permission or occupies a mutually agreed zone with a clear meaning. Its identity and original storage information remain visible, and cold storage still meets independent safety requirements. You have not signed anyone else's consent. If the sharing rule is unsettled, mark the item private, place it without compromising safety, and continue the discussion.

### Common errors and recovery

- **“The middle shelf means shared”:** Location cannot replace agreement. Add an explicit private/shared mark or common rule.
- **The note has only a name; no one knows what is inside:** Add the food name and keep original package information. Assess safety dates from real evidence separately.
- **The sticker covers manufacturer instructions:** Move it; a handwritten date does not replace the original words.
- **Moisture makes the label fall:** Attach a tag to the container or bag, or use an agreed zone. Do not claim that a failed label transmitted permission.
- **Roommates disagree on what shared means:** Treat it as private for now and agree which food, people, and uses are covered. “I'll replace it” is not consent to take it.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Homes, roommate arrangements, and institutional fridges have different managers. Weill Cornell Housing suggests zones or mutually chosen labels; one NYU communal fridge requires names and dates. Neither is a rule for every home. Use large print, tactile marking, or a screen-readable list plus a fixed location if writing or reading is difficult, so other fridge users can understand it without hiding food-safety information.

The side effect is one label or agreement and perhaps fewer mistaken claims that your breakfast was offered to the public.
