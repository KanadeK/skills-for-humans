## 中文

当你已经在允许参观的展厅，时间有限又不想只匆忙扫过所有标签时，用这份 Skill 选**一件此刻在展、你能安全接近的作品**认真看一轮。结果是你自己观察到的三个细节和一个问题，不需要猜出策展人的“正确答案”，也不要求拍照、画草图或购买纪念品。事前挑场馆和展区是另一任务。

### 准备与输入

先看展厅是否开放、作品周围有无围栏或特别规则、是否允许书写/摄影；遵守现场工作人员要求。选一件让你停住的作品或展示物，可以是形状、材料、故事引起兴趣，不限名作。找不挡通道、不靠近作品到危险距离、可坐或站一会儿的位置；若视觉、听觉或移动有限，查看场馆是否提供放大文字、口述说明、音频文字稿、座椅或工作人员协助。不要求裸眼看清你实际看不见的细节。

### 执行

1. **先不看完整解说。** 在允许位置用一小段安静时间看整体，问“我第一眼注意到哪里？”记一个可见的颜色、形状、材质或布局，不先给它贴历史结论。
2. **找三个具体细节。** 沿不同部位再观察两处，例如边缘重复、材料接缝、光线或人物之间的方向。每个用能指回作品的短句记录；“很有文化”“我不懂艺术”不是具体观察。不要摸展品或跨护栏求近看。
3. **再读允许的说明。** 看标签、官方导览或可及说明，核对作品名称、作者/年代（若展示）及馆方已证实的背景。区分“我看到了____”和“标签告诉我____”；不把自己的联想写成馆方事实。
4. **留下一个真问题。** 写“我想知道为什么____”或“这处材料是怎样____”，也可以问工作人员是否有公开说明。即使没有答案，一处有对象指向的问题也比速刷十个展签更可继续。若展厅太拥挤或不适，换开放的安静位置或结束，不硬挤。
5. **按现场规则收工。** 记作品名/展区（允许时）以便以后在馆方资料中找回；不需要给作品拍照或复制大段标签。让出观看位置，按场馆路线继续或离开。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你能指出这件真实展品、三个自己确实观察过的细节，以及一个还想了解的问题，并遵守现场边界。看完一整面标签但说不出作品上的一处具体细节，还需回到观察。

- **计划看的作品不在展：** 选同厅另一件实际在展的对象；网站图片不是现场承诺。
- **标签内容太多，忘了自己看到什么：** 先写“我看到”，再写“标签说”，用两行分开。
- **展厅拥挤、无法靠近：** 在允许距离看整体或换较安静展区，不能因此跨界触摸。
- **看不清又没有合适可及说明：** 向馆员/工作人员询问辅助资源；仍无法核对时如实停下，不编造三个观察。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

展厅的开放、摄影、声音、距离和辅助服务随馆而异。可用短文字、语音便签（场馆允许时）、可信口述协助或记忆复述代替写字；不得录下旁人私人谈话。你的联想可以有价值，但与观察和馆方证据分开。此处完成一次聚焦观看，不代表掌握艺术史或获得展品复制权。

## English

Use this Skill when you are already inside an open museum gallery, have limited time, and do not want to rush past every label. Choose **one object actually on view that you can examine safely** and spend a focused stop with it. Leave with three details you observed and one question. You need not guess a curator's “right answer,” take a photo, draw, or buy anything. Choosing the venue and gallery beforehand is a separate task.

### Preparation and inputs

Check that the gallery is open, whether the object has barriers or special rules, and whether writing or photography is allowed. Follow staff directions. Choose an object that draws you through shape, material, or story; fame is not required. Find a place to stand or sit briefly without blocking a passage, touching a work, or approaching past a safe boundary. For sight, hearing, or mobility needs, check whether the venue offers enlarged text, verbal description, audio transcripts, seating, or staff support. You need not pretend to see details you cannot see.

### Execution

1. **Look before reading all the text.** From an allowed spot, quietly view the whole object. Ask where your eye first went. Note one visible colour, shape, material, or arrangement before declaring a historical conclusion.
2. **Find three specific details.** Examine two other areas, perhaps repeated edges, joins, light, or directions between figures. Describe each in a short phrase you could point back to. “Very cultural” or “I know nothing about art” are not observations. Do not touch the work or cross a barrier for a closer look.
3. **Then read allowed information.** Use the label, official guide, or accessible description to check displayed title, maker or date if given, and documented context. Keep “I saw ____” separate from “the label says ____”; a personal association is not a museum fact.
4. **Leave one real question.** Write “I wonder why ____” or “how was this material ____?” You may ask staff if a public explanation exists. Even without an answer, a question tied to the object offers a next path. If the gallery is too crowded or uncomfortable, move to another open quiet area or stop rather than force your way through.
5. **Close under venue rules.** Note title or gallery location if permitted so you can find official material later. A photo or a long copied label is unnecessary. Leave viewing space for others, then continue or exit by the venue route.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You can identify the actual on-view object, three details you truly observed, and one question, while keeping to venue rules. Reading a wall of text yet naming no feature of the work means you still need an observation pass.

- **The planned object is off view:** Choose another actual object in an open area. A website image is no on-site promise.
- **The label overwhelms your own view:** Put “I saw” and “the label says” on two separate lines.
- **Crowds prevent a close look:** Observe the whole from an allowed distance or switch to a quieter area, without crossing a barrier or touching.
- **You cannot see enough and lack usable access information:** Ask staff for supported descriptions. If you still cannot check, stop honestly rather than invent three details.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Gallery openings, photography, sound, distance, and access services differ by venue. Use short text, a voice note where allowed, trusted description, or a memory recap instead of handwriting; do not record others' private conversations. Associations can matter, but keep them apart from direct observation and museum evidence. One focused look is not mastery of art history or permission to reproduce the work.
