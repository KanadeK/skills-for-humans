## 中文

当你确实缺一条普通信息，想问另一个人，却发现自己会把背景、猜测、请求和多个问题全挤进一句话时加载。目标是**说出一项真实缺口、一句必要背景和一个对方能回答或明确说不知道的问题**。这不是索取帮助、追问对方隐私、加入陌生群谈或检验对方是否在乎你；对方无需按你的期待回答，提问本身可由你完成。

### 准备与输入

先写“我具体不知道__；知道后我能做__”。可从公开、方便的场所说明获得的事实先自己看一眼，不把本可查的整份说明都转给一个正在忙的人。判断谁实际上可能知道且适合被问，是否可通过口头、文字或正式服务渠道联系。不要问对方不应分享的私人/第三方资料，或要求他们替专业人员做判断。你自己希望多久需要答复，可以如实说明，但不能变成对方的义务。

### 执行

1. **把一个问题从一串问题里抽出来。** “怎么去、带什么、你几点来、能不能帮我”有多个结果，先选当前最需要的一项。把自己的猜测从事实中分开；真的不确定就说不确定，不在问句里塞预设答案。
2. **给最少但够用的背景。** 提供对方判断问题所需的地点、日期、对象或你已试过的普通方法中的一两项。避免长篇自我辩护和无关细节，也不公开自己的账号、完整地址或他人私事。
3. **问一句可回答的话。** 模板：“我在__，想确认__。你知道__吗？不知道也没关系。” 需要二选一时只给真实选项，别写“你也觉得 A 比 B 好吧？”来诱导。具体日期/地点比“那个时候、那里”更容易回答。
4. **给对方合适的出口。** 不急时可先问“现在方便问一件小事吗？”；文字消息里说明什么时候需要，而不规定对方立刻回复。听觉、视觉、语言或注意力条件不同，可以改用双方都能理解的文字、简短语音或指示图，不强求眼神接触/即兴回答。
5. **按实际答案收口。** 对方回答时复述关键名词/时间一次，确定自己没把“可能”记成“确定”；回答不全只补一个紧接的澄清。对方不知道、拒绝或没回，就记“仍未知”，感谢或换合适的正式来源，不连发催问直到得到想要的答案。

### 完成与停止

你向合适对象发出了有必要背景、只含一个真实缺口、允许不知道或不答的问题，并能把答复/未答复准确记下，本轮完成。**成功不是让对方必须告诉你、同意你或改变想法。** 若涉及敏感隐私、专业医疗/法律/财务判断、对方已明确不愿继续或关系出现威胁，退出这份日常提问流程，使用相应正式渠道或停止。

### 常见报错与补救

- **问题太宽：“你怎么看这件事？”：** 说明具体哪一方面的信息会帮助你做下一步决定。
- **一条消息连发六问：** 先选关键一问，待有真实答复后再决定其他问题是否还存在。
- **问句偷偷写了想要的答案：** 改成中性的事实问题，让“没有/不知道/不方便说”都能成立。
- **对方答了“可能”，你写成“确认”：** 回读一次不确定性，必要时去正式信息源核查。
- **消息已读但没有回复：** 当作尚未知，不作为同意或拒绝；在合适时机另查来源，不无限催促。

### 假设、替代与副作用

适用于自愿的普通日常信息交流，不提供操控、心理治疗、危机筛查或跨文化统一礼节。关系、语言、设备、场合和回答时间因人而异；简短不代表粗鲁，背景不足也需要补足。若说话、听力、视觉、阅读或现场环境不适合，可换双方能用的文字/语音/正式窗口，不把一种沟通方式当标准。副作用是你可能收到“我不知道”，它比一个被误记成事实的猜测更有用。

## English

Load this Skill when you genuinely need one ordinary fact from someone but find yourself packing background, guesses, a request and several questions into one sentence. Your result is **one real gap, enough context and a question the person can answer or honestly say they do not know**. This is not asking them to do a task, extracting private information, entering a group conversation or testing whether they care. They do not have to answer as you hope; asking well is within your control.

### Preparation and inputs

Write “I do not know __; knowing it lets me __.” Check easy public venue information yourself first when practical rather than transferring an entire document to a busy person. Identify who might actually know and whether speech, text or a formal service channel suits the moment. Do not ask for private third-party information they should not share or a professional decision they cannot make. State when you need an answer if relevant, without making your deadline their duty.

### Execution

1. **Extract one question from a bundle.** “How do I get there, what do I bring, when will you come, and can you help?” asks for several outcomes. Choose the one needed for your next decision. Separate guess from fact, and say when you are uncertain instead of planting an answer in the wording.
2. **Give the smallest useful context.** Share one or two of the place, date, object or ordinary step already tried that the person needs to understand the question. Avoid a long defense or irrelevant details, and do not post account data, full private addresses or other people's affairs.
3. **Ask one answerable sentence.** Template: “I am at __ and need to confirm __. Do you know __? It is okay if you don't.” For two options, use genuine choices, not “You agree A is better, right?” A date or named place beats “that time” or “over there.”
4. **Leave a real exit.** For a nonurgent spoken question, “Is now a good time for one small question?” may help. In text, state when the information matters without ordering an immediate reply. For hearing, sight, language or attention needs, use mutually usable text, a short voice note or a labelled picture rather than demanding eye contact or an improvised answer.
5. **Close on the actual reply.** Read back one key name or time so “maybe” is not recorded as “confirmed.” Ask only one narrow follow-up if the answer is partial. For “I don't know,” refusal or silence, mark the gap still open, thank them or use a better official source instead of chasing the answer you wanted.

### Success and stop

You sent a suitable person a question with enough context, one real gap and permission not to know or answer, then accurately recorded its answer or no-answer state. **Success does not require them to disclose, agree or change their mind.** Leave this ordinary task for private data, specialist medical, legal or financial judgment, an explicit wish not to continue, or a threatening situation; use a suitable formal route or stop.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Too broad: “What do you think about this?”:** Name the specific piece of information that changes your next action.
- **Six questions in one message:** Choose the critical one first, then decide after a real reply whether any others remain.
- **The preferred answer hides inside the question:** Make it a neutral fact request where “no,” “unknown” or “not comfortable sharing” remain possible.
- **They say “perhaps” and you record “confirmed”:** Read back the uncertainty and verify from an official source when needed.
- **A read message gets no reply:** Treat it as unknown, not consent or refusal. Find another source at a suitable time rather than endlessly nudging.

### Assumptions, alternatives and side effects

This is voluntary everyday information exchange, not manipulation, therapy, crisis screening or a universal social script. Relationship, language, device, situation and reply time vary. Brief can be respectful, while missing context still needs adding. For speech, hearing, sight, reading or setting needs, use text, voice or an official counter both people can manage. “I don't know” is often more useful than a guess falsely stored as fact.
