## 中文

当购物袋已经进门，而你正考虑“先坐五分钟应该没事吧”时加载这份 Skill。目标是让每件食物得到一个位置、一个使用优先级，或一个诚实的“情况不明，先别吃”。

### 准备与输入

先洗手，腾出一块干净台面。确认：

- 每件食物是什么、是否开封、包装是否渗漏或损坏；
- 标签上的冷藏、冷冻、开封后储存和日期说明；
- 从购买到现在经过多久，期间是否保持低温；
- 冰箱、冷冻室和阴凉干燥处的可用空间；
- 这几天准备吃什么，以及家里是否已有更早买的同类食物。

看不清标签时拍照放大或请人读出关键信息。搬动重物或弯腰困难时，先把常用和较轻物品安排到容易拿取的位置；收纳系统不应要求你每天参加冰箱障碍赛。

### 执行

1. **先处理需要低温的食物。** 肉、海鲜、乳制品、熟食和其他标签要求冷藏/冷冻的东西优先，不先整理纸巾、罐头或收据。
2. **做一次异常检查。** 渗漏、鼓胀、严重破损、超过安全使用日期、长时间温度史不明的食物先隔离。不要让它在整理过程中接触其他食物。
3. **服从标签。** 包装给出的储存方式和开封后要求优先于通用经验。适合常温的食物放在清洁、干燥、远离热源与日化用品的位置。
4. **防止生食滴漏。** 生肉、禽肉和海鲜密封或放入防漏容器，置于即食、熟食和已清洗食材下方。重力不会阅读你的菜单。
5. **冷冻前先考虑未来用途。** 只有食物和标签适合冷冻、你也有可用空间时再分装冷冻。写上名称和日期；按一顿能用完的份量分装，减少反复解冻。
6. **给冷空气留路。** 不把冰箱塞到无法循环空气，也不让门因为一颗西瓜进入半关闭状态。需要降温的熟食使用合适的浅容器并尽快按当地指导冷却储存。
7. **建立先吃顺序。** 综合安全使用日期、开封状态、易腐程度、旧库存和计划餐食排序。“最佳食用期”通常关注品质，但不能推翻储存说明或腐败迹象。
8. **让旧的站前面。** 同类且都安全时，把更早的放到容易看见的位置。先进先出是管理方法，不是要求你把可疑食物吃掉以完成指标。
9. **结束前清点。** 每项应有明确目的地、容器/隔离方式、标签日期、使用优先级或待核实问题。

### 成功条件

所有需要低温的食物已经进入合适位置；生食不会滴到即食食物；日化用品与食物分开；你能一眼找到最先要用的三样东西。购物袋可以退役，不必成为永久家具。

### 常见报错与补救

- **ERR_NO_COLD_SPACE：** 不要硬塞。优先移除过期或确认不再使用的物品；把适合立即烹调的安排进最近一餐，或减少本次保留量。
- **标签与习惯冲突：** 服从具体产品标签。仍不清楚时查当地官方指导或联系生产商。
- **不知道离开冷藏多久：** 外观和气味不能重建时间线。隔离并按保守的当地食品安全建议处理。
- **生肉包装漏了：** 套入防漏容器，清洁被污染表面和双手；受污染的即食食品不要靠冲洗“恢复”。
- **忘了哪天开封：** 今天补写标签，但不要捏造过去日期。无法判断且风险较高时选择丢弃。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

冰箱布局、门格温度、冷冻能力和本地日期标签含义不同；以你的设备说明、产品标签和当地权威为准。若嗅觉、视觉或记忆不能提供某个线索，依赖日期、包装、温度记录和他人协助，不把感官差异当成错误。

现实副作用包括几只要清洗的容器、几张标签，以及明天打开冰箱时短暂体验到“系统真的有状态”。

## English

Load this Skill when the shopping bags are home and “sitting down for five minutes” is becoming the leading storage proposal. Every item should receive a location, a use-first priority, or an honest “history unclear; do not eat yet.”

### Preparation and inputs

Wash your hands and clear a clean work surface. Confirm:

- what each item is, whether it is open, and whether the package leaks or is damaged;
- label instructions for chilling, freezing, opening, and dates;
- time since purchase and whether cold food stayed cold;
- usable refrigerator, freezer, and cool dry-storage space;
- meals planned soon and older versions of the same food already at home.

Enlarge a label photo or ask someone to read key details if needed. If lifting or bending is difficult, place lighter and frequently used items within easy reach. A storage system should not require a daily refrigerator obstacle course.

### Execution

1. **Handle cold food first.** Prioritize meat, seafood, dairy, cooked food, and anything labelled for refrigeration or freezing. Paper goods, tins, and receipts can wait.
2. **Check for exceptions.** Isolate leaks, swelling, major damage, safety dates that have passed, and food with an unknown period out of temperature control. Keep it away from other food while deciding.
3. **Follow the label.** Product-specific storage and after-opening instructions beat general habit. Store suitable shelf-stable goods somewhere clean, dry, away from heat and household chemicals.
4. **Contain raw-food drips.** Seal raw meat, poultry, and seafood or place them in leak-resistant containers below ready-to-eat, cooked, and cleaned food. Gravity does not read your meal plan.
5. **Freeze for a future use.** Freeze only when the food and label allow it and space is available. Mark the name and date; divide into amounts you can use at once so you do not repeatedly thaw it.
6. **Leave room for cold air.** Do not pack the refrigerator so tightly that air cannot circulate or the door negotiates with a watermelon. Use suitable shallow containers and local guidance when cooling cooked food.
7. **Create a use-first order.** Combine safety dates, opened state, perishability, older stock, and planned meals. A best-before date commonly concerns quality, but it never overrides storage instructions or spoilage evidence.
8. **Put safe older stock in front.** First-in, first-out is an organizing tool, not a requirement to eat doubtful food for metric completion.
9. **Account for every item.** It needs a destination, container/separation choice, label/date, use priority, or unresolved question.

### Success

Every food that needs cold storage is in an appropriate place; raw items cannot drip onto ready-to-eat food; household chemicals are separate; and you can identify the next three foods to use. The shopping bag can retire instead of joining the furniture.

### Common errors and recovery

- **ERR_NO_COLD_SPACE:** Do not force the door. Remove expired or confirmed unwanted items, schedule suitable food for the next meal, or reduce what you keep.
- **Label and habit disagree:** Follow the product label. Use local official guidance or contact the producer when it remains unclear.
- **Time outside refrigeration is unknown:** Appearance and smell cannot reconstruct the timeline. Isolate the item and follow conservative local food-safety advice.
- **Raw packaging leaked:** Put it in a leak-resistant container and clean affected hands and surfaces. Do not “restore” contaminated ready-to-eat food by rinsing it.
- **You forgot the opening date:** Label it today without inventing the past. Discard when the uncertainty and food risk make a safe decision impossible.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Refrigerator zones, door temperatures, freezing capability, and date-label meanings vary. Follow your appliance manual, product label, and local authority. If smell, vision, or memory cannot provide a clue, use dates, packaging, temperature history, or another person's accessible observation instead.

Known side effects include containers to wash, labels to write, and a brief moment tomorrow when the refrigerator appears to have state management.
