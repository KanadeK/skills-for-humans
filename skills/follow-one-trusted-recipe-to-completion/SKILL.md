## 中文

当你已经选定一份**步骤完整、来源可靠、适合普通家庭制作**的配方，想从第一步做到有依据的成品时，用这份 Skill。它管理一次配方执行中的单位、先后、检查与停止；不替代某种技法的专门教学，也不按菜名重新编食谱。罐藏、发酵、酒精、危险刀工和医疗饮食不在这里尝试。

### 准备与输入

从头到尾读完原配方：产量、原料状态和用量、量具单位、所需器具、事先准备、每步顺序、需要等待的阶段、完成信号和剩余食物安排。把配方来源、日期与具体产品/设备说明保留在旁；不同单位不能凭直觉互换。若有生动物性食品，确认配方提供或能从当地权威取得相应的安全熟度检查方法；只写“看起来熟了”而缺关键安全信息时不要开做。

### 执行

1. **先核对这份配方是否能执行。** 食品状态可靠、量够、器具/容器真实可用、步骤没有需要你不会安全操作的技术，才能开始。缺一项时先用相邻的备餐就位、份量或食材替代任务解决；无法解决就选另一份可信配方，而不是边加热边猜。
2. **把用量与动作对应起来。** 按配方原单位量取。留意“先量后切”和“先切后量”等措辞差异；粉、液体和整件数使用适合的量具与可读刻度。需要换份数时先完成独立的配方调整，不在热锅旁心算小数。
3. **一次只执行当前节点。** 给当前步骤标出要加入什么、是否需先完成上一阶段、用哪个器具、什么观察点才进入下一步。按配方处理预热、混合、等待或搅动；计时器是提醒检查，不是允许跳过状态观察的印章。
4. **保持食品安全动作在流程里。** 按实际食物洗手、清洁并让生食与即食食品分开；用该配方、产品及当地权威适用的熟度检查完成需烹调的食物。颜色、气味、时间或“看起来差不多”不能替代必须的安全证据。热源、刀具或电器异常时先安全停止，别为了完成配方硬撑。
5. **把偏差记成明确决定。** 食材少了、锅具变了、配方看不清，先暂停进入下一高风险步骤。能按可信替代/器具方法处理的，记录调整和后续检查；没有依据则停或改做法。已经做坏的成品交给对应补救流程，不能倒填“原配方本来如此”。
6. **按完成信号结束并交接。** 做到配方与食品安全要求的成品状态后关热源、稳放热器具、按配方静置/装盘。准备立即食用；需要保留的部分按实际食品和当地指导及时处理。写下这次哪些步骤确实执行、哪些改变了，供下次判断，而不是给未完成的菜贴“成功”标签。

### 成功条件

你得到一份和可信配方目标一致、关键单位与先后可追溯、需熟食物已按适用方法检查、热源与剩余食物有去向的成品。若配方或设备缺少必要安全证据，明确停止并说明停在哪一步，也是一次完成的判断，不冒称成品已做好。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只看原料表就开火，后面才发现要提前浸泡/预热：** 在安全时暂停，重读全步骤并重新判断时间；不能赶上就改制作或开饭计划。
- **杯、克、毫升混在同一计算里：** 返回原配方的单位和适用量具；换算依据不明就停止数值替代。
- **计时结束就宣告熟了：** 把提醒变成实际检查，按食品与当地要求取得熟度证据；不能取得时不端出需要彻底烹调的食物。
- **一处失败后继续补料掩盖：** 先确认食物安全与原配方关键结构，确属安全质量问题再进专门补救；未知历史不能靠调味修复。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里的“可靠”指来源、原料、关键动作和安全完成条件可核实，不等于必须是某一个国家的名厨。家庭口味、设备、海拔与文化会改变做法和结果；产品标签与当地食品安全规则优先。视力、听力、握力或持续站立不便时可放大配方、分成可读步骤卡、用震动/视觉计时、在可及工作面操作或请可信的人协助；任何辅助都不自动证明食物已熟。副作用可能有用过的器具和一份记录，但配方终于有了可追溯的执行状态。

## English

Use this Skill after choosing a **complete, trustworthy recipe suitable for ordinary home cooking** when you want to carry it from the first action to a justified finished result. It manages units, order, checks, and stops during one execution. It does not replace a specialist technique or write a new recipe for every dish. Canning, fermentation, alcohol, hazardous knife work, and medical diets are outside it.

### Preparation and inputs

Read the entire source recipe: yield, food condition and quantities, units, tools, advance preparation, sequence, waiting stages, finish cues, and plan for any food kept afterward. Keep its source and any exact product or appliance directions visible. Do not guess conversions between unlike units. For raw animal food, confirm the recipe supplies or a local authority supports the necessary safe doneness check. Do not start if a safety-critical step is only “looks cooked” with no way to verify it.

### Execution

1. **Decide if this recipe can run here.** Begin only when food condition is known, amounts suffice, tools and containers work, and no step requires a technique you cannot perform safely. Resolve missing inputs through the separate workstation, portions, or ingredient-substitution tasks. If that fails, choose another trusted recipe rather than guessing next to a hot pan.
2. **Match quantities to actions.** Measure in the recipe's stated units. Wording such as “measure, then cut” differs from “cut, then measure.” Use appropriate tools and visible markings for dry quantities, liquids, and whole items. Resize a recipe in the separate task before heat, not with hot-pan decimal arithmetic.
3. **Execute the current node, then advance.** For each step, name what is added, which earlier stage must be complete, the tool needed, and the observable cue that permits the next step. Follow its preheat, mixing, resting, and stirring directions. A timer reminds you to inspect; it is not a stamp proving the food's condition.
4. **Keep food safety inside the flow.** Wash hands and tools, keep raw and ready-to-eat food apart, and check cook-required food under the recipe, product, and applicable local authority. Colour, smell, elapsed time, and “close enough” cannot replace required safety evidence. Stop safely for unusual heat, knives, or appliance behavior instead of forcing a finished label.
5. **Make deviations explicit decisions.** If an ingredient is missing, a pan changes, or directions cannot be read, pause before the next risky stage. Use and record an adjustment only when a trusted method gives later checks. Otherwise stop or change the method. A failed finished dish belongs to the separate recovery workflow; do not rewrite what the original recipe said.
6. **Finish at a real cue and hand off.** Once recipe and safety conditions are met, turn off heat, park hot tools, and rest or serve as directed. Eat promptly or handle any kept portion under the food and local storage guidance. Note which steps happened and what changed for next time; do not label unfinished food “complete.”

### Success

You have a result matching the trusted recipe's intended outcome, traceable units and order, applicable safe doneness evidence, and a destination for heat and remaining food. If the recipe or equipment lacks a necessary safety check, a clear stop with the last completed step is a complete decision, not a claim that the meal was made.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You read only ingredients and discover a soak or preheat later:** Pause when safe, read all steps, and reassess time. Change the cooking or serving plan if the prerequisite cannot fit.
- **Cups, grams, and millilitres are mixed in one calculation:** Return to original units and suitable measures. Stop numerical substitution if the conversion cannot be verified.
- **The timer sounds so you announce doneness:** Use it as a prompt for the actual food-specific check. Do not serve food needing thorough cooking without applicable evidence.
- **You keep adding ingredients to hide one failure:** Establish safety and the recipe's crucial structure first. Use a specialist recovery only for a safe quality problem. Seasoning cannot repair an unknown history.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

“Trustworthy” means source, ingredients, critical actions, and safe finish conditions can be checked; it need not be a celebrity chef from one country. Household taste, equipment, altitude, and culture change methods and results. Product labels and local food-safety rules govern. If sight, hearing, grip, or standing are difficult, enlarge the text, make an accessible step card, use visual or vibrating timers, work at a reachable surface, or use trusted help. None proves food is cooked by itself. You may have used tools and a short record, but the recipe has a traceable execution state.
