## 中文

当可靠配方要求你用**浅锅少量油做普通煎炒**，而炉头还没开时，加载这份 Skill。你要先得到**合适锅具、已备材料、明确下料顺序和可安全停止的工作台**，使短时间的热锅步骤不必一边等待切菜一边冒烟。这里不处理深油炸、极高温锅法、失控明火、燃气/电器维修或危险刀工。

### 准备与输入

读这次食物的可靠配方和锅具说明：所需锅面、热力与油量、食物预处理、下料顺序、翻动方式、完成条件。确认食物安全状态，需生熟分开时按已有安排操作；生肉、禽、海鲜的安全终点由[熟度流程](../check-doneness-and-store-leftovers/SKILL.md)负责。准备好所有将很快加入的食材和调味，切配在冷态完成；没有必要为了缩短等待用热锅当菜板计时器。

### 执行步骤

1. **挑一口真正合适的锅。** 锅底稳定适配炉头，锅面能让本批食物按配方铺开，锅柄牢固，涂层/材料和可用火力符合制造商说明。锅已严重损坏或你无法确认它能承受这次热度时，换锅或换可靠技法，不现场试极限。
2. **让热源周围可操作。** 锅柄朝内，移开可能碰到热锅或燃烧的物品，给翻动工具与耐热落点留空间。用你能持续看守且可安全触及开关的炉头；需要离开厨房就不开火。
3. **按配方把材料排成一条短队列。** 哪些要先入锅、哪些后入、何时加少量液体或调味，写在纸上或摆在顺手位置；湿食材若配方要求沥干，先按说明处理，避免入油时飞溅。任何危险切工、腌制安全和配方外改变不在热锅阶段即兴完成。
4. **准备能执行的工具与批量。** 用适合锅具表面的铲、夹具或勺；本批量若无法在锅内按配方操作，就先分批。近处放可耐热的成品容器和实际可用的计时方式，不把下一批生食放到成品容器里。
5. **开火前读一次停点。** 确认你看到烟、油剧烈异常、锅不稳或食物状态不明时能够关热并停下；有火或燃气异味按当地紧急指导退出。锅、食材、工具、空间与停止路径都明确，才进入预热。

### 完成与停止

你能按顺序指明本批食材、锅与炉头为何适用、翻动/盛出的工具、完成证据和异常时怎么停，就完成准备。缺可靠配方、食材仍需危险切配、锅不稳/有损或你无法持续看守时先不开火；较高的火力不能补齐缺少的准备。

### 常见报错与补救

- **锅已热，配料仍在包装里：** 先关热，等锅安全稳定，再完成冷态准备；不要让油在无人操作的锅里等你拆包装。
- **本批挤满锅面：** 先把未下锅的部分分批排好；已下锅时按配方和安全条件调整，不把锅换成堆料容器。
- **锅柄伸向通道：** 在锅未热或安全可操作时转向内侧；不越过热锅拉扯把手。
- **涂层或把手有问题：** 改用明确适用的锅，不以一次成功煎炒证明损坏器具安全。
- **想用大火追回切菜时间：** 先停热把食材备好，再按配方重启；火力不是时间旅行。

### 假设、替代与副作用

这里的普通煎炒使用少量油和可控炉头，不等于深油炸或专业高温快炒。锅材、炉头、油和食物各有实际限制；制造商说明、可靠配方与当地食品安全指导优先。站立、握力、视力或听力不便时可坐着冷态备料、使用易握工具和可访问计时器、让可信的人现场协助；不能安全控制热锅就换较低负荷技法。现实副作用是多一个备料碗，却少一口被迫等待食材的热锅。

## English

Load this Skill **before turning on the hob** when a reliable recipe calls for ordinary shallow-pan sautéing with a little oil. Prepare a **suitable pan, ready ingredients, loading order, and safe stop path** so a short hot-pan step does not wait and smoke while you chop. This does not cover deep frying, extreme-heat techniques, uncontrolled flame, gas or electrical repair, or dangerous knife work.

### Preparation and inputs

Read the food-specific recipe and pan directions: cooking surface, heat and oil amount, food preparation, loading order, turning, and endpoint. Confirm food is in a suitable state and keep raw and cooked foods separate as appropriate. [Doneness guidance](../check-doneness-and-store-leftovers/SKILL.md) remains responsible for raw meat, poultry, and seafood safety. Prepare all ingredients and seasonings that will be added quickly; finish cutting while the pan is cold. A hot pan is not a kitchen timer for chopping.

### Execution steps

1. **Choose a pan that actually fits.** Its base must sit securely on the hob, its surface must hold this batch under the recipe, its handle secure, and its coating, material, and heat within the maker's directions. Replace a badly damaged pan or change to a reliable method when its heat suitability cannot be confirmed; do not test its limit on this meal.
2. **Leave room to work around heat.** Turn the handle inward and move things that can hit or burn near the pan. Reserve space for a turning tool and heat-safe landing place. Use a burner you can watch and switch off safely. Leave it off if you must leave the kitchen.
3. **Put ingredients in a short queue.** Record which go in first and later, and when a little liquid or seasoning belongs. Keep them within safe reach. If the recipe calls for draining wet food, do that before it meets oil to reduce splatter. Do not improvise risky knife work, food-safety marinades, or recipe changes over the hot pan.
4. **Prepare tools and batch size.** Choose a spatula, tongs, or spoon compatible with the pan. Split a batch that cannot be moved as the recipe requires. Have a nearby heat-safe finished-food dish and a timer you can use. Do not put the next raw batch on the finished-food dish.
5. **Read the stop conditions once before heating.** Be able to turn heat off for smoke, abnormal oil, an unstable pan, or uncertain food condition. Fire or gas odour exits to local emergency guidance. Begin preheating only when pan, ingredients, tools, space, and stop path are ready.

### Success and stop

You can name the batch and its order, why the pan and hob are suitable, your turning and serving tools, the food endpoint, and how you will stop for an abnormality. Do not start heat without reliable directions, while food still needs unsafe cutting, with a damaged or unstable pan, or when you cannot attend it. Higher heat cannot replace missing preparation.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The pan is hot but ingredients are still wrapped:** Turn heat off, wait for control, and finish cold preparation. Do not leave oil heating in a pan while you unpack food.
- **The batch fills the pan:** Divide food not yet added. For food already in, follow the recipe and safety requirements; the pan is not a storage bin.
- **The handle points into traffic:** Turn it inward while cold or when safe to adjust. Do not reach over a hot pan to yank it.
- **A coating or handle is faulty:** Use a pan clearly suited to the task; one successful meal is not evidence a damaged pan is safe.
- **You want high heat to recover chopping time:** Stop heat and prepare first, then restart under the recipe. A burner is not time travel.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Ordinary sautéing here uses a small amount of oil on a controllable hob; it is neither deep frying nor professional extreme-heat wok work. Pan, burner, oil, and food have real limits. Maker directions, reliable recipes, and local food-safety guidance take priority. For standing, grip, visual, or hearing limits, prepare while seated and cold, use manageable tools and accessible timers, or arrange trusted in-person help. Choose a lower-load method if heat cannot be controlled safely. You may use one extra prep bowl and spare a hot pan from waiting for ingredients.
