## 中文

当你是一次普通、自愿、短时来访的主人，希望它在你能接受的时间和条件下结束，而不是等双方猜谁先开口时，加载这份 Skill。目标是提前说明边界、临近结束时给出清楚提示、让来客整理并完成送别。它不负责强迫对方离开、处理威胁，也不要求你用下一次邀约换取这一次结束。

### 准备与输入

在来访前或刚开始时，确认你实际能接待到何时，是否必须出门、工作或休息，以及客人离开需要经过的门、楼梯或其他已知条件。若对方的出入方式需要调整，直接问什么有帮助，不从外表或身份猜。结束边界可以是时间，也可以是已约定活动完成，但要能在现场判断，不能只写“差不多”。

### 执行

1. **早一点说出实际边界。** 例如“今天可以聊到五点，我之后要处理自己的事”。不需要编一个更戏剧性的借口；真实的时间本身足够。若最初忘了说，到访中发现时间不够，也应尽早补充。
2. **临近结束给一次明确提示。** 在还来得及收尾时说“我们还有约十分钟，我想把刚才的话题收一下”，或“这件事做完我就需要结束今天的来访”。不要突然切断谈话，也不靠反复看表期待对方读心。
3. **留出实际离开的动作。** 让对方完成一句话、收好随身物品、确认自己的外套/包和可用的出门路线；需要不同速度或协助时按本人意愿调整。你可以提示“你的杯子先放这儿”，不必为了收拾而延长来访。
4. **按边界完成告别。** 简短说“今天就到这里，谢谢你来”，带到你能开放的出口并确认如何离开。若对方提议下次，只有你真的愿意时才约具体时间；否则可以说“以后再看”，但不要用含糊承诺转移眼前的结束。
5. **边界被忽略时再说清一次。** “我现在需要结束并关门，不能继续待客。”给对方一个现实的收拾和离开机会；不要继续端出食物或开启新活动。如果出现威胁、强迫或你无法安全处理的情况，停止这份普通待客流程，使用你所在地和场地的实际求助方式。

### 完成条件

来客知道结束条件，获得了收拾与离开的现实机会，并从可用出口离开；你没有违背自己明确说出的边界，也没有被迫许诺下次。若对方拒不离开且情况超出普通沟通，完成结果是停止本流程并寻求适当帮助，而不是假装正常送别已发生。

### 常见报错与补救

- **最初说“想待多久都行”，现在需要结束：** 承认自己先前说得太宽，给一个真实可行的收尾时间，不要求对方猜你的改变。
- **提示太隐晦：** 用完整句子说出结束时间或动作，少使用眼神、哈欠和反复收桌子作为暗号。
- **为了礼貌又开启新话题：** 记下以后再聊的点，但结束眼前这次；新话题不自动重开访问时长。
- **客人离开较慢或入口不便：** 询问需要怎样协助并留出合理动作时间，不把行动条件当成故意拖延。
- **客人要求你当场答应下次：** 只有确定愿意时才承诺；“我需要看安排”是比虚构日期更清楚的答复。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

本篇适用于有安全沟通空间的普通来访，不假定某种地区文化必须久留、不断供茶或陪同到楼下。住房、门禁、夜间交通和身体可及性各异；说清你能提供的离开协助即可，不凭空保证交通或他人接送。听觉或语言沟通不方便时，用双方能理解的文字、手势配合明确时间或事先约定的提醒方式。

副作用是访问结束得比“再坐一会儿”更可预测。明确结束不是不欢迎对方。

## English

Load this Skill as the host of an ordinary voluntary short visit when you want it to end within a time or condition you can actually support, instead of waiting for someone to guess who should speak first. State the boundary early, give a clear closing cue, allow belongings to be gathered, and see the guest out. This is not a way to force someone out, handle a threat, or buy the end of this visit with a promise of another.

### Preparation and inputs

Before or near the start, know how long you can truly host, whether you must work, leave, or rest afterward, and which door, stairs, or other known conditions affect the guest's exit. Ask directly if a different way out would help; do not infer from appearance or identity. The end can be a time or the finish of an agreed activity, but it must be observable rather than just “about then.”

### Execution

1. **State the real boundary early.** “I can talk until five today; then I need to do my own work.” You do not need a more dramatic excuse. If you forgot to say it at first and later find you have less time, add it as soon as you know.
2. **Give one clear cue before the end.** While there is still time to wrap up, say “We have about ten minutes; let me finish this topic,” or “When we finish this activity I need to end today's visit.” Do not cut off a sentence without warning or expect repeated clock glances to communicate for you.
3. **Allow the real departure tasks.** Let the guest finish a thought, gather their bag or coat, and check the available route out. Ask what assistance or pace works for them rather than treating access needs as intentional delay. You may say where a cup can stay without extending the visit to clean everything.
4. **Close at the boundary.** “That's all for today. Thank you for coming.” Go with the guest to an exit you can open and explain how to leave. If they mention next time, set a date only if you really want to; otherwise say that you need to check your plans. An invented future invitation is not required to end today.
5. **Restate a missed boundary once.** “I need to end the visit and close up now; I cannot keep hosting.” Give time for the practical act of gathering and leaving. Do not serve another course or begin a new activity. If there is coercion, a threat, or a situation you cannot manage safely, stop this ordinary hosting flow and use the actual local or venue support available.

### Success

The guest knows the end condition, has had a realistic chance to gather things, and has left through a usable exit. You have kept your stated boundary without having to promise another visit. If someone refuses to leave and ordinary conversation is no longer safe or effective, success here is stopping this flow and seeking appropriate help, not claiming a normal goodbye occurred.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You first said “stay as long as you like” but need to stop:** Acknowledge that you spoke too broadly and give a real wrap-up time. Do not require the guest to detect your changed need silently.
- **The cue is too subtle:** State an end time or action in a full sentence instead of relying on glances, yawns, or repeatedly clearing cups.
- **Politeness starts a new topic:** Note it for another day while still ending this visit. A new topic does not automatically restart hosting time.
- **The guest needs a slower or different exit:** Ask how to help and allow the needed actions. Do not read access conditions as bad intent.
- **You are pressed to promise next time:** Commit only if you want to and know you can. “I need to check” is clearer than inventing a date.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This covers an ordinary visit where it is safe to communicate. It does not assume one region requires longer stays, constant refreshments, or walking a guest to the street. Homes, building access, night transport, and physical access differ; offer only exit help you can provide without promising transport or another person's lift. Use a mutually readable note, explicit time, or agreed cue if hearing or language makes speech difficult.

The visit may end more predictably than “just a little longer.” A clear ending is not a rejection of the person.
