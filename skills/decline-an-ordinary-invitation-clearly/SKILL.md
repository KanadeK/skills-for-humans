## 中文

你收到一个普通、可选择的活动邀约，已经决定这次不参加时，用这份 Skill **及时给出明确答复**，让对方可以调整自己的安排。你不需要编造理由，也不必给一个并不想兑现的“下次一定”。这里处理尚未答应的邀约；已经确认参加后退出，属于取消既有安排，不靠本 Skill 假装还没承诺。

### 准备与输入

先核对对方邀请的是哪一次活动、时间和是否存在实际订位或费用截止。如果你之前只说“我看看”，这是待答复；如果已经明确答应，先承认已有安排并转入取消流程。问自己：这次的答案确实是“不”吗？若只是需要某项具体信息才可决定，可先问清，不把疑问伪装成拒绝；若只是不好意思说不，也别用“也许”拖住对方。

### 执行

1. **从结论开始。** 用对方能收到的合适渠道，在活动或订位截止前尽早回复：“谢谢邀请，这次我不参加。” 可加一句与你关系相称的简短善意，但不要把“不参加”藏在一大段解释末尾。
2. **只给愿意给的理由。** “这次不方便”已经足够；想说原因可以说真实且必要的一点，不必公开私人日程、健康或财务情况，也不用编会被追问的借口。对方是否认同理由，不是你能否拒绝的前提。
3. **分清礼貌和新承诺。** 只有真想另约，且有你能提出的实际窗口时，才说“我下周有空，再约一次好吗？” 不想另约就停在这次拒绝；“改天一定”若只是缓和尴尬，会生成下一次的待办债务。对方若曾替你占位或付费，明确问是否有已经发生、需要处理的实际交接，不自行承诺未知费用或法律责任。
4. **让状态落地。** 发送后检查消息确实发出；若是面对面，确认对方听到你的结论。对方询问一次可重复简短答复或说明你愿意说的范围；你不必进入辩论。若对方继续施压，保持同一决定并结束普通协调。涉及威胁或安全问题时，退出本日常流程，寻求适合情形的支持。

可以用你自己的话说：“谢谢你想到我。这次周六的活动我不参加，希望你们玩得开心。” 如果确实想换时间，再另加一个真实可行的选项；不要把模板中的“周六”原样发给周三的活动。

### 成功条件

邀请者已通过可用渠道收到或能读到清楚的“不参加这次”，你没有留下会被合理理解为“可能参加”的假状态；真实的订位或物品交接若存在，已明确由谁继续核对。完成条件是你把自己的决定表达清楚，不是对方喜欢这个答案或停止失望。

### 常见报错与补救

- **“再看看”其实已决定不去：** 在仍能调整安排时补发明确答复，不继续占着待定名额。
- **“改天一定”只为结束尴尬：** 改成“这次不参加”；只有真实意愿和时间才提出下一次。
- **先讲十条理由，结论听不出来：** 把“不参加”放在第一句，再决定是否需要一句解释。
- **已经答应或有人代买票：** 不把它写成普通拒绝；说明先前承诺已变化，转入取消与实际后续处理。
- **对方反复追问原因：** 重申结论和你愿意提供的范围，不为了让对方接受而编新理由。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

本 Skill 适用于双方可自由选择的普通活动，不处理工作命令、权力胁迫、骚扰或危机。文化和关系可能影响礼貌措辞与回复渠道，但不会让含糊或假承诺更可靠。文字沟通不便可用对方能接收的通话或当面答复；听力或表达障碍可选择可访问的文字/辅助方式。拒绝可能让对方失望，或使活动人数变化；及时清楚说明比让对方按你会到场来做不可逆准备更可控。

## English

Use this Skill when you have received an invitation to an ordinary optional activity and have decided **not to attend this time**. Reply clearly and early enough for the inviter to adjust. You need not invent a reason or promise “next time” when you do not mean it. This covers an invitation you have not accepted; withdrawing after a clear yes is cancellation of an existing plan, not a fresh decline.

### Preparation and inputs

Check which activity and time the invitation concerns and whether a reservation or real cost cutoff exists. If you previously said “I'll check,” you still owe a decision. If you already said yes, acknowledge that commitment and use a cancellation process. Ask yourself whether the answer is truly no. If you need one specific fact before deciding, clarify that fact instead; if you know no but feel awkward, do not keep the inviter waiting on a false “maybe.”

### Execution

1. **Lead with the answer.** Through a channel the inviter can receive, reply before the activity or booking cutoff when possible: “Thank you for inviting me. I won't come this time.” Add a short kind phrase that fits your relationship if you wish, without hiding the no at the end of a long explanation.
2. **Give only the reason you choose to give.** “It doesn't work for me” is enough. You can offer one truthful relevant reason without disclosing private schedules, health, or finances, or creating an excuse that invites a debate. The inviter's approval of your reason is not required for you to decline.
3. **Separate courtesy from a new promise.** Suggest another meeting only if you genuinely want one and can name a workable window: “I can do next week; would you like to plan that separately?” Otherwise stop at this decline. An empty “definitely another time” creates a future obligation. If someone reserved or paid for a place for you, ask what actual handover is pending; do not guess fees or legal responsibility.
4. **Make the state real.** Check that the message sent, or that the person heard you in conversation. If asked once, repeat the short answer or share only what you want to share. You do not need to debate the decision. If ordinary persuasion continues, maintain the same answer and end the scheduling discussion. Threats or safety problems require a different support path outside this everyday Skill.

In your own words, for example: “Thanks for thinking of me. I won't join Saturday's outing, but I hope you have a good time.” Add a different date only when you truly want and can offer one. Do not copy “Saturday” into a reply about Wednesday.

### Success

The inviter has received, or can read, a clear “I am not attending this time,” without a false state that reasonably suggests you may still come. If a real booking or item handover exists, its responsible next check is named. Success is stating your decision accurately, not making the other person enjoy it or feel no disappointment.

### Common errors and recovery

- **“I'll see” remains after you already know no:** Send a clear answer while arrangements can still change; stop occupying a tentative place.
- **“Definitely another time” is only an exit phrase:** Replace it with this one decline. Offer another date only with real intent and availability.
- **Ten reasons obscure the answer:** Put “I won't attend” first, then decide whether one explanation helps.
- **You already agreed or someone bought a ticket for you:** Do not call it an ordinary refusal. Acknowledge the change and handle cancellation and actual follow-up.
- **The inviter presses for a reason repeatedly:** Restate the answer and the limit of what you are willing to share; do not invent a new excuse to win agreement.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is for an optional ordinary activity between people able to choose, not an employment order, coercion, harassment, or crisis. Culture and relationship can change wording and channel, but vague or false promises still leave the plan unclear. If text is inaccessible, use a call or face-to-face reply the person can receive. If hearing or speech is a barrier, use an accessible written or assisted channel. Refusal may disappoint the inviter or change the group size. Early clarity gives them a better chance to adjust than a false expectation that you will arrive.
