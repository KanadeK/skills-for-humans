## 中文

当你今天要出门，却不确定现有衣服是否同时适合**天气、活动和实际场所**时，用这份 Skill 在离家前确定一套可穿组合，或及时调整计划。目标是能按当天所需走、坐、进出室内外并处理可预见的冷热或小雨，而不是让陌生人给外观打分。极端天气、职业防护或专用安全装备的要求高于这份普通穿衣流程。

### 准备与输入

写下会去的地点、出门/返回时段、交通与步行量、会坐多久、室内外各占多少，以及邀请函或场所**明确写出的**着装与鞋类限制。查看对应地点和时段的可信预报、降雨/风/气温变化及当地天气警报；不要只凭家里窗外的十分钟。再看手里哪些衣物和鞋子干净、干燥、闭合完整、你穿着舒服；需要的雨具或可增减层是否真的在身边。没有可靠预报时把未知记下来，选可调整的组合或缩小户外计划，不编一个天气结论。

这份 Skill 不代替官方高温、寒潮、雷暴等警报的行动建议；看到影响行程的警报，先按当地权威信息决定是否改时段或取消。防护服、医疗衣物和特殊运动装备另走对应说明。

### 执行

1. **把一天拆成真实片段。** 按先后列出最冷/最热、可能下雨或有风、步行/静坐、室内/室外，以及鞋会踩的地面。只在场所确实提出要求时记录其文字，不从“别人可能会觉得”推断额外规矩。
2. **从现有衣物里先选能完成活动的底组合。** 挑已可穿、能做计划中动作且不持续压迫或滑落的衣物和适合步行/地面的鞋。H066 的动态试穿专门处理尺码；这里只需确认这套组合不会因层次或鞋的加入而让坐、走、伸手变困难。高温下优先考虑轻薄、宽松且实际可穿的选项；寒冷时考虑可增减的保暖层，具体以本人和当地条件为准。
3. **加一层处理会变化的条件。** 若预报与行程确实有风或雨，核对可用外层/雨具能否承担所需遮挡；室内变热或活动增加时能否脱下并拿着，休息或晚归变冷时能否加回。把可变层放在自己能取用的地方，不为了“备用”背一包今天用不到的衣服。没有合适外层时，改交通、减少暴露或改计划，而不是把普通薄衣称为防雨装备。
4. **检查场所要求与个人可及性。** 将实际明示的衣着/鞋要求与天气及行动需求比对。若冲突，试可行的普通替代，例如到室内再换鞋或增加一层；替代会造成行动障碍、过热或无法携带时，联系场所问清可行选项或调整行程。是否合适由实际要求和本人需求决定，不羞辱体型、性别表达或预算。
5. **离开前做最后一轮实物确认。** 穿上底组合与预计外层，走几步、坐下或按自己的行动方式试一个关键动作；确认鞋、扣件、口袋/包、可增减层都在。再看一次出门时段的最新预报或警报。条件变化且原组合不够时及时替换或改行程，不把五分钟前的判断冻结到夜里。

### 成功条件

你能说清这套现有衣物与鞋如何覆盖今天的地点、时段、活动量、室内外变化和真实场所要求；本人穿着与携带都可行，天气恶化时有可执行调整。若警报、衣物短缺或要求冲突使普通组合不可靠，明确改时段、改活动或先问场所，也完成判断。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只看现在温度，晚归才发现风雨：** 回到完整时段和地点的预报，增加可拿取层或调整路程，不凭窗外片刻推一天。
- **备了外套却必须一直抱着、无法使用辅助设备：** 换可携带的层或缩小备选，真正拿得动才算备用。
- **场所规则只是猜测，先替自己加一堆限制：** 查邀请函/网站/现场负责人给出的明确要求；不清楚就问，别把审美担忧冒充规则。
- **鞋好看但走不完计划路线：** 换已经能走的鞋，或按场所许可带替换鞋；鞋的功能不是镜前照片可证明的。
- **薄衣被称作防雨，雨具其实没有：** 改交通/室外时间或计划，别宣称普通衣物有未验证防护性能。
- **出现当地危险天气警报：** 停止普通搭配优化，查当地官方行动建议并考虑延期；多穿一层不能替代风险决定。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里只处理普通日常外出的现有衣物组合，不给极端天气、工作防护或专用运动装备提供安全承诺。每个人对冷热、覆盖、行动、文化和场合的需求不同，天气预报也随时变化，因此不提供“某温度必须穿几件”的表格。没有联网、看不清预报或不便搬运衣物时，可用可信的天气播报、放大/语音读出、现场协助和更短可调整的行程。可能多带一层，也可能决定不出门；两者都是实际成本。

## English

Use this Skill before leaving when you are unsure whether your **available clothes** fit today's weather, activity, and actual venue. Choose one workable outfit or adjust the plan in time. The result should allow the walking, sitting, and indoor/outdoor changes you need while accounting for predictable warmth, chill, or light rain. It is not a rating of your appearance. Extreme weather, workplace protection, or specialist safety gear overrides this ordinary outfit task.

### Preparation and inputs

List the places, departure and return hours, travel and walking, time seated, and indoor/outdoor portions. Record only dress or footwear rules **actually stated** by an invitation or venue. Check a reliable forecast for those places and hours, including rain, wind, temperature changes, and local alerts; a ten-minute view out the window does not cover the day. Review clothes and shoes you already have that are clean, dry, intact, and comfortable. Check that a needed outer layer, rain item, or removable piece is actually available. Without a reliable forecast, record the uncertainty and choose an adjustable outfit or reduce outdoor exposure rather than inventing weather facts.

This Skill does not replace official action advice for severe heat, cold, storms, or other alerts. If an alert affects the trip, use local authoritative guidance first to decide whether to reschedule or cancel. Protective clothing, medical garments, and specialist sports gear follow their own instructions.

### Execution

1. **Split the day into real segments.** In order, note the coldest/hottest part, likely rain or wind, walking versus sitting, indoors versus outdoors, and the ground your shoes will meet. Record only a venue's actual stated rule, not extra rules you imagine other people might prefer.
2. **Choose an available base outfit that supports the activity.** Use clothes already fit for wearing and shoes appropriate to the walking and surface. Check that planned movements remain possible and nothing persistently presses or slips. H066 covers fit in detail; here make sure the combination and shoes do not block sitting, walking, or reaching. In heat, consider light, loose, wearable options; in cold, consider a removable insulating layer, guided by your own needs and local conditions.
3. **Add one way to handle change.** For meaningful wind or rain in the forecast and itinerary, check whether the available outer layer or rain item meets that need. Can you remove and carry it if indoors or active, and add it back for a cooler pause or late return? Keep the changeable piece accessible. Do not carry a bag of unlikely spares simply because “backup” sounds safe. Without a suitable outer item, alter transport, exposure time, or the plan rather than calling thin fabric rain protection.
4. **Compare actual venue rules and access needs.** Match explicit clothing or footwear requirements against weather and movement. If they conflict, consider an ordinary workable alternative, such as changing shoes indoors or adding a layer when permitted. If that blocks movement, overheats you, or cannot be carried, ask the venue about options or adjust the visit. Actual rules and your needs decide, without shaming body size, gender expression, or budget.
5. **Make a final physical check before leaving.** Try the base outfit and intended outer item. Walk a few steps, sit, or make one key movement in your own usual way. Confirm shoes, fasteners, pockets/bag, and removable layer are present. Recheck the latest forecast or alert for departure hours. If conditions have shifted beyond the outfit, change the clothes or trip; do not freeze a five-minute-old decision until nightfall.

### Success

You can explain how the available outfit and shoes cover today's places, hours, activity, indoor/outdoor transitions, and real venue requirements. They are wearable and carryable for you, with a practical response to changing weather. If alerts, missing clothes, or conflicting rules make an ordinary outfit unreliable, rescheduling, changing activity, or asking the venue is also a complete decision.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Only today's current temperature is checked; wind and rain arrive later:** Check the full hours and locations, add an accessible layer, or alter the route.
- **A backup jacket must be carried all day and conflicts with an assistive device:** Use a carryable layer or a smaller plan. A backup must be usable, not just owned.
- **Venue rules are guessed, then extra limits imposed on yourself:** Read the actual invitation/site or ask staff; aesthetic worry is not a rule.
- **Shoes look good but cannot handle the planned walk:** Choose shoes you can already walk in, or bring an allowed change. A mirror photo does not test footwear function.
- **Thin clothing is called rainwear when no rain item exists:** Change transport, outdoor time, or plans. Do not claim unverified protection.
- **A local hazardous-weather alert appears:** Pause outfit optimization, check local official action advice, and consider delay. Another layer is not a risk decision.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers available clothing for ordinary daily outings, not safety assurance for extreme weather, work protection, or specialist sports. People differ in warmth, coverage, movement, culture, and venue needs, and forecasts change. There is no universal temperature-to-item count. Without internet, readable forecasts, or easy carrying, use a trustworthy broadcast, enlarged or spoken information, in-person help, and a shorter adjustable trip. An extra layer has a carrying cost; a changed plan has a time cost.

- [REI: Layering Basics](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/layering-basics.html) — layers can be added or removed with weather and exertion; an outer layer must fit the actual wind or rain need. Its outdoor examples are not a fixed city outfit formula.

- Original synthesis — combine today's time-specific trip, available clothes, stated venue rules, and the wearer's ability to move and carry an outfit, without appearance judgment.
