## 中文

当你演奏或拍打一小段节奏时觉得“哪里不稳”，却没法边做边确定是哪一拍，用这份 Skill **录下并回听一次**，找出下次要改的一处。结束物是带位置的具体观察，不是“我节奏差”的总评，也不是一段必须发布的作品。普通短时练习的组织归另一任务；本篇的关键是录音给了你第二次观察机会。

### 准备与输入

挑你自创、老师许可或你有权使用的几拍/一小句，先在纸上画简单的顺序标记，如“1 2 3 4”或四个方格。你可以用乐器、拍手、敲安全的桌面，也可以用适合自己的动作；无需下载专门软件。选择一个要听的点：某拍是否过早、过晚、漏掉或不均匀。若用节拍器，先选一个你能跟得上的速度，不把速度数字当成绩。只录自己的声音，避免录到他人的谈话；若不能避开旁人且未获同意，换场所或停止录制。确认录音应用和设备不会自动云同步或分享；做不到就换本地设备或暂停。确认能正常录、放和找到文件。

### 执行

1. **做一段短测试录音。** 录数秒并播放，确认音量可辨、没有削波失真，节拍器声（若使用）与动作声都能听见。录音过小就合理挪近设备，失真就离远一点；不用为了“清楚”把音量推得刺耳。
2. **录一次完整小段。** 先数出或轻打你选的脉搏，再做原定几拍，结束录音。不要边录边反复重开到“完美”；第一份可听的记录就是观察材料。记下是拍手、乐器还是其他动作，以及有无节拍器。
3. **只听目标点。** 回听一次，沿纸上的格子指出具体位置，例如“第 3 拍提前接近第 2 拍”或“最后一拍漏了”。有节拍器时听动作声和点击声的相对位置；没有时听相邻间隔是否与你数出的脉搏一致。若录音听不清，不猜偏快还是偏慢，回到测试录音并重录。
4. **给下一轮一个改法。** 只选最明确的一处，写“位置 → 听见的情况 → 下次做法”，例如“第 3 拍 → 提前 → 放慢后先数两遍，再拍这一小句”。可以马上慢速试一次，但本篇完成条件是定位与下一步，不要求当场练好所有拍子。
5. **处理录音并收工。** 若你需要下次比较，把文件留在自己可找的位置并用不暴露他人的简短名称标记；不需要就确认笔记已写好后删除。没有分享要求。若设备录到了他人声音或私密内容，停止使用该文件并按设备可用方式删除，再到合适环境重做。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你有一段可辨认的本地短录音，能在节奏顺序中指出**一处具体位置、实际听到的现象和下一次改法**。只写“快一点/慢一点”却没有位置，仍需回听；无法听清时不强下结论。

- **回听觉得每拍都有问题：** 先固定最明显的一处，下一轮再检查其他位置；本篇不做整段评分。
- **点击声盖住动作声：** 调低点击声或改变设备位置，先做数秒测试，不以被掩盖的录音判定对错。
- **没有节拍器：** 用你数出的脉搏和纸上顺序作参照，只描述可听见的相对间隔，不声称测得精确拍速。
- **没有可录放设备或听觉无法独立核对：** 不虚构回听结果。借用可信的本地录放设备，或在你同意的情况下请可信的人一起定位；这份录音任务当下可暂停。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这不是听力诊断、专业混音或乐器维修。你不必会读谱；方格、文字或触觉顺序都可标节奏位置。若无法安全发声或重复动作，另约时间或选择适合的低强度动作，不靠忍痛完成。设备文件可能占空间，保留与删除由你按真实需要决定；有旁人和版权材料时，优先本地、短段、本人有权使用且不分享。

## English

Use this Skill when a short rhythm you play or tap feels unsteady but you cannot identify the beat while performing. **Record and listen back once** to locate one place to change next time. The result is a specific observation with a location, not a verdict on your ability or a piece you must publish. Organising an ordinary short practice session is a separate task; here the recording gives you a second chance to observe.

### Preparation and inputs

Choose a few beats or a short phrase you made, your teacher permits, or you may lawfully practise. Mark a simple sequence on paper, such as “1 2 3 4” or four boxes. You may use an instrument, clapping, a safe tabletop tap, or a suitable action; no special app is required. Choose one listening question: is a beat early, late, missing, or uneven? If using a metronome, pick a pulse you can follow; the number is not a grade. Record only your own sound. Avoid other people's conversations; if you cannot avoid bystanders and lack their consent, change location or stop recording. Check that the recorder and device will not automatically sync or share the clip; use a local device or pause if you cannot establish that. Check that you can record, play, and find the file.

### Execution

1. **Make a short test clip.** Record a few seconds and play them back. Check that the sound is distinguishable, not distorted, and that you can hear both the click and your action if you use a metronome. Move the device sensibly closer if too quiet or farther away if distorted; do not turn playback painfully loud.
2. **Record one complete short pass.** Count in or lightly mark your chosen pulse, then make the planned beats and stop recording. Do not restart repeatedly in pursuit of perfection. The first intelligible take is evidence. Note whether you clapped, played, or used another action, and whether a metronome was present.
3. **Listen for the chosen point.** Play the clip once and mark a place in your paper sequence, such as “beat 3 crowds beat 2” or “last beat missing.” With a metronome, compare your sound with the click. Without one, compare intervals with the pulse you counted. If the clip is unclear, do not guess whether you were fast or slow; return to the test and record again.
4. **Choose the next adjustment.** Pick the clearest single place and write “location → what I heard → what to try next,” such as “beat 3 → early → count twice more slowly before tapping this phrase.” You may try slowly now, but the finish line here is a located issue and next action, not fixing every beat immediately.
5. **Handle the file and finish.** If you need a later comparison, keep the clip where you can find it with a short name that exposes nobody else. Otherwise, after writing the note, delete it if you wish. Sharing is not required. If the recording includes another person's voice or private material, stop using it and delete it using your device's available controls, then retry in a suitable setting.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You have an intelligible local short recording and can point to **one place in the rhythm, what you actually heard, and one change for next time**. “Faster/slower” without a location still needs another listen. An inaudible clip supports no timing verdict.

- **Every beat seems wrong:** Fix the most obvious place as the next target; check others on another pass rather than grade the whole phrase now.
- **The click masks your action:** Lower click volume or move the device, then make a few-second test; do not score a masked recording.
- **No metronome is available:** Use your counted pulse and paper sequence as a reference. Describe audible relative intervals, not a precise measured tempo.
- **No record/playback device or you cannot check sound independently:** Do not invent a listening result. Borrow a trusted local device or, with your agreement, ask a trusted person to help locate the issue; pause this recording task for now.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is not hearing diagnosis, professional mixing, or instrument repair. You need not read notation: boxes, words, or tactile position markers can locate beats. If sounding or repeating an action is unsuitable, reschedule or choose a comfortable low-effort action rather than play through pain. Files use device space; you decide whether to keep or delete them. With bystanders or copyrighted material, keep the clip local, short, authorised, and unshared.
