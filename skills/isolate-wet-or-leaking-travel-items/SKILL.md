## 中文

当你的短途行李中有普通湿毛巾/衣物、用过的干衣物或可能渗漏的日常洗护容器，而其他衣物和用品要保持干净干燥时加载。目标是**根据眼前实际湿度或渗漏风险做临时分隔，并安排抵达后尽快打开和处理**。这里不包装危险化学品、未知液体、需要冷链的物品或航空专属液体限制；易碎物防震另有流程。

### 准备与判断

把所有要放进同一包的物品摊在可清洁处，先看有没有水分、脏污或瓶口残液，分清“已经湿/漏”和“担心可能漏”。有时间且物品允许时先正常晾干或清洁，不把密封袋当烘干机。确认容器本身适合这趟交通的携带规则；本 Skill 不让禁带物变成可带。准备可关闭、没有明显破损的内袋或适合物品的容器。

### 执行

1. **按实际风险先分组。** 干净干燥衣物、已用但干燥物、确实潮湿物和有液体的普通容器分别放；不要只按“都是衣服”塞一层。未知、刺激性或不明来源的泄漏物先停止装包，按其正式说明处理。
2. **能先处理的不要留到路上。** 湿物可按原物品许可先晾/拧/擦，瓶子可按盖子或瓶身实际结构检查是否关紧并擦净外侧；瓶口损坏、持续漏液时不要用更多衣服包它，而是更换合规容器、舍弃或问服务方可行办法。
3. **做适度的临时内层。** 将不能再干燥的普通湿物单独放入可封合的内袋，把日常洗护容器单独放在能接住少量渗漏的二次容器中；把已用干衣物与清洁衣物分开放。不要把有液体的瓶子与纸张、电子设备或易被污损的衣物无隔离地混放。检查内袋闭合后轻轻改变方向，观察外部是否仍干燥；不把一次测试当永久防漏保证。
4. **在包里保留可取出性。** 让这些内袋有自己可辨的标记/位置，不被重物压破。湿物只是为了途中暂存；记到达后第一件事是打开、晾干或送入正常清洗流程，继续封着可能使原本干净的行李也难以处理。
5. **抵达立即对一次。** 从包中取出内袋，检查外层是否真的保持干燥、是否有渗漏或染污；湿物按材质与场所允许的方法摊开/处理，受污染的其他物品单独留待正常清洁。若临时包破了，就记录哪些物品实际被弄湿，不假装隔离成功。

### 完成与停止

出发时湿/用过/可能渗漏物和干净物清楚分开，内层没有明显外漏，且抵达后有实际打开与处理动作，就完成临时分隔。**这不保证容器在整个路程中绝不漏，也不表示湿物被密封后会自己干。** 无法可靠封住普通液体、物品本身不允许携带或泄漏来源不明时停止装入共用行李，使用该物品和承运方正式规则处理。

### 常见报错与补救

- **湿衣物被“包了两层”但一直密封：** 抵达后立即取出处理；包装只是短时间隔离，不是储存方法。
- **瓶盖拧紧仍在漏：** 检查密封件/瓶口并改用确实可用的容器或不携带，不靠干衣服吸漏。
- **袋口或袋底破损：** 更换内袋并再次检查外侧，不把原破袋叠加成可靠屏障。
- **只有干燥的已穿衣服：** 与清洁衣物分袋即可，别按“湿物”假定需要密封很久。
- **行李外层已湿：** 把实际受影响物品分出来按材质处理，必要时更换/晾干包体；不要继续把其他干物塞进去。

### 假设、替代与副作用

适用于普通短途个人物品，不包括危险化学品、医疗/冷链运输、航空液体规则或专业材料保护。湿度、容器和路程长短不同，内袋只提供有限、可观察的临时隔离。视力、行动或物品操作不便时可在安全位置用手触/可见检查、请可信协助或减少要带的湿物；不需使用特殊品牌包装。副作用是抵达后必须主动拆开检查和清洁。

## English

Load this Skill when a short-trip bag contains an ordinary damp towel or clothes, dry worn clothing, or a daily-care container that may leak, while other clothes and supplies should stay clean and dry. Your result is **temporary separation based on actual moisture or leak risk and a prompt plan to open and deal with it on arrival**. This does not pack hazardous chemicals, unknown liquids, cold-chain items or items restricted by airline liquid rules. Fragile-item impact protection is another task.

### Preparation and inputs

Lay the items for this bag in a cleanable area. Look for actual moisture, dirt or liquid at a cap and distinguish “already wet or leaking” from “might leak.” Dry or clean an item normally first if time and its care permit; a sealed pouch is not a dryer. Check the container is allowed by the actual transport service; this Skill does not make a prohibited item permitted. Use an inner bag or container you can close that has no obvious damage.

### Execution

1. **Group by real risk.** Separate clean dry clothing, worn but dry items, actually damp items, and ordinary liquid containers. “They are all clothes” is not enough. Stop packing an unknown or irritating leak and follow the item's actual directions instead.
2. **Deal with what you can before travel.** Dry, squeeze or wipe a damp item where its care permits. Check a bottle's real cap or seal and wipe its outside. For a damaged opening or continuing leak, do not wrap it in more clothes; use a suitable permitted container, leave it or ask the provider what is allowed.
3. **Make a modest temporary inner layer.** Put ordinary damp items you cannot dry further in their own closeable bag, and everyday toiletries in a separate secondary container able to catch a small leak. Keep dry worn things apart from clean clothes. Do not put a liquid bottle directly against paper, electronics or easily stained fabric. Close the inner bag, change its orientation gently and check that its outside remains dry. One check is not a lifetime leak guarantee.
4. **Keep the inner bags retrievable.** Give each a place or label you can recognise, protected from heavy items that could crush it. Wet items are held only for the trip; make opening, drying or normal laundry the first arrival action. Leaving them sealed can make a manageable problem harder.
5. **Check once on arrival.** Remove inner bags and see whether the outer layer stayed dry or anything leaked or stained. Open and handle damp material according to its care and the venue's usable space. Separate other affected items for normal cleaning. For a broken temporary layer, record what actually got wet instead of claiming separation succeeded.

### Success and stop

At departure, damp, worn and possibly leaky items are visibly separated from clean things, no inner layer clearly leaks outside, and you will open and handle it on arrival. **This does not guarantee no leak throughout the trip or make wet fabric dry inside a bag.** Stop putting a liquid into shared luggage if it cannot be contained reliably, is not permitted for the journey or has an unknown leak source. Use item and carrier instructions.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Wet clothes are double-bagged and then left sealed:** Open and process them promptly on arrival; packing is a short-term barrier, not storage.
- **A cap still leaks when tightened:** Check seal and opening and use a sound allowed container or leave it rather than soaking up leakage with dry clothes.
- **The inner bag has a hole:** Replace it and check the outside again; several damaged layers do not make a reliable barrier.
- **Worn clothes are dry:** Separate them from clean clothing without treating them as wet things needing long sealing.
- **The outer bag is already damp:** Separate affected items for their real care and dry or replace the bag as needed; do not add more dry goods into it.

### Assumptions, alternatives and side effects

This covers ordinary personal things on a short trip, not hazardous chemicals, medical or cold-chain transport, airline liquid rules or specialist conservation. Moisture, containers and trip duration differ. An inner bag is limited, observable temporary isolation. For sight, mobility or handling needs, inspect safely by touch or sight, ask trusted help or carry fewer damp items. No special brand is required. You must open and check the package on arrival.
