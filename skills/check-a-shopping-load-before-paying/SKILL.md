## 中文

当你已把东西选进购物篮、**还没付款**，需要确定这一篮能否按实际返程方式安全带走时，加载这份 Skill。即使只逛一家店也适用。它的输出是**这一次的分袋与携带方案，或删项/暂不买**；不代替事前排跨店路线，也不负责买回家后的保存。

### 准备与输入

看清篮里**实际**有哪些整包物品，哪些冷藏/冷冻、哪些是生肉或海鲜、哪些即食或容易压坏，哪些是清洁用品。确认你手边真正有几只完好的袋、保冷袋、冰袋或经允许使用的推车，回家需要走多远、上下什么车或楼梯，以及你要留几只手做扶栏、开门或操作行动辅助工具。标签看不清时可拍照放大或请店员读出保存要求；借用的购物车是否能离店、能否上车，须以店方和运营方当期规则为准。

### 执行

1. **先停止往篮里加东西。** 用眼前的整包物品检查，不沿用出门前估计的重量。把“还没决定要不要”的商品单独放在可删组；结账队列不是新物品自动获准的场所。
2. **先按不能混装的条件分组。** 生肉、禽肉和海鲜应有防漏包装，与即食食品分开；清洁用品与食品分开。需要保冷的食品按标签和返程条件准备合适的保冷袋与冰袋。不要用一只不防漏的袋子，把几类要求写成“回家再分”。
3. **给每组分配真实容器和携带位置。** 标出哪只袋装什么、由哪只手或哪个合适的推车位置带走。重且结实的物品放在不会压坏易碎品的位置，不超过你已知能稳定携带的负担，也不超过袋或推车的标示承重。不要把重袋挂在未获设备说明许可的行动辅助工具或婴儿车把手上。
4. **按最后一段路复核。** 从收银台到家，逐段想清扶栏、转弯、换乘、台阶、等车和开门时，物品怎样保持稳固、通道怎样保持畅通。商店里的大购物车承得住，不证明你自己的袋子、身体或返程交通也承得住。
5. **在付款前做可行性检查。** 店方允许时，可用未拆封商品试装，或请店员按可及方式协助装袋；不允许试装时，用真实包装尺寸和袋子空间保守估计，关键条件不明就不要硬买。若袋数、保冷、握持或返程条件不足，先放回未付款的可选项；仍装不下就拆成另一次采购，或在付款前核实合适的商品/取得方式。
6. **确定结账版本。** 再看一眼删项是否让本次用途无法完成；必要时连同依赖项一起延期。能付款时，你应能指出每只袋/推车位置的物品、冷食的直接返家路线，以及抵达后谁先需要低温。若只有“买了再想办法”，这次检查输出为暂不买。

### 完成条件

你有一份针对**实际篮内物品**的分袋图和返家方式：原料、即食物和清洁用品不错误混装；冷食有可行的返家条件；重量、体积和要空出的手都在你的实际范围内。也可以通过明确删项或暂不付款完成，不能以收银小票代替可携带性。

### 常见报错与补救

- **推着店内车觉得不重：** 把物品逐组映射到你会带走的袋或获准的推车；缺少合适载具就减少篮内物品。
- **只有一只袋却有生食、即食食品和清洁用品：** 在可用且可负担时取得能分开的袋或容器，否则放回冲突商品；不要让生食汁液替你决定分区。
- **袋把手磨损或装到鼓起：** 换完好且承重合适的袋，重新分装或减量；不要用手指勾住即将断开的提手赌回程。
- **保冷装备缺失、排队或返程突然变长：** 付款前重算冷食能否按标签要求带回；不能确认就把冷食放回，不用外观或气味推断将来的安全。
- **删掉一件后本次用途不成立：** 把相关组一起延期，或重新选择能完成用途的组合；不为保留一张购物小票制造半成品任务。

### 假设、替代与副作用

这份检查不假定你有车、能提固定重量、能读小字、能站在长队里，或店员一定能代为装袋。可用放大、语音阅读、同行者描述或店员在其服务范围内协助；无法确认的关键条件就暂停购买。地区的交通、推车和商店装袋规则可能不同，按当日规定和具体包装标签办理。副作用是可能少买一件，但你保留了一条能走完的回家路线。

## English

Load this Skill when the goods are in your basket but you have **not paid**, and you need to know whether this actual load can get home using your real route. It works after a single-store shop too. The result is **a bag-by-bag carrying plan or a clear decision to remove items or wait**. It does not plan a multi-store route or put food away at home.

### Preparation and inputs

Identify the **actual full packs** in your basket: which are chilled or frozen, raw meat or seafood, ready to eat, crushable, or household cleaners. Check the sound bags, cool bag, ice packs, or permitted cart you really have. Know the walk, vehicle or stairs on the way home and how many hands you need free for a rail, door, or mobility aid. Enlarge a label photo or ask staff to read storage directions if needed. Whether a store cart may leave the shop or board transit depends on the shop and operator's current rules.

### Execution

1. **Stop adding items for a moment.** Check the full packs in front of you, not your pre-trip estimate. Keep undecided extras in a group you can remove. The checkout queue does not grant automatic approval to one more item.
2. **Group what must stay apart.** Raw meat, poultry, and seafood need leak-resistant wrapping away from ready-to-eat food. Keep household cleaners away from food. Prepare a suitable cool bag and ice packs for chilled items when their labels and return journey require them. One leaking bag cannot become three separate zones merely because you intend to sort it at home.
3. **Assign each group to a real container and carrying position.** Name the bag and hand or suitable cart position for every group. Put sturdy heavy goods where they will not crush fragile ones. Stay within what you know you can move steadily and within any labelled bag or cart load limit. Do not hang heavy bags from a mobility aid or stroller handle unless its instructions permit that load.
4. **Check the final journey.** From checkout to home, think through rails, turns, transfers, steps, waits, and doors. How will the goods remain stable without blocking a vehicle aisle? A large store trolley carrying the basket says nothing about your own bags, body, or transport.
5. **Test feasibility before paying.** If shop policy allows, test the fit with sealed products or ask staff for accessible bagging help. Otherwise estimate conservatively from the actual package and bag sizes; if a critical condition is unknown, do not force the purchase. If bags, cooling, grip, or the return journey are inadequate, remove unpaid optional items first. If the load still fails, make another trip or verify a suitable product or acquisition method before paying.
6. **Set the checkout version.** Check whether removing an item makes the intended use impossible; defer its dependent items together if needed. If you can pay, you can name every bag or cart position, the direct journey home for cold food, and what needs refrigeration first on arrival. If the plan is merely “pay now, solve later,” wait instead.

### Success

You have a bag-by-bag plan for the **goods actually in the basket** and a workable way home. Raw, ready-to-eat, and household products do not end up in the wrong bag; cold items can meet their travel conditions; load, size, and free hands fit your real ability. Clearly removing items or waiting to pay is also a complete result. A receipt is not proof of carrying capacity.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The store cart makes the basket feel light:** Map every group to a bag or cart you may actually take home. Reduce the basket if you lack a suitable carrier.
- **One bag must take raw food, ready-to-eat food, and cleaners:** Obtain separate suitable containers if available and affordable, or put the conflicting item back. Raw juices cannot be a sorting method.
- **A handle is worn or the bag bulges:** Use a sound bag with suitable capacity, redistribute, or remove items. Do not make the trip depend on a handle about to fail.
- **Cooling is unavailable or the queue or return grows longer:** Recheck labelled cold-food requirements before paying. Put the cold item back if the route cannot be confirmed; future safety cannot be inferred from how it looks or smells now.
- **Removing one item breaks the intended job:** Defer its dependent group too or choose another complete combination. A receipt is not a reason to keep half a task.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This check assumes neither a car, a fixed lifting ability, small-print vision, tolerance for a long queue, nor guaranteed staff bagging. Enlarge text, use audio reading, ask a companion to describe items, or seek help within the shop's available service. Pause the purchase when a critical fact stays unknown. Transit, carts, and bagging rules vary by location; follow current operator rules and the item's label. You may buy one thing less and still keep a way home you can finish.
