## 中文

当你看到公共图书馆、社区中心或博物馆的一门普通课程，想参加却不确定材料、水平、费用或报名方式时，用这份 Skill 在承诺出席前做一次**课程适配核对**。结果是“符合条件且已按要求报名/可直接到场”，或“不合适，明确原因”；不是靠“第一次/独自”标签猜课程会照顾你，也不是实际上完第一节。

### 准备与输入

写下你想从课程得到的一件小事，以及你能用的日期、地点或线上方式、费用上限、已会的基础、愿意自带的材料和必要的行动/感官/语言支持。从主办方正式课程页核当期日期、年龄/人群、水平先修、是否需要报名、可用名额、材料谁提供、是否有额外费用/照片记录、取消与支持渠道；旧帖和相似名字的班不一定相同。涉及危险工具、医疗康复、专业资格或儿童监护责任的内容超出本篇 Everyday 范围，需专门核实。

### 执行

1. **先比水平与目标。** 把课程写的“入门/进阶/需带已有作品”等要求和你已有基础对照。说不清时向主办方问一句具体问题，如“没用过这个工具的人能参加吗？”不因为喜欢主题就跳过先修条件。
2. **核实材料与参与形式。** 列出必须自带、由主办方提供、可选材料，以及是否必须到场每次或只是一场体验。没有明说的材料别默认免费提供；器材是否可及、摄影/拍摄是否可拒绝也向主办方确认。
3. **核完整承诺。** 看日期、地点、到达方式、课程总次数、价格/额外材料费、报名截止、取消规定和可及服务能否满足你。任何会影响是否参加的关键点仍未知时先询问，不把“网页没写”补成“没有费用”。
4. **作一次 go/no-go。** 条件可行且你接受时按主办方正式链接/电话/柜台报名，或记清“无需报名，按时到场”的官方说明。若不合适，记录哪一项不合适并停止，不为了不尴尬而付款或占位。
5. **核报名状态。** 需要报名的课程要有主办方确认、等待名单状态或工作人员答复；提交表单后没收到确认就写“报名待核”，不直接把日历标为入选。把场次和必须带的东西留在你自己的私下记录中，不记账号凭据。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你能说出水平、材料、费用/时间、参与方式与支持条件，并知道自己是正式报名成功、候补、可直接到场，还是明确不参加。只有点开活动页面、没有对照真实条件，尚未做出适配决定。

- **课程写“初学者友好”但列了先修工具：** 以具体要求为准，问主办方能否无经验参加，不靠宣传语推断。
- **材料未写全：** 问“请自带什么、现场提供什么、是否另外收费”，等实际回答再决定。
- **名额已满：** 确认是否有正式候补，或选择别的课程；不把候补当报名成功。
- **可及服务或照片规则不清：** 先向主办方确认能否满足你的具体需要，不默许拍摄或只靠现场碰运气。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

课程可能免费、付费、仅需现场到场或必须提前报名；规则由当期主办方决定，本文不复制任何机构的价格与截止。可用电话、柜台或可信协助替代网页操作，但付款/报名决定由你作。确认适配不是课程教学质量保证；如果条件不合适，选择退出也是有效的结果。

## English

Use this Skill when an ordinary public-library, community-centre, or museum class interests you but the material, level, cost, or sign-up method is unclear. Make a **fit check before committing**. The result is “fits and registered as required / officially drop-in” or “does not fit, for a stated reason.” A first-time or solo label does not guarantee that the class will adapt to you. Actually attending the first session is another task.

### Preparation and inputs

Write one thing you hope to gain and your available date and place or online method, spending limit, current skills, materials you will bring, and any mobility, sensory, or language support you need. On the organiser's current class page check date, audience or age, level and prerequisites, registration, space, supplied or required materials, added costs, photography notice, cancellation, and support channel. An old post or similarly named class may differ. Hazardous tools, therapy, professional qualifications, and child-supervision duties need other specialist checks outside this Everyday Skill.

### Execution

1. **Compare level and purpose.** Match “beginner,” “advanced,” or “bring prior work” conditions to what you actually know. If unclear, ask the organiser a specific question such as “Can someone who has never used this tool join?” Interest in the topic does not erase a prerequisite.
2. **Confirm materials and participation mode.** List what you must bring, what the organiser supplies, and what is optional. Check whether every session is required or this is a one-off. Unlisted supplies are not automatically free. Ask whether equipment is accessible and whether photography can be declined if relevant.
3. **Check the whole commitment.** Can you meet date, location, journey, session count, price and materials cost, sign-up deadline, cancellation rule, and access support? Ask about any unknown that would change your decision. A blank price field is not proof that no charge exists.
4. **Make a go or no-go decision.** If conditions work and you accept them, use the organiser's official site, phone, or desk to register, or note its actual “no registration, just attend” instruction. If not, record the specific mismatch and stop; do not pay or hold a place to avoid awkwardness.
5. **Confirm your status.** A registration-required class needs an organiser confirmation, waitlist status, or staff answer. A submitted form with no result is “registration pending,” not admitted. Note session and required supplies privately without writing account credentials.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You can state level, materials, cost and time, participation method, and support conditions, and know whether you are registered, waitlisted, allowed to drop in, or declining for a reason. Opening a listing without matching it to your actual constraints is not a fit decision.

- **It says “beginner-friendly” but lists prior tools:** Use the specific prerequisite and ask whether zero experience is accepted instead of relying on a slogan.
- **Materials are incomplete:** Ask what to bring, what is supplied, and whether there is another cost before deciding.
- **Places are full:** Check for a formal waitlist or another class; do not call a waitlist confirmed enrollment.
- **Access or photo rules are unclear:** Ask whether your actual needs can be met rather than assume consent or hope for a solution at the door.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

A class may be free, paid, drop-in, or registration-only; the current organiser decides. No institution's price or deadline is copied here. Phone, desk, or trusted help can replace solo web use, while you make payment and enrollment choices. A fit check does not guarantee teaching quality. Declining an unsuitable class is a valid outcome.
