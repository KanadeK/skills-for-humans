## 中文

当可靠配方要求普通少油煎炒、上色或快速翻动，但食材一次下锅会把浅锅铺满、叠高或挤到不能翻时，加载这份 Skill。你的结果是**每一批都有按配方接触锅面的空间和独立完成检查**，不是把同一总时长平均给更多食材。食物安全熟度仍按具体食物判断，不因锅面有颜色就自动通过。

### 准备与输入

确认这次食物量、切块规格、锅面可用面积、配方要求的翻动与上色方式，以及成品会等多久。生食、熟食分开用可耐热或适用的容器盛放；工具触过生肉等原料后按食品安全要求处理，不回到已熟食物上。开始前把一批量放在冷锅或旁边容器中目测是否能铺开并移动，别在油已热时才重算场地。

### 执行步骤

1. **先按动作确定批量。** 配方若要求翻动或某一面接触锅，食物要能在锅上移动，不能堆成厚层。没有跨锅具的“每批 X 克”标准；食材大小、锅面和你可控制的工具共同决定一批。太多就分出下一批并放在安全位置。
2. **逐批进入已经受控的锅。** 第一批按[预热与下锅流程](../preheat-and-load-a-saute-pan/SKILL.md)开始；完成或需暂离锅时用合适工具移到单独的干净容器。下一批入锅前核对锅面是否还有烧焦残留、油是否异常或需要按配方重新调热；不能直接把上一批的计时器递给下一批。
3. **为每批记自己的起点和终点。** 记录真正下锅时间、翻动/取出依据；同配方不同批的食材量、锅温和残液可能不同。若第一批未达所需熟度，先按食物专属流程处理，不用第二批的时间替它盖章。
4. **不要让完成批次无限等待。** 配方若要求合并回锅、马上上桌或按食品安全要求保温，提前计划；生肉/禽/海鲜与即食食物分别处理。若你无法安全安排等待与复热，就减少总批量或改成可按时完成的做法，不把台面当保温设备。
5. **最后复核每批。** 需要安全核心温度的食物分别用适用[熟度检查](../check-doneness-and-store-leftovers/SKILL.md)。全部符合后再按配方合并、调味或上桌；若某批差异太大，保持分开继续或停止，不用漂亮的第一批替最后一批作证。

### 完成与停止

每一批都有可解释的下锅、翻动、完成与暂放路径，合并后仍符合食物安全和配方结果，就完成了分批。锅开始冒烟、油剧烈异常、熟食与生食混放、没有安全暂放位置或你无法看守热源时停下；让锅空一会儿比强塞下一批更可控。

### 常见报错与补救

- **食材堆成山，底部冒水：** 如果配方目标是锅面上色，先按安全条件分出一部分；把这批实际变成的湿煮状态如实记录，不宣称已完成原煎炒效果。
- **第二批使用第一批的计时：** 重新从真实下锅时刻观察锅面，不复制时长当熟度证据。
- **熟食放回生食盘：** 立刻停止这条盛放路径，按适用食品安全指导处理污染风险；下次开火前标出独立的熟食落点。
- **第一批等到变凉：** 按配方与适用食品安全要求决定及时合并、保温或其他安全处理；不是一律把火开大重炒。
- **锅面残留变黑或冒烟：** 关热，在安全后核对是否还能用本锅继续，不直接把新食材盖到焦糊上。

### 假设、替代与副作用

本 Skill 只处理普通浅锅、少油、可控煎炒。配方可能追求柔软、上色或炒匀，分批规则服务于**那个目标**而非通用口感。握力、站立或注意力不足时，可减小每批、用较轻的工具、坐着在冷态分份或请现场可信的人协助；不因分批多就让热锅无人看守。副作用是多洗一个盛放盘，但每一批都得到真实锅面。

## English

Load this Skill when a reliable ordinary shallow-pan recipe calls for sautéing, browning, or quick turning, but all the food at once would cover, stack, or crowd the pan so it cannot move. Give **each batch room for the pan contact the recipe requires and its own completion check**. Do not divide one total cooking time by the number of pieces. Food safety remains food-specific; a brown surface is not an automatic pass.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm the amount and cut size, usable pan surface, turning and colour goal, and how long finished food may wait. Have separate suitable dishes for raw and cooked food. Tools that touched raw meat or other risky ingredients need handling under food-safety rules before touching finished food. Judge a workable batch on the cold pan or in a nearby container before heating oil; a hot pan is a poor place to calculate floor space.

### Execution steps

1. **Set batch size by the required movement.** Food must be able to move and make the recipe's intended pan contact rather than forming a deep pile. There is no universal “X grams per batch” across pans. Piece size, pan area, and the tool you control define this batch. Set the rest aside safely.
2. **Use the controlled pan one batch at a time.** Start the first under [Preheat and Load a Sauté Pan](../preheat-and-load-a-saute-pan/SKILL.md). Move a completed or temporarily removed batch to a separate clean dish. Before the next batch, check the surface for burnt residue, abnormal oil, and any recipe-directed heat adjustment. The first batch's timer does not transfer to the second.
3. **Record an independent start and finish.** Note real pan contact, turning, and removal evidence for each batch. Even under one recipe, food amount, pan temperature, and leftover liquid may differ. If batch one is underdone, handle it under its food-specific process rather than letting batch two's time certify it.
4. **Do not let completed batches wait indefinitely.** Plan any recipe-directed return to the pan, immediate service, or food-safe holding. Keep raw animal foods separate from ready-to-eat food. If you cannot hold or reheat safely, reduce the total quantity or choose a manageable method rather than calling a countertop a warmer.
5. **Check every batch before combining.** Use applicable [doneness evidence](../check-doneness-and-store-leftovers/SKILL.md) separately when core temperature matters. Combine, season, or serve only when each batch qualifies. If one differs markedly, keep it separate and continue safely or stop; the beautiful first batch does not certify the last.

### Success and stop

Every batch has an explainable loading, turning, completion, and holding route, and the combined food still meets recipe and safety conditions. Stop for a smoking pan, abnormal oil, raw/cooked cross-contamination, no safe landing place, or no one able to attend the heat. An empty pan for a moment is safer than forcing in another batch.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Food piles up and liquid gathers underneath:** If the recipe's goal is pan browning, divide it where safe. Record that this batch moved toward moist cooking instead of claiming the original sauté result.
- **The second batch inherits the first timer:** Observe from its actual pan-entry event. Copied minutes are not doneness evidence.
- **Cooked food returns to a raw-food plate:** Stop that holding route and handle contamination under applicable food-safety guidance. Mark a separate finished-food place before heating next time.
- **The first batch cools while waiting:** Choose timely combining, suitable holding, or another safe action under the recipe and food rules, not automatic maximum-heat re-frying.
- **Residue blackens or the pan smokes:** Turn off heat and assess safely whether this pan can continue; do not bury scorched residue under the new batch.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers a controllable ordinary shallow pan with little oil. The recipe might aim for tenderness, browning, or even mixing; batching serves **that goal**, not a universal texture. For limited grip, standing, or attention, use smaller batches, lighter tools, cold portioning while seated, or trusted in-person help. More batches never authorize leaving a hot pan unattended. You may wash one extra dish while each batch receives real pan space.
