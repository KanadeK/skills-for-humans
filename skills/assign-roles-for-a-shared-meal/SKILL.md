## 中文

当两人或更多人要做**同一顿普通家常餐**，菜单与大致顺序已经决定，却还没有说清谁准备、谁接手、谁收尾时，在开始烹调前加载。你要得到一张每个关键动作都有负责人的简短表，不是让最快或最会做饭的人默默接管全厨房。

本 Skill 不选择菜单、重算份量或给多道菜重新排锅灶顺序；这些结果各有自己的任务。做饭过程中突然中断后的实际状态交接也另处理。这里不教刀工、熟度判断或危险设备操作。

### 准备与输入

把既定菜品及准备、烹调、上桌和清理的粗略步骤列出来。让每位参与者说清可用时间、愿意做的事、不会做或不能安全做的事，以及是否需要坐着、放大文字或听到提示。先确认大家同意参加哪些任务；有人只是来吃饭，不会自动变成洗碗岗位。

### 执行

1. **按结果分任务，不按人头平分动作。** 将洗切准备、照看正在加热的食物、上桌前核对、端上桌及清理列成几项可结束的任务。具体菜序仍沿用已经定好的计划。
2. **每项写一个负责人和必要协助。** 负责人能确认它何时完成并报告变化；协助者不替代负责人。把需要持续注意的热源交给能一直在场且知道何时停止的人，不能把“大家看一下”写成安全责任；无人明确接手而负责人须离开时，先关闭灶具热源。
3. **标出计划内交接。** 在任务旁写“谁完成什么信号后，交给谁，接手者要收到什么信息”，例如“蔬菜已洗净且台面可用 → 交给负责切配者”。交接前由原负责人复述当前状态，接手者确认自己已接到；无人接手就暂停这一步。
4. **把清理写进同一张表。** 给用过的工具、台面、普通垃圾和饭后餐具分别指定归位或清理负责人及触发点。涉及生食接触的器具/表面另按适用食品安全指导清洁，不能用“最后谁有空”代替。
5. **做一次空缺检查。** 指着每一项让大家回答“谁做、何时交、完成怎么知道”。关键检查、热源或清理无人能承担，就减小菜单、请合适的人加入，或不开工。

### 成功与停止

开工前你有一张能让所有参与者看懂的责任表：每项任务一位负责人、计划交接信号和清理归属。完成不要求工作量完全相等，也不保证每个人都愿意接受最初的安排；不同意时先改表，不暗中分配。

### 常见报错与补救

- **两个人都以为对方在看火：** 明确唯一在场的热源负责人；找不到就先不点火。
- **一个人连续承担所有高注意力步骤：** 重新分给能安全完成的人，或减少同时进行的菜品；不靠催促增加注意力。
- **交接只说“好了”：** 补上具体完成状态、仍未做的动作和接手者名字。
- **清理无人认领：** 在开工前分配触发点；没人能清理就调整任务规模，别把饭后责任留给空气。

### 假设、替代与副作用

这是一顿普通家庭餐的协作约定，不是餐厅排班制度。家庭、文化和能力差异会影响谁愿意碰生食、热源或清洁；让当事人说明并同意，避免按性别、年龄或做饭经验猜角色。听说或阅读不方便时把表写大、口述并请对方复述，或用可访问的共享记录。副作用是“我以为你会做”需要先找签收人。

## English

Load this Skill **before cooking one ordinary home meal together** when two or more people have a menu and rough sequence but no clear answer for who prepares, receives, checks and cleans. Your result is a short ownership map for the essential work, not an assumption that the fastest or most experienced cook silently owns the kitchen.

This does not select the menu, resize portions or reorder several dishes. An unexpected interruption after cooking begins needs an actual-state handoff elsewhere. Knife technique, doneness decisions and hazardous equipment operation are outside this plan.

### Preparation and inputs

List the chosen dishes and their broad preparation, cooking, serving and cleanup steps. Ask each participant about available time, tasks they agree to do, tasks they cannot do safely, and any need for seated work, larger text or audible cues. Confirm consent to a role first. A person who came to eat does not automatically become the dishwasher.

### Execution

1. **Divide by finishable results, not headcount.** Write down preparation, attention to food on heat, the pre-serving check, serving and cleanup as bounded tasks. Keep the dish sequence already chosen.
2. **Give each task one owner and any helpers.** The owner can say when the task is done and report a change; helpers do not replace that owner. Give an active heat source to someone who can remain present and knows when to stop. “Everyone will watch it” is not a safety assignment; turn off a hob burner if its owner must leave and nobody has explicitly taken over.
3. **Mark planned handoffs.** For each, write what completion signal releases the task, who receives it, and what information they need. For example, “vegetables washed and counter free → prep owner.” The outgoing owner states the current condition; the receiver acknowledges it. Pause that step if nobody can receive it.
4. **Put cleanup on the same map.** Give used tools, counters, ordinary waste and after-meal dishes an owner and trigger for clearing or returning them. Surfaces and utensils that contacted raw food still need cleaning under applicable food-safety guidance; “whoever is free later” is not an owner.
5. **Check for blanks.** Point to every task and answer who does it, when it transfers and what done looks like. If an essential check, heat task or cleanup has no willing capable owner, reduce the meal, find suitable help or do not start yet.

### Success and stop

Before starting, you have a map all participants can use: one owner for each essential task, a planned handoff signal and cleanup responsibility. Success does not require equal task counts or force anyone to accept the first draft. Change the map if someone disagrees instead of assigning them silently.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Both people think the other is watching heat:** Assign one present heat owner. Do not start heating until that is possible.
- **One person has every high-attention step at once:** Reassign to a capable person or reduce simultaneous dishes; rushing cannot manufacture attention.
- **A handoff says only “done”:** Add the actual completion state, unfinished action and receiver's name.
- **Nobody owns cleanup:** Assign triggers before starting. If no one can clean, reduce the work instead of leaving responsibility to the air.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is an agreement for an ordinary home meal, not a restaurant staffing rule. Family, culture and ability affect willingness to handle raw food, heat or cleaning; ask and obtain agreement instead of guessing from gender, age or experience. If speech, hearing or reading is difficult, write a large card, read it aloud and have each person repeat their part, or use an accessible shared note. The side effect is that “I thought you were doing it” now needs a named recipient.
