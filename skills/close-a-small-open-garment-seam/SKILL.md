## 中文

当你的一件**普通、非防护用途**衣物只有一小段原接缝线断开，两片布本身仍完整、原缝线位置可辨时，用这份 Skill 把两片布重新连起来。结果是衣物在正常轻拉和试穿时不再张开；不承诺手缝强度等于原工业车缝。若布料纤维已撕裂、边缘缺失，或这个连接承担防护/承重功能，不把它当“只少几针”处理。

### 准备与输入

先脱下衣物，找开口的两端、原接缝位置、两侧预留的布边、附近仍牢固的旧线。确认不是拉链、扣眼、弹力带固定处或大面积磨薄的材料。准备能穿过面料而不明显撑大针孔、也不会弯断的手针，和与原衣物厚度/颜色相近的线；再备剪刀、明亮稳定桌面，必要时用夹子或珠针暂时对齐。无法判断面料、旧缝路径或衣物价值较高时，先找制造商或缝纫专业人员。

防护服、承重背带、运动安全装备、特殊涂层/防水缝或严重破损不在此补。针线只是在普通布片间恢复接缝，不能出具功能安全证明。

### 执行

1. **先判定“线断了”还是“布断了”。** 将衣物翻到反面，轻轻分开开口，看两片布边是否还有完整纤维与足够原接缝余量；布边大片散开、线孔连成裂口或损伤继续延伸时停下，交补片/专业判断。不要剪去看似多余的布边来“修整”到无布可缝。
2. **沿原位置对齐。** 把两片布按未损坏处的缝线方向合拢，使外面纹路与边缘自然接续，别为了藏开口把衣物拉得更窄。用手持、夹子或少量可计数的珠针暂时固定；确认没有把前后两层衣物错缝在一起。穿着的状态可用于比对，实际下针前必须脱下。
3. **从牢固旧线处起针。** 穿线、打结，让针从布反面穿出，在开口起点之外的完整旧接缝附近做几针锚定。沿原接缝线用短而均匀的回针把两片布接起来：每次向前出针后，下一针回到上一段末端再向前穿出，形成紧密接续。线要贴合，不要拉到面料起皱。平针可以临时定位，但承受日常拉力的最终连接需要更稳固的针路。
4. **跨过开口再稳固收尾。** 接到另一侧完好旧线附近，继续少量针脚把新旧接缝连起来；在布反面固定线结并剪去多余线。取走所有夹子与珠针，确认针尖和断针均有去向，不把锐物留在衣服里。
5. **在低风险状态下验收。** 翻回正面，平铺检查外形是否偏斜，手轻拉接缝两侧看有没有再开口；再安全试穿做一次普通动作。若布边开始撕、针孔扩大、穿着紧绷或新线立刻松开，停止穿用并请专业人员修补，别在同处叠更多紧线。

### 成功条件

原缝线被一小段手缝接续，两片完整布边仍对齐，轻拉和普通试穿不再露出开口，线结稳固且没有遗留针。若发现其实是布面损伤或高应力连接，停止手缝并明确转交，也算完成正确判断。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只缝开口中间，不接到旧牢固线：** 拆掉不稳的小段，从旧缝完整处开始并在另一端完整处结束。
- **线一拉布就皱：** 停止收紧，重新按原轮廓对齐并用较均匀的回针；不要用缩小衣服换取“看不见缝”。
- **把里布或另一侧袖管一起缝进来：** 在继续前检查每针的背面路径，必要时拆到误缝处重来。
- **旧布边已经磨薄或开裂：** 停止加针，转补片或专业维修；重复穿孔可能让洞更大。
- **找不到针或珠针：** 暂停试穿，清点工作面与衣物；锐物确认移除前不交还给别人。
- **暂时没法完成：** 把衣物放在不穿的待修位置，并标明开线处；松松别几针不能当作可承受正常拉力的永久修复。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里只处理短、直观、低应力的普通衣物接缝，两边布料仍可承针且原线位置可见。复杂内衬、弹力运动衣、拉链固定、大片裂损与功能性密封缝需不同技术。视力不便时用放大灯或可信帮助；手部力量不足时用顶针、夹子与桌面支撑，不在身体上缝。内侧手缝可能比原机缝明显，但衣物不应因此变窄或刺肤。

## English

Use this Skill when a **normal, non-protective garment** has a short opening where seam stitches failed, while both fabric pieces remain sound and the original seam line can be followed. Rejoin the two pieces so the opening stays closed under a light pull and ordinary try-on. Hand stitching is not a claim that the seam now equals factory stitching. If the fabric itself tore, an edge is missing, or the join carries protective or load-bearing function, this is more than a few missing stitches.

### Preparation and inputs

Take the garment off. Find both ends of the opening, original stitch line, fabric allowances on both sides, and intact nearby stitching. Check that this is not a zipper, buttonhole, elastic attachment, or broad worn-through area. Have a hand needle that crosses the fabric without enlarging holes or bending, thread suited to the garment's thickness and colour, scissors, and a steady well-lit table. Clips or a few countable pins may hold alignment. If the material, seam route, or a valuable garment is unclear, ask its maker or a sewing professional.

Protective clothing, load-bearing straps, sports safety equipment, special coatings or waterproof seams, and extensive damage are outside this repair. Thread can restore an ordinary join; it cannot certify functional safety.

### Execution

1. **Distinguish broken stitches from broken fabric.** Turn the garment inside out and gently inspect the opening. Both edges need sound fibres and usable original seam allowance. Stop for patch or professional assessment if an edge has frayed away, needle holes have become a tear, or damage keeps spreading. Do not trim away the very cloth needed for a seam.
2. **Align the original route.** Bring the two pieces together following intact stitching so outer pattern and edges continue naturally. Do not narrow the garment just to hide the gap. Hold by hand, clips, or a few counted pins. Check that the front and back of a garment or a lining have not been accidentally caught together. You may compare fit while worn, but remove the garment before sewing.
3. **Start in sound old stitching.** Thread and knot the needle. Bring it up from the wrong side and anchor a few stitches near the intact seam beyond one end of the opening. Use short, even backstitches along the original line: after each forward exit, take the next entry back to the end of the preceding segment, then emerge ahead, making a close join. Keep thread snug without puckering. Running stitches can position pieces temporarily; the everyday join needs a stronger stitch path.
4. **Cross the gap and finish securely.** Continue a little into intact stitching beyond the other end so new and old sections meet. Tie off on the wrong side and trim excess thread. Remove all clips and pins. Account for the needle point and any broken needle; do not leave a sharp object in the clothing.
5. **Test without high load.** Turn it right side out and inspect for a crooked shape. Pull lightly on either side of the seam and see whether it opens, then try it on for one ordinary movement. If fabric starts tearing, holes enlarge, fit pulls tight, or the new thread loosens at once, stop wearing it and seek professional repair rather than layering more tight stitches there.

### Success

A short hand-stitched section joins the original seam, both sound fabric edges align, light pulling and ordinary try-on do not reopen it, the knot holds, and no needle remains. Discovering fabric damage or a high-stress join and stopping for the right repair also completes the decision.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Stitches only cross the middle of the gap:** Remove the unstable section and begin and end in sound old seam stitching.
- **Thread puckers the cloth:** Stop tightening, realign to the original outline, and sew more evenly. Do not shrink the garment to make the repair appear invisible.
- **Lining or the far side of a sleeve gets caught:** Inspect the wrong side as you sew; undo stitches back to the mistake and repeat.
- **The original edge is thin or torn:** Stop adding holes. Choose a patch or professional work; repeated punctures may widen the tear.
- **A needle or pin is missing:** Stop the try-on and account for the work area and garment. Do not hand it to someone before all sharps are removed.
- **You cannot finish now:** Put the garment aside, mark the opening, and do not wear it under load. Loose temporary basting is not a permanent everyday seam.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers a short, visible, low-stress seam on ordinary clothing, with two sound edges and a followable original line. Complex linings, stretch sportswear, zipper attachments, broad tears, and functional sealed seams require other techniques. Use magnification or trusted help when small stitches are hard to see. For limited grip, use a thimble, clips, and table support rather than sewing on the body. The inside repair may be more visible than factory stitching; it should not narrow the garment or scratch the skin.
