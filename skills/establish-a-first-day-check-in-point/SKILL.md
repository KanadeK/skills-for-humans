## 中文

当你第一天**已经到达**新工作、学习或志愿场所，但尚不清楚要找谁、在哪等、先做什么时，用这份 Skill 完成普通报到和实用定位。结果是一个被现场人员确认的联系人、今日起点和下一检查点。出门前收集地址和材料属于本域另一份 Skill；接受具体任务前问清产出归 H242。不要自行进入受限区域或开始专业设备操作。

### 准备与输入

拿出正式报到通知中的姓名/部门、入口、时间和联系渠道。注意现场标识或接待台的当前说明可能比旧地图更近；冲突时问有职责的接待人，不跟陌生人走到不确定区域。远程第一天则以正式会议或组织沟通入口作为“报到点”，但不要把能进入群聊当成已被负责人接收。

### 执行

1. **在允许的入口完成报到。** 向接待台或通知指定的人说“我是今天来参加 X 的，通知让我找 Y；请问在哪里报到？”若需登记，按现场正式流程，只提供必要资料；本篇不处理证件或背景审核规则。远程时在约定频道向指定联系人发一条到场消息并等实际回应。
2. **确认一个负责的联系人。** 记下谁负责你今天的安排，以及其可用的现场位置或正式消息方式。接待人若只负责指路，问下一位真实负责人的名字；“有人点头”不是完整的交接。
3. **核今天的三个实用点。** 向负责人确认：现在先待在哪里、今天第一段安排/集合时间、需要先领或带哪件普通材料/设备。若有可及入口、座位、听力/视觉形式或休息需求，具体说明并问可用方案；无需公开多余私人信息。
4. **按许可进入下一步。** 跟随明确路线到获允许的等候、介绍或培训位置，核时钟与集合点。没有说明时在接待处或明确公共区域等候并联系负责人，不自行登录别人的账号、取钥匙或动设备。
5. **留下回找路线。** 记录联系人、当前地点、下一活动时间、未解决材料/权限问题和遇到变化时该问谁。若负责人迟到或计划改变，把实际答复更新到记录；无需假装第一天已经掌握所有流程。

### 完成、常见问题与补救

你已被合适的现场/远程联系人接收，知道此刻在哪里、下一件正式安排何时开始，以及缺材料/权限时该找谁；或明确停在安全的公共等待点并已按正式渠道求助。仅走进楼里，不算完成报到。

- **接待处说查不到你：** 核通知日期和联系人，再通过原正式渠道确认；不借别人身份进入。
- **负责人不在：** 留在允许的等候区，联系通知中的备选人或组织接待；记下你实际到场的时间。
- **当天安排和邮件不同：** 请负责人确认新的时间/地点，更新记录，不靠旧截图独自行动。
- **缺材料或账号还没开：** 把缺口交给负责人员，先参加允许的环节；不使用共享凭据或借他人权限。
- **入口或说明不适合你的可及需求：** 请求实际可用的路线/呈现方式；无法安全到达时保持联系并停止移动。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

有的场所无接待台，有的只有远程入口或志愿协调人；此时“报到点”是通知指定的正式联系人。不同组织有各自安全、登记、材料和培训规则，本篇只帮你确认事实，不发布统一政策。第一天仍可能有未知，但至少知道谁能给出答案和何时再核。

## English

Load this Skill on the first day **after arriving** at a new ordinary work, study, or volunteer setting when you do not know whom to meet, where to wait, or what happens first. Complete a basic check-in and locate a confirmed contact, today's starting point, and the next checkpoint. Collecting address and materials beforehand is a separate Skill; clarifying a particular assignment is H242. Do not enter restricted areas or operate specialist equipment on your own.

### Preparation and inputs

Have the official check-in notice with person or department, entrance, time, and contact route. Current signs or reception instructions may be newer than an old map; ask a responsible receptionist when they conflict rather than following an unknown person into uncertain space. For a remote first day, use the formal meeting or organisation channel as the check-in point. Entering a group chat alone does not mean a coordinator has received you.

### Execution

1. **Check in through an allowed entrance.** Tell reception or the named person, “I'm here for X today. My notice says to find Y. Where do I check in?” Follow the site's formal registration process with only necessary information. This Skill does not decide ID or background-check rules. Remotely, send one arrival message to the designated contact and wait for an actual response.
2. **Confirm a responsible contact.** Note who handles your day and where or how to reach them. If the first person only directs traffic, ask for the actual coordinator's name. A nod from someone nearby is not a complete handoff.
3. **Check three practical points.** Ask where to be now, the first activity or meeting time, and which ordinary material or equipment to collect or bring first. State a concrete access need for an entrance, seat, hearing, vision, or break if relevant, and ask for a usable arrangement without disclosing more private detail than necessary.
4. **Move to the permitted next step.** Follow a clear route to an allowed waiting, introduction, or training area, confirming time and meeting point. With no directions, wait at reception or another clearly public area and contact the coordinator. Do not use another person's account, take keys, or operate equipment without instruction.
5. **Leave a route back to help.** Record contact, present location, next activity time, unresolved materials or access needs, and whom to ask if plans change. Update it with the coordinator's actual reply if they are late or the plan moves. A first day need not contain instant mastery of every procedure.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

The appropriate in-person or remote contact has acknowledged you, and you know where to be now, when the next official activity starts, and whom to ask about missing material or access. Alternatively, you are waiting in an allowed public place and have requested help through the formal route. Merely entering the building is not a complete check-in.

- **Reception cannot find you:** Check date and contact in the notice, then confirm through the original official route. Do not borrow someone else's identity to enter.
- **The coordinator is absent:** Wait in an allowed area, reach the named backup or reception, and record when you actually arrived.
- **The day plan differs from email:** Have the coordinator confirm the new time or place and update your record rather than following an old screenshot.
- **A material or account is missing:** Tell the responsible person and join only permitted activities. Do not use shared credentials or another person's access.
- **The entrance or explanation is inaccessible:** Request a genuinely usable route or format. Stay in contact and stop moving if you cannot reach it safely.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Some places have no front desk; others have only a remote entry or volunteer coordinator. The check-in point is then the formal contact named in the notice. Each organisation sets its own security, registration, material, and training rules. This Skill confirms the facts instead of issuing a universal policy. Unknowns may remain on day one, but you know who can answer and when to check again.
