## 中文

当普通箱即将交运、你需要在途中和到达后第一段时间仍能做基本日常活动时加载。你要做出一个**按实际需要可拿到、有人保管、不会误入普通货物的必需物路线**。这里不决定新住处怎么布置、如何处理延误索赔，也不提供医疗、航空或其他承运法规解释。

### 准备与输入

先画出从旧住处离开、途中、到达、能打开普通箱之前的实际间隔；如果它不确定，就保留不确定，并按可行替代安排。问每位参与者在这段间隔内真实需要什么：洗漱、更换衣物、喝水或普通食品、充电、沟通、辅助使用物等按人和环境确定，不照搬一张标准购物单。核对交通方式、携带空间、物品本身的保管说明和实际服务方规则。

### 执行

1. **只收这段时间真正要用的东西。** 对每件写“谁在什么时候用 → 不用等普通箱打开的原因”。某件只在新家几周后才用，就仍可进常规组；即将使用的东西要保持可达。私人证件或特殊用品由本人或有权者管理，不公开放进大家都能翻的箱。
2. **选真实可携的容器和路径。** 优先让近期必需物随实际使用者或指定可信的人走；包的尺寸、重量与取用方式必须适合他们。若无法随行，不把“最后上车”当成自动能拿到的保证，而是向实际搬运方确认装载、到达和先取条件；条件不成立就调整替代方案。
3. **写明唯一保管人和取用点。** 告诉每位需要用的人，这组东西由谁携带、途中放在哪里、抵达时先放在哪里。随行包与普通搬家箱外观相近时加双方能识别的标记，并明确告诉装车者“这件不随普通箱装走”。
4. **做一次物品与路径回读。** 不必拆普通箱，从开口或可见清单核对关键物仍在可达包中；让保管人与使用者各自说出怎样取到。若某件仍在旧住处最后使用，按最后使用后的移交点加入，不提前从手里抽走。
5. **出发前再确认。** 核对人、包和实际路线。途中政策或空间不允许带某物时，遵守实际规则并取得合适替代；找不到替代就修改这段时间的活动或行程，不能将未知写成“已备好”。

### 完成与停止

在普通箱可打开前的真实时间段，每项基本活动都有可达物品或已确认替代，保管人和取用点能互相说清。包被装进普通货物、关键物品仍未找到、携带规则不明或保管人无法接手时，停止宣称“必需物已保留”，先重新安排。

### 常见报错与补救

- **写了“随身”，实际被装上车：** 在可安全处理且符合服务方安排时立即追回；否则确认何时能取出，并为之前的间隔安排真实替代。
- **包到了但某人打不开或拿不到：** 换适合其实际能力的位置、开合方式或指定愿意的协助者；不靠别人猜包里有啥。
- **预计当晚到，行程却可能延后：** 按实际可用窗口重新核对消耗品与替代地点；不把某家服务方的“首夜包”当成统一时长承诺。
- **多个人都需要同一件物品：** 约定使用顺序，取得另一可用件或不同替代，不在两个包清单中虚报同一件。
- **服务方拒绝某物随货同行：** 询问具体允许的路线；不要藏在普通箱里，危险内容交适用专业渠道。

### 假设、替代与副作用

适用于普通生活用品和有权接触的人；特殊储存、医疗使用、航空安检和危险物规则必须按真实物品/服务方要求另行核对。少预算时可用已有包和现有替代，不需购买“搬家专用箱”。行动、视听或阅读受限时，用本人习惯的可读/可触方式标记，安排愿意且有能力的人接手，而不是把整套物品放到只能别人找到的地方。副作用是必需物包有实际保管人，不再靠记忆在几十只箱里搜索。

## English

Load this Skill before ordinary boxes are handed over, when you still need to carry out basic daily activities in transit and during the first period after arrival. You will make **a route for a small set of real essentials that stays reachable, has a custodian and does not disappear into the normal shipment**. This does not set up the new home, manage delay claims or interpret medical, airline or other transport rules.

### Preparation and inputs

Map the actual interval from leaving the old place through travel and arrival until ordinary boxes can be opened. If that interval is uncertain, keep the uncertainty and plan a feasible alternative. Ask each person what they will really need during it: washing, clothes, water or ordinary food, charging, communication or access equipment according to the person and setting. Do not copy a generic shopping list. Check the travel mode, carrying space, each item's care instructions and the actual service provider's rules.

### Execution

1. **Select items for this interval.** For each one, record “who needs it when → why it cannot wait for normal boxes.” Something needed weeks later can stay with the normal goods; something due soon must be reachable. Personal documents or special items stay with the person or someone authorised, not in a box anyone can search.
2. **Choose a bag and route that really work.** Prefer to keep near-term essentials with the actual user or a named trusted person. Size, load and access method must suit them. If they cannot travel together, do not assume “loaded last” guarantees access. Confirm loading, arrival and first retrieval with the actual mover; if those conditions fail, arrange a real alternative.
3. **Name one custodian and pickup point.** Tell each person who holds the set, where it sits during travel and where it first lands on arrival. If it resembles ordinary moving boxes, mark it in a way both parties can identify and clearly tell loaders that it is outside the normal shipment.
4. **Read back both contents and access.** Check the key items in the reachable kit from its opening or visible list, without searching ordinary boxes. Have the custodian and user say how they will get each item. If one item is still in last use at the old place, attach it at its final-use transfer point rather than taking it too early.
5. **Check again before departure.** Confirm person, bag and real route. If a transport rule or limited space prevents carrying an item, obey the actual rule and secure a suitable alternative. If none exists, adjust the activities or journey; do not label an unknown “ready.”

### Success and stop

Until ordinary boxes are accessible, each real basic activity has a reachable item or confirmed substitute, and users and custodians can explain where to get it. If the kit has entered the ordinary shipment, a key item is still missing, carry rules are unclear, or the custodian cannot take it, stop claiming the essentials are ready and re-plan.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Marked “with me” but loaded into the truck:** Retrieve it immediately when safe and allowed by the provider. Otherwise confirm the first real retrieval time and arrange a substitute for the gap.
- **The bag arrives but someone cannot open or reach it:** Change its position or closure to suit their actual ability, or name a willing helper. Do not rely on others guessing the contents.
- **Expected tonight, arrival may slip:** Recheck consumables and real alternatives for the changed window. One provider's “first night” example is not a universal duration promise.
- **Several people need the same single item:** Agree on use order, find another available item or a different substitute. Do not list a nonexistent copy in two bags.
- **The provider will not carry one item:** Ask for an allowed route. Do not hide it in an ordinary carton; specialist channels handle dangerous contents.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary living supplies and people authorised to use them. Check special storage, medical use, airline screening and hazardous-goods rules against the real item and provider. Use an existing bag and available substitutes if money is tight; a purpose-made moving carton is unnecessary. For limited mobility, vision, hearing or reading, mark the kit in a familiar readable or tactile way and arrange willing capable help. Do not put the whole set where only somebody else can find it. The side effect is a named custodian for the kit instead of a memory-based search through dozens of cartons.
