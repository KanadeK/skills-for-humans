## 中文

当你已选好一顿普通家庭餐，却在**开火前**发现时间、器具、人手或注意力不足，按原菜单做不完时，加载这份 Skill。目标是删去非必要菜或换成确实能完成的一餐，并安排未使用食材；不教你用更大火力追回丢掉的时间，也不处理锅里已经发生的失败。

### 准备与输入

确认现在到预计开饭还有多少现实时间、确定有几个人吃、原菜单每道菜需要的主要食材、设备和必须看守的步骤。检查哪些食材已拆包或解冻、它们实际的标签与保存史、空闲的冷藏位置，以及谁能可靠协助。视力、站立、握持或同时看多个计时器有困难时，把这些当作实际容量；不需要为了履行原菜单让一个人临时增加手和眼睛。

### 执行

1. **先说出为什么做不完。** 是有一条长等待步骤、共用一口锅、两个需要持续看守的步骤撞在一起，还是材料/人手没有到？只用已确认的条件重估，不把原菜谱上的总分钟数直接相加或假设所有步骤都能并行。
2. **保留一顿饭的主线。** 确认人数、已知普通限制和一套能成立的主要食物；把装饰菜、重复功能的配菜和只是“原来答应过想试试”的额外项目标为可删。不是每道菜都要保留，才能说这是一顿饭。
3. **逐项判定：做、延期、删去。** 优先保留安全可用且你熟悉、能在现有设备上完成的主线。无法在当前时间完成的菜可以延期；如果它需要持续看守而你已经安排另一个高注意力步骤，不同时开做。换一道你知道能完成的简单菜可以，但不用未经验证的烹饪捷径、临时过敏替代或极高火力来替原方案续命。
4. **检查缩水后是否真的能开工。** 为保留的每道菜写食材、器具、执行者、关键等待和适用的熟度确认。核对共用设备、可同时看守的步骤和真实开饭时间。仍做不完，就再删一项、改成更简单的完整餐食或诚实延后；不要把“不可能”藏在漂亮的时间表里。
5. **给没用上的食材去处。** 未开封或已开封食品按**这件产品**标签和实际时间/温度史及时归回合适条件；需要低温的食品不放在台面等“下次有空”。保存史不明或安全条件已失效时停止把它算作可用库存，按当地食品安全指导处理。已经开火时，这份开工前计划停止适用，转去处理真实的烹调状态。
6. **宣布新版本。** 用一句话或短表写明“保留什么、取消什么、何时吃、谁做、剩余食材在哪里”。如果上桌内容或时间会变，及时告知相关人；完成不要求任何人假装原来的四菜一汤仍在运行。

### 完成条件

留下的菜构成一顿符合已确认需求、能在现有时间与设备下完成的餐食；食品安全要求和需要看守的步骤没有被当作可删项；未用食材有真实去向。若连最小方案也不成立，明确延后或另选可行餐食是本次有效输出。

### 常见报错与补救

- **先备了三道菜才决定哪道不做：** 开工前就删项目，只准备最终版本需要的食材；已拆包食品按真实状态及时保存。
- **以更大火力补回所有延迟：** 重新评估菜数和开饭时间；高火力不能替代适用的熟度检查，也不能让你同时看守更多锅。
- **删到只剩不成餐的零散配料：** 回到确认人数和这顿饭的主线，选择一个能完成的组合或诚实改时间；不用“已删三道菜”本身当成功。
- **被删的易腐食材已经在室温等太久：** 不把它放回冰箱就宣布历史清零；依具体食品和当地指导决定是否还能保留，未知时停止使用。
- **有人临时增加或减少：** 人数变化先按该任务重新确定份量和全桌安排；这里处理的是既定规模下菜单做不完。

### 假设、替代与副作用

这份 Skill 面向普通家常饭，没有统一的“完整菜单”或人均克数。设备、熟练度、文化和行动能力会改变可完成范围；用你家的现实条件决定主线，必要时减少菜数或得到已确认的协助。严重过敏、医疗饮食、危险刀工、失控热油、电气/燃气维修都不在范围内。食品需按具体标签和所在地权威保持安全，本文不发明统一时限。副作用是少一道菜和可能变更开饭时间；两者都比把安全步骤从计划里删掉更诚实。

## English

Load this Skill when an ordinary home meal has already been chosen, but **before heat starts** you discover that time, equipment, helpers, or attention will not support the full menu. Remove optional dishes or choose a genuinely finishable meal, and give unused food a destination. Do not make up lost time with more heat or try to repair a failure already in the pan.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm the real time until serving, the confirmed diners, and each planned dish's main ingredients, equipment, and steps that need watching. Check which food is already opened or thawed, its actual label and storage history, reliable cold space, and help that is truly available. Limits on seeing, standing, gripping, or following several timers are real capacity limits. You do not have to grow extra hands or eyes to fulfil an earlier menu.

### Execution

1. **Name the bottleneck first.** Is there a long waiting step, one shared pan, two attention-heavy steps at once, or food or help that did not arrive? Re-estimate from confirmed conditions. Do not merely add recipe times or assume every step can run in parallel.
2. **Keep the meal's essential job.** Confirm diners, known ordinary restrictions, and a main combination that makes sense as a meal. Mark decorative dishes, duplicate sides, and “we thought it might be fun to try” extras as removable. Serving a meal does not require preserving every original dish.
3. **Decide for each dish: do, defer, or drop.** Keep food known to be safe and a main dish you can make with current equipment and skill. Defer work that cannot finish now. Do not start two steps that both need continuous attention when only one person can watch them. Choose a simpler dish you already know how to make if useful, but avoid unverified shortcuts, improvised severe-allergy substitutions, and extreme heat as replacements for the old plan.
4. **Check whether the reduced menu can actually start.** For each retained dish, note ingredients, equipment, owner, critical waits, and its applicable doneness check. Check shared tools, watchable steps, and the serving time. If it still does not fit, drop another dish, choose a simpler complete meal, or honestly serve later. A neat timeline does not make an impossible task possible.
5. **Give unused food a destination.** Return unopened or opened ingredients promptly to conditions required by **this product's** label and real time and temperature history. Do not leave cold food on the counter for a future “when we have time.” If history is unknown or required conditions have failed, stop counting the food as usable and follow local food-safety guidance. If heat has already started, stop applying this pre-cooking plan and respond to the actual cooking state.
6. **Announce the new version.** In a sentence or small table, record what remains, what was dropped, when people will eat, who does what, and where unused food went. Tell relevant people when serving time or food changes. Completion does not require anyone to pretend the original four dishes are still running.

### Success

The retained dishes make a meal that meets confirmed needs and can be completed with current time and equipment. Food-safety checks and steps that require attention have not been treated as optional. Unused food has a real destination. If even the smallest version cannot work, clearly deferring or choosing another feasible meal is a valid result.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You prepare three dishes before deciding what to drop:** Remove work before starting and prepare only the final version. Store any already opened food according to its real condition promptly.
- **You raise the heat to recover every delay:** Reconsider dish count and serving time. More heat cannot replace a relevant doneness check or let you watch more pans at once.
- **The result is just unrelated ingredients:** Return to confirmed diners and the meal's main job. Choose one complete combination or say the meal will be later. Dropping three dishes is not itself success.
- **Perishable ingredients for a dropped dish have sat out too long:** Putting them back in the fridge does not reset their history. Follow guidance for that food and place; stop using it when safety cannot be established.
- **The diner count changes:** Recheck portions and the whole-meal plan under that separate task first. This Skill handles an overfull menu at an already established scale.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is an ordinary home meal; there is no universal complete menu or grams-per-person rule. Equipment, experience, culture, and mobility change what can be finished. Use your real conditions, fewer dishes, or confirmed help. Severe allergy, medical diet, dangerous knife work, uncontrolled hot oil, and gas or electrical repair are outside scope. Follow the specific label and local authority for food safety; no universal storage period is invented here. You may lose one dish or change serving time. Both are more honest than deleting a safety step.
