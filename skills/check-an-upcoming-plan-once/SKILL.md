## 中文

你们已经确认一个普通、自愿的约定，离出发或准备的时间近了，想让重要信息重新可见，或有一项会影响是否成行的真实疑点时，用这份 Skill。目标是**给一次有用、克制的提醒，并准确记录仍有效/待确认/已变化的状态**。它不把原本清楚的计划重新变成必须获即时回复才算存在，也不把未回复当新的同意。

### 准备与输入

打开双方最近确认的日期、当地时间、地点/入口或线上方式、活动及负责人。检查你是否只是想提醒，还是有具体未知：场馆是否开放、天气是否改变活动、谁持票或对方是否已表达可能不能来。若原约本来就只有“也许”，先按接受或多人排期流程核对，不用提醒语气掩盖未成立的计划。选在别人仍能调整出门、准备或订位的时点联系；没有跨文化通用的“提前几小时”数字。

### 执行

1. **先判要不要发。** 原约信息清楚且双方一直能看到，未必需要额外消息；若活动临近、入口/时间容易忘，或你有一个真实变化疑点，可发一条短提醒。一次有目的的提醒，比“在吗”“看到没”连环消息有效。
2. **把已确认和待确认分开写。** 例如：“提醒一下，我们原定周六 15:00 在〔明确入口〕见，预计一小时。场馆今天提示周六可能调整开放；我正在核实，确认后再发。” 若只是提醒，不加无意义的“你确定还来吧？”；若确需确认，问具体问题和你需要答复的真实时点。
3. **核实变化来自哪里。** 对营业、交通、票或天气等客观条件，用这次活动的运营方/场馆当期说明核对；不要把群友的猜测写成已取消。你自己无法赴约时，直接走改期或取消，不把“确认一下”当作含糊退出。
4. **给回复和沉默正确状态。** 对方明确回复“照旧”就记录仍按原版；提出新条件则先明确谁接受、是否需改期。没回复时，只能说“提醒已发，尚未读回”；原约是否仍可执行要依据先前确认和真实条件作自己的决定，而不是宣称对方刚刚确认或自动取消。若你必须在某时点决定是否出门或付款，事先说明该时点和你将如何处理，再按实际回复作决定。
5. **到达一个可行动的结论。** 继续成行则记原版或已共同同意的新版本，并让需要行动的人看到；不能成行就及时用改期/取消流程通知。关键联系人未读且可能已经在路上，可用双方平常使用的更及时渠道做一次核对，不连续跨平台轰炸。

### 成功条件

一条必要且可读的提醒或针对性问题已送出，重要事实按官方渠道核实；状态明写“原约照旧 / 具体疑点待答 / 已协商变化 / 已取消”，各自的下一行动清楚。提醒发出不是活动已经发生，未读不是同意或取消。若本来无需提醒，保留清楚的原约并按它行动，也是一种完成。

### 常见报错与补救

- **原约清楚却每隔半小时问“还来吗”：** 停止重复催促；只在有真实新问题或临近不可逆准备时再联系。
- **活动地点的开放状态只是传言：** 查场馆或运营方的当期正式信息，并把未核实部分标未知。
- **对方没回，记录里写“已确认”：** 改为“提醒已发、尚未回复”，按既有约定和自己需要的决定点行动。
- **你其实已不能参加，却发“明天还正常吗”：** 及时明确提出改期或取消，不把责任推给对方先否定。
- **新时间只有一人回复：** 旧约是否取消、新时间是否成立分别核对，不把一人的“行”应用到所有人。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

适用于已成立的小型普通约见，不处理需要专业通知、工作排班、紧急事故或已开始活动的多人恢复。各人的回复习惯与时区不同，提醒时机应围绕实际出行/准备节点，而非死板的社交倒计时。文字不便可用双方可访问的语音或当面方式；听力/视觉障碍可用可读的消息或可信协助。额外提醒可能暴露变化，但并不能保证对方及时看到；你只能准确管理自己的通知与行动。

## English

Use this Skill when an ordinary voluntary plan is already confirmed and departure or preparation is near, so key details should be visible again or one real unknown may affect whether it proceeds. Finish with **one useful, restrained reminder and an honest still-on / pending / changed state**. This does not turn a clear plan into one that requires an immediate new reply to exist, and silence is not fresh consent.

### Preparation and inputs

Open the most recently agreed calendar date, local time, place and entrance or online mode, activity, and responsible people. Decide whether this is only a reminder or there is a concrete unknown: opening hours, weather that changes the activity, who holds tickets, or an earlier indication someone may not attend. If the old plan was only “maybe,” use acceptance or group-scheduling steps rather than disguising an unmade plan as a reminder. Contact people while they can still adjust travel, preparation, or a booking. There is no universal cultural number of hours in advance.

### Execution

1. **Decide whether to send at all.** A clear plan visible to both people may need no extra message. If departure is near, the entrance or time is easy to forget, or a real condition changed, send one short reminder. One purposeful note is more useful than a chain of “Are you there?”
2. **Separate confirmed facts from new questions.** For example: “A reminder: we agreed to meet Saturday at 15:00 at [named entrance] for about an hour. The venue says Saturday hours may change; I am checking and will update you.” If you only remind, avoid a needless “Are you definitely still coming?” If an answer is necessary, ask the exact question and state a genuine decision point for the reply.
3. **Check the source of any change.** For opening hours, transport, tickets, weather, or other objective conditions, consult this event's current venue or operator information. A group member's guess is not an official cancellation. If you yourself cannot attend, propose a change or cancel clearly rather than using a “quick check” as a vague exit.
4. **Give replies and silence their real status.** A clear “as planned” keeps the old version. A new condition needs agreement about who accepts it or whether to reschedule. No reply means only “reminder sent, no readback”; decide your own travel or payment under the earlier agreement and real conditions, without claiming a new yes or automatic cancellation. If you must decide by a certain point, state that real point and your intended handling in advance, then act on the actual response.
5. **Reach an actionable conclusion.** For a plan still on, keep the original or a newly agreed version and make sure people who act on it can see it. If it cannot proceed, promptly use the change or cancellation flow. If an essential contact has not read the message and may already be traveling, make one check through a faster channel both usually use, not repeated cross-platform bombardment.

### Success

One needed, readable reminder or targeted question was sent, and important objective facts were checked through an official channel. The state is explicit: “original plan stands / named question pending / mutually changed / canceled,” with the next action clear. Sending a reminder is not proof the event happened; unread is not agreement or cancellation. If no reminder was needed, retaining a clear existing plan and acting on it is also completion.

### Common errors and recovery

- **A clear plan draws “Still coming?” every half hour:** Stop repeated nudges; contact again only for a real new issue or an imminent irreversible preparation.
- **Venue closure is only a rumor:** Check the venue's or operator's current official information and mark anything unresolved as unknown.
- **There is no reply, but the record says “confirmed”:** Change it to “reminder sent; no reply” and decide from the existing agreement and your actual decision point.
- **You cannot attend but sent “Are we still on?”:** Propose a change or cancel directly rather than making the other person be the first to undo the plan.
- **Only one person answered about a new time:** Check whether the old plan ends and the new time is accepted by everyone required; one person's “yes” does not apply to all.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers an agreed small ordinary meeting, not professional notice, work rosters, emergencies, or multi-party recovery after an event begins. Reply habits and time zones vary; choose reminder timing around real travel or preparation, not a rigid social countdown. If text is inaccessible, use a mutually accessible voice or in-person method. For hearing or sight needs, use readable messages or trusted help. A reminder may surface a change, but it cannot guarantee timely reading; you can only manage your own notice and actions accurately.
