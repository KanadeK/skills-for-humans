## 中文

当你有普通纱线和合用钩针，想**从锁针起始、实际钩出几排短针**形成小长方片时，加载这份 Skill。结果是一片行数可指认、每行针数稳定、末端仍有一活圈的小样；断线藏尾另做。这里明确用**美式术语 single crochet（短针）**，不同地区“double crochet”翻译会变，不能混用图样缩写。E012 的两根织针操作不能代替钩针一活圈法。

### 准备与输入

按纱线标签选圆滑不勾线的钝钩针，备浅色可看清针目的线、稳定座位和能固定暂时活圈的小夹。选约 10–14 个起始锁针作为小练习，不是成衣尺寸。线团放稳，钩针远离眼睛和儿童跑动处。线太细、手疼或看不清针目时改粗一点的合用线/钩针或请可信现场协助。

### 执行

1. **打滑结并钩起始锁针。** 滑结套钩，不把滑结算一个锁针；工作线从后方绕过钩，拉过钩上圈得到第一个锁针。逐次重复、别拉到钩针不能穿过，按选定数量点数。
2. **从第二个锁针做首排短针。** 跳过钩针旁第一锁针，把钩针穿入第二锁针，绕线拉出，此时钩上两圈；再绕线穿过两圈，留下一个活圈。逐个处理剩下的锁针，首排短针数应比起始锁针少一。最后一格不要漏掉。
3. **每行转面并核数。** 钩一个转向锁针，翻面；从上一行第一枚短针顶上两股线下穿入，按短针方法做到末枚。单短针行的转向锁针不算本行一枚短针。每行末点数，发现少/多就暂停，不带着错误继续越织越斜。
4. **做到几行后留安全暂停点。** 重复几行直到有小长方形，记录起始锁针数、每行短针数、完成行数；用合用夹固定钩上唯一活圈，防线团拉走。样片暂不作承重物，也不称已完整收尾。

### 完成、常见问题与恢复

小样有起始锁针与数排可辨短针、行末针数稳定、一只活圈已固定；断线和藏线是另一结果。只钩一条长锁针链但没形成第二维度，不算此长方片。

- **首排少一针以上：** 查是否漏最后一个起始锁针，拆回该排重做。
- **每行逐渐变窄：** 查翻面后第一枚和行末最后一枚，别把转向锁针当普通短针。
- **边缘渐宽：** 查是否在边缘同一孔误钩两次，退回出错行再数。
- **锁针太紧穿不进去：** 放松重钩短链，不用尖钩硬戳手或线。
- **手眼受限：** 粗浅色线、针目标记、放大光和可信协助计数，疼痛时停。

### 假设与边界

简单小样的针数是练习选择，不是推荐衣物尺寸。不同钩法（美式/英式名称）与线粗会改变说明；产品标签和现有图样的术语系统须先统一。

## English

Load this Skill when ordinary yarn and a suitable crochet hook are ready and you want to **start with a chain and actually make several counted rows of single crochet**. Finish with a small flat rectangle whose rows and stitch counts can be named, leaving one live hook loop; fastening off comes later. This uses **US single-crochet terminology**. UK names such as “double crochet” can mean a different term, so do not mix pattern systems. E012's two-needle knitting is a different mechanism.

### Preparation and inputs

Use a smooth blunt hook suggested for the labelled yarn, light yarn that shows stitches, a steady seat, and a clip to protect a paused live loop. Pick about 10–14 foundation chains for a practice piece, not garment sizing. Keep the ball steady and hook away from eyes and running children. Use compatible thicker yarn/hook or trusted help if thin stitches or hand pain limit access.

### Execution

1. **Make a slip knot and foundation chains.** Put a slip knot on the hook without counting it as a chain. Yarn over from behind and pull through the hook loop for chain one. Repeat without tightening so much the hook cannot enter, counting the chosen chains.
2. **Work the first row from chain two.** Skip the chain closest to the hook. Insert hook in the second chain, yarn over and pull up a loop (two on hook), yarn over and pull through both (one remains). Repeat through every remaining foundation chain; first-row single crochets should be one fewer than foundation chains. Do not miss the last chain.
3. **Turn each row and count.** Chain one to turn, rotate the work, and insert into the top two loops of the first previous-row single crochet. Work across to the final stitch. The turning chain for these single-crochet rows does not count as one ordinary stitch. Count at each row end and stop a narrowing or widening edge before adding more rows.
4. **Pause after several rows.** Repeat until a small rectangle exists. Record chains, stitches per row, and completed rows, and protect the only live hook loop with a suitable clip against a pulling yarn ball. It is not load-bearing or fully finished yet.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

A sample has a foundation and several visible single-crochet rows, stable row counts, and its one live loop secured. Cutting yarn and weaving ends are a separate result. A very long chain without rows is not this rectangle.

- **First row is short by more than one:** Check the last foundation chain and undo that row to repair.
- **Each row narrows:** Check first and final stitches after turning, and do not count the turning chain as an ordinary single crochet.
- **Edge widens:** Look for two stitches in one edge point, then undo to the wrong row and recount.
- **Chain is too tight to enter:** Redo a short loose chain instead of forcing a sharp point into hand or yarn.
- **Sight or grip is limited:** Light bulky yarn, stitch markers, magnified light, and trusted counting help can assist; stop for pain.

### Assumptions and limits

Small sample counts are practice choices, not clothing dimensions. US/UK crochet terminology and yarn thickness affect directions. Establish one terminology system with any real pattern first.
