## 中文

当普通谈话中你发现自己刚打断、盖过或抢走了别人的话，加载这份 Skill。目标是**停止占用回合、简短承认、把继续或不继续的选择交回对方**，再根据实际听到的内容回应。它不要求对方原谅、立即发言或证明你本来“只是太兴奋”；若是持续骚扰、强迫或危机，已超出这份普通失误修复。

### 准备与输入

看清发生了什么：对方话是否被你截断、是否还有其他人同时开口、你现在能否用口头、文字或其他双方可用的方式让出回合。不必先证明自己的动机是好的。若对方在说明重要事项，修复的第一步是让信息有机会说完，而不是抢先写结论。

### 执行

1. **立刻停下当前句子。** 不把你本来要说的话“快速讲完”后才归还话语权。停顿本身就是一个看得见的动作。
2. **用一句话承认并交回。** “抱歉，我打断了你。你刚才说到哪里？你想继续吗？”若能记得内容，可具体说“你刚在讲周五的安排，先请你说完。”不要解释自己为什么兴奋、为什么也有经验。
3. **等对方决定。** 对方继续时听完，不在每个停顿里插一段补充；用其真正说完的意思回应。对方说“不用了”或暂时不想讲，也要接受，不要求他们配合你的修复表演。
4. **多人同时开口时先厘清。** “我刚盖住了谁？你们谁先说比较方便？”按现场同意的顺序让出空间，别替所有人指定胜者或在问完后马上自己接回。
5. **事后才发现时做一次短补救。** 可以私下或在适当渠道说“刚才我抢了话；如果你还想说完，我愿意听”。对方不回复，就停止追问，并在下次对话中给足发言空间。

### 完成条件

你已停止自己的发言、承认这次打断，并给对方一个真实可用的继续机会；之后没有立刻再次抢回。对方继续、拒绝或暂时沉默都可作为其选择。完成不要求对方夸你会道歉或恢复原本的热情。

### 常见报错与补救

- **“让我把这点讲完”：** 先停，记下自己的要点，等对方完成再决定是否还需要说。
- **道歉变成自我解释：** 缩回“抱歉，先请你说”，不要让对方反过来照顾你的尴尬。
- **你用自己的话替对方总结但猜错：** 问“我理解得对吗？”并让对方修正，而非坚持你更懂其意思。
- **对方不想继续：** 接受，不在散场后连发消息催一个“没事”；下次靠实际让出回合改善。
- **在线通话延迟造成同时说话：** 简短说明重叠，约定谁先讲，文字或轮流举手也可以；不把网络延迟全归成某个人的品格问题。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

本篇处理普通、自愿的交流。谈话速度、停顿、手势与群聊规则因人而异；听觉、语言处理或网络延迟可能改变轮流说话的节奏，可用文字、明确点名或可访问的提示方式交还回合。是否继续说由被打断的人决定，你只能保证自己的停止和倾听。

现实副作用是你的补充晚一点出现。听到完整问题后，它可能还会变得更短。

## English

Load this Skill when you notice that you just interrupted, talked over, or took the floor from someone in an ordinary conversation. **Stop taking the turn, acknowledge it briefly, and return the choice to continue or not.** Respond to what they actually say next. This does not require forgiveness, immediate speech, or proof that you “were only excited.” Persistent harassment, coercion, or crisis is outside this ordinary repair.

### Preparation and inputs

Notice whether you cut off a thought, whether several people began together, and which spoken, written, or other channel both people can use to return the turn. You do not first need to establish that your intentions were good. If someone was giving important information, the first repair is room to finish it, not your early conclusion.

### Execution

1. **Stop the current sentence now.** Do not quickly finish the point you planned and only then return the floor. The pause itself is an observable action.
2. **Acknowledge and return it in one line.** “Sorry, I interrupted. Where were you? Do you want to continue?” If you recall the point, be specific: “You were explaining Friday's plan; please finish.” Do not explain your excitement or your similar experience first.
3. **Let the other person decide.** If they continue, listen through the thought instead of filling every pause. Respond to the meaning they actually complete. If they say “never mind” or do not want to speak now, accept that; they do not owe you participation in a repair performance.
4. **Clarify when several voices overlap.** “Whom did I cut off? Who would like to go first?” Use an order the people present accept. Do not appoint a winner for everyone and immediately take your own turn back.
5. **Make one brief delayed repair when needed.** Later, privately or on an appropriate channel: “I talked over you earlier. If you still want to finish, I can listen.” If there is no reply, stop chasing a “we're fine” message and leave more room in the next conversation.

### Success

You stopped speaking, acknowledged this particular interruption, and gave the other person a usable chance to continue without immediately taking it back. Continuing, declining, and pausing are their choices. Completion does not require praise for your apology or the original enthusiasm to return.

### Common errors and recovery

- **“Let me just finish this point”:** Stop first. Note your thought and decide after the other person finishes whether it is still needed.
- **The apology becomes an explanation of yourself:** Return to “Sorry, you first,” without making them manage your embarrassment.
- **You summarize their point incorrectly:** Ask “Did I get that right?” and let them correct it rather than insisting you know what they meant.
- **They do not want to continue:** Accept it. Do not send repeated messages later demanding “it's okay”; make room in the next conversation instead.
- **Online delay makes people start together:** Name the overlap, agree who goes first, and use text or an accessible turn signal if helpful. A lag is not necessarily anyone's character flaw.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This handles voluntary ordinary conversation. Pace, pauses, gestures, and group-chat rules vary. Hearing, language processing, or network delay can change turn-taking; use text, explicit naming, or accessible cues to return the floor. Whether to speak again belongs to the interrupted person. You can only control your own stopping and listening.

Your addition arrives a little later and may be shorter after you hear the full point.
