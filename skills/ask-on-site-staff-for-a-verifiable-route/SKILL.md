## 中文

你在普通、可退出的公共场所内，地图或标牌不足以让你确定下一步，而现场有管理方服务台或工作人员时，用这份 Skill提出**当前位置—目的地—下一处核对点**三个明确输入，得到一段可检验的公开路线。目标是安全走到下一处官方标识并确认方向仍对，或得到负责方的进一步帮助；不是让随机路人带你走进受限区域。找建筑入口、按楼层/分区自助读图、约会合点是相邻但不同任务。

### 准备与输入

先在不挡门、通道或交通的位置停下，记住自己身旁的入口、楼层、站牌或醒目标识。把目的地说成正式名称、房间/部门号或预约信中的**位置**部分，不必展示完整身份、付款或健康资料。优先找有明确标识的问讯处、场馆工作人员或官方服务电话；仅穿着制服的陌生人也需要能与场所关联。若你需要电梯、避免楼梯、短路程或文字说明，提前说成具体路径条件，不假设对方知道你身体情况。

### 执行

1. **用一句话给足定位输入。** “我现在在〔入口/楼层/标志〕，要去〔完整名称/编号〕。请问先走到哪一个公开标志？” 避免只说“那个办公室在哪里”，让对方猜同名的楼、翼楼或窗口。
2. **问一段可验证路线和限制。** 请对方指出第一处转弯/楼层、接下来的标志或信息台，以及是否有关闭入口、需要正式许可或可及通道。目的地很远时先记前两个节点，不必背下一整栋楼的每次左转。
3. **读回并确认。** “我先到〔可见标志〕，再沿〔楼层/区号〕走；如果门关着/标志不同，回这里问，对吗？” 记下楼层、分区、门牌或官方地图位置。对方若只说“跟那群人走”，请补一个可核对的标识或找正式地图。
4. **走第一段并对照现场。** 按公开、允许且自己能使用的通道到第一个节点，核对标牌名称/编号与回答一致。若标志冲突、路被封、需刷他人证件或必须跨危险区域，停止并回最近可信的信息点或问另一名管理方人员，不用热心解释取代实际许可。
5. **确认下一步属于谁。** 到目的地门口时核名称与开放标识；取号、排队、预约、检查或参观规则再按该服务执行。工作人员无法确认时，问“哪一个服务台/部门能确认”，保留当前安全位置与目的地名称，不无限向路人重复猜问。

### 成功条件

你获得了从实际位置到第一处官方标识、再到目标楼层/分区的可复述路线，并在现场至少核对了第一段；或你明确知道哪个正式服务点继续处理不可达/权限不明的路径。一次笼统的“往那边”而无核对点还不是可执行结果。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只有“左边”但周围有几条走廊：** 追问正式房间号、楼层、区名或第一个可见标志。
- **对方说走员工通道：** 问是否真的对访客开放及可用的公开替代；未确认不尾随进入。
- **地图和工作人员说法冲突：** 核对地图日期/当前位置与现场最新公告，向管理方再确认，不指责某一方或直接选近路。
- **听不清、记不住或语言不同：** 请对方慢说、写下/指给你看第一个标识，使用官方文字或可及沟通；只需先完成下一段。
- **问到的工作人员不负责该区域：** 请其指向准确的信息台/负责部门，而不是要求他猜一个保证能到的答案。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这是公共建筑或普通服务点的现场问路，不处理私人住宅、紧急撤离、医疗建议、安检绕过或合法进入资格。英国图书馆和公共医院官网只证明此类场所有正式地图与可询问人员；它们不是你当前地点的路线。无法说话时可把目的地名称/位置部分写给工作人员或指图；行动不便时问可用电梯/短路径，但须由场所确认当天实际开放。多问一句可能花时间，比凭一声“那边”走错楼层更少。

## English

Use this Skill when signs or a map in an ordinary, escapable public place do not settle your next move and a venue information desk or staff member is available. Give three concrete inputs—**where you are, the exact destination, and a next checkable point**—and obtain one public route segment you can test. Finish safely at the next official sign with direction confirmed or with a responsible staff next step, not by following a random person into restricted space. Finding the street entrance, reading floor/zone maps independently, and setting a meeting point are adjacent tasks.

### Preparation and inputs

Pause where you do not block a door, corridor, or traffic. Note a nearby entrance, floor, stop, or prominent sign. State the destination's formal name, room/department number, or only the **location** portion of an appointment; no full identity, payment, or health record is needed for wayfinding. Prefer a signed information point, identifiable venue staff, or official service phone. A uniform alone is not enough if the person cannot be connected to the venue. State a real path need such as a working lift, no stairs, less walking, or written directions instead of expecting a stranger to infer it from your body.

### Execution

1. **Give one answerable location question.** “I'm at [entrance/floor/sign] and need [full name/ID]. What public sign should I reach first?” A vague “where's that office?” makes the other person guess which building, wing, or desk you mean.
2. **Ask for one testable segment and constraint.** Request the first turn or floor, the next sign or information point, and any closed entrance, permission requirement, or accessible route. For a long path, record the first two checkpoints instead of memorizing every left turn in the building.
3. **Read back.** “First I go to [visible sign], then follow [floor/zone]; if that door is shut or the signs differ, I come back or ask here—correct?” Keep the floor, zone, room, or official map point. If the answer is only “follow that crowd,” request a checkable sign or manager map.
4. **Walk and verify the first segment.** Use public permitted paths you can actually navigate to reach the first node, then compare its sign/number with the answer. For a mismatch, closure, someone else's access-card door, or unsafe route, stop and return to the last trusted point or ask another venue employee. Helpful-sounding directions do not grant access.
5. **Identify the owner of the next step.** At the destination, compare its name and public access sign. Ticket, queue, appointment, screening, or visit rules belong to that service after arrival. If staff cannot confirm the route, ask which desk or department can, keeping your safe current location and destination name rather than guessing with more passersby.

### Success

You can repeat a route from your real location to an official first sign and target floor/zone, and you have verified at least its first segment on site. Knowing the responsible desk for an inaccessible or permission-unclear path also counts. A bare “over there” with no checkpoint is not yet executable.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Only “left” with several corridors:** Ask for a room ID, floor, zone, or first visible sign.
- **A staff-only door is suggested:** Check whether it is truly public to visitors and ask for the open alternative; do not tailgate.
- **Map and staff disagree:** Verify map date/current location and newer on-site notice with management rather than choosing the shorter way by instinct.
- **Hard to hear, remember, or speak the language:** Ask for slower or written directions or a pointed first sign; use formal accessible communication and complete just the next segment.
- **Staff member does not own that area:** Ask for the correct information desk or responsible department, not a confident guess.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is on-site wayfinding in a public building or ordinary service point, not private-property entry, emergency evacuation, medical advice, screening bypass, or legal entry permission. British Library and public-hospital pages show that venues can have official maps and staff; they are not your present route. If speech is difficult, show a written destination name or point to a map. Ask about usable lifts or shorter paths for movement needs, but the venue must confirm today's open route. One extra question takes time but beats a vague “that way” to the wrong floor.
