## 中文

当你的一件**普通、非防护用途**衣物拉链突然卡住，滑块附近看得到里布、衣摆或线头被夹入时，用这份 Skill 慢慢解除夹入并判断是否还能正常开合。结果是布料释放、拉链短程试用顺畅，或在可能撕布/损坏零件时及时停止。强拉不是诊断工具；在布已卡住时加油或撬开滑块，也不能让那片布自动走出来。

### 准备与输入

先别再拉拉片。能安全脱下衣物就脱下，把拉链放在明亮、稳固的位置；不便脱下时找可信的人帮助看清，但不要用力拉扯仍贴身的衣料。看滑块两侧是否夹着布或线，拉链齿、下止、拉片是否完整，有无明显弯曲、缺齿或撕开的布。确认这条拉链不承担防护、防水密封、救生或承重功能；这些用途需要制造商或专业人员。

若衣物夹住身体导致无法安全自行处理，先请现场可信的人协助解除穿着束缚；不要为了完整保留拉链牺牲身体舒适或安全。部件已坏、夹入位置看不清时停止家修。

### 执行

1. **让拉链不再承受额外拉力。** 稳住滑块两侧的衣料，放松被拉紧的衣襟；若有纽扣或钩扣可先按原衣结构扣好以减轻拉链张力。确认卡住的是哪片布或哪根松线，而非断齿在滑块中。
2. **试极小幅退回。** 沿滑块刚刚来的方向缓慢移动一点，同时用手把夹入布料轻轻朝进入前的方向退出；必要时暂停、换握点，一点点让布离开滑块。手指避开齿口，别用拉片拖整件衣服。阻力增大时立刻停，不以更大力量“突破”。
3. **布料出来后先检查。** 看被夹处有无新洞、被抽出的结构线、拉链齿是否仍在原位、滑块是否明显变形。只有衣物和零件都可用时，先在无布贴近的短距离内试向两边滑动，再按正常穿着方向轻合一次。把里布和边缘让开齿路，别在一上来就拉到头。
4. **分清其他故障，别继续同一动作。** 若没有可见夹布，只有灰尘或线屑，按衣物和拉链制造商允许的方式用柔软刷子清除表面杂物，再轻试；零件弯、缺齿、止点脱落或滑过后又分开时停止。拉链零件更换与特殊材料维修交制造商或专业修衣者，不靠钳子挤压、螺丝刀撬或临时润滑掩盖。
5. **决定衣物去向。** 一次解除且短程试用正常，可在这次普通穿用中留意是否重复夹布；反复卡住、布料破损或拉链闭合不可靠时暂停穿用/换衣物，并记录损伤部位交维修。夹布造成的小破口另按 H062 的材料范围判断；拉链顺滑不等于布没受伤。

### 成功条件

被夹布料或线头已经完整退出，滑块与齿没有新损伤，拉链在无额外拉力下能短程开合，衣物没有不明破口或遗留尖锐工具。若无法在轻力下退出、看不清机理或部件受损，停止强拉并转可靠维修同样是完成判断。

### 常见报错与补救

- **越卡越用力拉：** 立即停，回到可见夹入点；试缓慢退回和释放布料，不把拉片当杠杆。
- **看见线头就剪：** 先确认那不是固定里布或接缝的结构线；分不清就停，避免把一根卡线变成开线。
- **夹布未脱就加蜡、油或肥皂：** 先移除真正被夹的布；额外物质可能弄脏衣物，也不证明滑块完好。
- **用螺丝刀或钳子撑开滑块：** 停止家庭强拆；滑块形状改变会让齿无法正常咬合。
- **布出来了但拉链仍会裂开：** 不继续试到拉片掉落；检查是否缺齿/滑块损坏，转专业维修或换衣。
- **同一里布反复被夹：** 暂停使用，记录夹入位置和衣料状态；可能需要更改缝制或配件，不能只靠每次更小心拉。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里只处理普通衣物、滑块与齿原本完整、能看见夹入布或松线的卡阻。不同拉链材质和衣物结构有各自的清理/润滑规则，因此本文不建议统一化学品或专用喷剂。视觉或握力不便时可用放大光线、稳定桌面与可信协助；如果不能安全控制力量就停止。成功释放可能仍留下小孔或松线，需要另行检查，不把“能动了”当整件衣物无损。

## English

Use this Skill when the zipper on **ordinary, non-protective clothing** suddenly jams and you can see lining, an edge, or loose thread caught by the slider. Ease the caught material out and decide whether normal fastening remains possible. The result is released fabric and a smooth short trial, or a timely stop before cloth or parts tear. Force is not a diagnostic tool. Oil or prying the slider open will not make trapped fabric leave by itself.

### Preparation and inputs

Stop pulling the tab. Take the garment off if that is safe, and place the zipper where you can see and steady it. If it cannot be removed conveniently, ask a trusted person to help inspect it without pulling hard on fabric still against the body. Look for fabric or thread at either side of the slider, intact teeth, stop and pull tab, and any bent parts or torn cloth. Confirm this is not a protective, waterproof-seal, lifesaving, or load-bearing zipper; those uses require the maker or specialist.

If the garment traps your body and you cannot handle it safely alone, ask someone present for help freeing you first. Do not preserve the zipper at the expense of comfort or safety. Stop home repair when parts are broken or the jam cannot be seen clearly.

### Execution

1. **Remove extra tension.** Steady fabric on both sides of the slider and relax a stretched opening. If the original garment has a button or hook, fastening it first may take tension off the zipper. Identify the exact trapped cloth or loose thread rather than treating a broken tooth as the same problem.
2. **Try a very small reverse move.** Move the slider slowly back toward where it just came from while easing the trapped fabric out toward its entry side. Pause and change grip as needed; release a little at a time. Keep fingers clear of teeth and do not drag the whole garment by the pull tab. Stop immediately when resistance grows instead of trying a stronger breakthrough.
3. **Inspect after release.** Check the caught spot for a new hole or extracted construction thread. Check that teeth remain aligned and the slider is not visibly bent. Only if fabric and parts appear usable, trial a short move in both directions with fabric away from the path, then close gently in the normal wearing direction. Keep lining and edges away; do not start with a full-length pull.
4. **Separate other faults from this one.** If no cloth is trapped but surface dust or loose debris is present, use only a soft brush or cleaning method permitted by the garment and zipper maker, then trial gently. Stop for bent parts, missing teeth, a detached stop, or a zipper that separates after the slider passes. Part replacement and special-material work belong to a maker or garment repairer, not squeezing with pliers, prying with a screwdriver, or improvising a lubricant.
5. **Decide what happens to the garment.** After one release and a good short trial, watch for another snag in ordinary use. If it repeats, cloth is torn, or the closure cannot be trusted, stop wearing or choose another garment and record the spot for repair. A small new cloth hole belongs to H062's material decision. Moving again does not mean the cloth escaped damage.

### Success

Trapped fabric or thread is out, slider and teeth have no new damage, the zipper travels a short distance both ways without extra force, and the garment has no unexplained hole or left-behind sharp tool. If it will not release gently, the cause is unclear, or a part is damaged, stopping force and choosing reliable repair is a complete decision.

### Common errors and recovery

- **More force follows more resistance:** Stop. Return to the visible catch and try slow reversal while freeing cloth; the tab is not a lever.
- **A visible thread is cut immediately:** Check whether it holds lining or a seam first. If you cannot tell, stop rather than turning one snag into an open seam.
- **Wax, oil, or soap goes in before cloth comes out:** Remove the trapped material first. Added substance can stain fabric and does not establish that the slider is sound.
- **A screwdriver or pliers spreads the slider:** Stop household force. Changing slider shape may stop its teeth from meshing.
- **The cloth is free but the zipper still splits:** Do not keep cycling until the pull breaks. Check for tooth or slider damage and seek repair or another garment.
- **The same lining keeps getting caught:** Pause use and record where and how it snags. It may require a construction or part change rather than endless careful pulls.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers visible trapped fabric or loose thread in an ordinary clothing zipper whose slider and teeth were otherwise intact. Materials and makers have different cleaning and lubrication rules, so this Skill recommends no universal chemical or spray. Use better light, a stable table, or trusted help if vision or grip makes fine work difficult; stop if force cannot be controlled safely. A successful release may still leave a small hole or loose stitch, so inspect the garment beyond the slider.
