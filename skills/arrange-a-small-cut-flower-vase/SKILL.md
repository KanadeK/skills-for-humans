## 中文

当你已有几枝**已整理、可普通瓶插的切花**，想做一个能在自家安全摆放和观赏的小型展示时，加载这份 Skill。结果是一瓶稳固、花茎在水中、从预定方向可看到花的作品。清洗和补水花枝是前一步；手拿可携带的花束是另一个结果。这里不复制特定文化花道、专业婚礼设计或受保护作品。

### 准备与输入

选一只已洗净、装水后不容易翻的瓶，确认瓶口容得下花枝却不会让它们完全散倒。先决定从哪些方向观看以及放在哪个安全位置：远离强日晒、热源、通行边缘、儿童或宠物可碰倒处。花种对宠物或人是否有接触风险不清时，不在可接触处展示。手部不便可用宽底瓶、少量粗茎和可信协助。

### 执行

1. **放好水与第一枝。** 让瓶内水覆盖花枝切口，花头不泡水。先放一枝作为高度和朝向参照；若瓶重心不稳，先换瓶，而非靠塞更多花抵住。
2. **逐枝搭出可看的结构。** 先放较能支撑轮廓的枝，再把其余枝错开加入；转动瓶查看预定视角，保留花头呼吸的空隙。每放一枝看切端是否仍入水、叶片是否意外沉在水下；修整这些实际问题，不按某个固定花数填满。
3. **检视与收敛。** 从观看距离看高低、朝向和是否明显一侧倾倒。取出、换位或少放一枝也可改善；遇到会垂落到热源、餐食或行动通道的枝，调整位置或收短。
4. **定位置并记下照料点。** 在安全稳定的台面轻碰确认花瓶不晃，水无外溢、切端都在水中。记得之后检查水位和花况；作品完成不等于可以永远不管。

### 完成、常见问题与恢复

花瓶稳、花头可见、花枝有水、没有浸水腐叶或挡路枝，且站在预定位置可欣赏。漂亮与否由你决定；稳定与供水可当场核对。

- **花瓶向一侧倾或太轻：** 换低而宽、装水后稳定的容器，减枝；不要用易碎瓶在边缘试重心。
- **花头全挤在瓶口：** 抽出几枝或改变高度与方向，留出视线，不硬压折茎。
- **下部叶片落进水里：** 取出该枝，去掉确实浸水的叶，再放回；不要只遮住水线。
- **一枝开始枯败：** 先取出隔离，已有作品按维护步骤处理；不靠把枯花转背面假装没事。
- **难以从多角度查看：** 用可安全转动的轻瓶、镜子或可信描述，但不让人搬动装满水的沉重不稳瓶。

### 假设与边界

普通私人小型瓶插没有唯一正确配色或比例。花种寿命、花瓶材质、环境温度会改变维护需求；本篇只承诺当下可观察的稳定和入水，不承诺花期或专业审美认可。

## English

Load this Skill when you already have a few **conditioned ordinary cut flowers** and want a small display that is safe to place and view at home. Finish with a stable vase, stems in water, and visible blooms from the chosen viewing direction. Cleaning and hydrating stems is the previous task; a hand-held portable bouquet is another outcome. Do not copy a specific cultural floral form, professional wedding design, or protected artwork.

### Preparation and inputs

Choose a washed vase that stays upright when filled and whose opening supports the stems without packing them tightly. Decide where it will be viewed and placed: away from harsh sun, heat, path edges, and places children or pets may knock it over. If species contact risk for people or pets is unclear, do not display it within reach. A broad-based vessel, fewer sturdy stems, and trusted help can support limited grip.

### Execution

1. **Add water and a first stem.** Ensure the vase water covers the cut end without immersing the bloom. Put in one stem to set height and direction. If the vessel is unstable, change it now rather than wedging more flowers into it.
2. **Build a viewable shape one stem at a time.** Put in stems that establish a loose outline, then offset the others. Turn the vase to check the intended view and leave space around blooms. With each stem check that its cut end remains in water and leaves have not dropped below the waterline. Solve these actual issues rather than filling to a fixed count.
3. **Inspect and simplify.** From viewing distance, check heights, directions, and obvious one-sided leaning. Removing, moving, or shortening a stem can help. Adjust a stem that would hang over heat, food, or a walking route.
4. **Place and schedule a check.** On a safe stable surface, touch the vase lightly to check for wobble; water should not spill and every cut end should remain submerged. Remember to check water and flower condition later. Completion is not indefinite care.

### Success, problems, and recovery

The vase stands, blooms can be seen, stems have water, no decaying leaves are immersed, and no stem blocks normal use of the space. You decide beauty; stability and hydration can be checked now.

- **Vase leans or feels light:** Use a lower, wider stable vessel and fewer stems. Do not test a fragile vase at a table edge.
- **Blooms crowd the opening:** Remove some or vary height and direction, rather than folding stems by force.
- **Lower leaves sit in water:** Lift that stem, remove only immersed leaves, and replace it; hiding the waterline does not help.
- **A bloom begins to fail:** Remove it and use the maintenance task for the remaining display; turning a dead bloom backward is not recovery.
- **Viewing from several angles is hard:** Use a safe lightweight vessel, mirror, or trusted description without asking someone to move a heavy unstable filled vase.

### Assumptions and limits

A private small vase has no universal palette or proportion. Species, vessel, and temperature change future care. This Skill claims only present stability and water contact, not a promised lifespan or professional aesthetic approval.
