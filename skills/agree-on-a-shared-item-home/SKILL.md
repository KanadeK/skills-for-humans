## 中文

当几个人都要使用一件普通家用物品，例如卷尺、胶带座或桌游，而每次都要猜谁能拿、用后放哪、少了部件找谁说时，加载这份 Skill。你要得到**一条经常使用的人都同意并能实际执行的共用规则**。它不处理私人借物的归还债务，也不管理食品、药物、钥匙、凭据、重要文书或危险工具。

### 准备与输入

把这一件物品和不可缺的部件列出来，确认它确实被允许共用，而不是某个人暂时放在公共空间。请经常使用的人各自说出何时需要它、可否移动、哪个位置可到达、哪些部件必须跟它放在一起。不能到场的人先不替他们同意；可以先做临时规则并留待确认。

### 执行

1. **先定使用权限。** 写清谁可以直接取用，谁需要先问，是否有不能带离的边界。相关所有者或共同使用者不同意时，不靠“放在客厅就是公用”替他们签字。
2. **只选一个正常归位点。** 选经常使用者都能找回且允许存放的位置，留出物品和关键部件的空间。位置不能挡通道、压住别人的东西或只对最高的人可达。大字、触感或音频提示可以辅助，但不必为此购买标签机。
3. **说清一次使用的结束。** 约定用完后由使用者把物品和部件放回正常位置；确需占用到稍后时，在约定的共享渠道说明谁持有、在哪里、预计何时可取。它只是共用状态，不把私人借物混进来。
4. **给缺件一个报错入口。** 发现关键部件少了、损坏或物品不在原位时，写下“哪件、缺什么、最后确认在哪、何时发现”，通知共同使用者。不先把责任安到最后一个被看见的人身上；缺件影响普通使用时标为暂不可用，等查明后再恢复。
5. **试一次取用与归位。** 请至少两位经常使用者分别指出位置、取到完整物品并放回。有人看不清、够不到或找不到时，调整位置/提示并重试。最后把规则写成“谁可用 → 正常位置 → 用后动作 → 缺件通知方式”。

### 成功与停止

经常使用者能说出谁可取、取完如何恢复、关键部件在哪里，以及缺失时发给谁；至少两人可实际找到并归位。没有所有者同意、没有共同可达的位置，或权限仍有争议时，结果是“尚未约定”，不把临时摆放称作长期规则。

### 常见报错与补救

- **有人把物品放进第二个“常用位置”：** 回到唯一正常位置；确需临时占用就留下状态，不让两个位置同时自称权威。
- **标签太小或只靠颜色：** 用更大的字、触感或可听提示，并让实际使用者测试；颜色不自动能被所有人辨认。
- **部件少了但没人愿意报告：** 先把所缺和最后确认位置写清，通知共同使用者；重要部件未找到前标为不可用，而不是悄悄把盒子放回。
- **发现它其实是某人的私人用品：** 停止共用约定，先问所有者；私人借还另用准确的借用记录。
- **约定被打断：** 保留已同意和未同意的条款，未确认的权限不生效；下次从争议处继续，不让沉默算同意。

### 假设、替代与副作用

本 Skill 假设是低风险普通物品，相关的人可以协商，且归位不涉及住房改造或搬重物。家庭、合租与工作室对权限和隐私的习惯不同；被收纳的不是人的意愿。看不清、听不清、难握持或无法够到时，以当事人能真正使用的位置和提示为准；可请可信的人协助测试，不把障碍归咎于人。现实副作用是一件物品获得稳定地址，“大概在那边”不再够用。

## English

Load this Skill when several people use one ordinary household item, such as a tape measure, tape dispenser or board game, but must keep guessing who may take it, where to return it, or how to report a missing piece. You will test **one rule the regular users actually agree to and can carry out**. It does not settle the return obligation for a privately borrowed item or manage food, medicine, keys, credentials, important papers or hazardous tools.

### Preparation and inputs

Name this item and its essential pieces. Confirm that it is genuinely available for shared use, rather than a person's property temporarily left in a common room. Ask regular users when they need it, whether it may move, which places they can reach, and which parts must stay with it. Do not consent on behalf of someone absent; a temporary rule can wait for their answer.

### Execution

1. **Agree who may use it.** State who may take it directly, who should ask first, and whether it must stay in a particular area. A living-room location does not sign consent for an owner or co-user who disagrees.
2. **Choose one normal return place.** Pick a permitted place every regular user can find and reach, with room for the item and essential parts. Do not block a route, bury someone else's things, or choose a shelf only the tallest person can reach. Large print, touch or audio cues can help; a label printer is optional.
3. **Define the end of one use.** The user returns the item and its parts to the normal place. If they need it longer, leave a note in an agreed shared channel saying who has it, where it is, and when it may be available. This is shared status, not a private loan contract.
4. **Give missing parts a reporting route.** If an essential piece is absent, damaged, or the item is elsewhere, note the item, gap, last confirmed place and discovery time, then tell the regular users. Do not blame the last person someone remembers seeing. Mark the item temporarily unavailable when the gap prevents ordinary use; restore its status only when resolved.
5. **Test retrieval and return.** Have at least two regular users each identify the place, retrieve the complete item and put it back. If someone cannot see, reach or find it, change the place or cue and repeat. Write the rule as “who may use → normal place → after-use action → missing-part notice.”

### Success and stop

Regular users can name who may take the item, how to restore it, where essential parts belong, and whom to tell about a gap; at least two can actually find and return it. Without owner consent, a commonly reachable place or settled permission, the result is “not yet agreed,” not a temporary placement promoted to permanent policy.

### Common errors and recovery

- **A second “usual place” appears:** Return to the single normal place. Record a temporary hold rather than letting both places claim authority.
- **The label is tiny or only a colour:** Use larger text, touch or audio cues and test them with actual users. Colour is not readable by everyone.
- **A part is missing and nobody wants to report it:** Write the exact gap and last confirmed location, then tell the group. Mark the item unavailable while an essential piece is missing instead of silently returning its box.
- **It turns out to be someone's private item:** Stop the shared-use rule and ask the owner first. Private borrowing needs its own accurate loan record.
- **The agreement is interrupted:** Keep agreed and disputed terms separate. Unconfirmed permission does not take effect; resume at the unresolved part rather than treating silence as consent.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This assumes a low-risk ordinary item, people able to negotiate, and a return place needing no construction or heavy lifting. Households, shared flats and workshops differ in permission and privacy habits; storage is not a substitute for consent. Use the place and cue a person can genuinely access when sight, hearing, grip or reach is difficult. A trusted person can assist with the test without blaming the user. A side effect is that one item gets a stable address and “somewhere over there” no longer qualifies.
