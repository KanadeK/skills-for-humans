## 中文

当普通水基煮制已达到**该食物的可靠结束条件**，你需要把食物和热水分开，却无法或不愿端起整锅滚水倒进水槽时，加载这份 Skill。目标是**锅保持稳定、食物进入近处耐热容器、热水留锅中等待安全处理**。它不替你确认食物安全熟度；有要求时先用[适用熟度核对](../check-doneness-and-store-leftovers/SKILL.md)。米面吸水/沥水的烹饪机理由专门流程决定，这里只处理取出动作。

### 准备与输入

确认当前食物真的到了可靠配方或食品安全要求的取出时刻；某些配方还要留煮水，先别把它当废水。找一个紧挨灶边的稳固耐热碗或盘，选与你的食物大小、重量和握力相配的漏勺、夹具或**入锅前已放好的**耐热篮具。确认食物会从锅到容器的短路径，没有人或物在热水滴落处。工具不稳、锅晃动或你需要越过蒸汽伸手时先停热等待稳定并求助。

### 执行步骤

1. **先结束或暂停热输入。** 依配方在安全可达时关火，等猛烈翻滚和蒸汽减弱再取。盖子若在锅上，握稳后向远离脸与身体的方向掀开；不把脸探入锅中确认食物。
2. **让热水留在锅里。** 用漏勺或夹具一次取少量食物，在锅上方短暂停留让水滴回去，再移到近处的耐热容器。若使用预放入的篮具，先核对把手稳固且本批重量你能控制，缓慢提起并让水在锅内流回；过重就分批用更合适工具，不硬提。
3. **避开烫水路径。** 只移动能稳稳夹住或捞住的量，不把热勺高举过人或设备。食物滑落时别徒手去接；放下工具、等锅稳定，再重新取。
4. **保留配方需要的液体。** 若后续步骤需要一部分煮水，只在有稳定、可安全使用的量具时按配方取出那部分；没有安全方法就暂停后续步骤。其余热水留锅中冷却，不因食物已取出就端锅走动。
5. **让锅水变成可处理的温度。** 在炉灶关闭、锅不会被误碰的位置等待；按锅、灶与当地排水要求在安全时处理。若等待会妨碍他人使用灶台，先告知并隔离，必要时求现场协助，不靠“应该已经不烫了”试手。

### 完成与停止

食物已按要求移入合适容器，锅仍稳在原位，剩余热水有明确的冷却与后续去向，就是完成。若食物尚未符合熟度要求、工具无法稳抓、篮具太重、锅会倾倒或有人可能被滴落热水烫到，停止取出并改工具、减量或寻求协助。烟、火、燃气异味和设备故障退出日常流程。

### 常见报错与补救

- **端起整锅才发现太重：** 不继续端着热水走；若能安全做到，放在最近的稳固耐热位置。热源在安全可达时关掉，等锅稳定，再改用合适工具或请人现场协助；不为回到原炉头横穿厨房。
- **篮具装满后提不动：** 不在热锅上方硬拽；放稳并改用漏勺分批取，下一次在开火前就按可提重量分批。
- **食物在夹具里滑落：** 不徒手接，降低一次取出的量，换适合形状的工具。
- **锅水还需用于下一步：** 留下可靠配方要求的量，不因“沥干”二字全部倒掉。
- **旁人要使用仍热的灶台：** 明确告知锅水未冷却，防止被误碰；安排安全的替代灶位或等候。

### 假设、替代与副作用

这里只处理普通开放锅的热水与食物，不涉及热油、压力锅、设备维修或烫伤治疗。工具、锅、食物和身体能力差异很大，没有必须徒手提起的标准重量。握力、单手、站立或视力限制可通过预放篮具、小批捞取、近处耐热容器和现场协助解决；不要把不稳的动作当训练。现实副作用是锅水需要等候冷却，换来你不用带它穿过厨房。

## English

Load this Skill when ordinary water-based cooking has reached **the reliable endpoint for that food**, and you need to separate food from hot water without lifting and tipping a whole boiling pot into a sink. Keep the **pot stable, move food to a nearby heat-safe dish, and leave the water to cool in the pot**. This does not establish safe doneness; use the applicable [doneness check](../check-doneness-and-store-leftovers/SKILL.md) first when needed. Separate rice and noodle tasks decide their own absorption or draining method; this Skill covers the removal movement.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm that the food has reached the recipe or food-safety condition for removal. Some recipes need part of the cooking water later, so do not assume it is waste. Set a stable heat-safe bowl or plate close to the hob. Choose a slotted spoon, tongs, or heat-appropriate basket **placed in the pot before cooking** that matches the food, its weight, and your grip. Keep people and equipment out of the short drip path. If the tool is unstable, the pot rocks, or you would have to reach across steam, turn off heat and wait for control or arrange help.

### Execution steps

1. **End or pause heat input first.** Turn off the burner when the recipe allows and it is safe to reach. Wait for violent bubbling and steam to ease. Lift a lid away from your face and body only when you can hold it securely; do not lean into the pot to inspect food.
2. **Leave hot water in the pot.** Lift small amounts with a slotted spoon or tongs, pause briefly over the pot so water drains back, and move them to the nearby heat-safe dish. With a basket already in place, confirm its handle is secure and you can control this batch's weight. Raise it slowly and let water drain back. If it is too heavy, settle it and use a suitable tool in smaller portions rather than forcing the lift.
3. **Keep clear of drips.** Move only what the tool holds steadily. Do not raise a dripping tool over people or equipment. If food slips, do not catch it with a bare hand; set the tool down, wait for stability, and try with a smaller amount.
4. **Keep liquid the recipe still needs.** If the next step calls for some cooking water, remove only that amount using a stable, safe measure. Pause the next step if no safe way to take it exists. Leave the remaining water to cool in place; finished food does not make the pot safe to carry.
5. **Wait for water to become safe to handle.** With the burner off, leave the pot where it cannot be knocked. Follow pot, hob, and local drain directions when it is safe. Tell others if the hot pot occupies a needed burner and arrange a safe alternative or assistance. Do not test cooling by putting your hand in the water.

### Success and stop

The food is in a suitable dish, the pot remains stable, and leftover hot water has a clear cooling and disposal path. Stop if food has not met its required doneness, the tool cannot grip securely, a basket is too heavy, the pot could tip, or dripping water threatens someone. Change tools, reduce the load, or get help. Smoke, fire, gas odour, and equipment failure leave this everyday flow.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You lift the whole pot and discover it is too heavy:** Do not keep carrying hot water. If safe, set it on the nearest stable heat-safe surface. Turn off the burner if safely reachable, let the pot settle, then use a suitable tool or get in-person help. Do not cross the kitchen merely to return it to the original hob.
- **The full basket is too heavy:** Do not yank it above hot water. Settle it and remove food in smaller portions; next time size batches for a manageable lift before heating.
- **Food slips from tongs:** Do not catch it with a bare hand. Take less and use a tool suited to its shape.
- **The recipe needs cooking water:** Keep the amount it specifies instead of pouring it all away under the word “drain.”
- **Someone needs the still-hot burner:** Tell them that the pot has not cooled. Prevent accidental contact and use another safe space or wait.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This handles food and hot water in an ordinary open pot, not hot oil, pressure cookers, equipment repair, or burn treatment. Tools, pots, foods, and bodies differ; there is no required hand-lifting weight. A pre-placed basket, small spoonfuls, a nearby heat-safe dish, and in-person help can make the task accessible when grip, one-hand use, standing, or vision is limited. Do not treat an unstable movement as training. The water may need time to cool, but you need not carry it through the kitchen.
