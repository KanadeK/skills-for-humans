## 中文

当桌子被上一件事、几张纸和“先放一下”的物品占满，下一件普通任务无法开工时，加载这份 Skill。你只需恢复**这次任务真正需要的物理工作面**，不必在开始工作前整理整个房间。数字文件、重要文书的分类、共用物权限、设备维修与已经发生的泼洒事故不在本篇处理。

### 准备与输入

用一句话写出下一件要做的事，并指出它需要的最小桌面面积和物品，例如“笔记本电脑、鼠标和一张纸”。再找一个你已能使用的暂放位置；可以是桌边空角、已有文件夹或浅盒，不需要先买收纳用品。确认桌上哪些是你的、哪些属于别人，哪些未完事项一旦移动就可能失去进度。

如果坐着、站着、伸手或辨认纸张有困难，按你实际姿势选择可及范围；可以用一致位置、大字或触觉提示，但不靠颜色一种线索。不要为了这次复位移动不稳的重物、拉扯接着电源的线或擅自阅读、丢弃他人的材料。

### 执行

1. **定义“可以开工”。** 把这次要摊开的物品摆出来，圈出能完成第一步的工作区域。能放得下并自然取用即可；整张桌子空无一物不是验收条件。
2. **先保存上一件事的断点。** 对仍在进行的工作写一行“做到哪一步、下一步是什么、物品暂放哪里”。用自己的私密记录方式保存，不在公开便条上抄敏感内容。已经完成或属于他人的材料分别标明，不靠记忆替它们安排新位置。
3. **只做三路移动。** 本次必需物留在手边；有明确旧位置的物品放回；未完但本次不用的放到一个可找回的暂放点，并写下下一步。明显属于你的普通垃圾可以清掉；身份、归属或去留不明的东西不丢弃。
4. **让工作面和人都能进入。** 把本次常用物放在你舒适能取的范围，留下手、纸张、键盘或工具移动所需空间；椅子、轮椅或其他辅助方式也应能靠近。不要把所有杂物转移到脚下。接线、设备或液体状态不安全时，停下相应部分，按其专门说明处理。
5. **用第一个实际动作测试。** 坐下或站到工作位置，拿起这次的第一件材料，完成可立即做的一小步，例如写标题或打开要处理的纸张。若仍需不断挪物，改一个落点后再试；若根本没有安全可用的桌面，换合适的已有工作位置或缩小任务，别把摇晃的临时叠高物当桌子。
6. **给暂放物一个收口。** 写下何时继续或归位，并让它保持可找回。等这次任务结束再处理，不让“临时”变成一个没有期限的桌面地层。

### 完成与停止

你已经能在这块桌面完成下一件任务的第一步；所需物品触手可及，上件事的断点可找回，通行与身体位置未被挤占。到这里就停止整理。如果不明物品、他人材料、液体或不安全设备挡住工作，先隔离问题并换位置或请有权处理的人协助，不用丢弃或强行通电来完成桌面指标。

### 常见报错与补救

- **清理变成全屋大整理：** 回到那句下一件具体任务，只恢复它需要的面积；其余待办另记，不趁机建立完整收纳系统。
- **暂放点装满了：** 先把已完成且有旧位置的东西放回；未完成的只保留明确下一步与归位时间，不能无限叠高。
- **上一件事搬开后忘了做到哪：** 从断点记录、最近完成的实物结果恢复；没有线索就标“待核实”，不要编造完成状态。
- **桌子是共用的：** 只移动你有权移动的东西；别人的材料先询问或绕开，无法取得工作面时改用其他位置。
- **被打断：** 留下本次必需物和暂放物的位置及下一步；回来只从未完成的第一步继续，不从头再清一遍桌。

### 假设、替代与已知副作用

不同任务需要不同面积和取用方式；纸笔、电脑、坐姿、轮椅或视觉辅助都没有统一桌面模板。这里借用工作台“常用物易取、保留足够活动空间”的原则，不按职业场所的尺寸给家庭下指标。现实副作用是桌面可立即开工，但暂放事项仍需要以后解决；这一点应写明，而不是藏在一个新盒子里。

## English

Load this Skill when the previous task, loose papers, and things put down “for a moment” cover a desk so you cannot start the next ordinary job. Restore **the physical surface this one task needs**; you do not have to organize the entire room first. Digital filing, classification of important documents, shared-item permissions, equipment repair, and an already-spilled liquid are outside this Skill.

### Preparation and inputs

Name the next job in one sentence and identify its minimum space and materials, such as “laptop, pointer, and one sheet of paper.” Find an existing place you may use temporarily: a free corner, folder, or shallow container is enough. Check which items are yours, which belong to others, and which unfinished work would lose its place if moved without a note.

Choose the usable reach area for the way you sit, stand, or read. Consistent location, large print, or a tactile cue can help; colour need not be the only signal. Do not move an unstable heavy item, pull connected power leads, or read or discard someone else's materials to finish this reset.

### Execution

1. **Define “ready to begin.”** Set out what this job must spread across the desk and mark enough area for its first action. Fitting and reaching those items is the test; an entirely empty desk is not required.
2. **Save the previous task's stopping point.** For unfinished work, note what is done, the next action, and where its materials will wait. Use a private record for sensitive details. Mark finished or other people's materials separately rather than inventing a new location from memory.
3. **Move things in three directions only.** Keep what this task needs within reach; return items with a known home; put unfinished but currently unneeded items in one retrievable temporary place with a next action. Remove only obvious ordinary rubbish that is yours. Do not discard anything whose identity, owner, or fate is unclear.
4. **Leave room for the work and for you.** Put frequently used materials within comfortable reach and leave space for hands, paper, keyboard, or tools to move. Make sure a chair, wheelchair, or other access method can approach. Do not shift the whole pile under your feet. If a connection, device, or liquid is unsafe, stop that part and follow its appropriate instructions.
5. **Test with the first real action.** Sit or stand where you will work, take the first material, and do a small actual step, such as writing a heading or opening the relevant sheet. If you still keep moving things, adjust one landing place and try again. If no safe surface is available, use another suitable existing place or shrink the job; an unstable stack is not a desk.
6. **Close the temporary branch.** Note when you will resume or put waiting items away, and keep them findable. Handle them after this task rather than allowing “temporary” to become an undated layer of the desk.

### Success and stop conditions

You have completed the next task's first step at this surface. Needed items are within reach, the prior task's stopping point can be recovered, and your movement and approach are not blocked. Stop tidying there. If an unknown item, another person's materials, a spill, or unsafe equipment blocks work, isolate the issue and use another place or ask someone authorized to handle it. Do not throw things away or force power on to satisfy a desk metric.

### Common errors and recovery

- **A reset becomes a whole-home reorganization:** Return to the one named next job and free only its needed area. Record other jobs separately; do not build an entire storage system as a precondition.
- **The temporary place is full:** Return finished items with known homes first. Unfinished items need a next action and return time, not an ever-higher stack.
- **You forgot the previous job after moving it:** Use the stopping-point note or the last visible finished result. With no evidence, mark it to confirm instead of inventing completion.
- **The desk is shared:** Move only what you are entitled to move. Ask before touching another person's work or work around it; use another location if the needed surface is unavailable.
- **You are interrupted:** Leave the needed items, waiting items, and next action identifiable. Resume at the unfinished first step rather than clearing the entire desk again.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Different jobs require different surfaces and reach patterns; paper, computers, seated work, wheelchair access, and visual aids do not have one universal layout. This adapts the workstation idea that frequent items should be easy to reach with room to move; it does not impose occupational dimensions or medical advice on a home. The desk may be ready now while temporarily parked work still needs attention later. Record that obligation instead of hiding it in a new box.
