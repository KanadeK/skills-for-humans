## 中文

当你的一小片普通**非厨房家具桌面或搁板**有灰尘、手印或轻微日常污迹，且它在地面上可安全触及时，用这份 Skill 先处理桌上轻物，再按**准确家具的表面涂层**干取颗粒、决定能否湿擦、干燥归位。目标是可见轻污减少、涂层与边缘不被清洁品损伤、物品仍找得回来。餐食直接接触的厨房工作台归 H079；纺织软包归 H085，大量泼洒归 H086。

### 准备与输入

确认家具稳固，表面是上漆木皮、实木、贴面、漆面、石材、金属还是不明涂层；找到品牌、说明书或安装/购买资料里的清洁许可。检查是否只是普通灰尘与手印，或已有起泡、裂缝、潮斑、粘附不明物。准备一块无砂粒软布和可安全放置轻小物的地方；有电器/插线、重物、易倒摆件时先划出不触碰的区域。湿布和洗剂只有在此家具的准确说明允许时才准备，不凭隔壁那张桌子的做法推断。

高处要垫脚、重家具需搬动、液体已靠近电器、霉/体液/化学污染、涂层破裂或来源不明时，先停止普通擦拭，按对应安全/制造商途径处理。

### 执行

1. **先留出可用的小片。** 把能安全单手拿起的轻物逐件放到干净稳固处，记住原位置或按顺序排列；不拖重显示器、花盆或家具本体，不拔插线来“清空桌面”。若某物下方有潮湿或不明粘物，先隔离那片，不让普通干布把它铺开。
2. **干取灰尘和砂点。** 用洁净、柔软且无砂粒的布按表面纹理或家具说明轻带走浮尘；布变脏时换面，避免把颗粒当研磨剂压进漆面。对只允许干布的涂层，干取轻污就结束这条清洁路径，不强行加水。
3. **仅获许可时小处试湿。** 如果还有普通手印，且准确家具与清洁品标签明确允许，使用微湿、不滴液的软布或规定产品在不显眼一小处试擦，检查褪色、起毛、粘膜、鼓边或失光。厂家要求干布/禁止家用清洁剂时服从；没有说明时停在干式整理，先问制造商。
4. **局部轻擦并干燥。** 小处试验正常才分小片处理许可的表面，清洁品尽量按说明放在布上而非淋进接缝；用干净布按产品说明去残留，特别检查边缘、木皮拼接与物品原来压着的地方没有积液。用相容干布收尾并等实际干燥。不用钢丝球、研磨粉、强酸/强碱或混配清洁剂来追“更亮”。
5. **按原位置归还轻物。** 只有表面已干、没有黏膜或新损伤时，再把轻物稳妥放回，不让湿杯底重新留下水圈；有垫片或托盘则按原位放回。若原本已严重失色/起泡或污迹不响应允许方法，记录状态并停，清洁不是修补家具涂层。

### 成功条件

小片可触及家具表面普通浮尘/手印按准确涂层许可减少，布面无砂、表面无残液或清洁膜，轻物被安全归位，涂层无新褪色/划伤。若只确认干布许可或说明缺失，完成干取并暂停湿擦也算正确完成判断。

### 常见报错与补救

- **看见“木色”就拿万能喷剂湿擦：** 先分清木皮、漆、贴面或实木及准确说明；颜色不等于清洁许可。
- **布里夹砂还在擦：** 换洁净软布，先带走颗粒；加力会把灰尘变成磨料。
- **桌上物直接推到边缘或压住电线：** 停止移动，给轻物稳定暂放处；重物与带电设备留给合适人员和条件。
- **湿布把桌边/接缝弄潮：** 停止加水、用厂家允许的干布处理可见水迹，出现鼓边或起泡时联系制造商/维修。
- **清洁剂留膜，表面越擦越粘：** 按产品及家具说明去残留并停用失配产品，不叠加另一种化学品。
- **桌面本来就掉漆或有霉：** 停止日常擦拭，记录并转对应修复/专业处理，不能用“擦亮”证明问题消失。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里只处理普通居室**小范围、可从地面触及、非食物接触**家具表面的日常轻污。不同制造商对同为木皮的家具可能给出“温和湿擦”或“只用干布”的相反指令；具体产品说明是唯一可用许可。视力或握力受限时用好光线、分小片和可信协助，不搬重物也不站高处。物品暂移会改变桌上排列，记住原位可以减少“擦干净却找不到东西”的后续任务。

## English

Use this Skill for dust, fingerprints, or light ordinary soil on a small **non-kitchen furniture tabletop or shelf** reachable safely from floor level. Move light items first, lift grit dry, choose any damp work from the **exact furniture finish**, dry, and return items. The result is less visible light soil without new finish or edge damage and without losing where objects belong. A food-contact kitchen counter belongs to H079, upholstery to H085, and a major spill to H086.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm the furniture is stable and identify lacquered veneer, solid wood, laminate, paint, stone, metal, or unknown finish. Find its brand or product care instructions. Check that this is ordinary dust or hand marks, not bubbled finish, cracks, moisture damage, or unknown sticky matter. Have a soft grit-free cloth and a stable temporary place for light objects. Mark electrical devices, cords, heavy objects, or fragile decorations as areas not to disturb. Prepare a damp cloth or cleaner only if this exact furniture permits them; the table beside it is not authority for this one.

Stop routine wiping when the patch requires climbing, heavy lifting, liquid is near electrical items, contamination is mould, bodily fluid, or chemical, or finish damage and material identity are unclear. Use the corresponding safety or maker route.

### Execution

1. **Clear a manageable patch.** Move only light items you can safely lift one by one to a stable clean location. Remember or order their original positions. Do not drag a heavy monitor, plant pot, or furniture, and do not unplug cords to “clear” the top. Isolate a damp or unknown sticky area rather than spreading it with the routine cloth.
2. **Lift dust and grit dry.** Use a clean soft cloth without sand, following the finish grain or maker advice. Turn or replace a dirty cloth face so particles are not ground into the coating. For a dry-cloth-only finish, finishing light soil dry is the whole cleaning path; water is not an upgrade.
3. **Trial damp cleaning only with permission.** For remaining ordinary marks, if the exact furniture and product labels allow it, test a slightly damp, non-dripping cloth or directed product out of sight. Stop for fading, raised grain, tackiness, lifted edge, or lost sheen. Obey a dry-only or no-household-cleaner instruction. Without product care, keep the dry result and ask the maker before wetting it.
4. **Wipe locally and dry.** Only after a successful trial, work small permitted sections. Place product on the cloth when directed rather than flooding seams. Remove residue under both labels, checking edges, veneer joints, and former object spots for pooled liquid. Finish with a compatible dry cloth and allow real drying. Do not use steel wool, grit, strong acid or alkali, or mixed cleaners to chase more shine.
5. **Return light objects to their places.** Once the surface is dry and free of sticky film or new damage, replace items steadily. Do not return a wet cup base to make a fresh ring. Use original coasters or trays if present. Record and stop for severe pre-existing discoloration, bubbling, or a mark not responding to allowed care. Cleaning is not refinishing.

### Success

Ordinary light soil on a small reachable patch is reduced within the exact finish permission, with no grit in the cloth, product film, liquid, new fading, or scratch. Light items return safely. A dry-only result or a stop before damp work when care is unknown is also a correct decision.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Wood colour leads to an all-purpose spray:** Identify veneer, lacquer, laminate, or solid wood and its exact care first. Colour does not grant cleaner permission.
- **A gritty cloth keeps wiping:** Change to clean soft material and lift particles first. More pressure turns dust into abrasive.
- **Objects are shoved to the edge or onto cords:** Stop moving them. Give light objects a stable temporary place; leave heavy or powered items for suitable help.
- **Dampness reaches a tabletop seam:** Stop adding water and remove visible moisture by maker-approved drying. Ask the maker or repairer for lifting or bubbling.
- **Cleaner leaves a sticky film:** Remove residue under furniture and product instructions and stop using the mismatched product. Do not layer another chemical.
- **Paint was already peeling or mould is present:** Stop routine wiping, record the condition, and use the appropriate repair or professional route. Shine does not erase the problem.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers **small reachable ordinary light soil on non-food-contact furniture**. Different makers can give opposite dry-only versus mild-damp instructions for similar-looking veneer; the real product governs. Use good light, smaller sections, and trusted help for limited sight or grip. Do not move heavy furniture or climb. Temporary removal changes the layout; keeping an order prevents a clean table from becoming a search problem.
