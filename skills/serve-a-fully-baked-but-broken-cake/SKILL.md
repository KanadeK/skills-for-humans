## 中文

当你的一份普通蛋糕已经**按原配方确认烤熟、处理安全**，却塌陷、开裂或分成几块，无法按原本的完整外形上桌时，用这份 Skill 判断还能提供哪些可食部分，并换一种诚实的呈现。目标是让原定用餐者拿到安全、愿意吃的份量，或及时放弃。外形失败不自动等于不安全；外形漂亮也不证明中心已经烤熟。

### 准备与输入

找回原配方或包装的烘烤、冷却与熟度要求，核对中心与厚处已达到适用的完成状态，且原料、放置和接触表面安全。等到能安全拿取时，把蛋糕置于干净托盘上；确认这顿需要多少份、碎裂程度、是否有同桌不能吃的配料，以及是否有要加的水果、奶油或其他辅料。脱模时还粘在模具里的成品先按 H049 处理，别为抢外形猛撬热盘。

中心仍是湿生面糊、熟度不能确认，或曾掉在不洁处时，停止“装饰补救”；按原配方和当地食品安全指导处理或丢弃。不要尝未熟面糊来判断能不能藏进奶油里。罐藏、医疗饮食和婴幼儿食品不在这里重做。

### 执行

1. **把安全与外形分开确认。** 用原配方规定的观察点检查厚处或中心；对蛋糕可结合成品中心、弹性与测试器上是否仍有湿生面糊，不只看棕色表面或一次牙签。若它未达到该配方的熟度，先停止上桌计划；这份 Skill 不提供“把生中心裹进熟碎屑”的捷径。
2. **只保留接触史可靠的熟食部分。** 待温度适合拿取后，把完整、熟透且未接触不洁表面的部分放在干净托盘；移除包装纸、碎模具或其他异物。若失败来自脱模粘盘，先处理取出问题，不把模具碎片当糖粒。
3. **按能保持的结构选呈现。** 还能切成稳固块的，切成较小但真实的份；只剩可拿取的碎块，可分进独立小碗。熟透但已经成为细碎屑的，可少量用在适合的杯装分层甜点或按可靠食谱做碎屑甜点。若要加水果、奶油或糖霜，只用用餐者可吃且状态安全的材料；别用大量新材料硬把原蛋糕粘成假完整形状。
4. **先试一份，再决定整批。** 摆一份给目标用餐者看：它是否容易拿取、入口质地仍可接受、份量足够？若很干、很苦或一碰就散，降低承诺，改为少量配角或换甜点。清楚说它从完整蛋糕改成了小份或分层，而不是让人先用叉子排查结构事故。
5. **处理新增材料与剩余。** 按最容易变质的实际配料和当地食品安全指导决定上桌、冷藏或丢弃；加鲜果或奶油后别沿用原来裸蛋糕的保存想象。准备的份量不足时，诚实补充另一种甜点或缩减菜单，别把每一小碗写成完整一大块。

### 成功条件

你能确认使用部分已经按适用配方安全烤熟，明确选择稳固切块、小碗碎块、合适的熟碎屑用途或停止，且每份份量、配料和储存去向清楚。有人愿意吃这些真实形态，才叫补救成功；没人愿意吃时停止也完成判断。

### 常见报错与补救

- **塌陷被误认为只需多抹糖霜：** 先查中心熟度；生面糊不能靠装饰变熟。
- **一掰就碎仍坚持切完整大片：** 改成小份、托盘取用或杯装分层，别要求蛋糕履行已失去的结构职责。
- **把所有碎屑与大量奶油拌成球就说恢复原状：** 先试一小份的味道、结构和配料适用性；若无法成形或不适合用餐者，停止。
- **想用掉落地面或碰过不洁工具的碎块：** 丢弃该部分；外观干净不等于接触史可忽略。
- **加了鲜果或奶油仍按裸蛋糕放置：** 重新按实际添加品与当地指导处理剩余，不用原蛋糕的保存方式覆盖新配料。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里只处理**已烤熟**、状态可靠的普通蛋糕；玛芬、快手面包等可借鉴“按熟度与剩余结构决定呈现”，但不把一种蛋糕的烘烤时间套给它们。若不能看清中心或安全取样，可按原配方借助适用工具和可信的人判断；判断仍不可靠就停止。改变呈现可能增加小碗和配料，也可能只留下可食部分而减少总份量，须对实际吃的人说清。

## English

Use this Skill when an ordinary cake is **confirmed fully baked under its recipe and safely handled**, yet has sunk, cracked, or broken into pieces so it cannot be served in its intended whole shape. Decide which safe parts can be offered and present them honestly another way. The result is a safe portion the intended diners want to eat, or a timely stop. Failed shape does not automatically mean unsafe food; a handsome outside does not prove the centre is done.

### Preparation and inputs

Find the original recipe or package's baking, cooling, and doneness cues. Confirm the centre and thick parts meet its applicable finish condition, with reliable ingredient, holding, and contact history. Once cool enough to handle safely, put the cake on a clean tray. Note how many servings this meal needs, how badly it has broken, ingredients diners cannot eat, and any fruit, cream, or other additions you might use. If it is still stuck in the pan, address removal under H049 first rather than prying at a hot pan to save the silhouette.

If the centre is wet raw batter, doneness cannot be confirmed, or pieces touched an unclean surface, stop the decoration plan. Follow the original recipe and local food-safety guidance or discard. Do not taste uncooked batter to decide whether cream can hide it. Canning, medical diets, and infant food are outside this recovery.

### Execution

1. **Separate safety from appearance.** Check the thick part or centre with the original recipe's cues. For a cake, consider the set centre, spring, and whether a tester shows wet raw batter, rather than a brown surface or one toothpick alone. If it has not reached that recipe's doneness, stop serving plans; this Skill does not offer a shortcut that wraps a raw centre in cooked crumbs.
2. **Keep only reliably handled cooked parts.** After it can be handled safely, move intact, fully baked parts that have not touched an unclean surface to a clean tray. Remove paper, pan fragments, or foreign material. If release from the pan caused the damage, handle removal separately; broken pan bits are not sprinkles.
3. **Choose the presentation the structure can hold.** Cut stable sections into smaller honest portions. Put graspable broken pieces in separate bowls. Fully baked fine crumbs may go in a suitable layered cup dessert or a crumb dessert under a reliable recipe. If you add fruit, cream, or frosting, use only safe ingredients the diners can eat. Do not glue a fake whole cake with a large amount of new material.
4. **Trial one serving before scaling.** Show a portion to the intended diner. Is it easy to take, acceptable to eat, and enough for a serving? If it is very dry, bitter, or falls apart on contact, lower the promise: use a small accent or choose another dessert. Say that a whole cake became portions or layers instead of making forks investigate the structural incident.
5. **Handle additions and leftovers.** Use the ingredient with the more restrictive storage requirement and local food-safety guidance to decide serving, refrigeration, or disposal. After fresh fruit or cream is added, do not assume the plain cake's storage conditions still apply. If portions are short, offer another dessert or reduce the menu honestly rather than describing a small bowl as a full large slice.

### Success

You can confirm the used parts are fully baked under an applicable recipe, choose stable pieces, small bowls, a suitable cooked-crumb use, or a stop, and explain portions, additions, and leftover destinations. Recovery succeeds only when someone wants these actual forms. Stopping when nobody does is a completed decision.

### Common errors and recovery

- **A sunken cake is treated as a frosting shortage:** Check centre doneness first. Decoration cannot cook raw batter.
- **You insist on large slices from a cake that crumbles on touch:** Serve small portions on a tray or as cup layers; do not require the cake to provide a structure it no longer has.
- **All crumbs are mixed with abundant frosting and called restored:** Trial a small amount for taste, structure, and ingredient suitability first. Stop if it will not hold or diners cannot use it.
- **You keep pieces that fell on the floor or touched a dirty tool:** Discard those parts. A clean-looking surface cannot erase contact history.
- **Fruit or cream is added but storage follows the plain cake:** Reassess leftovers under the actual additions and local guidance.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary cake that is **fully baked** and has reliable handling history. Muffins or quick breads may use the decision about doneness and remaining structure, but not this cake's baking time. If you cannot see the centre or sample safely, use suitable tools and trusted help under the original recipe. Stop if the decision remains uncertain. New presentation may use bowls and additions, or it may leave fewer total portions. Tell the people eating the actual amount.
