## 中文

你已经从正确入口进入一座普通开放的大楼，却要找某个服务台、房间或部门时，用这份 Skill把**目的地名称/编号—楼层—分区/翼楼—开放通道**对起来，逐个路口核对。目标是到达标识相符、可进入的目的地，或在导向失效时回到官方信息点求助。它不负责在街上找建筑入口、擅入限制区域，也不复制医院就诊、展览参观或其他场馆的专属流程。

### 准备与输入

保留目的地的完整名称、房间/科室编号、预约或联系信息中可供导航的部分，尽量知道楼宇名称而不只街道地址。在入口查看管理方官方楼层目录、纸图、数字导览或柜台指示，核目的地今天是否在该楼层/分区开放。地图可能把楼层称作 G、B1、L1、1F 或“上层”，不要按自己熟悉城市的层数转换猜。只有陌生二维码时可改看公开标识或问管理方人员，不把它当唯一官方入口。

### 执行

1. **先找到自己在图上的位置。** 在管理方目录或入口地图读“你在这里”的楼层/入口，确认目的地的正式名称与编号；同名不同楼、不同翼或“咨询台”多个位置时，优先用编号、所在区域和本次任务匹配。若目录不列该目的地，先问正式信息台，而不是随机走楼梯。
2. **拆成楼层、分区和最后门牌。** 先选管理方允许的电梯、楼梯或平层通路到目标楼层，再按翼楼/区域字母、颜色加文字、走廊号码前进，最后核房间或服务台标识。每次转层或岔路只执行下一段，重新读当前位置与目标箭头；色块是辅助，文字和编号更能排除相似区。
3. **遇到关闭或冲突时停在公开区域。** 门锁着、标“员工专用”、地图与现场箭头不同或电梯不可用时，退到上一处可信的公开目录/服务台，向管理方核当前开放路线和可及替代。不要尾随工作人员穿受控门，也不要用消防出口当捷径；紧急疏散时只按现场人员和标识。
4. **在终点读回。** 对照楼层、区域、房间/部门名称和对外开放标识；若需要预约、取号或另一个窗口，那是该场所的后续办理规则，先确认你到对地方。目的地已迁移时记录新楼层/分区和给出信息的官方来源，再按开放通道过去。

### 成功条件

你从已确认入口沿公开、允许且自身可用的路径到达名称/编号/楼层/分区一致的目的地；或因目录缺项/关闭通道而在正式服务点获得可核对的新位置或暂停决定。“走到了同一层的一扇门”不等于找到正确窗口。

### 常见报错与补救

- **地图写 1，电梯按钮写 G/1/L1：** 查现场楼层对照或问工作人员，不按别栋楼的命名换算。
- **同名部门出现在两翼：** 用房间号、区域和预约单上的确切名称交叉核对。
- **锁着的门看起来是近路：** 回上一个公开导向点查开放路径，不跟别人刷卡进入。
- **地图或二维码上的位置与现场冲突：** 先信当前管理方现场公告/工作人员，保留目的地名称以便重新问路。
- **看不清标识或走不到楼层：** 找可及版地图、电梯/工作人员协助，不以进入禁区或独自爬不适合的楼梯作为替代。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这只处理普通、可以退出的公共建筑内部导航；开放时间、访客权限、排队、医疗服务和消防事故依场所自身规则。英国图书馆与医院目录只是楼层/分区方式的实例，不是你当前楼的地图。没有手机可用纸图、入口目录和管理方人员；视觉、听觉、行动限制应核实际可用通道，不把“无障碍”图标当电梯今天必定运行的保证。重新绕路会花时间，但比误闯受限区更可控。

## English

Use this Skill after entering a large ordinary public building by the correct entrance but still needing a named desk, room, or department. Match **destination name/ID — floor — zone/wing — open route** and recheck at each decision point. Finish at the signed permitted destination or return to an official information point when guidance fails. This does not find the street entrance, open restricted doors, or replace a hospital visit, exhibition, or other venue-specific procedure.

### Preparation and inputs

Keep the destination's full name, room/department ID, and only the part of any appointment/contact detail needed for wayfinding. Know the building name if possible, not just its street address. At the entrance, use the manager's floor directory, printed map, digital guide, or staffed notice to check that destination's current floor/zone and opening. Floors may be called G, B1, L1, 1F, or Upper; do not translate by habit from another building. If the only sign is an unfamiliar QR, use public signs or staff instead of assuming it is an official link.

### Execution

1. **Locate yourself on an official map.** Read the “you are here” entrance/floor and match the destination's formal name and ID. For duplicate names in another building or wing, use room number, zone, and the purpose of this visit. If absent from the directory, ask an official information desk rather than testing random stairs.
2. **Split the route into floor, zone, and final door.** Take a manager-permitted lift, stair, or level path to the target floor; then follow wing/zone letter, colour plus text, or corridor numbers, and finally the room/desk sign. At every level change or fork, do only the next segment and read back position and target arrow. Colour helps but words and numbers distinguish similar zones.
3. **Pause at a closed or conflicting path.** For a locked door, staff-only sign, conflicting map/arrow, or failed lift, return to the last trusted public directory or desk and ask the manager for a current open and accessible route. Do not tailgate staff through a controlled door or use a fire exit as a shortcut. In an actual evacuation, follow live staff and emergency signs.
4. **Read back at the destination.** Compare floor, zone, room/department name, and public access sign. Appointment, ticket, or queue rules belong to the venue after you reach the right place. If it moved, record the new floor/zone and official source, then use an open route.

### Success

From a confirmed entrance you used a public, permitted, personally usable route to a destination whose name/ID/floor/zone agree. A missing listing or closed path ending at an official desk with a verified new location or clear pause also counts. A door on the right floor alone is not proof of the right service.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Map says level 1; lift buttons say G/1/L1:** Check the building's own floor key or ask staff; do not reuse another building's numbering.
- **Same department name in two wings:** Cross-check room ID, zone, and exact name from the visit details.
- **A locked door looks shorter:** Go back to a public sign and use an open route; do not follow someone with a pass.
- **Map or QR conflicts with on-site guidance:** Check current manager signs or staff and keep the destination name to ask again.
- **Signs are unreadable or floor unreachable:** Find an accessible map, lift, or staff help; a restricted area or unsuitable stairs are not fallback routes.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is wayfinding in an ordinary public building that can be exited. Hours, visitor permissions, queues, medical services, and emergency response follow that venue's rules. British Library and hospital directories illustrate floor/zone methods, not your current building's map. Paper maps, entrance directories, and staff replace a phone. Verify a real usable route for visual, hearing, or mobility needs; an accessibility icon does not guarantee a lift works today. A detour takes time but avoids a restricted-area mistake.
