## 中文

你有一项普通、低风险的小事，确实需要别人自愿协助，却不想只说“帮个忙”让对方答应后才发现范围很大时，用这份 Skill。目标是提出**对方能据此决定是否帮忙**的请求：做什么、何时、预计多久、你自己负责什么，以及可以说不。成功是请求和答复清楚；别人必须答应或实际替你做完，不是你可控制的完成条件。

### 准备与输入

把需要帮的事缩成一项能结束的动作，例如“今晚帮我看一段文字有没有错字”，而不是“以后帮我处理文档”。估算时间、地点、需要的物品或技能，并确认你有权让对方接触相关材料；敏感账号、身份文件、医疗/法律/财务事务和危险搬运不借“普通帮忙”绕过专门边界。想好若对方不方便，你自己会怎么办或找正式服务渠道；没有退路时也不能把对方的自愿说成义务。

### 执行

1. **先问能否接收一个具体请求。** 在对方有机会回复、不会被公开围观逼答的渠道说：“我想请你帮一个约五分钟的小忙，可以吗？” 紧接着给内容，别在对方说“可以”以后才揭示真实负担。若是在公开空间，对方可能不方便当场说明原因，允许稍后答。
2. **把范围一次说清。** 说出动作、数量或完成样子、希望的时间点、地点，以及你自己负责的部分。例如：“你周四前有空帮我看这两段有没有错字吗？我自己改，约五分钟；不方便直接说就好。” 没有真实截止就别装成急件；涉及费用、工具或额外往返时先说。
3. **给对方可拒绝和改范围的空间。** 对方说不，简单感谢并执行自己的备选；不追问理由、不在其他群再点名施压。若对方只能帮一部分，复述真实可做范围，决定是否有用；“可能有空”标待确认，不当作已安排。
4. **接受后核对交接点。** 说清材料怎样给、什么时间可联系、完成后如何交还、哪些事仍由你做。不要把“帮忙看两段”扩大成“顺便改十页”，也不要在未经同意时把对方的名字写成负责人。若任务变化，重新征求同意。
5. **在结果后收尾。** 对方实际帮了就核对是否收到成果并感谢；若没来得及，就按约定沟通和你的备选行动，不用“你不是答应了吗”把普通请求变成无期限义务。未取得帮助时如实记为未完成，继续用自己的方法处理原任务。

### 成功条件

对方收到具体、有限且可拒绝的请求；答复是“同意此范围 / 拒绝 / 仅能做一部分 / 尚未确认”中的真实一种，下一步归属明确。得到“同意”只是帮助安排成立，不能写成任务已实际完成；没有答复不是同意，也不是对方欠你一个解释。

### 常见报错与补救

- **“能帮忙吗”先套到同意才说任务：** 补完整动作、所需时间和约束，允许对方重新决定。
- **估计五分钟，实际要半天：** 及时更正范围；若超出对方已同意的量，再次询问或自己换方案。
- **对方说“我看看”，你写进计划当确定人手：** 保留待确认，给自己另设行动时点。
- **在群里公开点名要求帮忙：** 改用合适的私下渠道，给对方不受围观的拒绝空间。
- **对方拒绝，你想追问到他改口：** 停止劝说，转到自己的备选或正式服务。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

适用于自愿的普通小事，不适用于工作职责裁定、强迫、危险操作、账号代管或专业判断。身份、权力和文化关系会影响拒绝是否真的容易；若你有决定对方工作或机会的权力，要尤其避免把个人请求伪装成“自愿”。文字沟通不便可用双方能接收的口头或辅助渠道，仍说明范围并留出答复时间。清楚的求助可能得到“不”，但能避免两个人对“五分钟小忙”形成不同版本。

## English

Use this Skill when you genuinely need another person's voluntary help with one ordinary, low-risk task and do not want “Could you help?” to conceal a much larger job. Make **an answerable request**: what to do, when, approximate effort, what you will handle, and room to decline. Success is a clear request and response. The other person agreeing or doing all the work is not a result you control.

### Preparation and inputs

Reduce the need to one finishable action, such as “check two paragraphs for typos tonight,” rather than “help me with documents from now on.” Estimate time, place, supplies, and any skill involved, and confirm you may share the material. Sensitive accounts or identity papers, medical/legal/financial matters, and hazardous lifting do not become ordinary help by changing the label. Decide what you can do if they decline or which formal service you might use. Even without an alternative, their voluntary choice is not an obligation.

### Execution

1. **Ask whether they can consider a specific request.** Use a channel where they can reply without public pressure: “Could I ask a small task that I expect to take about five minutes?” Give the actual task immediately rather than securing a yes before revealing the burden. In a public place, allow a later answer if declining aloud would be awkward.
2. **State the whole scope once.** Name the action, amount or finish condition, hoped-for time and place, and your own part. For example: “Could you check these two paragraphs for typos before Thursday? I'll make the edits. I expect about five minutes, and it's fine if you can't.” Do not invent urgency. State real costs, tools, or extra travel up front.
3. **Leave room to refuse or narrow it.** For no, thank them briefly and use your alternative; do not demand a reason or tag them in another group. For partial help, restate what they can actually do and decide whether it helps. “Maybe” remains pending, not a booked helper.
4. **Confirm the handover after a yes.** Say how to send materials, when contact is appropriate, how the result returns, and what you still own. Do not grow “check two paragraphs” into “edit ten pages,” or name them responsible for the whole task without agreement. Ask again if the task changes.
5. **Close after the result.** If help actually arrives, check receipt and thank them. If it does not, communicate under the agreed plan and act on your fallback rather than treating an ordinary favor as an unlimited debt. Mark the original task unfinished until it truly is done.

### Success

The person received a concrete, bounded, declineable request. The real response is “yes to this scope / no / partial / not yet confirmed,” with a clear next owner. A yes arranges help; it does not prove the task was done. No answer is not agreement and does not entitle you to an explanation.

### Common errors and recovery

- **“Can you help?” secures a yes before the task is revealed:** Supply action, effort, and constraints, and let them decide again.
- **The five-minute estimate becomes half a day:** Correct the scope promptly. Ask again before exceeding what they accepted or use your own alternative.
- **“I'll see” is treated as confirmed staffing:** Keep it pending and set your own next action point.
- **A public group callout pressures one person:** Move the request to a suitable private channel with a real way to decline.
- **They decline and you want to keep negotiating until they agree:** Stop persuading and move to your alternative or formal service.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers voluntary ordinary favors, not deciding work duties, coercion, hazardous operations, account access, or professional judgment. Roles, power, and culture affect whether “no” is truly easy. If you control the person's work or opportunities, take particular care not to disguise a personal request as voluntary. If text is inaccessible, use a spoken or assisted channel both can receive, still stating scope and allowing answer time. A clear request may get a no, but it prevents two versions of “a tiny favor.”
