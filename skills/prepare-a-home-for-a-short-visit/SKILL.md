## 中文

当一次普通、低风险的短时来访已经约好，而你需要在客人到达前让家**实际能接待**，加载这份 Skill。目标不是全屋大扫除、准备宴席或表演一种统一的待客文化。来客的具体饮食、行动、感官与时间需求应向本人确认；食物安全、家电维修和已到场的意外另有任务。

### 准备与输入

确认约定的到达时间、预计人数、这次主要做什么、预计何时结束、你已答应提供什么，以及自己实际有多少准备时间和体力。若还不知道来客是否需要无障碍入口、避开宠物、安静环境或其他具体条件，发一条简短问题，不从身份或外表推断。没有得到答案前，只承诺你已能确认的条件。

把家分为“来客会用的入口—活动位置—必要时的洗手间”和你不打算开放的私人区域。只处理前一组。没有某件设备或用品时，不必为了待客临时买一套；先确定能否用现有条件完成这次已约定的活动。

### 执行

1. **固定可接待的最小范围。** 写下入口如何找到、客人会坐或站在哪里、需要用洗手间时怎么到达，以及你能提供水或其他已答应物品的方式。若访问目的只需门口交接，活动范围就不必升级为客厅参观。
2. **清出安全、可走的路线。** 从门到实际使用的位置移开会绊脚或挡路的普通杂物；检查门能打开、灯能用、座位稳固。行动或感官需求若已确认，就按本人给出的信息调整路线与说明，不用“通常大家都可以”代替询问。
3. **让实际使用点能工作。** 腾出可用座位或桌面，确认你愿意开放的洗手间可进入、洗手用品可找到。若承诺了饮水或简单点心，只提供你已有、能按实际标签安全处理且客人愿意接受的选项；没有承诺就不必在到访前启动一场厨房工程。
4. **画清私人边界。** 把不想被看见或碰到的个人物品收在自己能管理的位置，关好不开放的房间或柜子。涉及宠物、易碎物或他人物品时，按实际家庭安排处理，不替同住者决定他们的空间可以开放。到场时用一句话说明可用区域和需要先问的地方。
5. **做一次短走读。** 从门口以客人视角走到座位和洗手间：能找到门、能进入、能坐下、能知道哪里可去、你答应的东西确实有。把明显的阻碍修掉；不要因为看见一个没擦的角落就把任务扩成全屋清洁。
6. **条件做不到就提前改约。** 若入口不可用、座位或必要设施无法提供、已确认需求无法满足，及时告诉客人真实情况并提出可行的时间或地点替代。来客提前到达时先处理眼前交接，不靠加速清扫假装原计划未变。

### 完成条件

客人有可找到且可使用的入口、这次活动所需的一处位置和清楚的可用范围；你已经履行或如实修正承诺。个人空间有边界，未知的特殊需要已被询问或明确列为待确认。屋子不需要像样板间，准备也不以客人必须称赞为成功。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只剩五分钟：** 先确保门、路线、座位、洗手间和已承诺物品；把其余整理停止在“之后再说”。
- **来客需求还没回复：** 不猜饮食、行动或文化习惯；只完成已知条件，给对方一个简单确认入口，必要时说明目前的限制。
- **屋里很乱但活动区可用：** 收拢活动区的障碍物并说清可用范围；不临时承担整屋清洁债务。
- **承诺的食物或设备无法安全提供：** 提前撤回这项承诺并给出真实替代；不要为了面子用不确定的食品或故障设备。
- **临时多来一人或提前到门口：** 暂停扩张准备，确认空间和同意，再转入人数变化或提前到达的应对流程。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这份 Skill 假设一次普通、自愿、已约定的短时到访；鞋、饮料、隐私、宠物和停留时间没有统一地区或家庭默认值。你和客人可以商量不用家中场地、缩短到访或改到可及的地点。准备能力、住房大小、金钱和身体条件都不是人格评分。若有人不能安全进入，诚实改约比让他现场解决更好。

现实副作用是客人可能看到一个有人生活的房子，而不是展示厅。这仍可以是一次准备充分的来访。

## English

Load this Skill when an ordinary, low-risk short visit is agreed and your home needs to be **usable for that visit** before the guest arrives. The goal is not a whole-house deep clean, a banquet, or one universal style of hospitality. Ask the visitor about any specific food, mobility, sensory, or timing needs. Food safety, appliance repair, and problems after arrival belong to their own tasks.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm the arrival time, expected number of people, purpose, likely end time, what you have promised, and the time and energy you actually have. If you do not yet know whether the visitor needs step-free access, distance from pets, a quiet place, or another specific condition, send a short question. Do not infer it from identity or appearance. Until they answer, promise only conditions you can verify.

Divide the home into “entrance — place for this activity — toilet if needed” and the private spaces you do not plan to open. Work on the first group. A missing accessory does not require a pre-visit shopping system; first check whether what you already have can support the agreed activity.

### Execution

1. **Define the smallest hosting area.** Note how the guest finds the entrance, where they will sit or stand, how they reach a toilet if needed, and how you can offer water or anything else you promised. If the purpose is a doorstep handoff, the activity area does not need to become a tour of your living room.
2. **Make the route safe and usable.** Move ordinary trip hazards or obstacles between the door and actual use area. Check that the door opens, lighting works, and seating is stable. Adapt the route and explanation to any access or sensory need the visitor has confirmed; “most people can manage” is not a substitute for asking.
3. **Check the actual use points.** Clear one seat or table area and make sure any toilet you offer can be reached and handwashing supplies found. If you promised a drink or simple food, offer only options you already have, can handle safely under their actual labels, and the guest accepts. Without such a promise, there is no need to start a kitchen project.
4. **Set private boundaries.** Put personal items you do not want seen or touched where you can manage them, and close rooms or cupboards you will not open. Handle pets, fragile objects, or another resident's things under the household's real arrangements; do not volunteer someone else's space. When the guest arrives, say briefly which spaces are usable and which require asking first.
5. **Do one short walkthrough.** From the door, follow the guest's path to the seat and toilet. Can they find and enter, sit, know where they may go, and receive what you actually promised? Fix an obvious blocker. One unpolished corner does not turn this into a whole-house cleaning job.
6. **Change the plan early if the minimum fails.** If the entrance, seat, necessary facility, or confirmed need cannot be met, tell the guest promptly and offer a feasible time or place. If they arrive early, handle the live handoff; faster cleaning cannot make the original timing true again.

### Success

The guest has a findable usable entrance, a place for the agreed activity, and a clear boundary for accessible spaces. You have met your promises or corrected them honestly. Private space remains private, and unknown needs have been asked about or marked to confirm. A showroom finish and praise from the guest are not completion criteria.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Only five minutes remain:** Check door, route, seat, toilet, and promised items first. Stop the rest of the tidying at “later.”
- **The guest has not replied about a need:** Do not guess food, mobility, or cultural habits. Prepare known conditions, leave an easy reply route, and state any limit in time.
- **The house is messy but the visit area works:** Remove hazards from that area and explain the usable scope. Do not take on the whole home's cleaning debt.
- **Promised food or equipment cannot be provided safely:** Withdraw that promise early and offer a real alternative. Do not use uncertain food or broken equipment for appearances.
- **An extra person appears or the guest arrives early:** Pause expansion, check capacity and consent, then use the separate participant-change or early-arrival response.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This covers a voluntary, agreed, ordinary short visit. Shoes, drinks, privacy, pets, and length of stay have no single regional or household default. You and the guest may choose a shorter visit, a different accessible location, or no home venue. Time, housing size, money, and physical capacity are inputs, not character grades. If someone cannot enter safely, an honest change is better than leaving them to solve it at the door.

The side effect may be a guest seeing a home where someone lives, rather than a showroom. That can still be a well-prepared visit.
