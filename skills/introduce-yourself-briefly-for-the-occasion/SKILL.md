## 中文

你第一次在普通聚会、课程、小组或邻里活动中获得一个简短介绍自己的机会，却不确定该说多少时，用这份 Skill。目标是**让别人知道怎么称呼你、你与这次场合有什么关系，然后把话轮交回去**。介绍完成不要求所有人喜欢你、记住你，或立即开始新友谊。求职面试、证件核验和正式演讲各有专门要求，不靠这份日常介绍代替。

### 准备与输入

先看场合真正需要什么：主持人是让每人报名字，还是要说为何来参加、希望参与哪一项活动？选你愿意使用的名字或称呼，另选一条与这次活动有关、真实且愿意公开的信息。例如“我住附近，第一次来这个读书小组”；不是把年龄、住址、工作、家庭或健康状况全部交出去。别人若已经约定每人几句话或有限时间，按那个范围收敛。

### 执行

1. **用最小可用结构起头。** 说清称呼，再给与本次场合有关的一句事实或目的：“大家好，我叫林，今天来看看这个读书小组怎么讨论。” 若在需要协作的小组，可说这次参与的角色或你能做的部分；不要为了显得专业夸大经验。
2. **只补一项能帮助继续交流的信息。** 如果轮到你多说一点，补一句兴趣、正在学的事或你希望了解的问题。只选一项，别把介绍变成一个无法回应的个人档案。你不想公开的事情可省略；普通社交不要求用私事证明你有资格在场。
3. **给出结束信号。** 说“很高兴认识大家”“我先听听大家的分享”或适合场合的一句收尾，然后停下，让主持人或下一位继续。若是两人交谈，可以把话轮交给对方，但不需要立刻抛出一连串难答的问题。
4. **按实际反馈做小修正。** 对方没听清名字就重复、拼写或换一种能看懂的方式；场合需要你说明角色就补上角色。没有热烈回应不证明介绍失败。若有人追问你不愿分享的个人信息，可以说“这部分我先不展开”，回到本次活动或结束话轮。
5. **卡住时使用短版。** 说“我叫〔称呼〕，今天来参加〔活动〕。很高兴认识大家。” 然后停下即可。你可以事先把三段关键词写在纸上，必要时照着读；不是每个自我介绍都要现场生成一篇人生回顾。

### 成功条件

别人有一个可用的称呼，知道你为什么在这次场合或与你如何协作，你已明确结束自己的话轮；所说信息真实且在你愿意公开的范围内。若名字未听清，可补一次；对方是否热情、是否邀请你继续聊天不是此 Skill 的可控完成条件。

### 常见报错与补救

- **介绍变成长履历，主持人在等下一位：** 停在名字、场合关系和一项相关信息，给出收尾。
- **为了融入而编造经验：** 改为真实的“我正在学”“我第一次来”，不靠虚构身份建立后续关系。
- **名字被听错：** 再说一次或用文字/拼写辅助，不把对方猜对当唯一方式。
- **有人要求私事细节：** 只说你愿公开的部分，回到活动或结束；不必给不相干的信息。
- **紧张到忘词：** 用称呼、来这里的原因和结束语三项短版完成，后续有机会再聊。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

适用于普通自愿交流，不用于正式身份认证、简历面试、销售操控或治疗。不同文化、语言和群体对称呼、代词、头衔及自我披露的习惯不同；你可用该场合合适且自己愿意的称呼，不推断他人的要求。难以说话或听清时，可用写好的短文字、手机显示或适合场合的辅助方式；不要求标准音量或眼神接触才算成功。短介绍可能留下后续问题，那是可选择的下一段谈话，不是本次必须完成的考核。

## English

Use this Skill when you get a short turn to introduce yourself at an ordinary gathering, class, group, or neighborhood activity and are unsure how much to say. Help others know **what to call you, why you are here, and that your turn is finished**. Completion does not require everyone to like or remember you, or an instant friendship. Job interviews, identity checks, and formal talks have their own requirements.

### Preparation and inputs

Notice what the setting actually asks: just names, why each person came, or what part of an activity each person hopes to join. Choose a name you are comfortable using and one true fact relevant to this event that you are willing to share. “I live nearby and this is my first book group” works; your age, address, job, family, and health history need not all come with it. Match any agreed short turn or time limit.

### Execution

1. **Start with the smallest useful structure.** Give your name, then one fact or purpose connected to this setting: “Hi, I'm Lin. I'm here to see how this book group discusses.” In a group doing a task, name your role or contribution. Do not inflate experience to sound qualified.
2. **Add only one detail that helps the exchange continue.** If there is room for more, add an interest, something you are learning, or what you hope to understand. Choose one, not an unanswerable personal dossier. Leave out private facts you do not want to share; ordinary conversation does not require a personal disclosure license.
3. **Signal the end.** Say “Good to meet you,” “I'll listen to everyone else's ideas first,” or another fitting close, then stop so the facilitator or next person can speak. In a two-person exchange, you may hand over the turn without firing a series of difficult questions.
4. **Make a small correction from real feedback.** Repeat or write your name if it was not heard; add a role if the activity needs it. A quiet response is not proof you failed. If asked for private detail you do not want to give, say “I'd rather not go into that,” return to the activity, or close your turn.
5. **Use a short fallback if you lose your words.** “I'm [name]. I came for [activity]. Good to meet you.” Then stop. You may write those three cues down beforehand and read them. Not every introduction needs to generate a life story live.

### Success

Others have a usable name, understand your connection to this occasion or how you will work with them, and have a clear end to your turn. What you said is true and within your chosen disclosure boundary. Repeat a missed name once if needed. Warmth, later invitations, or other people's memory are not success conditions you control.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The introduction becomes a long biography while the next person waits:** Stop after name, connection to the event, and one relevant detail, then close.
- **You invent experience to fit in:** Use a truthful “I'm learning” or “It's my first time here” instead of a fabricated role.
- **Your name is misheard:** Repeat it or use writing/spelling support; guessing correctly is not the other person's only option.
- **Someone asks for private detail:** Share only what you choose, return to the activity, or end the turn.
- **Nerves erase the script:** Use the three-part name, reason for being here, and closing line; talk more later only if you wish.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is for ordinary voluntary conversation, not formal identity verification, a job interview, sales manipulation, or therapy. Customs for names, pronouns, titles, and self-disclosure vary by culture, language, and group. Use a form of address that suits the setting and you are willing to share, without assuming what others require. If speech or hearing is difficult, a short written note, phone display, or appropriate assistive method can do the work. Standard volume or eye contact is not a condition. A short introduction may invite later questions; that is an optional next conversation, not this task's exam.
