## 中文

当你**还没付款**，却发现原计划的冷藏或冷冻食品无法在回程保持所需低温，或到家后没有可靠冷藏空间时，用这份 Skill 改选能完成眼前用途的商品。它只做购买前的决定；已买食品的温度史、入户保存和过敏专用替代另有规则。

### 准备与输入

拿着原商品或购物清单，确认：它要完成哪一顿饭或哪个具体用途；何时开封和吃完；从拿起商品到使用前，是否真的能满足标签的冷藏条件；家里有无安全饮用水、开罐工具、加热设备和干燥储存处；你已有的忌口及预算上限。看不清标签时，放大照片或请店员读出**未开封、开封后、食用前**三处说明。读不清关键条件就先不买。

### 执行

1. **找出失效的那段。** 若只是回程无法保冷，原冷藏品仍需从卖场到家一路满足适用储存条件；若家中也无法可靠制冷，保冷袋不能替代长期冰箱。不要用“天气还行”推断食品内部温度。
2. **写下原商品的工作。** 例如“今晚这道菜的一份配料”或“明早无需烹调的一餐”。只保留这次必须完成的功能、份量与已知限制；不宣称另一种食品在营养或过敏安全上等价。
3. **找真正适合常温运输的候选。** 可查看明确标示可在你的实际环境中未开封常温保存的罐装、无菌包装或干货。**罐头、真空袋、纸盒只是外观，不是常温许可证**；有“需冷藏”字样、包装破损或储存说明不明的候选立即排除。
4. **核对使用门槛。** 干货是否需要足够的水、烹调时间和设备？罐装食品是否有可用的开启方法？即食与待烹调不是同一个功能。若无法完成原来的用途，就改菜单或延期，而不是把依赖写成愿望。
5. **先解决开封后的去向。** 即使未开封可常温放置，开封后也可能必须冷藏。没有可靠冷藏时，只选你能按标签在这次使用中处理完的包装与份量；不要把未吃完的部分留在室温等待下次。若必须留剩余，就换更小且合适的包装、改一次用完的安排，或不买。
6. **收束选择。** 在仍合格的候选中，比较你现场看到的价格、口味、可得性和真实准备时间。写下“买什么—何时用—开封后怎么办”；没有合格候选时删去这一项并重排餐食。空白购物袋比虚构冷链安全。

### 成功条件

你选中的商品在购买、回程、未开封保存和这次使用中都有明确可执行的条件；开封后的剩余有符合标签的去向，或你明确决定延期/不买。买下商品并不等于完成使用。

### 常见报错与补救

- **“货架上卖”被当作常温证明：** 回头读具体包装的储存说明；需要冷藏的罐或真空包装仍按标签处理，无法确认就换候选。
- **未开封合格，开封后剩一半：** 付款前改成一次可用完的规格或用途；没有冷藏时不要计划把余量留到明天。
- **干货更好带，但来不及煮：** 换能按说明直接使用的候选，或改今晚的餐；不要把未煮熟的食物当即食替代。
- **回程或家中低温条件突然改变：** 在付款前重新筛选；若已经买下，停止使用本 Skill，按标签与当地食品安全指导处理实际食品，不凭气味推断安全。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这是普通食品的采购选择，不处理医疗饮食、婴幼儿食品或过敏替代。品牌、国家和包装工艺会改变储存条件，所以以**手中这一件**的标签为准；来源示例不替代本地规则。若阅读、开罐、站立或搬运不方便，可用放大、可信协助或更易开启的合格包装，仍无法安全核实时停止。改选可能改变口感、菜名、包装废弃物和准备时间。

## English

Use this Skill **before paying** when a planned chilled or frozen grocery cannot stay appropriately cold on the trip home, or you have no reliable cold storage on arrival. It chooses a product for the intended use. It does not reconstruct the safety history of food already bought, put groceries away, or improvise an allergy substitute.

### Preparation and inputs

Keep the original item or list in view. Confirm its job in a particular meal, when you will open and finish it, whether the trip and your home can actually meet its storage instructions, and whether you have safe drinking water, an opener, cooking equipment, and a suitable dry place. Note existing exclusions and your spending limit. If you cannot read a label, enlarge a photo or ask staff to read the **unopened, after-opening, and preparation** directions. If a critical condition remains unclear, leave the item out.

### Execution

1. **Locate the cold gap.** If only transport is the problem, the chilled item still needs its required conditions from shop to home. If home storage is also unavailable, a cool bag does not become a long-term refrigerator. Pleasant weather is not a food-temperature measurement.
2. **Define the original job.** It might be one ingredient for tonight or a breakfast requiring no cooking. Keep the needed function, amount, and known restrictions. Do not claim nutritional or allergy equivalence between different foods.
3. **Filter for genuinely shelf-stable candidates.** Consider cans, aseptic packs, or dry goods whose own labels permit unopened storage in your actual conditions. A can, vacuum pouch, or carton shape is **not** permission to store it at room temperature. Reject anything labelled for refrigeration, damaged, or unclear about storage.
4. **Check the dependencies for using it.** Do dry goods need water, cooking time, and equipment you have? Can you open the can? Ready-to-eat and cook-before-eating products do different jobs. If the candidate cannot serve the meal, change the meal or defer the purchase.
5. **Account for the opened package first.** A shelf-stable unopened food may require refrigeration after opening. Without reliable cooling, choose a package and amount you can handle within this use and the label's instructions. Do not plan to leave an unfinished portion at room temperature for next time. Choose an appropriate smaller pack, a one-use plan, or no purchase.
6. **Make the decision.** Among valid candidates, compare prices you can see now, taste, availability, and preparation time. Record “buy—use when—what happens after opening.” If none works, remove the item and revise the meal. An empty bag is safer than an imagined cold chain.

### Success

The selected food has workable conditions for purchase, transport, unopened storage, and this use, with a label-compatible plan for anything left after opening. Deferring or declining the purchase is also a complete result. Paying is not the same as using it safely.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Assuming a shop shelf proves stability:** Read this package's storage directions. Some canned or vacuum-packed products still require refrigeration; if unclear, choose something else.
- **Fine unopened, half left after opening:** Before checkout, switch to a suitable one-use amount or plan. Without cold storage, do not schedule the remainder for tomorrow.
- **Dry food travels well but cannot be cooked tonight:** Choose an option that can be used as directed, or change tonight's meal. Do not treat uncooked food as ready to eat.
- **The trip or home cooling plan changes:** Filter again before paying. If the item is already bought, stop this Skill and follow its label and local food-safety guidance for the actual item; smell cannot reconstruct its history.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary food purchasing, not medical diets, infant food, or allergy substitutions. Brands, regions, and packaging methods change storage requirements. The label on the item in your hand governs; linked examples do not replace local rules. If reading, opening, standing, or carrying is difficult, enlarge text, use trusted help, or select an easier-to-open valid pack. Stop if safety-critical directions remain inaccessible. Your meal, taste, packaging waste, and preparation time may change.
