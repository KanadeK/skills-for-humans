## 中文

当菜单和用餐人数已经确定，尚未开火，你需要决定每道菜实际做多少时，用这份 Skill。目标是让各道菜合起来够这一餐，又不把每一道配菜都按完整主餐份量制作。人数临时变化、医疗饮食份量和烹调后的剩菜处置属于其他任务。

### 准备与输入

确认**这次会吃的人**，他们愿意说出的普通食量与偏好，以及哪些人可能不吃某一道菜；不从年龄、性别或体型推算统一份量。写下每道菜在这一餐的角色（主食、主菜、配菜或共享点缀）、你家曾经实际吃完的量或配方标注的产量、可用食材和锅/盘容量。若准备有意留下一餐，先确认真正会在何时吃，以及可靠冷藏/冷冻空间和容器；没有安排的剩余不算“计划好的第二顿”。

### 执行

1. **先数确定的餐位。** 只计已确定吃这顿的人和明确想留的份，不给“也许有人来”做默认整份。若人数尚未确认，先使用可后加的普通配菜或等待确认，不开始无法缩回的大锅。
2. **区分菜的工作。** 给主食/主菜分配本餐的核心份量；配菜是分享同一餐位的内容，不是自动再做一整餐。若有人明确不吃某菜，不把他的那份硬加到这道菜里。
3. **用你已知的产量换算。** 从家里惯常的实际一份或可信配方的“可供几份”出发，写出每道菜要完成的总份数或容器量；记下你这次依据的具体饭量。没有可信基线时，选能后续加做或添上的温和小批量，而不是发明标准人均克数。敏感配方需另行核对放大/缩小方法。
4. **同时核对可执行上限。** 原料够不够、锅具装不装得下、食物能否按适用说明完成烹调与检查？若做不到，改菜的数量或配方，不把超容量的原料压进锅里等奇迹。
5. **只给有去向的剩余加量。** 额外份必须有具体下一餐、可用的冷藏/冷冻与干净容器，并能按当地指导及时处理；否则先只做本餐量。对已开封但未下锅的食材，按其原说明安排保存，不把它算成已煮剩菜。
6. **写一张出锅前计划。** 每道菜记“预计做几份、给谁/是否共享、是否有明确另存份、用哪个锅/盘”。开火前再读一遍，确保总量覆盖确定餐位且没有无人接收的热食。

### 成功条件

你能给每道菜说出可解释的准备量，整桌覆盖已确认的人，额外食物只有在真实可保存并计划使用时才被计入。发现食量、人数或冷藏条件不确定时，输出较小可调整的方案或停止这道菜，同样完成判断。

### 常见报错与补救

- **每道菜都按每人一大份：** 重新标出主线和配菜，再以整桌而非单盘衡量是否足够。
- **照陌生“每人多少克”做：** 回到家庭真实饭量与配方产量；没有基线时先做能加量的小批，不把人当成统一容器。
- **多做的食物没有冷藏空间：** 付款和开火前就删掉额外份或选可按实际条件处理的另一种菜单，不把台面当作临时冰箱。
- **人数在开始后改变：** 进入临时增减人数的任务，不用这张旧清单继续假装人数没变。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里处理普通家庭一餐，不作营养治疗、儿童专用食量或过敏处置。文化、预算、饥饿程度、菜式与可及性会改变真实份量。看不清配方时可放大、朗读或请可信的人协助核对；称量不方便时用家里熟悉的容器量并保留可观察加量点。少做可能需要再添一道简单食物；多做则带来容器和冷藏责任，二者都是真实后果。

## English

Use this Skill when the menu and diners are known but cooking has not started, and you must decide how much of each dish to make. The whole meal should feed the group without turning every side dish into another full meal. Last-minute changes in diners, medical portions, and handling food after cooking belong to separate tasks.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm **who will eat this meal**, ordinary appetite and preferences they choose to share, and which dishes someone will not eat. Do not infer a universal portion from age, gender, or body size. Mark each dish's role as staple, main, side, or shared accent. Use amounts your household has actually finished or a reliable recipe yield, plus real food and pan or serving capacity. If you intend a future meal from extra food, first check when it will be eaten and whether cold storage and suitable containers truly exist. An unassigned remainder is not a planned second meal.

### Execution

1. **Count confirmed places.** Include people eating now and only an explicitly planned extra portion. “Someone might visit” is not a default full serving. If attendance is unknown, choose something you can add later or wait rather than starting a large batch that cannot be reduced.
2. **Assign each dish a job.** Give the staple or main enough to anchor the meal. A side shares a place at that meal; it does not automatically need another full serving per person. If someone will not eat a dish, do not allocate their portion to it.
3. **Convert from a known yield.** Start with your household's familiar portion or a recipe's stated number of servings. Note the total servings or container amount each dish must produce and the evidence for that estimate. With no baseline, choose a modest batch that can be supplemented later instead of inventing a standard weight per person. Check sensitive recipes separately before changing their scale.
4. **Check what can actually be made.** Is there enough food? Will the pan hold it? Can the food be cooked and checked under its applicable directions? If not, change a dish or its scale rather than pressing an oversized batch into one pan.
5. **Add extra only with a destination.** Extra servings need a specific later meal, reliable cooling or freezing, clean containers, and a way to handle them promptly under local guidance. Otherwise make only this meal. Opened uncooked ingredients follow their own label; they are not cooked leftovers yet.
6. **Write a pre-cooking card.** For each dish, record expected servings, who it serves or whether it is shared, any clearly assigned future portion, and the pan or dish you will use. Check it again before starting: the total should cover confirmed diners without hot food that has no recipient.

### Success

You can explain the planned amount for each dish. The full meal covers confirmed diners, and extra food is counted only if it can truly be stored and used. When appetite, attendance, or cooling remains uncertain, a smaller adjustable plan or stopping one dish is a complete decision.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Every dish is a full meal per person:** Mark the main and sides again; judge the whole table rather than each plate alone.
- **You follow an unfamiliar “grams per person” rule:** Return to real household portions and recipe yields. With no baseline, start with an addable batch rather than treating people as identical containers.
- **There is no cold space for the extra food:** Remove the extra servings before cooking, or choose a menu your actual facilities can handle. A counter is not a temporary refrigerator.
- **The number of diners changes after cooking starts:** Switch to the separate last-minute-diner task; do not pretend this old card is still current.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is one ordinary household meal, not medical nutrition treatment, a universal child portion, or allergy management. Culture, budget, appetite, dishes, and access needs change real amounts. Enlarge or listen to a recipe or ask trusted help if print is hard to read. If precise weighing is inaccessible, use a familiar household vessel and leave an observable chance to add more. Cooking less may require an extra simple item; cooking more creates container and cold-storage work. Both are real consequences.
