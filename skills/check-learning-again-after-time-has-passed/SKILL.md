## 中文

当某项普通学习动作上次练过、**中间已经隔开一段时间**，现在想知道自己还能否独立做到时，加载这份 Skill。用一项与真实目标相符、可核对的**新任务**试一次，记录现在的表现与需要再学的地方。本文不给统一的“第 1/3/7 天”间隔或成绩承诺；时间按你实际需要使用内容的时点与可安排的机会选择。

### 准备与输入

拿出上次的学习目标或尝试记录，记下上次练习日期、当时能做什么、是否用了提示。选一项相近但不相同的新题、短解释或普通操作模拟，能用可靠答案、评分点或可询问的人核结果。若是正式测验或课程作业，遵守允许的资料/合作规则；本篇只用于低风险自查，不帮你绕过考核。

### 执行

1. **定这次要检验的实际动作。** 例如“读到这个条件后选择方法并完成答案”，而不是“感觉这一章熟”。题型尽量匹配日后需要做的事；只认词条的卡片不能证明会写、会判断或会操作。
2. **把旧答案收起并先尝试。** 只使用目标场景允许的资料，先在纸、可访问文档或口述录音中做出完整短答/步骤。遇到卡点时记下停在哪，不立刻看上次解答；到合理停点仍不会，就如实标“本次未独立完成”。
3. **完成后再核对。** 用可信标准查结果、关键条件和理由，而非只看结论像不像。标准缺失或相互冲突时写“暂未验证”，不把自我感觉当掌握证据。需要详查错因归 H256。
4. **与上次的真实状态比较。** 记录本次日期、任务来源、是否用提示、独立完成/部分完成/未完成/未验证，以及与上次不同的一处。若上次根本没有可比记录，只保留这次基线，不编一个“退步百分比”。
5. **选一个合理下一步。** 能独立完成且理由通顺，可安排下一个真实使用机会或以后再核；卡住则先回具体来源学习，再做另一项新任务。只补本次暴露的缺口，不把“做错一次”解释成整个学科都忘了。下次何时回顾取决于目标、现实日程和本次结果，无万能数字。

### 完成、常见问题与补救

你留下了隔时新任务的原始作答、可靠核对状态和一个由结果决定的下一步。重读旧笔记后觉得熟悉，没有完成本次“还能不能做”的检查。

- **只拿上次做过的同一题：** 换条件相近但答案不能靠记忆直接搬的新任务。
- **先翻答案再说会了：** 标“用了提示”，另找新题再做；不把本次算独立成功。
- **忘了很多：** 记下具体卡点，回原材料复习，再试可核任务；遗忘是输入，不是羞耻指标。
- **标准不可靠或没有答案：** 先标未验证，向课程允许的人核；不凭自信打分。
- **身体、感官或时间条件变化：** 用与真实目标可比的可及形式，记下变化，避免把环境差异全部归因于记忆。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

“隔了一段时间”只要求这不是刚看完答案的即时重复；不同目标和人的间隔选择会不同。纸笔、语音、屏幕阅读或其他可及方式都可保留原始作答。一次新题只说明此刻在这些条件下的表现，不能证明长期保持或保证正式成绩。

## English

Load this Skill when you practised an ordinary learning action before, **some time has passed**, and you want to know whether you can still do it independently. Try a **different, checkable task** that matches the real goal, then record present performance and what needs more learning. This gives no universal day-1/day-3/day-7 schedule or grade promise. Choose a later check around when you expect to use the material and when you can realistically practise.

### Preparation and inputs

Find the earlier learning goal or attempt record. Note when you tried it, what you could do then, and whether hints were used. Choose a comparable but different problem, short explanation, or ordinary operation simulation with a reliable answer, rubric, or person who can check it. Follow rules for allowed resources and collaboration if it resembles formal work. This is a low-stakes self-check, not a way around assessment conditions.

### Execution

1. **Name the action to test now.** For example, “Given this condition, I can choose a method and produce the result,” rather than “the chapter feels familiar.” Match the response form to what you will later do. Recognising a word on a card cannot prove writing, deciding, or performing.
2. **Hide the old solution and attempt first.** Use only resources the target setting allows. Produce a complete short answer or steps on paper, in an accessible document, or as spoken audio. If stuck, mark where; do not immediately open the previous solution. At a reasonable stopping point, write “not completed independently” if that is true.
3. **Check afterward.** Use trustworthy criteria to examine result, conditions, and reasoning rather than whether the conclusion looks familiar. If criteria are missing or conflicting, mark “not yet verified” rather than claiming mastery. Detailed error diagnosis belongs to H256.
4. **Compare with an actual earlier state.** Record today's date, task source, hint use, independently complete/partial/incomplete/unverified, and one difference from the prior attempt. If no comparable prior record exists, keep this as the current baseline instead of inventing a percentage decline.
5. **Choose a reasonable next action.** If you completed independently with sound reasoning, use it in a real later opportunity or schedule another check. If stuck, review the specific source and try another new task. Repair the gap this attempt revealed rather than declaring a whole subject forgotten. The next interval depends on the goal, schedule, and current result; no magic number applies.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

You kept an original response to a different task after time passed, a reliable check status, and a next action chosen from the result. Rereading old notes until they feel familiar does not answer “can I still do it?”

- **You reuse the exact old problem:** Choose one with comparable conditions but an answer that cannot be copied from memory.
- **You look up the answer first:** Mark “hint used” and try another new task; this was not independent success.
- **Much has been forgotten:** Record the precise sticking point, review the original material, and try a checkable task again. Forgetting is input, not a shame score.
- **The key is missing or unreliable:** Mark unverified and ask a course-approved person; do not grade yourself on confidence alone.
- **Physical, sensory, or time conditions changed:** Use an accessible form comparable to the real goal and record the difference, instead of blaming all variation on memory.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

“After time has passed” means more than an immediate repeat after seeing the answer. Appropriate intervals differ by goal and person. Paper, audio, screen reading, or another accessible method can preserve the original response. One new task shows performance now under these conditions, not guaranteed long-term retention or formal grades.
