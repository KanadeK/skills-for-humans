## 中文

当你所在的小组已经到齐，准备开始一次普通共同学习，而不是继续敲定时间地点时，加载这份 Skill。目标是让**每个人实际练一次**、比较思路、把未解决的问题交到可核来源，并准时收尾；仅坐在一起重读资料或由一人代做不算这次结果。安排会前目标与角色归本域“约一次小组学习”Skill。

### 准备与输入

开场看约好的学习目标、结束时间和课程合作规则。确认练习/材料允许在小组中讨论；需要独立完成并提交的作业保持个人边界。核对参与者能否看到、听到或以其他可及形式拿到题目；不要让设备、语言或发言速度决定谁能参与。

### 执行

1. **重申这轮产出与小角色。** 用一句话说“今天每人会尝试 X，结束时留下各自结果和最多几个待核问题”。定谁看时间、谁提醒轮换、谁记下未解处；角色可轮换，没人需要担任永久讲师。
2. **先各自做一小段。** 给同一允许练习或等价任务一段安静时间，每人自己写、说、画或用可及工具作答，并标自己在哪一步没把握。未准备好者可以先说出第一步；不要让最快的人抢先公布完整解法。
3. **轮流比较理由。** 每个人简短说明自己选的方法和一处依据，不只报答案。若结果不同，回题目条件和可信课程资料核，而非按声音大小投票。别人说时也可在自己答案上标一个要修改或追问的地方。
4. **把无法核的内容隔离。** 小组都说不清、来源相互矛盾或超出允许合作范围时，记下题目位置、各自已试的办法和一个可回答的问题；定谁在许可渠道向教师/助教求证。不造“大家同意就正确”的结论，也不发布别人的私人作答。
5. **到停点做实际收尾。** 每人写一项自己现在能独立做到的动作和一项仍需检查的点。核今天完成的是练习、讨论还是只找到问题；若要再会，另约明确目标/时间，不把本次顺延成无限会。材料和共享笔记按课程规则保管。

### 完成、常见问题与补救

每个实际参与者有自己的尝试或明确卡点，小组留下经过来源核对的共识与待核问题，按约定时间收尾。只听懂别人讲、没有自己的动作，不代表个人练习完成。

- **一个人一直在讲：** 暂停，按顺序给每人一段独立尝试和说明的机会；不强迫公开私人表现。
- **时间快用完还在争一个答案：** 记为待核，指派许可渠道求证，留一点时间收尾。
- **有人拿独立作业互抄：** 立即转为允许讨论的概念或旧练习，按课程规则保留个人提交边界。
- **线上有人听/看不到材料：** 用允许的可访问文本、口述、字幕或其他形式；无法平等参与时暂停该活动。
- **大家都觉得“差不多懂了”：** 各自做一次短答或说出下一步并核对，不用集体熟悉感替代个人结果。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

小组规模、时长和媒介可变，但每人有参与机会、任务合乎课程规则、答案能回源核对。不同表达与不确定可以并存，不必为了和谐假装一致。一次讨论不能保证个人长期掌握；真正要测保留情况可在以后独立做新任务。

## English

Load this Skill when a small group has assembled to **run** an ordinary study session, rather than keep arranging its time and place. Give **each person a real attempt**, compare reasoning, route unresolved questions to checkable sources, and end on time. Sitting together while one person reads or solves everything is not this outcome. Planning the goal and roles beforehand belongs to the separate setup Skill.

### Preparation and inputs

Open the agreed goal, end time, and course collaboration rules. Check that the practice or material may be discussed in a group; individually submitted work keeps its own boundary. Make sure everyone can see, hear, or otherwise access the task. Device, language, and speaking speed should not silently determine who gets to participate.

### Execution

1. **Restate the output and light roles.** Say, “Everyone will try X; at the end we will keep our own results and a few open questions.” Decide who watches time, prompts turns, and records unresolved items. Rotate roles when useful; no one must become a permanent lecturer.
2. **Let everyone try a short part first.** Give quiet time on the same permitted exercise or equivalent task. Each person writes, speaks, draws, or uses an accessible tool to answer and marks one uncertain step. Someone unprepared can name a possible first step. Do not let the fastest person reveal a full solution before others attempt it.
3. **Compare reasons in turns.** Each person briefly describes their approach and one supporting point, not just an answer. When results differ, return to prompt conditions and trustworthy course material rather than vote by volume. While others speak, each person can mark one revision or follow-up on their own attempt.
4. **Separate what cannot yet be checked.** If the group cannot explain a point, sources conflict, or a matter exceeds permitted collaboration, record the prompt location, attempts, and one answerable question. Decide who asks a teacher or tutor through an allowed route. Group agreement is not automatically correctness, and classmates' private work is not for public posting.
5. **Close at the stopping point.** Each person writes one action they can now do alone and one point still to check. Name honestly whether this session produced practice, discussion, or only better questions. If meeting again, make a separate plan with a goal and time rather than extending indefinitely. Store materials and shared notes under course rules.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

Every actual participant has an attempt or a clear sticking point, and the group retained source-checked agreements and open questions before ending at the agreed time. Listening to someone else without your own action does not prove individual practice.

- **One person keeps lecturing:** Pause and offer each person an independent attempt and a turn. Do not force disclosure of private performance.
- **The group spends all its time disputing one answer:** Mark it to verify, name an allowed question route, and leave time to close.
- **Someone starts copying individually submitted work:** Switch to permitted concepts or older practice and preserve each person's submission boundary.
- **An online member cannot hear or view the material:** Use permitted accessible text, spoken explanation, captions, or another form; pause the activity if equal participation is impossible.
- **Everyone says it “mostly makes sense”:** Have each person make a short response or name the next step and check it. Collective familiarity cannot replace individual evidence.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Group size, duration, and medium may vary, while everyone needs a participation route, the task must respect course rules, and conclusions should be checkable against sources. Different answers and uncertainty can coexist; harmony does not require pretending agreement. One discussion does not guarantee lasting individual learning. A later independent new task can check retention.
