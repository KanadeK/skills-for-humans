## 中文

当你按可靠配方做好**巧克力加奶油**的甘纳许，仍可确认安全，却出现油光、颗粒和不顺滑的团块时，用这份 Skill 在小样里试修。目标是为原定的淋面或馅料得到可用的顺滑质地，或及早停止。巧克力固形物、脂肪和水相的失衡不同于油醋汁临用摇匀；这里的温水法也不套用到蛋黄酱、奶油霜或不明巧克力制品。

### 准备与输入

确认原配方确实以可食用巧克力和奶油为主体，记录原本要做淋面、涂层还是能定形的馅料；再确认材料来源、制作与放置史可靠。准备干净耐热小碗、打蛋器、能饮用且温热但不烫手的水，以及可量取少量水的勺。把甘纳许从猛烈热源移开，先看它是仍有未融化的巧克力块，还是已出现油和细颗粒的分离；后者才走这份试修。若安全史不明，停止试吃和修复，按当地食品安全指导处理。

### 执行

1. **先留住原份。** 只舀出少量已分离甘纳许到干净小碗，另留一口原状作对照。记录小样大概多少；原锅不继续煮沸或反复高火加热。若只是尚未融化的整块巧克力，回到原配方核对完成方法，不把它误诊为油粒分离。
2. **用少量温热可饮用水试水相。** 在小样里先加很少一点，持续打匀，再观察油光和颗粒是否逐渐合回顺滑的混合物。King Arthur 的巧克力加奶油示例使用约 32–35°C / 90–95°F 的温水；没有量温工具时只用安全、温热而不烫手的可饮用水，不需要把水烧沸。一次加大量水会直接改变质地和用途。
3. **只在改善时逐次继续。** 仍有油粒而小样正变顺滑，可以再加一点、继续打匀并记录累计用量；若越加越稀、出现无法消除的团块或没有改善，就停止这份实验。不要改用冷奶油或更高火力追赶，前者增加脂肪与温差，后者可能让原问题恶化。
4. **按原定用途验收。** 看小样是否均匀、有光泽、没有明显油圈，并在适合实际使用的状态下试淋或试涂。若原来要做定形馅料，还需按可靠配方确认冷却后是否仍能完成该用途；刚打顺滑不等于会按原计划凝固。确认味道和安全状态也可接受。
5. **再决定原份去向。** 只有小样成功、原份仍安全且最终用途成立，才用已记录的小量步骤逐次处理原份的一部分，并持续核对。水加多导致太软但仍安全时，可考虑改成适合的淋面用途；无法完成用途、无法安全储存或无人愿意吃时，停止并丢弃。新增水不延长奶油成品的保存期限，剩余按实际成分和当地食品安全指导及时处理。

### 成功条件

你得到一份仍安全、油粒重新分散、实际能完成这次淋面或馅料用途的甘纳许，并知道剩余怎么处理；或在小样失败、用途被稀释掉或安全条件不明时没有继续改动整份。顺滑只是质地结果，不是食品安全证书。

### 常见报错与补救

- **把所有分离甘纳许直接放回猛火上煮：** 停热，只试小样温水与打匀；高热本身可能是分离原因。
- **一次倒入很多水，油粒少了却不能做馅：** 停止扩大，检查它是否还能作淋面；不能用时接受失败，不把湿度继续推高。
- **用冷奶油“补奶味”：** 停下，先试来源支持的温水小样；额外冷脂肪和温差可能继续结团。
- **只看到亮面便立刻填入需要定形的甜品：** 先按目标用途核对冷却后的结构，必要时换用途。
- **原料或奶油的放置史不明：** 停止口感补救，按当地食品安全指导处理；重新乳化不能重置时钟。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这只适用于已知配方、已知安全的普通巧克力加奶油甘纳许，且有人能安全处理温热食物。巧克力类型、奶油比例和原定用途会影响需要的水量，所以没有整锅通用比例。握力或温度判断不便时，用稳定小碗、可读量具或可信的人协助；不能确认水温或安全操作时停止。成功修复可能增加总量、降低浓度并改变凝固效果，故原用途必须重新检查。

## English

Use this Skill when a **chocolate-and-cream** ganache made from a reliable recipe is still known to be safe but has become oily, grainy, and clumpy instead of smooth. Trial a repair on a small sample. The result is a texture that can still serve as the intended glaze or filling, or a timely stop. Chocolate solids, fat, and water behave differently from a vinaigrette briefly shaken before use. This warm-water method does not transfer to egg sauces, buttercream, or unidentified chocolate products.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm the original recipe primarily used edible chocolate and cream. Note whether it was meant to pour, coat, or set as a filling. Check reliable ingredient, preparation, and holding history. Set out a clean heat-safe small bowl, whisk, warm potable water that is not scalding, and a spoon for measuring small additions. Remove the ganache from fierce heat. Distinguish intact unmelted chocolate pieces from an oily, fine-grained split; only the latter takes this repair path. If safety history is unknown, stop tasting and follow local food-safety guidance.

### Execution

1. **Protect the original.** Put only a little split ganache in a clean bowl; keep a mouthful unchanged for comparison. Note the approximate sample amount. Do not keep boiling or repeatedly blasting the main batch. If there are only unmelted chocolate chunks, check the original recipe's completion method rather than diagnosing a split.
2. **Trial a little warm potable water.** Add a very small amount to the sample and whisk steadily, watching whether the oil sheen and grains begin to combine into a smooth mixture. King Arthur's chocolate-and-cream example uses water around 32–35°C / 90–95°F. Without a thermometer, use safe potable water that feels warm, not scalding; there is no need to boil it. A large pour changes texture and use before you can judge the effect.
3. **Continue only while it improves.** If oil and grains remain while the sample is becoming smoother, add another little amount, whisk again, and record the total. Stop if the sample becomes too loose, keeps stubborn clumps, or does not improve. Do not chase it with cold cream or higher heat; added cold fat and temperature difference or overheating may worsen the original problem.
4. **Test the original use.** Look for an even, glossy mixture without an obvious oil ring, then trial pouring or coating in the state it will actually be used. For a filling that must set, follow the reliable recipe to check whether it still reaches that structure after cooling. Smooth immediately after whisking is not a promise it will set. Check acceptable flavour and known safe condition as well.
5. **Decide what happens to the original.** Only if the sample succeeds, the batch remains safe, and the intended use still works should you apply the recorded small steps to part of the original, reassessing as you go. A safe but too-soft result may suit a glaze instead of a firm filling. Stop and discard when the use fails, storage is unsafe, or nobody wants it. Added water does not extend a cream-based product's keeping period; handle leftovers promptly under local guidance.

### Success

You have still-safe ganache with fat and grains recombined that actually works as this glaze or filling, and a destination for the remainder. Stopping before changing the batch when the sample fails, becomes too soft, or safety is unclear is also a complete decision. Smoothness is a texture outcome, not a food-safety certificate.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The whole split batch goes back onto fierce heat:** Stop heating and trial warm water with whisking in a small sample; high heat may have caused the split.
- **A lot of water removes grains but ruins a firm filling:** Do not scale the trial. Check whether it can serve as a glaze; otherwise accept the failure rather than adding more water.
- **Cold cream is added to “restore creaminess”:** Stop and trial the source-supported warm-water sample first. Extra cold fat and temperature difference can encourage more clumping.
- **A glossy look sends it straight into a dessert that must set:** Check cooled structure against the intended use first, and change use if needed.
- **Ingredient or cream holding history is unknown:** Stop texture repair and follow local food-safety guidance. Re-emulsification does not reset the clock.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers only ordinary chocolate-and-cream ganache from a known recipe and safe history, with someone able to handle warm food safely. Chocolate type, cream ratio, and intended use affect water needed, so there is no universal batch ratio. If grip or temperature assessment is difficult, use a stable bowl, readable measure, or trusted help. Stop if water temperature or handling cannot be kept safe. A successful repair may increase volume, weaken concentration, and change setting; recheck the original use.
