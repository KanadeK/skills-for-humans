## 中文

当你**有意从一份状态可确认的液体开始**，按可信无酒精配方把水分蒸发、让普通酱汁变得更浓时，用这份 Skill。它不是用淀粉浆加稠，也不处理已经做坏而急需补救的成品。水减少的同时盐、酸、糖等味道可能更集中，目标不只是“看起来黏”。

### 准备与输入

读完整配方的底液、预计起始量、目标用途/稠度、适用锅具、是否需要先完成某些食材的安全烹调和结束判断。只选已知可食用且保存史可靠的材料；这里不使用酒精，也不靠浓缩使不安全底液“变安全”。准备可稳放的小锅、适用搅拌器、能看见液面和锅底的光线，以及可安全取样冷却的勺。热液或长时间看火不方便时缩小批量或请可信的人协助，不离锅进行其他高注意力任务。

### 执行

1. **先标出目标与可减少的量。** 写下这份酱汁要涂、淋还是拌、何时停止、最终至少需要多少。若起始液体已经很咸、甜或酸，确认配方允许收浓，否则换做法；不要为了稠度把味道推到无法使用。
2. **给蒸汽一条出口。** 将底液放入适用、不过满的锅，按配方使用敞口或可让水分蒸发的设置。锅在热源上稳定、把手不朝易碰到处，脸和手不悬在蒸汽上方。不能确认食品或锅具适用时先停。
3. **维持配方支持的加热。** 按来源用可观察的温和沸动或其他要求加热，让水逐渐离开；适时搅动并查看锅底，避免小量酱汁在边缘烧焦。不要用猛烈滚沸代替看守，尤其有糖或容易粘底的材料。
4. **在安全温度下多次检查。** 按配方的体积线、流动、挂勺或覆盖目标食物的能力判断；取少量冷却后才试味。水蒸发会使浓度改变，接近目标时更频繁检查，而不是一口气煮到只剩锅底。需要安全煮熟的配料仍按其食品规则独立完成，稠度不证明熟度。
5. **在够用时停热。** 达到目标稠度且保留预定可用量，就关闭热源并把热锅放稳。需要后续与其他食物结合时，只按可信配方完成。剩余酱汁的冷却、冷藏或丢弃取决于实际成分和当地食品安全指导；浓缩不等于常温保存。

### 成功条件

这份小酱汁按可信做法失去部分水分，达到本次用途可观察的稠度与量，味道仍适用，食材安全状态可确认；或你在可能烧干、味道过浓、设备/来源不可靠时及时停止。

### 常见报错与补救

- **盖紧锅盖等它变浓：** 按这份配方核对是否需要敞口蒸发；水没出口时不要只延长时间。
- **快干了还想再稠一点：** 停热，核对已剩的量与味道；若已经明显过浓，转交成品补救，不靠继续加热修复。
- **底部开始焦糊：** 安全停止加热，不把刮起的焦物当作完成信号；下次减少批量、调整适用锅或按做法更常查看。
- **只盯挂勺，味道已很咸：** 停止收浓；浓度与体积要一起检查，不能假设减水只改变质地。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里只处理普通、无酒精、按可信来源有意收浓的小量酱汁，不教危险热油、罐藏、发酵或不安全食材的补救。底液组成、锅的面积、热源与地区会改变实际时间；无一套全球通用的“煮几分钟”。视力、握力或长时间看守困难时选稳定小锅、可读液面标记、提醒及可信协助；不能安全看守热液就停止。成品量会比开始少，味道也可能更集中，这是机制不是故障。

## English

Use this Skill when you **intentionally start with a liquid of known condition** and follow a trusted alcohol-free method to evaporate water into a thicker ordinary sauce. This is different from starch slurry thickening and from rescuing a sauce that has already failed. Salt, acid, sugar, and other flavours may concentrate as water leaves; “looks sticky” alone is not the target.

### Preparation and inputs

Read the complete method for base liquid, starting amount, intended use and texture, suitable pan, any food-specific cooking that must already happen, and the finish cue. Use edible ingredients with known storage history. This Skill uses no alcohol and cannot concentrate an unsafe base into safety. Have a stable small pan, suitable stirrer, enough light to see the liquid and bottom, and a spoon for cooling a sample. If handling heat or watching for long is difficult, use a smaller batch or trusted help rather than leaving the pan for another attention-heavy job.

### Execution

1. **Mark the target and the amount you can lose.** Write whether this sauce will coat, pour, or dress food, when to stop, and the minimum final amount you need. If the base is already strongly salty, sweet, or sour, confirm the recipe supports reduction; otherwise choose another method. Thickening must not make the flavour unusable.
2. **Give steam an exit.** Put the base in a suitable pan with room, and use an open top or other recipe-supported way for water to leave. The pan must be stable and its handle out of the bump path. Keep hands and face out of the steam. Stop if food or pan suitability is unclear.
3. **Use the supported heat.** Follow the source's gentle simmer or other stated heating cue so water leaves gradually. Stir as appropriate and inspect the bottom; a small sauce can scorch at the edges. Hard boiling is not a substitute for attention, especially with sugar or ingredients that stick.
4. **Check repeatedly at a safe temperature.** Use the recipe's volume mark, flow, spoon coating, or ability to cover the intended food. Cool a small sample before tasting. Concentration changes during evaporation, so inspect more often near the goal rather than cooking until only the bottom is wet. Ingredients requiring safe cooking still need their own food-specific check; thickness does not prove doneness.
5. **Stop while enough remains.** At the intended texture and usable amount, turn off heat and park the hot pan safely. Combine with other food only as a trusted recipe directs. Any surplus follows its actual ingredients and local cooling or storage guidance; reduction is not shelf-stable preservation.

### Success

Part of the water has left under a trusted method; the small sauce has an observable texture and amount suitable for this use, its flavour still works, and food safety conditions are known. Stopping before it burns dry, becomes too concentrated, or exceeds your equipment and source is also a complete result.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The lid is tight while you wait for thickening:** Check whether this method needs uncovered evaporation. With no exit for water, more time alone is not the same process.
- **Almost no sauce remains but you want it thicker:** Stop heating and check volume and flavour. A substantially over-reduced sauce belongs to the finished-food recovery workflow, not more heat.
- **The bottom starts scorching:** Stop heat safely; scraped burned material is not a finish cue. Next time use a smaller batch, suitable pan, or the method's recommended observation frequency.
- **You watch the spoon but the sauce is already salty:** Stop reducing. Check concentration and volume together; less water changes more than texture.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is an ordinary, alcohol-free small sauce deliberately reduced under a trusted source. It does not teach hazardous hot-oil work, canning, fermentation, or rescue of unsafe food. Base ingredients, pan area, heat, and location change timing; there is no universal number of minutes. If seeing, gripping, or watching a hot pan is difficult, use a stable small vessel, readable level mark, reminder, or trusted help. Stop if hot liquid cannot be attended safely. Final quantity shrinks and flavour may strengthen; that is the mechanism, not an error.
