## 中文

当你在别人家的普通来访接近约定结束时间，或主人已明确表示要收尾时，加载这份 Skill。目标是完成**来客一方的离开与简短感谢**，不把主人的边界拖成新话题，也不靠不确定的“下次一定”来证明礼貌。主人如何安排结束属于另一个任务；本篇不处理交通事故、威胁或非自愿情形。

### 准备与输入

确认原定结束时间或主人刚给出的提示、你自己的外套包和其他物品在哪里、主人指示的出口，以及你是否需要不同的离开速度或路线。若还借着主人的物品，问它应该放回哪里；不要自己翻私人房间找东西。之后是否发消息，只看这次是否有实际需要，不把“社交规范”当自动通知系统。

### 执行

1. **接住结束提示。** 主人说需要结束、开始按已约定时间收尾时，回应“好，我收一下东西”。把正在说的一点收成一句，别用“最后还有一件事”连续重启谈话。
2. **收好自己的东西。** 看一下你使用过的座位、桌边和入口，拿回属于自己的物品；食品或借用品按主人实际安排处理。不要因为收东西走进未开放的空间，也不要把主人的物品误当纪念品带走。
3. **按可用路线离开。** 需要主人开门、说明楼道或给更多时间时直接问。行动、视觉或感官条件让出口不方便，就说出你需要的具体协助，不假装一条不能用的路线可用；主人也不必承诺无法提供的接送。
4. **说一段真实的感谢。** “谢谢你今天招待我，聊天很开心”或“谢谢你借我地方把这件事办完”已足够。只感谢确实发生的事；不必评价每道菜或每个摆设，也不要求主人当场确认你是好客人。
5. **后续只做必要的那一条。** 若有借品归还、遗留物确认或双方说好要发的信息，离开后用一条短消息说明动作和时间。没有待办时，一句感谢可选，不要求重复告别。是否再次相约要另行真诚决定，不为了结束这次临时承诺。

### 完成条件

你已按主人可用的出口离开，带好自己的物品或明确约定未完成的交接，并给出符合事实的简短感谢。主人不必再回复，也不必接受下一次邀约。若发现遗留物，完成的是一次清楚联系而非未经允许返回屋内。

### 常见报错与补救

- **告别时又开了新话题：** 记下以后再谈，回到收拾和离开；不要求主人再提供一段完整的接待时间。
- **你觉得必须约下次：** 没有真实时间和意愿就不许诺；一句“今天谢谢你”已经完成感谢。
- **东西忘在屋内：** 先用约定渠道问主人何时方便交接，不自行折返进入私人空间。
- **出口或楼梯不适合你：** 停下问可用替代或具体协助；若没有安全路线，联系你实际能用的支持方式，不硬走。
- **主人说不用帮忙收：** 接受，只处理自己的东西；家务帮忙另按具体许可进行。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这份 Skill 假设一次自愿、普通的短时做客。告别话语、是否拥抱、送到门口还是楼下，都没有统一文化默认值；尊重这次双方表达的身体与空间边界。听觉、语言、记忆或行动需要不同方式时，可用简短文字、指向随身物品、慢一点的路线或实际可用的帮助。交通安排以真实服务和个人条件为准，不由道谢句子保证。

副作用是本次来访干净地结束，未来邀约仍可留到未来。礼貌不要求预支日历。

## English

Load this Skill when an ordinary home visit approaches its agreed end or the host clearly signals that they need to wrap up. This is the **guest-side departure and brief thanks**. Respect the host's boundary without starting new topics or promising “next time for sure” merely to sound polite. How the host sets an end belongs to another task. This does not handle transport accidents, threats, or involuntary situations.

### Preparation and inputs

Know the agreed end or the host's cue, where your coat, bag, and other things are, the exit the host indicates, and whether you need another pace or route. If you borrowed something, ask where to leave it. Do not search private rooms on your own. A later message depends on an actual reason; etiquette does not run an automatic notification service.

### Execution

1. **Accept the closing cue.** When the host says they need to finish or the agreed end arrives, say “Okay, I'll gather my things.” Finish one thought in a sentence rather than restarting the conversation with a series of “one last thing” requests.
2. **Gather your belongings.** Check the seat, table area, and entrance you used. Take your own things; follow the host's actual direction for food or borrowed items. Do not enter a closed room while collecting, or accidentally carry off their property as a souvenir.
3. **Use a workable exit.** Ask if the host needs to open a door or explain the building route. State a specific need if movement, vision, or sensory access makes an exit difficult rather than pretending it works. The host need not promise a ride they cannot provide.
4. **Thank them for something real.** “Thanks for having me; I enjoyed the conversation,” or “Thanks for letting me use the space to finish this.” No need to praise every dish or object or obtain the host's verdict on your guest performance.
5. **Follow up only on one actual need.** If an item must be returned, something was left behind, or you agreed to send information, use one brief message afterward with the action and time. Without a pending task, a later thank-you is optional; repeated goodbyes are not required. Make any future plan honestly and separately, not as a fee for ending today.

### Success

You left by an exit that works, took your belongings or clearly arranged a pending handoff, and gave a short truthful thanks. The host need not reply or accept a future invitation. If you find you left something behind, a clear contact attempt completes this step; re-entering without permission does not.

### Common errors and recovery

- **A goodbye becomes a new topic:** Save it for another time and return to leaving. The host does not owe a fresh full-length visit.
- **You feel obliged to schedule next time:** Without real availability and willingness, do not promise. “Thank you for today” is enough.
- **You forgot an item inside:** Ask on the agreed channel when handoff is convenient rather than going back into private space uninvited.
- **The exit or stairs do not work for you:** Stop and ask for an alternative or specific help. Without a safe route, use support actually available to you rather than forcing the stairs.
- **The host says no help tidying is needed:** Accept and manage only your own belongings. Household help has its own permission step.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This covers an ordinary voluntary short visit. Words of thanks, hugs, and whether someone walks you to the door or street have no universal cultural default. Respect the particular people's bodily and spatial boundaries. Use short text, pointing to your belongings, a slower route, or available help if hearing, language, memory, or mobility requires it. Transport depends on real services and personal conditions, not a sentence of thanks.

The visit ends cleanly and any future invitation can remain in the future. Politeness does not require borrowing time from an unscheduled calendar.
