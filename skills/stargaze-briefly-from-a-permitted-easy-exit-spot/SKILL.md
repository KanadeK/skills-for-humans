## 中文

当你想试一次普通肉眼观星，不想为了“最黑的天空”开到偏远陌生地区时，用这份 Skill 在**正式允许夜间停留、容易离开**的近处看一小段天空。结果是一次有起止与返程的真实观察，能说出自己实际看见的月亮、亮星或天空变化；看不见也如实记录，不声称已完成观测。本篇不教太阳、日食、危险天文器材或野外夜行。

### 准备与输入

从公园/场馆管理方当期说明确认夜间开放时段、入口、停车或公共交通、厕所与照明规则，选你熟悉、平整、远离车流/陡坎/水边的公开观测点；公共土地也可能夜间关闭，别凭白天能进推断夜间可停。最好白天先看清地面和回程，夜里不去无人管理的偏远位置。查当地天气、云量和场地公告，雷雨、危险天气或封闭就改日。可用肉眼观察月亮和明亮目标，不需要买望远镜；**绝不用本篇方法用望远镜、双筒镜或相机直视太阳**。备足够照路的灯、合适衣物和可用联系/返程方式；红光保护夜视只是可选，路面安全优先。

### 执行

1. **写清一个短目标。** 例如“看月亮今天的形状”或“找一组我能肉眼辨出的亮点”，不保证某颗行星、流星雨或银河会出现。设一个现场停留终点与最晚返程时间，夜间不临时追逐更暗地点。
2. **出发前核地点与天况。** 再看管理方夜间开放和天气更新；若光污染或月光亮，接受能看的目标更少，或改日。若最后一段路、停车和出口不确定，就选更熟悉的观测点或不去。
3. **到场先确认脚下。** 在允许范围内选稳定、开阔但不靠车道/边缘的位置，确认回程入口仍可使用；不要关掉所有照明让自己摸黑走。向同伴说明站点和离开时点，不把镜头或灯照向他人。
4. **停下来肉眼看。** 从安全位置看一小段天空，记录月亮是否可见、几处明亮星点的相对位置或云量变化；不知道名称就描述位置，不靠 App 标签把看不见的天体说成亲眼看见。若需要抬头影响平衡，坐在允许的稳固位置。视觉不便可听可信同伴的描述，但把“他人描述”与自己可感知的部分分开记录。
5. **按约定收工。** 到时间、起云、降温不适、场地要求离开或照明/返程变得不可靠，就沿已确认路线离开；雷声或闪电出现时立即终止户外活动并按当地官方指引到适当封闭地点。回到安全地点再写“实际看见____/未看见____；下次____”，不边走边盯屏幕。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你在允许夜间停留的地点完成一次短时肉眼观察，能区分**实际看见、他人说明与尚未看到**，并按预定路线离开。只在地图上标一个暗点、没有确认夜间许可和返程，不算完成。

- **天空多云或目标被建筑遮挡：** 若月亮/亮星仍可安全观察就缩小目标；否则改日，不去更远黑暗角落硬找。
- **公园夜间已关或入口锁住：** 直接退出并改地点/日期，不翻栅栏。
- **地面不平、照路灯不足：** 停止观测并按已知路线离开，亮灯走路优先于保护暗适应。
- **有人想临时开车去偏远处：** 不把同行冲动当新计划；此 Skill 只覆盖近便、易退出地点。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

天体能见度随天气、月相、城市光和视力变化，本文不承诺看到某个对象。场地和交通的夜间规则由所在地管理方决定；天气告警也以当地正式来源为准。可信同伴、可及口述、稳固座位和短时间都可帮助参与；结果需诚实区别感知来源。无望远镜、无摄影、无远途旅行仍能完成一次普通观星。严禁用任何未具备专用安全设备的光学方法看太阳；太阳观测不在本篇。

## English

Use this Skill when you want a simple naked-eye stargazing session without driving to an unfamiliar remote dark-sky area. Observe from a **nearby place officially open at night with an easy exit**. The result is a real short observation with a start and return: say what Moon, bright stars, or sky change you actually saw. If nothing is visible, record that honestly rather than claim a sighting. This does not teach solar or eclipse viewing, hazardous optics, or night wilderness travel.

### Preparation and inputs

Use current park or venue information to confirm night access hours, entrance, parking or transit, restroom, and lighting rules. Pick a familiar public level spot away from traffic, steep drops, and water. Public land can close at night; daytime entry is no night permission. If possible, inspect ground and return by daylight rather than choose a remote unmanaged site after dark. Check local weather, cloud cover, and venue notices; reschedule for storms, hazardous weather, or closure. The Moon and bright objects can be seen with eyes alone, without buying a telescope. **Never use this procedure to point a telescope, binoculars, or camera at the Sun.** Bring light adequate to see the ground, suitable clothing, and a contact and return method. A red light for night vision is optional; safe footing comes first.

### Execution

1. **Set one small target.** “Notice the Moon's shape tonight” or “find a pattern of bright points I can see” is enough. Do not promise a named planet, meteor shower, or Milky Way. Set a stop and latest return time; do not chase a darker place in the night.
2. **Recheck place and sky before leaving.** Read current night opening and weather. Accept fewer targets under city light or a bright Moon, or reschedule. Choose a more familiar place or stay home if the last approach, parking, or exit is unclear.
3. **Check footing on arrival.** Within allowed space, choose steady open ground away from roads and edges and confirm you can still use the return entrance. Do not turn off every light and walk blindly. Tell a companion your position and leaving time. Do not aim lights or cameras at other visitors.
4. **Look with your eyes from a stopped position.** Note whether the Moon is visible, the relative position of a few bright points, or changing cloud cover. If you do not know a name, describe location; an app label does not make an unseen object your sighting. If looking up affects balance, use a permitted stable seat. For limited vision, a trusted person's description can help, but mark it separately from what you sensed yourself.
5. **Finish as agreed.** Leave along the confirmed route at your time, with cloud cover, uncomfortable cold, staff direction, or unreliable lighting or return. For thunder or lightning, end the outdoor activity immediately and follow local official instructions to an appropriate enclosed place. Once safe, write “actually saw ____ / did not see ____; next time ____.” Do not study a screen while walking.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You made a short naked-eye observation from a place open at night, distinguish **your own sight, another person's description, and what remained unseen**, and left by the planned route. Marking a dark spot on a map without confirming night permission and return is not completion.

- **Clouds or buildings obscure the target:** Narrow to a safely visible Moon or bright star if any; otherwise return another day rather than seek a more distant dark corner.
- **The park is closed or gate locked:** Leave and change place or date; do not cross a barrier.
- **Ground is rough or light inadequate:** End the viewing and return by a known route. A bright walking light matters more than preserving dark adaptation.
- **Someone suggests a remote drive on impulse:** Do not turn this into a new night trip. This Skill covers only nearby easy-exit sites.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Visibility depends on weather, Moon phase, city light, and eyesight; no target is guaranteed. Local managers control night access and transport, and local authorities define weather alerts. A trusted companion, accessible description, steady seat, and short duration may help, while you state the source of each observation honestly. No telescope, photography, or long journey is needed. Never look at the Sun with an optical device without specialised certified safety equipment; solar viewing is outside this Skill.
