## 中文

当你一餐后有一轮**普通杯、盘、碗和非尖锐餐具**待手洗，想让它们恢复到可以正常再次盛食物的状态时，用这份 Skill 分批完成去剩食、清洗、漂净和干燥。先洗相对干净的，再处理更油的，避免一盆水把轻污餐具重新洗脏。本文不把普通清洁说成特殊消毒，也不处理刀具、砧板、焦锅或洗碗机装载。

### 准备与输入

先看餐具有没有破裂、掉釉、松动装饰，材质或制造商是否限制浸水/热水/刷具。准备干净水槽或盆、手洗餐具适用洗剂、干净海绵/布/软刷、能接住洗净餐具的干净沥水架或清洁铺垫。按洗剂标签确定用法和量，不拿自动洗碗机洗剂当手洗剂，也不把几种清洁化学品混在一起。把尖刀、砧板、严重焦锅或特殊材质餐具分开留给各自说明；尖锐物不藏在泡泡底下。

若水不可靠、餐具破碎或接触了需要额外处理的特殊污染，先停止普通一轮清洗并按当地食品安全/制造商指导处理。手碰到锋利碎边时别用布把它“擦圆”。

### 执行

1. **先处理看得见的剩食。** 把盘碗里的食物残渣移到适用垃圾/厨余去向，按实际条件处理剩菜；别让大块食物进入洗涤水。明显油脂先按当地排水规则处理，不把整锅油直接冲进下水道。为杯、较轻污餐具、盘碗、油污较重的普通器具排出次序。
2. **配一份可工作的洗涤水。** 按洗剂说明在干净水中配适用量，水温同时服从餐具材质、产品说明和你能安全接触的范围；不以烫手为“洗得净”的证明。一次放少量餐具，保留清洗动作空间。只有一只水槽时留好单独的净水漂洗位置/顺序，不让已洗净餐具掉回脏水。
3. **轻污先洗，逐件看全表面。** 从相对干净的杯和普通餐具开始，再洗盘、碗与普通盛器；对每件清洗内外、杯口、底面、把手与接触食物处，拿出水面看是否还有油膜或食物残留。不要把看不见的刀刃或玻璃碎片混在同一盆里。刷具明显脏了就换清洁面或清理。
4. **油污增加时换水。** 洗涤水明显油腻、浑浊、冷到不适用或洗剂已失效时，排走并重配；不用“既然都泡在水里”推断后面的杯也干净。特别难洗、材质敏感或需按厂家专法处理的东西留到相应 H072/H076/H077 任务，不用硬刷制造划痕。
5. **用干净水漂净并晾干。** 逐件用适用干净水把泡沫与洗剂残留冲掉，杯碗内侧也检查；放在干净沥水位，留间隙使水能流走。可按材料适用方式自然晾干，确需擦干时用干净、未沾生食物或脏水的布并在湿脏后更换。未干或仍有油膜、异味、泡沫的餐具不直接叠进柜里。
6. **结束这一轮。** 清点已洗净可用、还需材料专法或损坏不可用的三类，干燥后把可用餐具归位。把脏水倒掉、清理洗具与沥水区域，让它们按适用方法干燥；水槽的完整清洁归 H075，不要求这一份把厨房全部做完。

### 成功条件

这轮普通餐具没有可见食物或油膜，洗剂被漂去，放在干净位置充分干燥后可正常再用；特殊/损坏件有明确去向。破损、污染或材质说明阻止手洗时停止对应件，不把全批的“完成”伪装成每件都可用。

### 常见报错与补救

- **第一盆水已经全是油，还继续洗杯子：** 排掉重配，重新清洗受到油水影响的轻污件。
- **只洗盘面，杯口/碗底仍黏：** 逐件翻看内外、底部、把手和边缘，再漂净；泡沫遮住的地方不算检查过。
- **洗涤剂冲不净：** 回到干净漂洗水与产品用量，别用更多洗剂解决洗剂残留。
- **沥水架脏或餐具挤叠仍湿：** 先给洗净件干净、有排水空间的位置，充分干燥再归柜。
- **刀具或碎玻璃混入水中：** 停止盲摸，先按相应安全步骤移出和处理尖锐物；普通餐具轮次不能替代刀具/破损处理。
- **产品或餐具标明不能按本法浸水：** 单独按制造商说明清洁，不因旁边盘子可浸就一起泡。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里只处理普通餐后手洗，不提供特殊消毒、疾病或灾后污染处置。陶瓷、普通玻璃与金属餐具也可能各有涂层、金边或手绘限制，实际制造商说明优先。手部抓握或站立不便时，在可及高度分更小批次、用稳定盆和可信协助，不需要把所有餐具堆进同一水槽。分批换水可能增加几次操作，却减少油水重复污染；沥水架会暂时占空间。

## English

Use this Skill when a meal leaves **ordinary cups, plates, bowls, and non-sharp cutlery** to wash by hand and you want them ready for normal food use again. Work through food removal, washing, rinsing, and drying in small groups. Start with lighter soil before grease so one pan of water does not re-soil the cleaner dishes. Normal cleaning is not a special sanitizing claim. Knives, cutting boards, scorched cookware, and dishwasher loading belong elsewhere.

### Preparation and inputs

Check for chips, cracks, loose decoration, and any maker restrictions on immersion, hot water, or scrubbers. Have a clean sink or basin, hand-dishwashing detergent, a clean sponge, cloth, or soft brush, and a clean rack or mat for finished pieces. Follow the detergent label for method and amount. Automatic dishwasher detergent is not a hand-washing substitute; do not mix cleaning chemicals. Set sharp knives, cutting boards, badly scorched pans, and special materials aside for their own instructions. Nothing sharp belongs unseen below suds.

If the water is unreliable, a dish is broken, or an item contacted contamination requiring extra handling, stop the ordinary round for that item and follow local food-safety or maker guidance. Do not try to polish a sharp broken edge smooth with a cloth.

### Execution

1. **Clear visible food first.** Move scraps from plates and bowls to the appropriate food-waste destination and handle actual leftovers according to their conditions. Keep large pieces out of wash water. Handle substantial grease under local drainage rules rather than pouring a pot of oil down the drain. Order cups and lightly soiled cutlery before plates, bowls, and more greasy ordinary serving pieces.
2. **Prepare usable wash water.** Add the product-directed amount of detergent to a clean basin of water. Water temperature must suit the dish material, product guidance, and what you can handle safely; scalding your hand is not proof of cleanliness. Put only a few items in at once so there is room to wash. With one sink, plan a separate clean-water rinse sequence and do not drop washed dishes back into dirty water.
3. **Wash light soil first and inspect every piece.** Work through cups and ordinary cutlery, then plates, bowls, and serving items. Clean inside and outside, rim, bottom, handle, and food-contact areas. Lift each item to look for remaining grease film or food. Keep hidden blades and broken glass out of the basin. Refresh or clean a washing tool when its working face becomes dirty.
4. **Replace greasy water.** Drain and remake wash water if it becomes oily, cloudy, too cool to serve the method, or the detergent has ceased to work. Do not assume a later cup is clean merely because it sat in that water. Very stubborn residues or material-sensitive pieces go to the appropriate cookware or maker-specific task rather than abrasive damage.
5. **Rinse with clean water and dry.** Rinse each item to remove suds and product residue, including inside cups and bowls. Place it with room to drain on a clean rack or mat. Air-dry where the material permits. If wiping is necessary, use a clean cloth that has not touched raw food or dirty water, and change it when wet or soiled. Do not stack wet dishes or ones with grease, odour, or suds straight into a cupboard.
6. **Close this round.** Count items cleaned for reuse, those needing special material care, and damaged items that cannot be used. Put reusable dishes away after adequate drying. Empty dirty water and clean or handle the wash tool and draining area so they can dry as appropriate. The full sink-cleaning task is H075; one dish round does not promise to finish the entire kitchen.

### Success

This round's ordinary dishes have no visible food or grease film, detergent has been rinsed off, and they are thoroughly dry in a clean area before normal reuse. Special and damaged pieces have explicit destinations. Stop an item whose damage, contamination, or maker rule prevents this method rather than calling the entire batch usable.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The first basin is already oily but you keep washing cups:** Drain and remake it; rewash any light-soil pieces affected by greasy water.
- **Only the plate face is washed; rim or bowl base is sticky:** Check inner and outer surfaces, bottoms, handles, and edges piece by piece, then rinse. Suds hiding an area do not count as inspection.
- **Dish soap is hard to rinse away:** Return to clean rinse water and product amount. More detergent is not a fix for detergent residue.
- **The rack is dirty or dishes are stacked wet:** Give finished pieces clean space to drain and dry before cupboard storage.
- **Knives or broken glass enter wash water:** Stop blind searching and remove sharp hazards by the appropriate method. This ordinary round does not replace blade or breakage handling.
- **A dish or product forbids immersion under this method:** Clean that item separately by its maker's instructions rather than soaking it because nearby plates can be soaked.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers normal post-meal hand washing, not special sanitizing, illness-related, or disaster contamination work. Ceramic, ordinary glass, and metal may still have coatings, metallic trim, or paint requiring a maker-specific route. For limited grip or standing, work in smaller batches at an accessible height with a stable basin or trusted help; a full sink is not required. Changing water adds steps but avoids grease moving backward through the load. The drying rack needs temporary space.
