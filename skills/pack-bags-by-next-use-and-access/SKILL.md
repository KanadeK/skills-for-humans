## 中文

你已决定短途或普通旅程**带什么**，但途中有检票、换乘、临时寄存、入住或其他交接，担心要用的物品压在箱底或随大件离手时，用这份 Skill按“下一次何时要用、那个包当时能不能打开”安排取用顺序。目标是每个近期要用的物品都能在该节点及时、安全地拿到，其余物品不妨碍通道或交接。它不替你决定带什么，也不处理潮湿/易碎物的材料包装或航空专属物品限制。

### 准备与输入

写下接下来两三处真实节点，例如出门、车站、车上、换乘、寄存、住宿；每处列可能要出示或拿出的物品及时间。给每个包写明何时仍在你手里、何时会托运/寄存/放远处，以及是否能在车厢或服务台打开。查承运方和寄存方的本次包件与通道规则；外形合格不等于包可以堵门，也不等于任何内件都能托运。敏感证件、钥匙或本人必需随手取用的物品只按本次真实服务许可放在自己能控制的可及处，不把完整清单贴到外侧让别人看。

### 执行

1. **按下一次取用时点分组。** 把“进门/检票/第一段车上马上要用”“下一次换乘或交接才用”“到住处或目的地才用”分别列出。目的地名字不是分组依据；同一城市的车上物和住处物也应分开。
2. **按包能否够到决定位置。** 近期必用且按服务规则应留在身边的物品放进可控制、好开合的小包/外层；下一段才用的东西放在不过深且你能在安全位置打开的层；只在抵达后才用的放深层或允许离手的包。包要交给托运、寄存或后备箱前，再检查没有把下一步必须用的凭证/物品一起交走。
3. **做一次真实取用试验。** 关好包，按下一节点模拟：“现在取出〔物品〕。” 看是否能不卸下所有包、不在车门或楼梯上摊开行李地拿到，并能再放回固定位置。要翻箱倒柜、包太重无法独立够到或每次都找错同样的袋子时，换层位、减轻负担或请可信协助，不以“到时再说”结束检查。
4. **按运营方规则装载。** 在车上或公共空间将包放进该服务允许的行李区，不占过道、门口、别人座位或保留空间。NS 与英国 National Rail 都明确提醒行李不要堵门和过道，但具体件数/尺寸需查实际运营方；不能为了手边方便把大箱当临时地标放在通道上。
5. **每次交接后重置手边层。** 换车、寄存/取回、到住宿点或下一活动前，重看下一节点需用物，确认包的件数、凭证和位置；刚才常用的票据可能不再需要，下一段的地址或钥匙要变得可取。发现包可能离手或被要求临时托运时，按该服务实际规则重新核对哪些物品须保留在身边。

### 成功条件

下一两个节点所需物品有明确包位且能在安全位置迅速拿出、放回；离手包里没有尚需随手取的关键物；其余行李按实际服务放置、不堵公共通道。若实际承运/寄存规则使这个布局不可行，明确停下重排或问服务方，而不是把不能拿到的物品标成“已准备”。

### 常见报错与补救

- **下一段凭证放进已托运/寄存包：** 在交包前把它移到本段允许的可及位置；已经离手则联系负责服务方，不擅闯后台取。
- **同样的几个小袋都像票据袋：** 给自己一个固定位置或可辨触感/文字标识，别把票据分散到三个外袋。
- **车上要找物品只能把箱子摊在过道：** 停止翻找，等安全位置再取；下次把它移进符合运营规则的手边层。
- **计划中途突然加了一次换乘：** 重新排序下一节点，检查新站点/车上的可取用性，不沿用出发前一次装包顺序。
- **包太重或层位够不到：** 减少携带、调整位置或找该服务允许的协助；不能用挡门放箱换便利。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

此 Skill 从已决定要带的普通物品出发，只安排取用时点和行李可达性；航空危险物、液体/电池、海关、医疗物品规则、贵重品保管与具体材质防护需按各自服务和专门流程。无分隔袋可用现有口袋和固定顺序；看不清标签可用触感或可信帮助。坐车时包未必总在手边，所以排一次序之后还要在交接点复核。多花几分钟模拟取用，比在车门口现场考古更省事。

## English

Use this Skill once you have already chosen **what to bring** on a short or ordinary trip, but tickets, transfers, temporary storage, lodging, or another handover might leave a needed item buried or inside a surrendered bag. Arrange by “when is the next use and can that bag be opened then?” Finish with near-term items retrievable at the right node without blocking a path. This does not decide what to pack, material protection for wet/fragile goods, or airline-specific item restrictions.

### Preparation and inputs

Write the next two or three actual nodes—leaving home, station, vehicle, transfer, storage, lodging, for example—and which items each requires and when. For every bag, note when it stays with you, is checked/stored/put away, and whether you may open it on a vehicle or at a counter. Read this carrier or storage service's bag and walkway rules. A fitting bag can still block a door, and a permitted bag does not make every content suitable for hold. Keep sensitive documents, keys, and items you must reach personally in an accessible place under this service's actual rules; do not print their full list on the outside.

### Execution

1. **Group by next-use time.** List “needed at entrance, ticket check, or first vehicle,” “needed at the next transfer or handover,” and “needed only at lodging or destination.” Destination name alone is not a grouping rule; the same city still has on-vehicle and arrival items.
2. **Place by actual bag access.** Put immediate, permitted keep-with-you items in a controllable easy-opening pocket or small bag. Put next-leg things in a layer you can open at a safe place. Put arrival-only things deeper or in a bag allowed to leave your hands. Before a bag is checked, stored, or put in a trunk, confirm it holds nothing needed before you get it back.
3. **Run a real retrieval trial.** Close bags and simulate the next node: “Take out [item] now.” Confirm you can do it without unloading all cases or spreading goods by a vehicle door or stairs, then put it back in a fixed place. If it requires a full excavation, is too heavy to reach, or identical pouches keep confusing you, change layers, reduce load, or seek permitted trusted help. “I'll work it out later” is not a test.
4. **Stow under the operator's actual rule.** On a vehicle or in public space, use the storage area that service permits, not an aisle, doorway, another seat, or reserved space. NS and UK National Rail both tell passengers not to block aisles/doors, while exact size and count come from the real operator. A case at your feet must not become a corridor landmark for everyone else.
5. **Reset the reachable layer at each handover.** Before another vehicle, storage/retrieval, lodging, or the next activity, check what the next node needs, bag count, credential, and location. A ticket used earlier may move back while the next address or key becomes accessible. If a bag may be surrendered or gate-checked unexpectedly, recheck what must remain with you under that service's rules.

### Success

Items for the next one or two nodes have known bag locations and can be taken out and returned at a safe point. No soon-needed critical item is trapped in a surrendered bag; the rest is stowed within the actual service rules without blocking shared paths. If those conditions cannot coexist, re-pack or ask the service instead of labelling the unreachable item “ready.”

### Common errors and recovery

- **Next-leg credential is inside a checked or stored bag:** Move it to an allowed reachable place before handover. After the bag leaves, contact the responsible service; do not enter staff areas to retrieve it yourself.
- **Several small pouches all look like the ticket pouch:** Give it one fixed place or a readable/tactile distinction instead of scattering proof among pockets.
- **An item can only be found by emptying a case into an aisle:** Stop searching until a safe place, then move it to a permitted reachable layer for next time.
- **An extra transfer appears:** Re-sort the next node's needs and access rather than following the packing order from departure.
- **Bag is too heavy or layer unreachable:** Reduce load, reposition, or use permitted help; blocking a door is not an access solution.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This starts from already chosen ordinary travel items and arranges retrieval time and bag access only. Airline dangerous goods, liquids/batteries, customs, medical items, valuable-property custody, and wet/fragile material protection follow their actual service and dedicated processes. Existing pockets and a fixed order can replace organizer pouches; tactile marks or trusted help can replace small labels. Bags do not always stay at hand, so recheck at handovers. A few minutes' retrieval rehearsal beats archaeology at a vehicle door.
