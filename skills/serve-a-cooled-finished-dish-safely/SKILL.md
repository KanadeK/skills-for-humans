## 中文

当一道普通家常菜**已按要求完成烹调，却在计划上桌前凉了**，用这份 Skill 决定现在能否上桌、是否需要按适用规则彻底复热，或应停止。它不把“尝起来不够热”当成唯一判断，也不教提前备餐或把已冷藏剩菜反复复热。凉了多久、怎样放的，比重新摆盘更先要弄清楚。

### 准备与输入

确认菜名与主要成分、做完的时间、凉下来的过程、是否持续合规保温或及时冷却冷藏、是否曾上桌被多人取用、当地对该食物的存放与复热要求。包装或可靠食谱若有特定上桌说明，一起读。准备干净餐具；打算复热时，还需适用容器、热源和按当地规则检查食品中心的方法。

**无法重建时间温度史、存在生食交叉污染或已经超出适用安全条件时停止食用。** 再次加热不能让保存史不明的食物变安全。婴幼儿、医疗饮食、罐藏与发酵食品不靠本篇作决定。

### 执行

1. **先判现在是否仍可安全处理。** 依据实际记录和所在地权威指导，分出“可立即上桌”“可按规定复热后上桌”“不能再上桌”三种结果。只看表面温热、闻起来正常或尝一口，都不能代替这一步。若食物原本应冷藏且已按规则冷藏，后续按剩菜/复热流程处理，不把它伪装成刚做好的菜。
2. **决定这道菜需要什么上桌状态。** 核对食谱、包装和食物本身：本来可在合规条件下冷食的，按其要求上桌；需要热食的，才考虑复热。别因为想保住脆皮、酱汁或摆盘，就把需要彻底复热的食物只暖一下表面。
3. **只处理这次实际要吃的量。** 把其余部分继续放在已核实的安全保温或冷藏条件下；没有这些条件就不为下一轮客人预留台面上的一大盘。需要复热时，按当地食物安全要求与这道菜适用方法加热到**整体**达标，必要时分小份、搅动或转向，并检查可能较冷的中心。不能核实整体结果就延后上桌或放弃；保温设备是否能复热，要看设备说明。
4. **重新安排上桌，而非重煮所有东西。** 核实状态后，把可直接吃的部分先摆好；需要热的部分完成复热和检查后再装盘。热、冷组件若配方允许分开摆放，就到上桌前再组合。若一道菜赶不上，说明会晚些上或诚实更换配菜；同桌的人不需要从冷掉的口感推理后台发生了什么。
5. **按实际结果结束。** 现在就上桌，按食物要求安全保温或冷藏剩余部分；需要留下的食物走既有熟度/剩菜流程。不要为了维持外观让易腐菜继续在室温排队，也不要反复冷却和复热同一份来追求刚出锅口感。

### 成功条件与常见报错

你知道这道菜为何仍可吃、以什么状态吃、哪一份已完成适用的复热或上桌条件；端出的份量有人立即食用，其余部分有安全去向。若证据不足而停止上桌，也是完整结果。

- **“看着只是凉了一点”：** 回到实际时间和存放方式。口感不能验证安全窗口。
- **表面热、中心仍冷：** 不上桌；按具体食物方法继续均匀复热并复查，无法做到就停止。
- **只有暖菜设备，没有可靠复热方式：** 查设备说明；不能达到适用复热要求时，改上另一道可安全提供的菜。
- **上桌后又想把同一份收回去热：** 先核对取用和温度史及当地规则；无法确认时弃置，不用频繁冷热切换修口感。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

本篇面向普通家用菜，不给全球统一室温小时数、复热温度或设备按钮。不同地区、成分与烹饪方式要求不同，以所在地权威、包装和设备说明为准。看不清记录或温度读数时，用可读计时/温度工具或可信现场协助；手部或移动能力不足时用小份、稳定容器，热锅不可安全处理就停止。改变上桌顺序可能让配菜先出场；这是可解释的菜单变化，不是食品安全豁免。

## English

Use this Skill when an ordinary home dish has **finished its required cooking but cooled before its planned serving**. Decide whether to serve it now, reheat it thoroughly under applicable rules, or stop. “It no longer feels hot enough” is not the only check. This is not a plan for advance meal preparation or repeated reheating of refrigerated leftovers. First find out how long and how the food cooled.

### Preparation and inputs

Identify the dish and main ingredients, when cooking finished, how it cooled, whether it stayed under appropriate hot holding or was promptly cooled and refrigerated, whether diners have already served from it, and your local holding and reheating guidance for this food. Read any serving directions on the pack or reliable recipe. Have clean serving ware. For reheating, you also need a suitable container and heat source plus a way to check the centre against applicable local rules.

**Stop if the time and temperature history cannot be reconstructed, raw food contaminated it, or applicable safe conditions were exceeded.** Reheating cannot make food with an unknown holding history safe. Infant food, medical diets, canning, and fermentation need their own guidance.

### Execution

1. **Decide whether the food can still be handled safely.** Use the actual record and your local authority's guidance to choose “serve now,” “serve after compliant reheating,” or “do not serve.” Surface warmth, a normal smell, or a taste cannot replace this check. If it was deliberately chilled under the applicable rules, use the leftovers and reheating workflow rather than treating it as just-cooked food.
2. **Choose the required serving state.** Check the recipe, pack, and food. A dish meant to be eaten cold may be served that way only under its safe handling conditions. For a dish that requires heat, consider reheating. Do not preserve crust, sauce, or plating by warming only the surface when thorough reheating is required.
3. **Handle only what people will eat this time.** Keep the rest in verified safe hot holding or refrigeration; without those conditions, do not leave a large platter out for the next round. If reheating, follow the food-specific method and local safety rule until the **whole portion** meets it. Divide, stir, or turn as appropriate and check likely cold centres. If you cannot verify the result, delay or cancel serving. Check the appliance instructions before using a warmer as a reheater.
4. **Resequence service instead of recooking everything.** Once safety is established, prepare any components ready to eat. Plate components requiring heat after reheating and checking. If the recipe allows hot and cold parts to stay separate, combine them just before serving. Tell diners if a dish will arrive later or if you must replace a side. They need not infer the kitchen timeline from its temperature.
5. **Finish by the actual outcome.** Serve now, and use compliant hot holding or prompt cooling and refrigeration for any retained portion. Use the existing doneness and leftovers workflow for food kept later. Do not queue perishables at room temperature to preserve presentation or repeatedly cool and reheat the same portion to chase a just-cooked texture.

### Success and common errors

You know why this dish is still safe to eat, its required serving state, and which portion completed the applicable reheating or serving checks. People can eat the served amount now; the rest has a safe destination. Stopping service when evidence is missing is a complete result.

- **“It looks only a little cool”:** Return to the actual times and holding method. Texture does not verify a safe window.
- **The surface is hot but the centre is cool:** Do not serve. Reheat evenly and recheck using this food's method; stop if that cannot be done.
- **You have a warmer but no reliable reheating method:** Read its instructions. If the applicable reheat endpoint cannot be reached, offer another dish that can be served safely.
- **You want to heat a portion again after it sat on the table:** Check exposure and handling history against local rules first. If you cannot establish them, discard it rather than cycling its temperature to fix texture.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is ordinary household food guidance, not one worldwide room-temperature limit, reheat temperature, or device setting. Rules vary with location, ingredient, and method; follow the local authority, pack, and appliance. If records or a temperature display are hard to read, use accessible timers or thermometers or trusted help present. For limited grip or mobility, use small portions and stable containers; stop if hot food cannot be handled safely. A changed serving order may put a side dish first. That is an explainable menu change, not a food-safety exemption.
