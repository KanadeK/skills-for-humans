## 中文

当你已经找到一件普通商品，但包装和货架牌没有说清**一项会改变购买决定的事实**时，加载这份 Skill。例如，你想知道一把清洁刷的刷毛是什么材料。你的任务是拿到对应这件商品的可核对信息，或明确记下“现在不知道”；店员不必为了让任务完成而给出答案。

这里不决定买多少、怎样比价、尺寸是否适配、标签上已写明的限制，或买后的投诉与退换。食品过敏、健康功效、危险化学品等关键问题若无法从适用的正式资料确认，就暂停购买，不让口头猜测承担安全责任。

### 准备与输入

先确认品牌、品名、型号或款式、规格及货架位置，把你缺的事实写成一句能回答的问题，例如“这件蓝色款的刷毛材质是什么？”如果同一排有多个版本，先核对手里的商品与货架牌是否相配。准备记下答案和来源；不需要拍店员或记录私人资料。

### 执行

1. **先找现成资料。** 看商品包装、同一型号的展示卡或制造商说明。只读与这项疑问有关的部分；二维码打不开时，不假装已经读过。未经店方同意，不为找资料自行拆开密封包装。
2. **指向确切商品发问。** 对店员说：“你好，我在考虑这件〔品名/型号〕。标签没写清〔具体事实〕，请问能在哪里确认？”把商品或货架牌指给对方看，而不是让对方猜“那个蓝的”。
3. **把答案接回来源。** 如果得到回答，问它来自这件商品的包装、产品页、说明书，还是店员的经验。核对来源上的型号、款式及条件。口头印象可以是线索，不能自动替代另一型号的书面资料。
4. **允许诚实的“不知道”。** 店员不知道时，可礼貌询问是否能查到制造商资料或另一个有权查看资料的岗位。没有就停在这里，不用把询问升级成争执。
5. **做一次有依据的决定。** 记下“已确认的事实＋来源”或“仍未知的事实”。只有这项信息对你不重要时，才自行决定是否继续考虑；它若是购买前提，就暂缓、找更清楚的商品或日后向制造商查询。

### 完成与停止

你能说出确切商品、原问题、所得事实及其来源；或者清楚说出谁也没有确认它，并停止把猜测写成答案。完成不要求购买，也不要求店员同意你的选择。

### 常见报错与补救

- **答的是旁边款式：** 重新指向商品和型号；仍对不上就把答案标为未确认。
- **只听到“应该是”：** 请求看对应资料；没有资料时保留未知状态。
- **问题越问越多：** 先处理会改变本次决定的那一项。其他问题另记，不让一次购物变成无上限访谈。
- **资料只在二维码里或字太小：** 请求纸质说明、店内可读页面或请对方逐项读出；关键文字可请可信同伴帮忙核对。若只能听到转述，仍无法核对关键型号或措辞，就保留“未确认”。无法使用所需 App 时，改问其他渠道或暂停购买。
- **被打断后想不起问到哪：** 保留商品名、原问题与已看到的来源；回来时先核对型号，再从未确认的那一步继续。

### 假设、替代与副作用

这里假设商店允许一般商品信息询问，且你能用说话、文字卡片或辅助沟通表达问题。各店可提供的资料和沟通渠道不同；听说、阅读、站立或使用手机不方便时，改用写好的问题、店内文字渠道、座位或可信同伴。记录来源比记住店员的语气更可靠，副作用是购物可能慢几分钟。

## English

Load this Skill when you have found an ordinary item but its package and shelf card leave **one fact that would change your decision** unclear. For example, you may need to know what material a cleaning brush's bristles are made of. Your result is a checkable answer for this exact item, or an honest record that the answer is still unknown. The staff member does not have to produce an answer for you to finish.

This does not decide quantity, price comparisons, physical fit, restrictions already stated on a label, or complaints and returns after purchase. If an unresolved question affects food allergies, health claims, or hazardous products, pause the purchase until suitable official information is available. A guess from a conversation cannot carry that risk.

### Preparation and inputs

Identify the brand, product name, model or variant, size, and shelf position. Put the missing fact into one answerable sentence, such as “What are the bristles of this blue model made of?” If several versions share a shelf, match the item in your hand to its card first. Have a way to note the answer and its source. You do not need to photograph staff or collect personal details.

### Execution

1. **Look for an existing answer.** Check the package, a display card for the same model, or a manufacturer document. Read the part relevant to your question; an unopened QR page is not a source you have checked. Do not open a sealed package to search for details without the shop's permission.
2. **Point to the exact item.** Ask, “Hi, I'm considering this [name/model]. The label doesn't explain [specific fact]. Where can I confirm it?” Show the item or shelf card instead of asking someone to guess which blue thing you mean.
3. **Tie the answer to a source.** Ask whether the answer came from this item's package, product page, manual, or the staff member's experience. Match model, variant, and any conditions. A spoken impression is a lead, not a written specification for another model.
4. **Accept an honest unknown.** If staff do not know, you can politely ask whether a manufacturer document or another staff role is available. If neither is, stop rather than turning a question into a dispute.
5. **Make a grounded decision.** Record “confirmed fact + source” or “fact still unknown.” Continue considering the purchase only if that missing fact does not matter to you. If it is a buying condition, defer, choose a better documented item, or contact the manufacturer later.

### Success and stop

You can name the exact item, original question, answer, and source; or you can say plainly that nobody confirmed it and stop treating a guess as an answer. Completion does not require a purchase or the staff member's agreement.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The answer describes the adjacent variant:** Point to the item and model again. If they still do not match, mark the answer unconfirmed.
- **You only hear “probably”:** Ask for the matching document. Keep the fact unknown if none is available.
- **One question becomes many:** Resolve the one that changes today's decision first. Note the rest separately; a shop visit needs an endpoint.
- **The answer is locked in a QR page or tiny print:** Ask for a paper sheet, an accessible store page, or someone to read the relevant text aloud. A trusted companion can check critical wording. If you only hear a paraphrase and cannot confirm the crucial model or wording, keep it unconfirmed. If you cannot use the required app, ask for another route or defer the purchase.
- **You are interrupted midway:** Keep the product name, original question, and source already seen. On return, match the model again and resume at the unconfirmed step.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This assumes an ordinary store inquiry and a question you can express by speech, a written card, or assisted communication. Stores differ in information and access routes. If hearing, speech, reading, standing, or phone use is difficult, use a prepared written question, a store text channel, a seat, or a trusted companion. Note the source rather than the confidence in someone's voice. The side effect may be a few extra minutes in the shop.
