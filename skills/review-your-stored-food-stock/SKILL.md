## 中文

当食品在冰箱、冷冻室或储柜里放了一段时间，你想在它被遗忘或重复购买前做一次**有边界的复查**时，加载这份 Skill。目标是给现有食品逐项找出“可继续按要求存放、需要先安排使用、位置不符、待核实或停止保留”的状态；不据此自动生成菜单或购物订单。

### 准备与输入

一次只选一个你能在现实时间内检查的储存区，拿纸、手机或语音记录。确认设备是否正常、可以看到的包装说明、原购买或开封标记，以及上次确实核对过哪里。不知道的日期或来源写未知。高处、深柜和小字若不可及，可用可达层先做、拍照放大或请可信的人协助；不要为了盘点把需冷食品长时间堆在室温桌上。

### 执行

1. **划定这轮范围。** 写下“冰箱一层”“冷冻抽屉”或“一格储柜”及检查日期。选择下一次能重复的时间点，但不宣布所有食品都适用同一个复查周期；标签要求与家中使用速度会改变紧迫性。
2. **按实物建立短清单。** 对每件食品记录品名、确实还有多少、位置、未开封/已开封状态、包装上可读的保存说明和日期、已知购买或开封时间。相同食品分开放的，也各记一次；脑中以为有的不能算库存。
3. **先筛需要立即注意的条件。** 看要求冷藏/冷冻的食品是否处在可靠设备中，包装是否渗漏或破损，生食会不会接触即食食品，以及食品是否靠近清洁用品。设备故障、冷食历史不明或明显异常时，停止把该项算作可用，按食品和当地指导处理，不等盘点结束再决定。
4. **给每项一个有限状态。** 已核实条件且仍适用的留在“继续存放”；同类多份、确实需要优先安排使用的列“待先用”，但日期词义先按具体标签与所在地规则确认；位置/容器与说明不符的列“需改放”；身份、开封日或温度史无法证实的列“待核实、不可直接使用”；明确不应保留的列“停止保留”。不能用“闻着还行”把未知项移回可用栏。
5. **先关闭风险，再处理便利。** 让需要低温的食品尽快回到符合标签的条件；能按现有信息纠正位置的立即纠正。未知项隔开并交给保存信息核实任务，不在盘点表里给它补日期。只有确认仍可用的重复食品，才把较需先用的放到可见位置；详细日期排序和容器匹配另行处理。发现需丢弃的食品按当地指导处理，不用“减浪费”覆盖安全。
6. **留下一张可接手的结果。** 记录本轮覆盖了哪里、尚未检查哪里、待先用的已确认食品、重复份数、异常与待核实项的负责人或下一步。若接下来要购物，只把核实过的库存事实交给采购清单，别在这里替别人决定买什么。

### 完成条件

本轮选定区域内可见食品各有一条状态，安全相关异常已被立即处理或隔离；你知道哪件确实重复、哪件已核实可先用，以及哪些区域尚未检查。复查完成不要求清空冰箱，更不要求吃掉一件身份不明的东西。

### 常见报错与补救

- **翻出无名容器或开封日不明的食品：** 标为待核实而不是写今天；找原标签与真实处理记录，关键条件查不回时保守停止。
- **同一食品有好几包：** 分别记录包装和状态；不要简单按“旧的先吃”，先确认每包的保存情况和当地日期含义。
- **冰箱塞满或温度不可靠：** 先处理设备和空间问题，不继续把新旧食品挤进去；当前门能关上也不能证明过去一直够冷。
- **复查时间不够：** 记下已完成的区域和未查区域，先把需冷食品放回符合条件的位置；下次从未查处继续，不声称全家库存已核对。
- **高处或小字看不到：** 改用可达区域、放大/语音读数或可信协助；未看见的食品保留“未核对”标记，不靠猜测补表格。

### 假设、替代与副作用

这份 Skill 适用于普通家庭食品，不做医疗饮食、婴幼儿食品、过敏处置、罐藏或发酵。不同地区对日期字样的含义不同，设备和商品说明也不同；以实际包装和当地权威为准。它不规定通用复查频率或所有食品的保存期限。副作用是一张清晰的库存状态表和可能要处理的食物；删掉真实不安全项不算盘点失败。

## English

Load this Skill when food has been sitting in the fridge, freezer, or cupboard and you want a **bounded periodic check** before it is forgotten or bought again. Give each stored item a status: continuing under verified conditions, eligible to use sooner, placed incorrectly, unverified, or no longer kept. This is not an automatic meal plan or shopping order.

### Preparation and inputs

Choose one storage area you can inspect in the time available, with paper, a phone, or an audio note. Check whether its equipment works, what labels you can read, any real purchase or opening marks, and the area you last actually checked. Write “unknown” for missing dates or identity. For high shelves, deep cupboards, or small print, start with reachable space, enlarge photos, or seek trusted help. Do not leave cold food spread across a room-temperature table just to finish an inventory.

### Execution

1. **Bound the check.** Write “one fridge shelf,” “one freezer drawer,” or “one cupboard section” and today's check date. Pick another time you can revisit it, without declaring one review interval correct for every food. Labels and the household's real use change urgency.
2. **List what is physically there.** For each item, record its name, amount actually left, location, sealed or opened state, readable storage directions and date, and any known purchase or opening time. List separate packs of the same food separately. Something remembered but not seen is not confirmed stock.
3. **Screen conditions that need attention now.** Check whether food labelled for refrigeration or freezing is in reliable equipment, whether packaging leaks or is damaged, whether raw food can touch ready-to-eat food, and whether food sits beside cleaners. If equipment has failed, cold history is unknown, or an item is clearly abnormal, stop treating it as available and follow local guidance for that food now; do not wait until the whole audit is finished.
4. **Give each item one limited state.** Keep items whose conditions are verified in “continue storing.” Put confirmed duplicates and food eligible for earlier use in “use sooner,” after interpreting its date under the product and local rules. Mark a label-incompatible location or container “move/repair placement.” Mark missing identity, opening date, or temperature history “unverified—do not use directly.” Mark food that clearly cannot be kept “stop keeping.” A normal smell cannot move an unknown item back into usable stock.
5. **Close safety issues before convenience.** Return foods needing cold to conditions that meet their labels promptly. Correct a location when current information supports it. Separate unknowns and send them to the food-history check rather than writing an invented date. Only after verifying duplicate packs should you put the one eligible for earlier use where it can be seen. Interpret dates and choose containers in their own workflows. Handle discards under local guidance; reducing waste cannot override safety.
6. **Leave a usable result.** Record the area checked, areas not yet checked, verified use-sooner items, duplicate quantities, and an owner or next step for each exception or unknown. For later shopping, pass only confirmed stock facts to the purchase list; do not decide here what someone else should buy.

### Success

Every visible item in the selected area has a status. Safety-related exceptions have been addressed or isolated promptly. You know which food is truly duplicated, what has been verified for earlier use, and which areas remain unchecked. Finishing the check does not require emptying a fridge or eating unidentified food.

### Common errors and recovery

- **An unnamed container or unknown opening date appears:** Mark it unverified rather than giving it today's date. Find the original label and real handling record; stop conservatively if a critical condition cannot be recovered.
- **There are several packs of one food:** Record each package and its condition. Do not simply eat the oldest first before checking each pack's history and the local meaning of its date.
- **The fridge is overfull or its temperature unreliable:** Address equipment and space first, rather than forcing more food inside. A door that closes now does not establish that everything stayed cold before.
- **You run out of review time:** Note completed and unchecked areas, return cold food to required conditions, and resume with the unchecked area later. Do not claim all household stock was inspected.
- **High shelves or small print are inaccessible:** Use reachable areas, enlargement, audio reading, or trusted help. Leave unseen food marked “unchecked” instead of completing the table by guesswork.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary household food, not medical diets, infant food, allergy handling, canning, or fermentation. Date words, appliances, and package directions vary by place and product; follow the actual label and local authority. There is no universal review frequency or safe storage period here. The side effect is a readable stock status list and perhaps some food to handle. Removing something genuinely unsafe is not an audit failure.
