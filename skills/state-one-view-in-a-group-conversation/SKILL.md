## 中文

你已经在一个普通、允许发言的小组谈话中，想就**当前话题**表达自己的看法，但担心说太散、抢话或把看法说成事实时，用这份 Skill。目标是用有限的话轮清楚说出一个观点和依据，再留出别人回应的空间。它不保证别人同意，更不负责赢下普通分歧；若关键任务已变成与某个观点不同，H196 有专门的分歧表达边界。

### 准备与输入

先听清当前的问题：大家是在交换经历、决定一个普通安排，还是自由聊天？用一句话写出你真正想补充的观点，并选一个相关理由、例子或可核对事实。分开“我亲眼看到”“我听说”“我倾向于”；没有把握的消息不要装成确定事实。观察主持人、举手或轮流的实际规则；你已经获准在场不等于随时能打断下一位。

### 执行

1. **挑与当前话题相接的切入点。** 等别人说完一个意思，或按主持/举手规则取得话轮。先用短句标连接：“接着刚才的时间安排，我有一个看法。” 若这个点只是重复别人刚说过的，可以简短支持或不说，不必为占有话轮重讲一遍。
2. **把观点和依据分开讲。** 先说“我倾向于〔选择〕”，再说“因为〔一个具体理由或例子〕”。若事实尚未核实，说“我记得可能是……，需要再查”，别把推断包装为群体已确认的信息。若你只想分享经历，标明是自己的经历，不要求别人都一样。
3. **收在一个可回应的位置。** 发言到对方能理解你主张和依据即可，结束时停下或说“这是我的想法，大家怎么看？” 不通过连续补充、重复同一句或提高音量占满后续话轮。给别人不同意见或补充的空间。
4. **按反馈修正表达，不抢结论。** 若对方没听清，重复或换简短说法；若被理解成绝对结论，可说明你的范围和不确定处。有人提出不同依据时先确认你听懂，再决定要不要回应；正常分歧不需要立刻变辩论。若多人同时讲话，按现场规则请求一个轮次，而不是压过别人。
5. **留不下话轮时换可用路径。** 时间已经到下一项、你还没想好或发言方式对你不可用时，可以把观点写给主持人、用允许的聊天区，或留到下一轮；不要为了赶上这句在他人中途强插。无法发言不等于同意现有结论，必要时明确标“我还有一项未表达的意见”。

### 成功条件

你的一项相关观点及其真实依据已在可用的话轮里清楚表达，事实、记忆与个人判断的界限明确，发言已结束，其他人有机会回应。别人可能不同意、忽略或补充；这些反应不否定你已完成表达，也不能被你写成群体已采纳。

### 常见报错与补救

- **发言绕了三件事，听众不知主张：** 停下来选当前话题下的一点，用“我倾向于……，因为……”重说。
- **未经核对的消息说成“事实就是”：** 更正来源和不确定度，提醒群体这项尚待核实。
- **别人刚开始回应，你继续加长解释：** 停下让出话轮；需要补充可在下一轮说。
- **音量或语速导致别人没听清：** 用该场合可用的文字、麦克风或简短重述，不把所有问题归咎于听众。
- **有人不同意，话题变成谁压过谁：** 回到观点和理由，先听对方；若需要专门表达分歧，转入 H196 的流程。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

适用于普通自愿群体讨论，不处理危机、正式表决程序、工作人事决定或操控性说服。文化与场合的发言顺序不同；明确问主持人比推断他人的目光可靠。听力、语音或行动有障碍时可使用该场合允许的文字、麦克风、辅助表达或提前交给主持人的短句。观点被认真听见不等于被采纳；一次有边界的发言比整场无休止的自我辩护更容易让群体看清你的意思。

## English

Use this Skill when you are already in an ordinary group conversation that permits contributions and want to state **one view on the current topic** without rambling, interrupting, or presenting an opinion as fact. Finish with one clear point and reason, then leave room for others. Agreement is not guaranteed. If the main job becomes expressing disagreement with a specific view, H196 covers that boundary.

### Preparation and inputs

Listen for the actual question: are people sharing experiences, deciding an ordinary arrangement, or chatting freely? Put the point you want to add into one sentence and choose one relevant reason, example, or checkable fact. Separate “I saw,” “I heard,” and “I prefer.” Do not turn an uncertain report into a certain fact. Notice the real facilitator, raised-hand, or turn-taking rule. Being allowed in the group does not grant the right to interrupt any speaker.

### Execution

1. **Connect to the topic at an available turn.** Wait until someone finishes a thought, or use the facilitator's or raised-hand method. Begin briefly: “Following the time option you just mentioned, I have one view.” If the point only repeats someone else's, support it briefly or let it stand instead of taking a full turn to restate it.
2. **Separate view and support.** Say “I lean toward [option],” then “because [one concrete reason or example].” If a fact is unverified, say “I recall it might be..., and we need to check,” not “the group has confirmed it.” Label a personal experience as yours rather than everyone's.
3. **Stop where others can answer.** Once people can understand your point and support, pause or say “That's my view; what do others think?” Do not fill the next turn with repeated explanations or louder restatements. Leave room for additions and disagreement.
4. **Repair wording from feedback without seizing the decision.** Repeat or simplify if not heard. If your view sounds absolute when it is limited, name the limit and uncertainty. If someone gives different evidence, check you understood before deciding whether to reply. Ordinary difference need not become an instant debate. If several people speak at once, request a turn under the setting's rule rather than talking over them.
5. **Use another permitted route when there is no turn.** If the group moves on, you are not ready, or speech is inaccessible, send a short written point to the facilitator, use an allowed chat, or wait for another round. Do not interrupt midway just to catch the moment. Not speaking does not automatically mean you agree; if necessary, state that you still have one unexpressed point.

### Success

One relevant view and its real support were clear in a usable turn. Facts, memory, and personal judgment were distinguished, your contribution ended, and others had room to respond. They may disagree, ignore it, or add to it; that does not undo your completed expression or prove the group adopted your view.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The turn wanders across three topics and the point disappears:** Stop and restate one point under the current topic: “I lean toward... because....”
- **An unchecked rumor becomes “the fact is”:** Correct its source and uncertainty, and mark it for verification.
- **Someone starts answering and you extend your explanation:** Stop and give up the floor. Add detail in a later turn if useful.
- **Volume or pace makes your point hard to hear:** Use an available written channel, microphone, or short restatement rather than blaming listeners for every barrier.
- **A difference turns into a contest of who talks over whom:** Return to the point and reason, listen, and use the separate disagreement flow if needed.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary voluntary group talk, not crises, formal voting procedure, employment decisions, or manipulative persuasion. Turn-taking norms differ by culture and setting; asking the facilitator is more reliable than interpreting a glance. For hearing, speech, or mobility barriers, use permitted text, a microphone, assisted expression, or a short note to the facilitator. Being heard does not mean being adopted. One bounded contribution often makes your meaning clearer than continuous self-defense.
