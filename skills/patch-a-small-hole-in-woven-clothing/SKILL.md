## 中文

当你的一件**普通、非防护、非承重**梭织衣物在布面上出现小洞，而周围一圈布料仍结实、能够承针时，用这份 Skill 选一块相容布片支撑缺失的面料，再检查普通穿用是否可行。洞在布片中间，与“接缝线断了但布边还在”不同：把洞两边硬拉合只会缩小衣物或继续撕裂。若周边已磨薄成大片、洞在高应力位置或面料有特殊涂层，停下交专业处理。

### 准备与输入

先脱下衣物，确认洞的位置、大小、周围布料是否会轻拉就继续裂、衣物是否有弹性/功能涂层，以及背面能否平放一块补片。找与原布厚度和活动性相近、已清洁的布片，尺寸须越过破洞和松散边缘，落在结实原布上；选择合适手针、线、剪刀和能稳定操作的桌面。确认不会把前后两层裤管或里布一起缝住。重要衣物、重复磨损处或材料判断不清时，请缝纫专业人员评估。

不处理防护服、雨衣密封层、承重带、鞋子、弹力运动衣、婴幼儿可接触的小配件或大面积破损；小补片不能验证这些功能。

### 执行

1. **先判断能否承针。** 把洞周围轻轻摊平，观察是否只是局部缺少几根布纱，还是整片都薄、脆、继续裂。后者先停；补片缝在脆布上会把应力移到新针孔。若只是开线而两片布边完整，改走接缝补缝任务。
2. **准备能覆盖结实原布的补片。** 把清洁布片置于破洞内侧或外侧，依穿着接触和外观选择，确保边缘在洞外的结实布面上。只剪去完全游离、会碍事的线头，不挖掉尚结实的布来做一个整齐的大洞；补片如会磨皮肤、挡住口袋或妨碍弯曲，换更合适的布或停止。
3. **先固定位置，避免错缝。** 把衣物平放、让补片与衣物都自然舒展，用夹子或可清点的少量珠针定位。用手检查背面没有别的衣料被夹住，尤其裤腿和袖管不能缝成一片。若标记位置，先确认所用标记可从该面料去除。
4. **把补片边缘缝到结实布上。** 选针能过原布和补片而不撑大洞孔的线。沿补片边缘做短而均匀的手缝，针脚进入洞外的结实原布，转角处保持补片平整；松散洞边可用适合面料的少量包边针脚约束，别把线勒成一个紧皱圈。起终点在线的反面收牢，不用未经确认的熨烫胶或溶剂来代替连接。
5. **检验穿用边界。** 取掉并清点全部夹子与珠针，平铺看补片没有翘边或牵皱；轻拉周围布料，确认针脚不继续撕原布。安全试穿做普通动作，检查是否磨皮肤、限制伸展或突然出现新裂。若承针布料开始松散或补片妨碍功能，停止穿用并交专业维修或换衣物。

### 成功条件

补片跨过小洞、固定在结实原布上，松散边缘不继续扩开，普通低应力穿用时平整且不刺肤，没有遗留针或珠针。若周围布料不结实、位置承高负荷或材料有功能要求，明确停止家修也是完成判断。

### 常见报错与补救

- **把洞两边直接拉拢，衣服变紧：** 停止抽紧；缺失的布需要支撑补片，不能靠拉力凭空出现。
- **补片太小，只缝在发毛边上：** 换能覆盖松散区并落在结实布面的布片；继续在弱边穿孔不会加固。
- **补片很厚，磨皮肤或挡活动：** 拆下换相容布或交专业处理；外面看不见不等于穿着可用。
- **裤腿、袖管或里布被缝在一起：** 在继续之前检查背面；已错缝则小心拆错针，重新展开并固定。
- **补后新针孔旁又裂：** 立即停止家修与正常穿用；周边布料可能已失去承载能力，不能无限加大针脚。
- **找不到针或珠针：** 暂停穿用并清点衣物与工作台，锐物未确认移除前不交给别人。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这是一处小、低应力、周边仍结实的**普通梭织布面洞**；针织循环、皮革、功能涂层、结构复杂或反复磨损位置有不同修复机制。布片和线是否相容需根据手里衣物的厚度、摩擦和洗护条件判断，没有通用尺寸或耐久承诺。看不清洞边时用放大光线或可信帮助；手部力量不足时使用顶针与稳定桌面。可见补片和增加厚度是实际代价，穿着舒适与不继续撕裂比“隐形”重要。

## English

Use this Skill for a small hole in the **woven cloth** of an ordinary, non-protective, non-load-bearing garment when a sound ring of fabric around it can hold stitches. Add a compatible supporting patch, then check whether ordinary wear works. A hole in the cloth differs from failed seam thread with two intact edges: pulling a gap shut can shrink the garment or extend the tear. Stop for professional help if a broad area is thin, the spot is highly stressed, or the material has a functional coating.

### Preparation and inputs

Take the garment off. Identify the hole's position and size, whether nearby cloth keeps tearing under a light pull, whether the item stretches or has a special coating, and whether a patch can lie flat behind it. Find a clean patch fabric with compatible weight and movement, large enough to extend beyond the hole and loose edge into sound original cloth. Have a hand needle, thread, scissors, and a stable table. Make sure a trouser leg, sleeve, or lining will not be sewn shut. Ask a sewing professional for a valuable garment, repeatedly worn area, or unclear material.

Do not use this process for protective clothing, sealed rainwear, load-bearing straps, shoes, stretch sportswear, infant-accessible small pieces, or widespread damage. A small patch cannot certify those functions.

### Execution

1. **Check whether the cloth can hold stitches.** Spread the area gently. Is this a local loss of a few woven threads, or is a whole patch of fabric thin, brittle, and spreading? Stop for the latter; stitching into weak cloth moves stress to new needle holes. If only seam thread opened while both fabric edges remain sound, use the separate seam-repair task.
2. **Prepare a patch that reaches sound cloth.** Place a clean patch inside or outside the hole according to skin contact and appearance. Its edges must reach sound fabric beyond the damaged zone. Trim only completely loose thread ends that obstruct the work. Do not cut away sound cloth to make a tidy large hole. If the patch rubs skin, blocks a pocket, or restricts bending, choose another material or stop.
3. **Hold position without closing another layer.** Lay garment and patch in their natural shape. Use clips or a small countable number of pins. Check underneath that another garment layer is free; a sleeve or trouser leg must not become one sheet. If you mark a line, confirm the mark can be removed from this material.
4. **Sew patch edges onto sound fabric.** Choose a needle that crosses original cloth and patch without enlarging the holes. Use short, even hand stitches around the patch edge, taking each stitch into sound original cloth beyond the hole. Keep corners flat. Where suitable, a few overcast stitches can control loose hole edges; do not cinch them into a tight pucker. Secure thread on the wrong side at start and finish. Unverified iron-on glue or solvent is not a replacement for this join.
5. **Test the limit of use.** Remove and count every clip and pin. Lay it flat: patch edges should not lift or gather. Pull lightly around the area to see whether new stitches tear the original cloth. Try it on for an ordinary movement when safe, checking rubbing, restricted motion, or a new split. If the fabric stops holding stitches or the patch blocks function, stop wearing and seek professional work or choose another garment.

### Success

The patch spans the small hole and attaches to sound cloth; loose edges do not keep spreading, and normal low-stress wear is flat and comfortable. No needle or pin remains. Stopping home repair when the fabric is weak, the place carries high load, or the material has functional requirements is also a completed decision.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Hole edges are pulled together and the garment tightens:** Stop cinching. Missing fabric needs patch support; tension does not create cloth.
- **The patch is tiny and stitched only to fuzzy edges:** Use a patch reaching beyond damage into sound cloth. More holes in a weak edge do not reinforce it.
- **A thick patch rubs or blocks movement:** Remove it and choose compatible cloth or professional repair. Hidden from view does not mean usable to wear.
- **A trouser leg, sleeve, or lining is sewn into the repair:** Check underneath before continuing. Undo mistaken stitches, reopen the garment layers, and refix the patch.
- **Cloth tears beside the new needle holes:** Stop home repair and ordinary wear. The surrounding fabric may no longer carry stitches; do not keep enlarging the stitch pattern.
- **A needle or pin is missing:** Pause use and account for garment and work area. Do not hand it to another person until the sharp item is removed.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers one small, low-stress hole in **ordinary woven cloth with sound surrounding fabric**. Knits, leather, functional coatings, complex construction, and repeat-wear spots have different repair mechanisms. Choose patch and thread for the garment's real thickness, friction, and care conditions; there is no universal patch size or durability promise. Use magnification or trusted help if the damaged edge is hard to see, and a thimble and stable table for limited grip. A visible patch and added thickness are honest costs. Comfort and no further tearing matter more than invisibility.
