## 中文

当几件普通物品占着位置，而你不确定该留、交给别人还是以后再想时，加载这份 Skill。一次只处理**三到五件你有权决定的东西**，给每件一个可执行的“保留 / 确认转交 / 暂存待决定”状态。完成不以丢弃数量计算；纪念物可以被认真保留。食品、药品、危险物、身份凭据和需要专门文书判断的材料不在这份普通取舍流程内。

### 准备与输入

选一个小批次和你现有的可找回暂放位置；纸、备忘录或可朗读记录都行。对每件先确认：它归谁、状态是否适合继续使用、你是否还有实际用途或保留意愿、别人是否已经明确愿意接收、什么信息还缺。没有所有权或处理许可的东西不进入你的取舍表。

能否看清、提起或走到存放处也是输入。只拿你能安全处理的小件；重物、损坏的不明设备和有特殊处置要求的东西先保持原状，按相应说明另行处理。你不需要为了证明“能断舍离”搬开危险的堆放。

### 执行

1. **给每件写一条事实，不做人格评语。** 记下它是什么、谁有权决定、目前能否按原用途使用，以及你实际想保留它的原因。经常用、偶尔真会用、选定留作纪念都可以是保留理由；“别人说该扔”不是自动指令。
2. **先分出保留。** 对已决定留的东西给一个你能找回、放回的现有位置。暂时找不到合适位置时，记下待安排位置，不假装它已归位。留物不要求向别人证明最优性。
3. **把转交写成待完成的交接。** 只有物品状况适合、归属允许、接收方明确愿意接收，才列“确认转交”；写下给谁、如何交、何时确认。店、慈善机构或朋友未答应时先标“待确认接收”，不要把物品留在门口就当转交完成。费用、运输和当地接收规则现场核对。
4. **让暂存区保存问题，不保存遗忘。** 对还不能决定的，写一条未决原因，例如“先问共同拥有者”或“想确认下次是否会用”，放在一个你能找回的位置。可以约一个与你相关的复查时机；纪念物不因人工倒计时被强制丢弃。暂存区满时先处理已明确的转交/归位事项，不自动把待定物扔掉。
5. **给三种状态做一次回读。** 随机看一件保留物，能说出它的新位置；看一件拟转交物，能说出接收是否确认及下一步；看一件暂存物，能说出还缺什么信息。做不到就修记录或位置，不再扩大本次批量。
6. **在边界处停下。** 若物品损坏、用途或归属不明到你无法负责决定，标“需专门核实”并暂时隔开，不把它包装成普通可转交物。完成本批后停止；整间房不必在一次运行中做完。

### 完成与停止

本批每件普通物品都有有据的保留位置、已确认且仍待实际完成的转交，或可找回、带未决问题的暂存位置。真正交到接收者手中之前，转交状态仍是“待完成”。没有处理权、不宜安全搬动或需专门处置的物品保持未解决，不为清单漂亮擅作决定。

### 常见报错与补救

- **“要转交”袋子放了几个月：** 检查接收者是否真答应、何时可交；无渠道就改为待确认，不继续称已完成。
- **暂存区越来越大：** 先完成已明确的归位和转交，再给剩下的每件保留一个未决问题；空间不够就缩小下一批，不强迫当场丢弃。
- **纪念物让你犹豫：** 允许明确保留；需要时间也可暂存。照片或故事记录只是选项，不能替你决定实物是否应留下。
- **碰到他人的物品：** 停下并询问拥有者，不因它在你房间里就获得处理权。
- **中途被打断：** 留下已分类的几件、未处理小批和各自位置；回来从未处理批继续，不把暂放位置误报为完成。

### 假设、替代与已知副作用

居住空间、家庭关系、文化和对纪念物的看法不同；本篇不推行极简主义，也不要求每人保留同样数量。看不清或记不住位置时，用一致位置、较大字或可信协助，但最终去留由有权的人决定。副作用是可能留下很多物品，也可能得到一项真的需要去做的交接；诚实的待办比虚假的空架子有用。

## English

Load this Skill when a few ordinary possessions occupy space and you have not decided whether to keep, pass on, or think again later. Handle **three to five items you are entitled to decide about** and give each a workable “keep / agreed transfer / hold to decide” state. Success is not measured by how many things you discard. Keeping a memento is a legitimate decision. Food, medicines, hazardous items, identity credentials, and materials requiring specialist document decisions are outside this ordinary flow.

### Preparation and inputs

Choose a small batch and an existing, retrievable holding place. Paper, notes, or an accessible spoken record can work. For each item, confirm who owns it, whether it can still serve its original purpose, whether you want to use or keep it, whether a recipient has actually agreed to take it, and what information is missing. An item you do not own or have permission to decide about does not enter your decision list.

Whether you can see, lift, or reach the storage place matters too. Handle only small things you can move safely. Leave heavy items, damaged unknown equipment, or things with special disposal rules in place for an appropriate separate process. You need not shift an unsafe stack to prove you can “declutter.”

### Execution

1. **Record a fact, not a character judgment.** For each item, note what it is, who may decide, whether it still works for its intended use, and why you want to keep it. Frequent use, a real occasional use, and a chosen memory are all valid reasons. “Someone said to throw it out” is not automatic authority.
2. **Separate definite keeps first.** Give an item you choose to keep an existing place you can find and return to. If you cannot yet give it a suitable home, record that placement as unfinished rather than claiming it is put away. You need not prove that keeping it is optimal to someone else.
3. **Treat transfer as an unfinished handoff.** Only mark “agreed transfer” after the item's condition is suitable, ownership permits it, and a recipient has clearly agreed. Record who, how, and when the handoff will be confirmed. Without a shop, charity, or person's acceptance, mark “recipient to confirm”; leaving a bag by the door has not completed a transfer. Check costs, transport, and local acceptance rules where they apply.
4. **Make the holding area store a question, not oblivion.** For an undecided item, write the missing fact, such as “ask the co-owner” or “check whether I will use this next time,” and put it where you can find it. You may choose a review occasion that fits your life. A memento does not expire because of an invented countdown. If the area fills, complete already-agreed handoffs and known placements first; do not automatically discard the undecided items.
5. **Read back the three states.** For a kept item, you can name its place. For a proposed transfer, you can say whether acceptance is confirmed and name the next step. For a held item, you can state the unresolved question. Repair the record or location if one answer fails, rather than enlarging this batch.
6. **Stop at the boundary.** If damage, purpose, or ownership is too uncertain for you to decide, mark “special check needed” and keep it separate for now. Do not present it as an ordinary transferable item. Stop after this small batch; the whole room need not finish in one run.

### Success and stop conditions

Each ordinary item in this batch has an evidenced keep location, an accepted but still-to-be-completed transfer, or a retrievable hold place with a question attached. Until it reaches the recipient, transfer remains pending. Items you cannot decide about, safely move, or handle under ordinary rules remain unresolved; do not make them disappear for a tidy list.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The “to transfer” bag sits for months:** Check whether the recipient agreed and when the handoff can happen. Without a route, mark it to confirm instead of calling it completed.
- **The hold area keeps growing:** Finish clearly identified placements and transfers first, then keep one question for each remaining item. If space is limited, shrink the next batch rather than forcing a discard now.
- **A memento makes the choice hard:** You may choose to keep it; you may also hold it for more time. A photo or story record is an option, not a decision made on your behalf.
- **It belongs to someone else:** Stop and ask the owner. Being in your room does not grant disposal rights.
- **You are interrupted:** Leave the decided items, one unfinished batch, and their locations clear. Resume with the unfinished batch; temporary parking has not become a completed result.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Homes, relationships, cultures, and feelings about keepsakes differ. This is not a minimalism rule and does not set one correct number of belongings. If sight or memory makes locations hard to use, choose consistent places, larger writing, or trusted assistance; the person entitled to decide still makes the choice. You may keep many things or gain a real handoff task. An honest pending action is more useful than a falsely empty shelf.
