## 中文

当清单本身合理，但一趟买齐会超过你实际能带回家的重量、体积或可用双手时，加载这份 Skill。它把**同一需求拆成几次各自能完成的采购**。这里不重新判断该买什么、买多大包装，也不把“改由商家配送”伪装成第二趟。

### 准备与输入

确认清单上每项的用途、最晚需要时间、是否与别的东西一起才能完成一顿饭或一项家务、预计整包重量和体积、实际商店与营业时间。写下你在真实返程中能稳定携带或推动的范围，并把楼梯、换乘、需要空出一只手、包装和保冷装备一起算进去；没有通用的“应该能提几公斤”。还要知道每趟能何时出发、如何回家，以及家里是否能按标签保存先买回的食品。

看不清包装重量或商店信息时，放大照片、请店员读出，或把该项标为待确认。不要靠猜测把它塞进刚好满载的一趟。

### 执行

1. **先圈出最先要完成的用途。** 例如今晚的一顿饭或明天要用的普通清洁用品。已有物品和真正的购买量应在这之前核对；本 Skill 只分配已经确认的清单。
2. **把会互相卡住的物品连成组。** 如果少了其中一项就无法完成这次用途，把它们放在同一趟；或者先确定家中已有合适物品。不要先买一半易腐食材，再把另一半寄托给下周可能没开的店。
3. **先装第一趟。** 从最早需要、不可缺的组开始，按实际**整包**负重与体积累计，留出返程必需的手和空间。到了你能稳定携带或推动的范围就停；可选项转后趟，不通过临时多拎一袋提高容量。如果一个不可拆的必需组本身就带不走，暂停这一方案，改用已核实的轻型商品或可行取得方式。
4. **给每趟一个完整返家终点。** 写出这趟在哪买、何时买、怎样回家。需冷藏或冷冻的食品尽量在该趟最后购买并直接返家；较长路程只在能按标签维持合适条件时使用保冷袋和冰袋。先买回的食品回家后按标签处理，不能把袋子当作两趟之间的冰箱。
5. **把剩余清单继续分趟。** 每趟重新从空载开始计算，也分别核对店时段、交通与携带范围。第二趟若暂时无法确定日期或路线，把它标为未承诺；第一趟不要买必须等第二趟才有用途、又无法按标签保存的易腐品。
6. **出门前做一次失败预演。** 如果后一趟取消，第一趟买回的东西仍应有明确用途和合适保存去向。若不能，就调整两趟的分组、延期整组，或先解决取得方式。把每趟写成“本次买什么—完成什么—怎样带回—可删什么”。

### 完成条件

你得到至少两张可单独执行的采购卡：每趟都有用途、物品组、预计负担和返家方案；第一趟不会留下必须靠不确定第二趟才能使用的易腐物品。任一趟不成立，就没有完成拆分。

### 常见报错与补救

- **平均分了件数，第一趟仍太重：** 改按整包重量、体积和携带方式累计；少件数不等于轻。
- **两趟各买半顿饭：** 把必需配料合并到同一趟，或改成各趟都能独立完成的用途；不要为完成表格而制造无用食材。
- **第二趟改期或商店关门：** 先检查已买物品的保存与使用安排，再重新排未买的组；不把未购商品写成已到家。
- **冷食被排到需要继续办事的一趟：** 把它移到该趟最后一站，拆出直返的一趟，或暂不买；保冷条件无法确认时停止该项。
- **每趟都超出实际能力：** 停止靠拆分求解，转向合适的商品形式或已核实的其他取得方式；这不是意志力测试。

### 假设、替代与副作用

这份 Skill 假设你能够安排多次出行，但不假定你有车、能爬楼梯、能看小字或当地公交允许任何推车。商店、交通、配送服务和保冷条件以当日当地信息及具体商品标签为准。若两趟本身不可达，不强迫执行；重新选择商品或取得方式。副作用是多花一次往返时间，换来每趟真实可完成。

## English

Load this Skill when the list itself makes sense but buying it all at once would exceed what you can actually take home by weight, volume, or available hands. It turns **one need into separate trips that each work**. It does not decide what you need, choose a pack size, or call a shop delivery a second trip.

### Preparation and inputs

For each listed item, confirm its purpose, when it is needed, whether other items are required to complete the same meal or household job, its approximate full-pack size and weight, and where and when it is sold. Write down what you can steadily carry or move on the real return journey. Include stairs, transfers, a hand you need free, packaging, and any cooling equipment. There is no universal number of kilograms you should be able to carry. Know when each trip is possible, how it gets home, and whether you can store food from an earlier trip as its label requires.

If a package weight or shop detail is inaccessible, enlarge a photo, ask staff to read it, or mark the item to confirm. Do not guess it into a trip that is already at its limit.

### Execution

1. **Circle the first use that must work.** It might be tonight's meal or an ordinary household supply needed tomorrow. Verify existing stock and real quantities before using this Skill; here you divide a list that has already been checked.
2. **Group items that depend on each other.** If the job cannot be done without all of them, keep them on one trip or confirm a suitable item is already at home. Do not buy half a perishable meal and leave the other half to a shop that may not open next week.
3. **Fill trip one first.** Start with the earliest essential group. Add the actual **full-pack** weight and size, leaving the hands and space you need to return. Stop at a load you know you can carry or move steadily. Move optional goods to a later trip instead of inventing more capacity. If one essential group is too much on its own, pause this plan and check a genuinely suitable lighter product or another verified way to obtain it.
4. **Give every trip its own way home.** Record where and when you will buy its items and how you will return. Buy chilled or frozen food towards the end of that trip and head home. For a longer journey, use a cool bag and ice packs only if they can meet the item's labelled condition. Once home, store earlier purchases as labelled; a shopping bag is not a refrigerator between trips.
5. **Divide what remains.** Start each later trip from an empty load, and check its opening hours, transport, and carrying limit separately. If you cannot yet commit to trip two, label it unconfirmed. Do not buy perishables on trip one that need an uncertain trip two to become useful and cannot be stored appropriately meanwhile.
6. **Rehearse a cancellation before leaving.** If a later trip fails, goods from trip one still need a clear use and suitable storage. If that is not true, move the dependent group, defer it together, or first solve how to obtain it. Write each trip as “buy these—complete this use—carry them home this way—drop these if needed.”

### Success

You have at least two shopping cards that can each be executed: every trip has a purpose, item group, expected load, and way home. Trip one does not strand perishables whose only use depends on an uncertain later trip. If any trip is infeasible, the list has not yet been split successfully.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You split by item count and trip one is still heavy:** Add full-pack weights, sizes, and carrying method instead. Fewer items do not mean a lighter load.
- **Each trip buys half a meal:** Put essential ingredients together or choose uses each trip can finish independently. A neat table is not a reason to create unused food.
- **Trip two moves or its shop closes:** Recheck use and storage for goods already bought, then replan only the unbought group. Do not count an unpurchased item as home stock.
- **Cold food lands on a trip with more errands afterward:** Move it to that trip's final stop, make a direct-return trip, or leave it out. Stop that purchase if reliable cooling cannot be confirmed.
- **Every trip is beyond your capacity:** Stop trying to solve it by splitting; consider a suitable product form or another verified way to obtain it. This is not a test of willpower.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This Skill assumes you can arrange more than one outing. It assumes neither a car nor stairs, small-print access, or permission to take a particular cart onto local transit. Check current local shop and transport information and the specific food label. If repeated outings are not feasible, choose another product or acquisition method rather than forcing this plan. The side effect is another journey in exchange for trips you can actually complete.
