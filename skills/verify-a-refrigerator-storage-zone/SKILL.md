## 中文

当你要把普通易腐食品放进新启用、搬动过、装得很满或温度不明的冰箱时，加载这份 Skill。目标是判断**打算放食品的区域**是否可靠制冷，而不是靠旋钮位置推断温度。它不判断一件已经经历未知温度史的食品能否继续吃，也不教你维修冰箱。

### 准备与输入

确认食品包装的冷藏要求、当地适用的食品安全温度、冰箱说明书和温度计使用方法。美国 FDA 对普通家用冰箱给出的上限是 40°F（约 4.4°C）；香港食物安全中心给出的冷藏条件是 4°C 或以下。这是两地各自的指引，不是全世界统一数字；包装有更具体的条件时先核对它。

你需要能读出冰箱**实际温度**的温度计。温控旋钮或“几档”通常只表示设置，不证明食品所在处的温度。没有可用温度计、也没有可信的现成读数时，把冷藏能力记为“未证实”；需要立即冷藏的食品要先用已核实能满足其要求的其他冷藏方式，不在未知状态下等待这份检查完成。

### 执行

1. **选要验证的位置。** 从将放易腐食品的架层或抽屉开始；门格、角落或特殊温区如果也要放这类食品，分别核对。按温度计说明放置，让探头或显示器能实际反映该位置，不拿它贴着冷冻壁或热食包装读一个无代表性的数。
2. **给读数稳定时间。** 关好门，按温度计或设备说明等待并读数。温度计刚放进去、开门后的一瞬间、或冰箱旋钮刚调整时的读数都不能独自证明长期条件。若说明没有清楚给出方法，查设备/温度计制造商或当地食品安全机构的用法；在此期间不要把未证实区域当作合格冷藏位。
3. **对照本地与包装条件。** 记录日期、位置、读数和适用上限。读数符合要求，才把该位置标为可用于相应食品；另一处合格不能代替这处的判断。温度是保存条件之一，不会追溯修复食品过去的温度史。
4. **读数偏高时先做普通检查。** 关严门，移开挡住通风口或挤得太紧的物品；按设备说明调整温控，等稳定后在相同位置复测。不要自行拆电器或把更冷的档位当成已经修好。持续偏高、门关不严或设备异常时停止用它保存需要冷藏的食品，并请合格人员处理设备。
5. **给食品单独做决定。** 如果已有食品可能在不合格温度下放过，记下能确认的时间和读数，按该食品标签与当地官方指导判断；时间线不清时不要靠闻味道宣告安全。把仍可安全转移的食品移到经核实的冷藏处，无法证明安全的食品交给“保存信息不明”的停止判断。

### 完成条件

你能指着每个打算使用的冷藏区域说出“符合要求 / 不符合要求 / 尚未证实”，并有位置、时间、读数及适用条件。只有符合要求的区域用于相应易腐食品；不符合或尚未证实的区域有明确停止及替代安排。当前合格读数不等于已存食品自动安全。

### 常见报错与补救

- **旋钮显示很冷，温度计仍偏高：** 相信稳定后的实际读数，按说明检查门、负载和设置；复测前不使用该区域存放需冷藏的食品。
- **一个位置合格，另一个偏高：** 只把已证实的区域作为候选；门格或特殊抽屉是否适合某食品按其标签、说明和实际读数判断。
- **没有温度计或读数看不清：** 借用或取得适用温度计，选大字/语音显示，或请可信的人读出数值和位置；不要用手摸冷不冷代替。
- **复测仍偏高或冰箱有电气异常：** 停止使用需冷藏食品的该空间；使用经核实的替代冷藏条件并联系合格维修，不打开电气部件。
- **已经存过食品却不知道偏高多久：** 本 Skill 停在温度史不明；另查适用官方处置指引，不把调低旋钮当作补救过去。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

冰箱布局、温控标记、区域温差和温度计稳定方法随设备变化。这里假设你能查到当地指引和包装要求；读数不便查看时可用可及温度计或请人协助，但食品安全结论必须仍以可靠条件为据。若冰箱需要维修，任务到停止和转移为止。

现实副作用是多一条温度记录，也可能发现原以为可用的冷藏空间暂时不能存放易腐食品。旋钮不会为自己的工作写验收报告。

## English

Load this Skill before relying on a newly started, moved, heavily loaded, or otherwise unverified refrigerator for ordinary perishable food. You are checking whether **the space where the food will sit** actually stays cold enough. A dial setting is not a temperature reading. This Skill does not decide whether food with an unknown past temperature history is still safe, and it does not repair the appliance.

### Preparation and inputs

Check the food label, your applicable local food-safety guidance, the refrigerator manual, and the thermometer instructions. The US FDA gives 40°F (about 4.4°C) or below for an ordinary home refrigerator; the Hong Kong Centre for Food Safety gives 4°C or below for chilled food. These are examples from two jurisdictions, not one global rule. Check any more specific requirement on the food's packaging.

You need a thermometer that shows the refrigerator's **actual temperature**. A control dial or numbered setting does not prove the temperature where food is stored. Without a usable thermometer or reliable existing readings, mark the cold space “unverified.” Food that needs chilling now must use another verified way to meet its storage requirement while this check remains incomplete.

### Execution

1. **Choose the intended food locations.** Start with the shelf or drawer that will hold perishables. Check a door bin, corner, or special compartment separately if you plan to store such food there. Position the thermometer as its instructions require so it represents that location rather than touching a freezer wall or a warm food package.
2. **Let the reading settle.** Close the door and wait as the thermometer or appliance instructions require before reading. A moment just after placing the thermometer, opening the door, or changing the dial cannot by itself establish stable conditions. If the method is unclear, consult the maker or local food-safety authority; do not treat the unverified zone as safe chilled storage in the meantime.
3. **Compare with the applicable condition.** Record date, location, reading, and required limit. Mark that location suitable for the relevant food only when its reading meets the requirement; a good result elsewhere does not prove this spot. Current temperature is one storage condition and cannot repair the food's earlier history.
4. **If a reading is high, make ordinary checks first.** Close the door fully and move food away from blocked vents or tight packing. Adjust the control according to the manual, allow it to settle, and measure the same location again. Do not open or repair electrical parts, or assume a colder dial position has solved the problem. If the reading remains high, the door will not close, or the appliance acts abnormally, stop using it for food that needs chilling and seek qualified appliance help.
5. **Decide about existing food separately.** If food may have spent time above its required temperature, record the time and readings you can establish and consult its label and applicable official guidance. Smell cannot recover an unknown timeline. Move food that can still safely be moved to verified cold storage; send unresolved food to the separate unknown-storage-history stop decision.

### Success

For every intended chilled-food zone, you can say “meets the requirement / fails / unverified,” with location, time, reading, and applicable limit. Only a verified suitable zone is used for that food. A failed or unverified zone has a clear stop and alternative. A good reading now does not certify food stored there earlier.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The dial says cold but the stable reading is high:** Trust the measured condition. Check the door, load, and setting as instructed, and do not use that zone for chilled food until it passes a new check.
- **One place passes and another fails:** Use only verified candidate zones. Decide whether a door bin or special drawer fits a food from its label, manual, and actual reading.
- **No thermometer or inaccessible display:** Borrow or obtain a suitable thermometer, use a large or talking display, or ask a trusted person to read the value and location. Feeling cold by hand is not a measurement.
- **The reading stays high or the appliance behaves electrically abnormally:** Stop using that space for food requiring refrigeration, use verified alternative cold storage, and seek qualified repair without opening electrical components.
- **Food was stored there and you do not know how long it was too warm:** Stop this Skill at the unknown history. Use applicable official disposition guidance; turning the dial down now cannot change the past.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Layouts, controls, zone differences, and thermometer stabilization methods vary by appliance. This assumes you can find local guidance and food labels. Accessible thermometers or another person's reading can help, while the food-safety decision still rests on reliable conditions. If repair is needed, this task ends with stopping use and moving food safely.

The side effect is one temperature record, and perhaps the discovery that a shelf you assumed was usable cannot yet hold perishables. The dial does not write its own acceptance report.
