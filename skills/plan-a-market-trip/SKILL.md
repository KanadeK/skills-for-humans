## 中文

当你准备去菜市场或超市，却只知道“家里没什么吃的”时，加载这份 Skill。它适合一次有边界的采购；如果需求仍只是“家里该补点东西”，可先用[缺口清单](../make-a-shopping-gap-list/SKILL.md)核对用途和存量。这里不负责营养治疗、实时价格预测，也不要求你在摊位前完成一场供应链博士答辩。

### 安装前准备

拿出纸、备忘录或任何不会在锁屏后消失的东西。先确认这些输入：

- 几个人吃、覆盖几顿或几天；
- 你愿意花多少钱，是否有不能突破的上限；
- 家里已经有、已经开封、应该优先吃掉的食物；
- 冰箱、冷冻室和常温储存还剩多少空间；
- 忌口、过敏避让清单和普通偏好；
- 从买完到回家要多久，是否有保冷袋，以及你实际会去哪些店。

如果你无法查看高处、搬重物、长时间站立或辨认细小标签，把“请店员拿取”“分两次买”“线上补货”写成计划的一部分，不要把身体条件当成运行异常。

### 执行

1. **先写餐，不先写商品。** 列出这次采购要支持的几顿饭，例如两顿快手晚饭、一顿汤面和两次早餐。没有用途的“看起来很健康”很容易在冰箱里申请长期居留。
2. **盘点旧库存。** 把已经有的主食、蛋白质、蔬菜和调味品放进餐里，优先安排较易坏或已经开封的食物。记下真正可用于这几顿饭的量；日期、保存情况或可用量不确定的东西先标待确认，不直接抵扣清单。
3. **为每项写用途与数量。** 数量按你家真实饭量、使用次数和储存能力估，不套用陌生人的标准份量。第一次买不熟悉的食材，先买能完成一次尝试的小份。
4. **分成必须、可替代、路过不要碰。** 必须项保证餐能成立；可替代项写清功能，例如“绿叶菜任一种”；第三栏专门收容容易冲动购买的东西。
5. **给预算留缓冲。** 先给主食、蛋白质、蔬果和其他用品分配大致额度；价格超出时，按餐中功能替换或减少非必须项，不凭空编一个“全国统一菜价”。
6. **按现场路线排序。** 不知道布局时，先常温和非食品，再蔬果、蛋，最后拿肉、海鲜、冷藏和冷冻品。生的动物性食品用防漏袋与即食食品、日用品分开。
7. **安排回家这段路。** 路程长或天气热时用保冷袋/冰袋，并把买完后的闲逛从计划中删除。冷链不是付完钱就结束的进程。
8. **出门前做一次编译。** 每个商品都应对应一顿饭或明确用途；总量装得下、拎得动、预算够，且你知道哪些可以不买。

### 成功条件

你的清单能回答五件事：买什么、买多少、为了哪顿饭、超价时换什么、怎样安全带回家。它适合拿着走，而不是一份需要二次整理的散文。

### 常见报错与补救

- **ERR_LIST_WITHOUT_MEALS：** 清单很长但拼不成饭。删掉孤立商品，先补餐的主食、蛋白质和蔬菜骨架。
- **ERR_FRIDGE_ALREADY_FULL：** 把大包装改成小份，删去低优先级易腐品，或先安排一顿清库存。
- **预算在现场超限：** 先去掉零食和重复调味品，再用同功能、当地可得的食材替换；不要因为已经排队就升级预算。
- **忘带清单：** 用手机重新写“人数、餐数、预算、三项必须”，先完成最小采购，不靠回忆重建整套系统。
- **看到的东西与计划不符：** 不确定质量、来源或储存条件时可以不买。空手离开一个摊位是合法输出。

### 假设、替代与已知副作用

这里假设你能获得当地商品和价格信息，并能决定自己的普通饮食。宗教、文化、经济和家庭习惯没有统一默认值；用你所在地的单位、摊位规则和常吃份量替换示例。严重过敏或医疗饮食请使用你已有的专业方案，不在市场里临时实验。

现实副作用包括：可能少买一点、可能仍会忘记一项，以及回家后必须执行另一个名为“收纳”的 Skill。

## English

Load this Skill when you are about to visit a market or grocery store and your entire plan is “we have nothing to eat.” It handles one bounded shop. If the request is still just “we should stock up,” you can first use the [shopping gap list](../make-a-shopping-gap-list/SKILL.md) to check uses and stock. This Skill does not provide medical nutrition treatment, predict live prices, or require a supply-chain dissertation beside the onions.

### Preparation and inputs

Use paper, a notes app, or anything that survives the lock screen. Confirm:

- how many people, meals, or days this trip covers;
- your spending target and any hard ceiling;
- food already at home, especially opened or use-first items;
- refrigerator, freezer, and pantry space;
- exclusions, allergy-avoidance rules you already follow, and ordinary preferences;
- travel time, cold-bag access, and the stores you can actually visit.

If reaching shelves, carrying weight, standing, or reading small labels is difficult, build assistance, two smaller trips, or delivery into the plan. Your body is an input, not an exception trace.

### Execution

1. **List meals before products.** Name the meals this trip must support. An attractive ingredient with no job tends to request permanent fridge residency.
2. **Inspect existing stock.** Assign usable staples, proteins, vegetables, and seasonings to those meals. Record how much can serve these meals. If an item's date, storage history, or usable amount is uncertain, mark it to confirm instead of subtracting it from the list.
3. **Give every item a purpose and amount.** Estimate from your own portions, number of uses, and storage. For an unfamiliar ingredient, buy enough for one attempt rather than a lifetime subscription.
4. **Separate must-have, substitute, and walk-past items.** A substitute preserves the meal function, such as any suitable leafy vegetable. The third list contains predictable impulse buys.
5. **Leave budget room.** Allocate rough amounts to meal essentials first. If prices differ, replace by function or remove optional items instead of inventing a universal market price.
6. **Order the route.** If the layout is unknown, collect shelf-stable and non-food goods first, then produce and eggs, and raw meat, seafood, chilled, and frozen food last. Keep raw animal products in leak-resistant bags away from ready-to-eat food and household chemicals.
7. **Plan the trip home.** Use a cold bag or ice packs when travel or weather requires it, and remove leisurely detours after chilled food enters the basket. The cold chain does not exit when payment succeeds.
8. **Compile before leaving.** Every item has a meal or clear use; the total fits your budget, storage, carrying capacity, and transport.

### Success

Your list answers what, how much, which meal, what substitute, and how it gets home safely. You can carry it through the shop without first converting it into another list.

### Common errors and recovery

- **ERR_LIST_WITHOUT_MEALS:** Remove isolated products and restore the staple/protein/vegetable structure of the planned meals.
- **ERR_FRIDGE_ALREADY_FULL:** Choose smaller packs, remove low-priority perishables, or schedule a use-what-you-have meal first.
- **The live total is too high:** Drop optional duplicates and replace essentials by function. Waiting in line does not authorize a budget migration.
- **You forgot the list:** Recreate only people, meals, budget, and three must-haves on your phone. Complete the minimum shop.
- **Reality differs from the plan:** If quality, source, or storage is doubtful, do not buy it. Leaving one stall empty-handed is a valid result.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This assumes you can get local product and price information and make ordinary diet choices. Units, portions, market etiquette, bargaining, and availability vary by region and household. Use your own. Follow an existing professional plan for severe allergies or medical diets rather than improvising at the market.

Known side effects include buying less, still forgetting one item, and unlocking the separate “put everything away” Skill when you get home.
