## 中文

当你要决定一件**普通衣物**现在是否合身可穿、哪里需要调整时，用这份 Skill 以自己真正会做的动作试穿。目标是得到“直接穿、用衣物自带调节、记录改衣需求、换另一件”中的一个明确决定。标签上的尺码是商品标识，不是身体评分；站在镜前不动也不能替你完成一整天的坐、走和伸手。

### 准备与输入

确认这件衣物干净到适合试穿、纽扣/拉链/接缝完整，找一个可私密更衣、地面稳定且能安全活动的位置。想清楚这次真实用途：需要坐多久、走路、抬手、弯腰、拿取物品或使用辅助设备中的哪些动作；只测你会做且能安全做的。准备计划穿在里面的内层和会影响裤长的鞋子，若它们确实属于这次穿法。可选镜子或可信的人协助观察背后，但要先征得同意，不要求别人未经许可触碰身体或衣物。

防护装备、医疗压缩衣、承重运动器材和特殊制服的尺寸需按其专门规则验收，不靠本流程判断保护性能。已经坏掉的扣件或裂开的接缝先记为衣物故障，不把它说成“你太胖/太瘦”。

### 执行

1. **按计划穿法穿上，不用力硬塞。** 把衣物穿到设计位置，正常扣合、调整本来就有的松紧带或抽绳。闭合需要明显强拉、会夹肤或无法自己按日常方式脱下时立即停止；别通过强闭合来取得一个“穿得上”的假结果。
2. **先看静止状态。** 在坐或站的舒适姿势观察肩、胸/背、腰/臀、袖口与下摆是否按你预期停在位置，布料有无持续压迫、滑落、缝线异常紧绷或你介意的暴露。版型本来可能宽松或贴身，不因“离身体远”自动判大，也不因“贴近身体”自动判小。
3. **用你的真实动作复查。** 做安全、熟悉的小幅动作，例如坐下再起身、走几步、伸手拿日常物品或弯腰；按自己的行动方式替换这些例子。观察腰带是否在坐下时压痛或滑下、衣摆/领口是否移动到不能接受的位置、袖子是否阻碍伸手、裤脚是否与鞋和地面冲突。需要坐姿或辅助设备的人就在实际姿势下测，不必为了流程勉强站立。
4. **把问题分成可调整与不可硬修。** 衣物自带的带扣、抽绳、卷袖或常规穿法能在不损伤衣物的情况下解决，就调整后再测一次。若是长度、腰位、肩线、活动余量或版型一直不合，用一句具体话记下“哪处、哪个动作、发生了什么”，再决定换尺码/版型、找改衣者评估或暂不穿。扣子脱落、拉链卡阻、开线分别走对应修复，不用选大一码掩盖故障。
5. **给出有限结论。** 所需动作都可做、感觉可接受、衣物不持续移位或拉坏，可以用这件。若必须忍受疼痛、限制动作、持续担心掉落/走光或无法安全脱穿，就换另一件或安排合适改动；不承诺“穿几次自然就合身”。购物退换与具体缝改不在本 Skill 内，先保留试穿证据和原状。

### 成功条件

你能说清这件衣物在本人实际穿法和动作下是否可用，若不合适能指出具体位置与动作，并选定直接穿、利用内置调节、交改衣评估或换件。结论不以别人的审美或尺码数字替代本人舒适与活动需求。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只看尺码标签或镜中静态照片：** 补做实际坐、走、伸手等会用到的动作；尺码相同也可能是不同版型。
- **扣合靠憋气或用力拉，坐下不舒服：** 停止强穿，记录压迫位置，换更合适的尺寸或版型，不让扣眼承担尺寸误差。
- **站着正好，弯腰衣摆或裤腰移位：** 以真实动作决定是否可接受，试衣物自带调节或换版型；不要用羞辱自己来解释设计差异。
- **裤脚绊鞋、袖子盖手：** 记录长度与实际鞋/内层，交改衣评估或换件；没有测量和方案前不直接剪布。
- **衣物故障被误当不合身：** 单独记录裂缝、拉链或纽扣问题，先走对应修复，不靠尺寸推断硬解决。
- **帮看的人给出令你不舒服的评论：** 收回试穿决定权，只要求描述“哪个动作出现什么移位”，或改用镜子/自己触摸检查。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这是一件普通衣物对**这次用途和这个人**的穿着检查，身体、版型和偏好没有统一“正确”轮廓。不同品牌同一尺码可能设计出不同松量；测量表只能帮助缩小候选，不能取代动态试穿。视力受限可用触摸、可信且经同意的辅助观察；行动受限则只测安全且真实需要的姿势。记录问题可能发现要换件或付改衣成本，但不要求为了标签数字忍受不适。

## English

Use this Skill to decide whether **one ordinary garment** fits and works for you now, and where any adjustment is needed. Try it with the movements you actually make. Finish with a clear decision: wear as is, use built-in adjustment, record an alteration need, or choose another item. The size tag identifies a product; it does not score a body. Standing still in a mirror cannot stand in for a day of sitting, walking, and reaching.

### Preparation and inputs

Check that the garment is clean enough to try and that buttons, zipper, and seams are intact. Choose a private changing place with stable footing and room for safe movement. Name this use: how much sitting, walking, reaching, bending, carrying, or using an assistive device actually matters? Try only movements you normally make safely. Have the intended underlayer and shoes if they affect fit or trouser length. A mirror or trusted person can help inspect the back, with your consent; nobody needs to touch you or your clothing without permission.

Protective equipment, medical compression clothing, load-bearing sports gear, and specialized uniform fit need their own acceptance rules, not this ordinary check. A broken fastener or seam is a garment defect; it is not evidence that your body is wrong.

### Execution

1. **Put it on as intended without forcing it.** Set it in its designed position, fasten normally, and use its existing waistband, drawcord, or other adjustment. Stop if closing takes hard pulling, pinches skin, or makes it impossible to remove in your ordinary way. Forced fastening is not a useful “it fits” result.
2. **Inspect at rest.** In a comfortable sitting or standing position, check whether shoulder, chest/back, waist/seat, cuff, and hem stay where you expect. Notice constant pressure, slipping, unusually strained seams, or exposure you personally do not accept. A deliberately relaxed cut is not automatically too large; a close cut is not automatically too small.
3. **Try real movements.** Make small familiar moves that are safe for you: sit and rise, walk a few steps, reach for an everyday object, or bend. Substitute your own ways of moving. Does the waistband press or slide when seated, the neckline or hem shift too far, the sleeve block reaching, or a trouser hem interfere with shoes and floor? Test in your real seated position or with an assistive device if that is what matters. The checklist does not require you to stand.
4. **Separate adjustable from structural issues.** If built-in tabs, drawcords, ordinary sleeve rolling, or intended styling solve a problem without damaging the garment, adjust and repeat the relevant move. If length, rise, shoulder placement, movement room, or overall cut still fails, write one concrete note: “where, during which move, what happens.” Decide on another size or cut, an alteration assessment, or not wearing it. A loose button, jammed zipper, or open seam belongs to the corresponding repair task; buying a bigger size is not a repair for a broken part.
5. **Make a limited conclusion.** If necessary moves work, feel acceptable, and do not keep displacing or straining the cloth, this item works for the use. If wearing it requires pain, restricted movement, constant worry about slipping or unwanted exposure, or unsafe dressing, choose another item or appropriate alteration. Do not promise it will fit after enough uncomfortable wears. Returns and actual cutting/sewing are separate tasks; keep the item unchanged while deciding.

### Success

You can say whether this garment works for your actual wearing method and movements. If it fails, you can name the spot and action and choose wearing, built-in adjustment, alteration assessment, or replacement. Another person's aesthetic view or a size number does not override your comfort and movement needs.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Only a tag or mirror photo is checked:** Add the sitting, walking, and reaching that this use actually involves. The same labelled size can have different cuts.
- **Fastening takes breath-holding or a hard pull, and sitting hurts:** Stop forcing it. Record the pressure point and choose a better size or cut rather than making a buttonhole absorb the difference.
- **It looks fine standing, but the hem or waist shifts on bending:** Decide from the real movement, try existing adjustments, or choose another cut. Design variation is not a reason to shame yourself.
- **A hem catches shoes or sleeves cover hands:** Record length with the intended shoes or layer. Seek alteration assessment or another item before cutting fabric.
- **A defect is mislabelled as poor fit:** Record the seam, zipper, or button separately and use the matching repair route.
- **A helper makes unwanted comments:** Keep the decision yours. Ask only for a description of what moves and when, or use a mirror or your own touch check.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This checks one ordinary garment on **this person for this use**. Bodies, cuts, and preferences have no single correct outline. Brands may design different ease under the same size label; a chart narrows candidates but cannot replace moving in a garment. Use touch or consent-based trusted observation if vision is limited. If mobility is limited, test only the safe postures that actually matter. A concrete finding may lead to another item or tailoring cost, but the tag does not require you to tolerate discomfort.
