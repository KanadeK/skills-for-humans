## 中文

当你还没付款，原计划的饮料、干货或普通日用品因重量、体积、握持方式或无车回程而带不走时，用这份 Skill 判断**另一种商品形式**能否完成同一用途。它不安排购物路线或配送，也不替代商品规格、单位价格的专门核对。

### 准备与输入

确认这件商品要完成的具体工作、这次实际需要的用量、你手上已有的物品、购物袋剩余空间，以及你在实际回程中能舒适携带的重量和件数。把楼梯、手杖、轮椅、婴儿车、扶手、需要腾出的手和疼痛限制算进可携带余量；不必向任何人证明“理论上举得起来”。准备阅读候选的净重、每次用量和使用说明；看不清时放大或请店员读出。

### 执行

1. **先写用途，再找形式。** 例如“完成本周几次洗衣”，而不是“买最大的洗衣液”。食品则写实际要做的那几顿，不以包装容量当需求。
2. **明确本次承载上限。** 把已买物品和回程必要物品占去的重量、体积与双手算进去。能短暂从货架举起，不代表能一路安全带回；无需硬试到身体报警。
3. **列出最多三种现场可买的形式。** 可包括较小的一次用量包装、经标签确认适用的浓缩品、干货或无需搬回大桶的补充装。**“浓缩”“补充装”“干货”都不是自动合格标签**：比较整包带走时的负担，以及它是否仍能完成第 1 步的用途。
4. **逐件查隐藏依赖。** 浓缩洗涤用品按**该产品**的每次用量说明估算可用次数，确认你的机器或使用方式允许；补充装要有兼容且可安全使用的原容器，不能为省重量临时混装不明产品；干货或冲调饮品要有安全饮用水、器具和准备时间。需要你没有的条件，就删掉候选。
5. **核算“带得走且用得上”。** 用候选整包的标示重量、体积、形状和预计件数，对照剩余承载上限。要买多包才够时，算**多包合计**；若改为分次购买，这次只承诺实际能带走且够当前用途的量。价格只拿同一时点看见、按相同用途换算后的数字比较，不把“每升便宜”误作“每次方便”。
6. **付款前做闭环。** 你能说出选中哪件、这次如何拿回、实际能用几次、还缺什么，以及预算是否接受。没有候选同时满足用途和承载，就不买原大包；把未来配送或分次购物交给相应计划处理。

### 成功条件

你买下的是一件或一组**这次确实能带走、回家确实能按说明使用**的商品，并知道它覆盖多少次实际需求；或者明确不买。购买完成不要求你挑战个人负重纪录。

### 常见报错与补救

- **小包装买了好几件，合计还是很重：** 按整趟合计重算；只买当前可携带的量，余下需求另排。
- **“浓缩”被误当成“少用同一瓶盖”：** 重新读新商品剂量和适用范围；说明不清或不兼容就放回，别靠猜稀释或混配。
- **补充装没有可用容器：** 选有合适原包装的商品或暂不买；不要把未知内容装进无标签瓶。
- **干货较轻却没有水或时间处理：** 换能按说明直接使用的形式，或改变这次用途。
- **标签无法读到关键用量或重量：** 请求店员读出、放大照片，仍不清楚就不把它列为合格替代。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这份 Skill 只处理普通食品与日用品，不推荐药物、保健品或危险化学品替代，也不承诺不同配方效果相同。商品和价格随地区、品牌及时间变化；承载限制由你的身体和路线决定。无法抓握、站立或提重时，可选择易握的小件、请可信的人协助拿取，或把采购安排交给配送/分次购物流程；不要用疼痛作可行性测试。小包装或不同形式可能增加单次费用、包装和准备时间，这些是选择的一部分。

## English

Use this Skill **before paying** when a planned drink, dry grocery, or ordinary household supply is too heavy, bulky, awkward to grip, or impractical to take home without a car. Decide whether a **different product form** still does the job. Route planning, delivery, detailed specification matching, and unit-price comparison remain separate tasks.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm the exact job, how much you need for this use, what is already in your basket, remaining bag space, and the weight and number of pieces you can comfortably carry on your actual journey. Count stairs, mobility aids, a stroller, handrails, hands you need free, and pain limits. You do not owe anyone a proof that you could lift it briefly. Have each candidate's pack weight, directions, and amount per use available; enlarge the label or ask staff to read it if necessary.

### Execution

1. **Name the job before the package.** Write “enough for these laundry loads” rather than “the largest detergent bottle.” For food, name the meals you will actually make rather than treating package volume as a goal.
2. **Set the load you can carry this time.** Subtract things already bought and essentials for the journey from your available space, weight, and hands. Lifting a pack off a shelf does not prove you can safely get it home. Stop before discomfort becomes a test result.
3. **List up to three forms available here.** Consider a smaller amount, a concentrate confirmed suitable by its label, dry goods, or a refill that avoids taking home a large rigid container. **“Concentrated,” “refill,” and “dry” are not automatic passes.** Check the full pack as carried and the job it will really do.
4. **Check hidden dependencies.** Estimate laundry uses from **this product's** dose and make sure your machine or method permits it. A refill needs a compatible, safely usable original container; do not improvise by mixing unidentified products. Dry food or drink mixes need safe drinking water, equipment, and preparation time. Remove any candidate whose dependency you cannot meet.
5. **Check both carrying and use.** Compare the labelled full-pack weight, size, shape, and number of packs with your remaining capacity. If the needed amount spans several packs, add their loads together. If you will shop in stages, buy only what you can take home and use now. Compare prices only at the same observed time and for the same actual use; a low price per litre does not tell you how many uses you can manage.
6. **Close the decision before checkout.** Be able to name the chosen item, how you will carry it home this time, how many uses it covers, any remaining dependency, and whether its cost fits. If none meets both use and carrying limits, leave the original large pack. Plan delivery or another trip in the appropriate separate workflow.

### Success

You buy an item or set of items you can actually carry **on this trip** and use as directed at home, with a clear number of needs covered. Explicitly buying nothing is also complete. A purchase is not a personal lifting record.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Several small packs are heavy in total:** Recalculate the whole load. Buy only the amount you can carry now and plan the remainder separately.
- **“Concentrated” becomes “use the old cap amount”:** Read the new product's dose and permitted use. If unclear or incompatible, put it back; do not guess dilution or mixing.
- **A refill has no usable container:** Choose a suitable original pack or wait. Do not put unidentified contents into an unlabelled bottle.
- **Dry food is lighter but you have no water or time to prepare it:** Choose a form usable as directed or change this use.
- **You cannot access the key weight or directions:** Ask staff to read them or enlarge a photo. If they remain unclear, do not count the item as a valid substitute.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary groceries and household supplies, not medicines, supplements, hazardous chemicals, or a claim that different formulas perform equally. Products and prices vary by region, brand, and time; your body and route set the load limit. If gripping, standing, or lifting is difficult, use easier-to-hold packs, trusted help to retrieve an item, or a separate delivery/staged-shopping plan. Pain is not a feasibility check. Smaller packs or different forms may change cost per use, packaging, and preparation time.
