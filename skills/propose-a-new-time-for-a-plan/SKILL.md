## 中文

当一项普通见面/通话/小活动**已被双方确认且尚未开始**，你知道自己无法遵守原时间，但仍愿意在合适的新时间继续时加载。目标是尽早、清楚地告诉对方原计划对你已不可行，提供真实可选时间，让对方自由接受或拒绝，并把旧/新安排状态记准。这不是已经失约后的补救，也不是单方面修改对方日历或强求对方改期。

### 准备与输入

找回双方真实确认的活动、原日期时间、地点/线上方式、参与者、已订位或已付款事项，以及你自己能接受的一个或两个新时间。写出星期、日期与当地时间；异地时区则另行明确两地表达。若某项票/场所预约会受影响，先看本次提供方的正式变更规则或问负责人，不假设免费、可退或能保留同一座位。不想说明私人原因时只说原时间无法兑现即可，不编造事故。

### 执行

1. **一知道变化就联系需要决定的人。** 用双方实际使用的渠道，先说清原来是哪项约定、哪个时间现在不行；不要只发“我们换个时间？”让对方猜你说的是哪次。
2. **给可执行、可拒绝的选项。** 例如“原定__日__时我不能赴约了。我还想见面，__日__时或__日__时我能到；如果你不方便，也可以不改，我们再决定是否取消。” 只给你真实能履行的选项，别把自己尚未确认的时间当承诺。
3. **把已有成本和责任点出来。** 若对方或场所已订位/买票，问谁能改、需何时答复、是否有费用或其他限制；需要你承担的一项现实动作要说清并执行。未经对方同意不以其名义改订、退票或确认新费用；法律/财务权利问题交实际提供方规则或合格渠道。
4. **等对方真正选择。** 对方接受某一时间，再回一条完整的新版本：活动、日期、时间、地点/方式与谁处理预订。对方说不方便，接受这一结果并按需要改走取消流程；没有回复只表示未确认，不把已发送或已读当同意。
5. **处理旧计划的状态。** 如果你明确不能参加原时间，即使新时段未定，也要写清“我不会按原时间到场”。双方同意新方案后，才更新自己可控的日程或通过正式流程发新日历邀请；有共享预订则先确认真实变更结果。避免让旧、新两个安排同时显示“确定”。

### 完成与停止

对方知道原约定对你已不可履行，并且新时间获得双方明确确认，或双方明确决定不改/取消，或你已准确记录“改期提议待答、原时间我无法到”的状态，本轮完成。**发出两个选项不等于对方接受，日历改动也不等于活动已经发生。** 若活动已开始、多人计划出现损失或存在权力胁迫，退出这份普通事前协调，使用相应恢复或专业支持。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只说“那天可能不行”：** 把现在已知的确定程度说清；确定不能赴原约就直说，不让对方继续为旧时段准备。
- **给出一堆新时间却都不确定：** 先查自己的真实可用时间，再给少量可履行选项。
- **对方不回复，自己把日历改好了：** 记新提议待答并说明原计划对你不成立，不把个人日历当双方协议。
- **订位或费用已经发生：** 查本次场所规则、联系实际负责人，先核费用/时间再决定，不承诺免费改期。
- **对方不愿改：** 接受决定，必要时处理取消和已有交接，不反复劝说到对方答应。

### 假设、替代与副作用

适用于自愿的普通两人或小范围未开始安排，不处理已发生爽约、大型集体活动、工作劳动权益、危机或操控对方。提前多久通知、理由说多少取决于实际约定与关系，没有跨文化统一分钟数。听觉、视觉、语言或设备不便时可以用双方能读回的短文字、电话复述或纸面确认；不要求所有人用同一日历软件。副作用是对方可能没空改，你需要接受旧计划失效的现实。

## English

Load this Skill when an ordinary meeting, call or small activity **was already agreed and has not begun**, you know the old time no longer works for you, and you would still like to do it at another suitable time. Tell the other person promptly and clearly, offer real alternatives and let them freely accept or refuse. Keep old and new plan states accurate. This is not recovery after a no-show, a unilateral calendar edit or a way to pressure someone into a change.

### Preparation and inputs

Find the real agreed activity, original date and time, place or online mode, participants and any booking or money already committed. Choose one or two new times you can actually keep, with weekday, date and local time. For different time zones, confirm both sides' time separately. For a ticket or venue booking, check this provider's current change terms or ask the responsible person; do not assume a free move, refund or preserved seat. You can say the old time no longer works without inventing a private emergency.

### Execution

1. **Tell the decision maker when you know.** Use the actual shared channel and name the specific plan and old time that no longer works. “Can we change it?” alone makes them guess which plan you mean.
2. **Offer workable options that can be refused.** For example: “I cannot make our __ at __ now. I would still like to meet and can do __ at __ or __ at __. If neither works for you, it is okay not to move it and we can decide whether to cancel.” Only offer times you can really keep, not placeholders you have yet to check.
3. **Name existing costs and ownership.** If the other person or venue booked or paid, ask who can change it, when an answer is needed and whether a fee or restriction applies. State and complete any real action you own. Do not change or cancel their booking or approve a charge in their name without agreement. Take rights or billing questions to the actual provider's rules or qualified help.
4. **Wait for their real choice.** When they accept a new time, reply with the full current version: activity, date, time, place or mode and booking owner. If they cannot change it, accept that answer and use the cancel flow if needed. No reply is pending, not consent, even if a message was delivered or read.
5. **Handle the old plan explicitly.** If you cannot attend at the original time, say “I will not arrive at the original time” even if a new slot is still undecided. Update a calendar you control or send a formal replacement invitation only after both sides agree, and check any shared reservation actually changed. Do not leave old and new entries both labelled confirmed.

### Success and stop

The other person knows the original time will not work for you, and a new time is clearly accepted by both, or you both choose not to move it or cancel, or you accurately keep “change proposed, reply pending, I cannot attend the original” as the state. **Two options sent are not acceptance, and a calendar change is not the event.** For an activity already underway, losses in a group plan or coercion, leave this ordinary advance-change task for the appropriate recovery or qualified support.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Only “that day might be hard” is said:** State how certain you are. If the old time is impossible, tell them directly so they stop preparing for it.
- **You offer many times that are not free:** Check your real availability and give a small number of usable options.
- **They do not reply but you changed your calendar:** Keep the new proposal pending and state the old time no longer works for you. Your private calendar is not mutual agreement.
- **A reservation or charge already exists:** Read this venue's rules and contact the actual owner; verify fee and deadline before deciding, without promising a free change.
- **They do not want to move it:** Accept their choice and handle cancellation and any handoff where needed, not repeated persuasion until they agree.

### Assumptions, alternatives and side effects

This is for voluntary ordinary plans for two or a few people before they begin, not a missed appointment, large group event, employment rights, crisis or manipulation. Notice time and how much reason to share depend on the real agreement and relationship, not a universal cultural minute count. For hearing, sight, language or device needs, use short readable text, a phone readback or paper confirmation. Everyone need not use the same calendar app. The other person may be unavailable, and the old plan still needs to be treated honestly.

### Sources

- Original synthesis. There is no universal etiquette deadline for moving an ordinary plan. For a specific reservation or fee, follow that venue's or provider's official terms for operations and cost.
