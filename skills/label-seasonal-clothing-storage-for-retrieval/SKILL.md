## 中文

当你已经把干净、干透的换季衣物按护理要求放进若干箱、袋或挂衣区，下一步需要**下季能准确找回其中一件**时，用这份 Skill 建立外部标识和位置指针。这不是泛用家中所有物品的收纳法，也不重新决定衣物是否可存；发现潮湿或虫迹先停止贴标签并回到衣物/存处处理。

### 准备与输入

确认每个容器或挂区当前实际有什么、放在哪个架位或柜层、还有谁需要取用，以及标签能否贴在**容器而不是衣物面料**上。准备便于阅读/触摸的标签和不易损坏箱体的标记方法。共享空间里不把姓名、贵重物品详情、尺寸、医疗信息或房门密码写到外露标签；需要辨别时用中性编号。箱子太重或位置太高，先把它移到可安全取放处再继续。

### 执行

1. **给每个单元一个稳定代号。** 例如“冬-外套-02”或“柜A-03”，一个箱/袋/挂区只对应一个代号；别把同一代号贴在两箱上。写在实际外侧能看见的位置，避免开箱或搬下整叠才知道它是谁。
2. **标签只写够用的内容。** 写季节或使用时期、衣物大类、1–2 件你最可能查找的物品、当前放置区；若多位同住者共用，按已同意的方式标识归属。箱内完整清单如非真实需要就不建，避免两个详细列表很快彼此冲突。
3. **需要跨柜查找时做最小位置表。** 在一张纸或现有可访问笔记里仅记录“代号 → 当前柜/架位”，容器外标签仍是内容摘要的权威。没有必要引入新软件。把位置表放在你实际会查的位置；搬动箱子时更新这一条位置指针。
4. **测试能否找回。** 任选一件已收存的衣物，从标签或位置表确定应开哪个单元；能在不翻开所有箱的情况下找到，并能按原单元放回，才算标识有效。找错箱则改标签或分组，不让一张好看的标签替代事实。
5. **后续变化时当场更新。** 拿走、放入或移动衣物后更改对应容器标签或位置指针；空箱移除旧标签，避免下一次把旧目录当现货。复查时若发现潮湿、异味变化或虫迹，先处理衣物与存处，再维护目录。

### 成功条件、常见问题与补救

每个收存单元有唯一、可读且不伤衣的外部标识；你能够按一个具体衣物线索找到正确单元并放回。位置变动有单一对应记录，不需要打开全部箱子猜。

- **标签写得很全但找不到箱子：** 增加代号到实际架位的指针，不复制一份完整清单。
- **两箱都叫“冬衣”：** 增加唯一编号与有区分度的大类/关键件，再做一次检索测试。
- **标签脱落或挡住开箱：** 换适用的容器外标记，别把强胶直接贴在衣物面料或装饰上。
- **箱子搬走后笔记仍写旧位：** 立即更正位置指针，不在两处都保留不同的“权威”地点。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

面向普通衣物的换季收存，不包含身份证件、贵重保险清单或租赁权利。住处大小、文字能力、视力与同住隐私不同；可用大字高对比、触觉编号或可信协助，但编号仍必须唯一。共享柜子先约定谁可取用，不能把标签当作拥有他人物品的授权。维护标签需要少量时间，换来下次真正找得到。

## English

Use this Skill after clean fully dry out-of-season clothes are already packed by their care needs in boxes, bags, or rail sections and you need to **find a particular garment next season**. Create external labels and location pointers. This is not a general system for every household item and does not reassess storage readiness. Stop labelling and deal with the item or place first if you find dampness or pests.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm what each packed unit actually contains, its shelf or closet location, who else needs access, and whether a label can go on the **container rather than the fabric**. Have readable or tactile labels and a marker suitable for the container. In a shared area, do not put names, valuable-item details, clothing sizes, medical details, or door codes on a public label. Use a neutral code when needed. Move a too-heavy or too-high unit to a safely reachable position before this task.

### Execution

1. **Give each unit one stable code.** For example “winter-coats-02” or “closet-A-03”. One box, bag, or rail section has one code; do not reuse it on another box. Put it on an exterior face you can see without opening or unstacking everything.
2. **Write just enough on the label.** Include season or use period, clothing category, one or two things you are likely to look for, and present storage zone. Where housemates share access, mark ownership only in the way everyone agreed. Do not make a second complete item inventory without a real need; two detailed lists soon disagree.
3. **Use a minimal location map when several closets exist.** On paper or an existing accessible note, record only “code → current closet/shelf”. The box label remains authoritative for its contents. No new software is needed. Keep the location map where you will actually check it. Update its one pointer when a box moves.
4. **Test real retrieval.** Pick one stored garment and use the label or map to choose the unit. The scheme passes when you find it without opening every box and can return it to the same unit. If the label led to the wrong box, change the label or group rather than trusting a neat label over reality.
5. **Update when things change.** Removing, adding, or moving a garment changes its unit label or location pointer at the same time. Remove old labels from empty boxes so an outdated directory does not pose as stock. If a later check finds dampness, changed odour, or pests, handle the clothing and place before editing the map.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

Each stored unit has a unique, readable external label that does not damage clothing. You can find and return one specific garment by its clue. Location moves have a single matching pointer; finding an item does not require opening every box.

- **The label lists everything but you cannot locate the box:** Add a code-to-shelf pointer, not a duplicate full inventory.
- **Two boxes both say “winter clothes”:** Give them distinct codes, categories, or key items and test retrieval again.
- **The label falls off or obstructs opening:** Use a suitable exterior container marker, not strong adhesive on garment fabric or trim.
- **A box moved but the note points to its old shelf:** Correct the location pointer now rather than maintaining two competing authoritative places.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is for ordinary seasonal clothing, not identity documents, insured valuables, or tenancy rights. Home size, literacy, vision, and shared privacy vary. Use large high-contrast text, tactile codes, or trusted help while keeping codes unique. Agree on access in a shared closet; a label is not authority over another person's property. Labels take maintenance time and make the item retrievable later.
