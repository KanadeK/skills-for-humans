## 中文

当你想练习**根据眼前实物**画，而不是凭记忆补出“杯子应该长什么样”时，用这份 Skill 画一张短轮廓草图。完成物是能看到你观察过边缘和几个关键转折的纸上记录，不要求写实画作或整页构图方案。明暗分区可另做一次练习；这里先处理实物的外形。

### 准备与输入

挑一个属于你或允许观察的普通静物，如杯子、叶子或盒子。摆在稳定、可看清边缘的位置，不选热物、锐器、活体或需要复制别人付费图样的对象。固定你看它的坐姿/站位，纸放稳；若移动或视觉不便，用较大物体、高对比背景、大纸、粗笔或可信协助。确认你能安全摸到物体时，也可沿外缘轻摸辅助理解形状，但不摸不明材质。

### 执行

1. **先看整体。** 眼睛沿实物外缘走一圈，找最高、最低、最左、最右的位置，再看一个明显凹口、把手或转角。用笔在纸上轻点出大致范围；不必先给对象贴“完美圆形”标签。
2. **把大边缘连起来。** 反复在实物和纸之间抬头对照，从一处清楚的边线开始画；遇到弯曲就留曲线，遇到折角就换方向。先保留整体相对宽高，不急着画花纹和阴影。
3. **补两个实际地标。** 在草图里标出至少两个从当前角度真正看见的内部或边缘特征，比如杯口椭圆、盒盖接线或叶脉主轴。看不到的背面不要按记忆补齐；草图记录的是这个观察位置。
4. **做一次实物对照。** 放下笔，看实物的最高点、最宽处和一个特征与草图的相对位置。只挑一处差别修正或旁注“这里看偏了”；重复把线描黑不是观察证据。
5. **收工。** 写一句“我原以为____，实物更像____”，例如“把手比杯身窄”。保留草图与备注；时间到就停，细节没画满不代表练习失败。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

纸上有一个从固定视角观察的整体轮廓、至少两个可在实物上找到的地标，以及一处对照修正或差别备注。相似度不是唯一评分；凭记忆画出一个通用符号却没抬头看实物，不满足本篇。

- **总从头修改，纸快磨破：** 用轻线先圈整体范围，只修最影响识别的一处，其余写备注。
- **对象移动或视角改变：** 放回原位或选定新视角重新标大范围，不拼接两个角度当同一次观察。
- **被细节吸走，外形没画全：** 暂停纹理和阴影，先把最高/最低/左右边界连成一圈。
- **看不清边缘：** 换高对比背景、放大对象或请可信的人描述；若仍无法观察，暂停，不虚构“看见”的线。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这是一张普通练习草图，不教人体解剖、工程测绘或受保护角色临摹。纸笔、握法和感官条件可调整；可用固定纸板支撑或易握笔。触觉探索仅限你确认安全的物体；需要协助时说明哪些线来自你的观察、哪些来自协助描述。草图可能很粗糙，但一处真实修正比整页装饰更有用。

## English

Use this Skill when you want to draw **from an object in front of you** instead of filling in from memory what a cup is supposed to look like. Make a short contour sketch that shows observed edges and a few landmarks. It need not be a realistic finished picture or a whole-page composition plan. Mapping light and dark can be another exercise; here the object shape comes first.

### Preparation and inputs

Choose an ordinary still object you own or may observe, such as a cup, leaf, or box. Place it where its edges are visible and stable. Avoid hot objects, blades, living subjects, or copying someone else's paid design. Keep one seated or standing viewpoint and steady the paper. If movement or sight is limited, use a larger object, contrasting background, larger paper, easy-grip pen, or trusted help. You may lightly feel an outer edge only when the object is known to be safe to touch.

### Execution

1. **Look at the whole.** Follow the outer edge with your eyes and find its top, bottom, left, and right points, then a notable notch, handle, or turn. Lightly mark an approximate extent on paper. Do not label it a perfect circle before looking.
2. **Connect the large edges.** Look back and forth between object and page. Start at a clear edge; keep curves curved and turn where the real edge turns. Preserve the overall width-to-height relation before adding pattern or shade.
3. **Add two real landmarks.** Mark at least two internal or edge features visible from this viewpoint, such as a cup rim, box lid seam, or leaf's main vein. Do not invent a hidden back from memory. The sketch records this observed position.
4. **Compare once with the object.** Put down the pen and compare the top, widest part, and one landmark with the sketch. Correct one meaningful difference or note “I misread this part.” Darkening the same line repeatedly is not observation.
5. **Close the pass.** Write “I expected ____; the object looks more like ____,” such as “the handle is narrower than the cup.” Keep the sketch and note. Stop when time is up; an empty area of the page is not failure.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

Your paper has an overall contour from one viewpoint, at least two landmarks you can find on the real object, and one comparison correction or note. Likeness is not the only score. A generic symbol drawn from memory without returning your gaze to the object does not meet this task.

- **You keep restarting and damage the paper:** Mark the extent lightly, correct one important difference, and note the rest.
- **The object moves or your viewpoint changes:** Restore the position or choose a new viewpoint and mark a fresh extent. Do not merge two angles as one observation.
- **Details distract you before the outline exists:** Pause texture and shade. Connect top, bottom, left, and right into an overall shape first.
- **The edge is unclear:** Use a contrasting background, enlarge the object, or ask a trusted person to describe it. Pause if you still cannot observe; do not invent a visible edge.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is an ordinary practice sketch, not anatomy training, engineering survey, or copying a protected character. Adjust paper, grip, and sensory method; a board can support the page and an easy-grip pen can help. Feel only a known safe object. With help, distinguish lines you observed from those described to you. The sketch may be rough; one real correction matters more than a decorated page.
