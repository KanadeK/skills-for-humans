## 中文

当第一批食物已经在普通浅锅里开始加热，你需要决定**何时翻、何时搅、何时调火和何时离锅**时，加载这份 Skill。结果是按当前可靠配方取得所需锅面状态，并用食物特定证据结束；不是把食物翻得越频繁越熟，也不是让外表上色代替内部安全。深油炸、失控明火、专业极高温锅法不在范围内。

### 准备与输入

确认配方对这件食物的动作：要连续翻动、等一面先接触锅、还是分阶段加料；还要有预计观察点、完成信号和任何适用核心温度。工具应适合锅具表面、握得稳；熟食落点与生食区域分开。食物量过多时先用[分批流程](../saute-in-manageable-batches/SKILL.md)，不要用更大火力争取所有食物同时接触锅。

### 执行步骤

1. **看锅面实际在做什么。** 从安全距离观察配方要求的颜色、质地、水分和声音；在食物接触锅的位置检查，而非只看锅边。油无烟、锅柄稳定、你能持续看守，是继续条件。没有反应时先查火力和批量，不靠突然开最大档测试。
2. **按配方的事件翻动。** 快速炒制可能需频繁翻动，另一些煎制需要先让一面接触锅再翻；使用适当工具轻稳动作，不为“忙起来”而不停铲锅。厚薄不一的食物若完成差异明显，分开处理或按可靠配方调整，不能用一面颜色替整批打勾。
3. **小幅调整热度并复看。** 锅面越来越深而内部仍未完成时，在安全可达时降热并按食物专属说明继续；若锅中积水使目标上色无法实现，分批或承认改成另一种烹法。油冒烟、焦味明显、剧烈飞溅时立即停止热输入，别把这种信号解释为“更香”。
4. **做食物特定完成检查。** 蔬菜可按可靠配方的颜色、质地与用途判断；肉、禽、海鲜等需安全温度/时间时用对应[熟度核对](../check-doneness-and-store-leftovers/SKILL.md)，正确测中心且避免交叉污染。不要生尝或只看汁液颜色。
5. **到点离锅。** 达到条件时关热或按配方取出到干净、合适的容器；余热仍会改变食物，不让它在热锅里无限等待配菜。若要回锅合并、加酱或另做后段，按相应配方步骤，而非延长这一段直到所有菜同时结束。

### 完成与停止

你能说出本批何时按配方翻动、实际锅面与食物中心各有什么证据、为什么现在离锅，就是完成。食物安全条件不可核实、锅开始冒烟、油失控、器具不稳或你无法看守时停止并处理原因；火灾、燃气异味或设备故障交当地紧急/专业渠道。

### 常见报错与补救

- **一直翻，想等上色：** 回到这道食物的配方事件；需要锅面接触时给它受控接触时间，不把搅拌当安全保证。
- **一面焦深但内部不足：** 降低高热、按食物特定做法继续并复查中心，不能端走当已熟。
- **锅面全是汁水：** 若配方目标是干煎上色，分批或改变计划；不能通过猛加油解决拥挤。
- **时间到了却没量到安全温度：** 对需要温度证据的食物继续受控加热和核对，不用表面色泽补签。
- **离锅后仍在烫锅上等其他菜：** 转入合适的盛放/保温路径，不让余热继续无记录地改变结果。

### 假设、替代与副作用

普通煎炒的翻动频率、油量和温度没有一套跨食物通用值；按可靠配方、锅具说明与当地食品安全指导做。视觉或听力线索不易取得时可用可访问计时器、可靠温度计、现场可信的人描述与适用工具；不能让某个感官成为唯一安全证明。握力或站立负担高时减批量、用更稳的铲具或换低负荷技法。副作用是你可能洗一个温度计，却不必向焦黑外表索取保证书。

## English

Load this Skill after the first batch begins heating in an ordinary shallow pan, when you must decide **when to turn, stir, adjust heat, and remove it**. Reach the surface result the reliable recipe asks for, then end on food-specific evidence. More turning is not automatically safer, and a brown outside is not proof of a safe centre. Deep frying, uncontrolled flame, and extreme-heat professional wok work are outside this task.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm this recipe's movement: continuous turning, waiting for one face to contact the pan, or staged ingredients. Know the next observation and any required core temperature. Use a tool compatible with the pan that you can grip reliably; keep a finished-food landing place separate from raw food. For excessive volume, use [manageable batches](../saute-in-manageable-batches/SKILL.md) rather than more heat to crowd every piece onto the pan.

### Execution steps

1. **Observe what the pan is doing.** From a safe position, check the recipe's colour, texture, moisture, and sound where food touches the pan, not only at an empty edge. Continue only with no smoking oil, a stable handle, and your attention present. If little happens, check heat and load before jumping to maximum power.
2. **Turn at the recipe's event.** Quick stir-frying may need frequent movement; another shallow-pan method needs one side to contact the surface first. Use a suitable tool and steady motion rather than stirring merely to look busy. If pieces of different thickness finish differently, separate or follow reliable food-specific adjustments; one browned face cannot certify the batch.
3. **Adjust heat a little and observe again.** If the surface darkens while the centre is not ready, lower heat safely and continue under food-specific directions. If excess liquid prevents the intended browning, divide the batch or acknowledge a different cooking method. Smoke, strong scorched odour, or violent splatter is a heat-stop signal, not “more flavour.”
4. **Check the food's actual endpoint.** Vegetables may have recipe-specific colour, texture, and use goals. Meat, poultry, seafood, and any food with required safe time or temperature need the applicable [doneness check](../check-doneness-and-store-leftovers/SKILL.md), with a correctly measured centre and clean tools. Do not taste raw food or rely on juice colour alone.
5. **Leave the pan when done.** Switch off or transfer to a clean suitable dish under the recipe. Residual heat continues changing food; it should not wait in a hot pan indefinitely for other dishes. If a later step combines batches or adds sauce, follow that stage's directions rather than extending this one until the whole meal is ready.

### Success and stop

You can say when this batch was turned under the recipe, what surface and centre evidence you obtained, and why it leaves the pan now. Stop when food safety cannot be established, the pan smokes, oil is uncontrolled, cookware unstable, or you cannot attend it. Fire, gas odour, and equipment faults belong to local emergency or qualified support.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You keep turning while waiting for browning:** Return to this food's recipe event. Allow controlled contact when required; stirring is not a safety certificate.
- **One face is dark but the centre is not ready:** Lower high heat, continue under food-specific directions, and recheck the centre rather than serving it as done.
- **The pan fills with juices:** If the recipe intended dry pan browning, divide the batch or change the plan. More oil cannot create room in a crowded pan.
- **Time ended but safe temperature was not measured:** Continue controlled cooking and check food that needs temperature evidence; colour cannot sign for it.
- **Finished food waits in the still-hot pan:** Move it to a suitable serving or holding path instead of allowing unrecorded residual cooking.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Ordinary sautéing has no universal turning frequency, oil amount, or temperature across foods. Follow reliable recipes, cookware directions, and local food-safety guidance. For limited visual or hearing cues, use an accessible timer, reliable thermometer, description from someone present, and suitable tools; no single sense alone proves safety. Reduce batch size, use a steadier spatula, or choose a lower-load method if grip or standing is difficult. You may wash a thermometer and avoid asking a blackened surface for a guarantee.
