## 中文

当一次普通多人活动已经安排好，但有人以为是周六、有人以为是周日，或对地点、分工、带什么、何时开始有不同理解时，加载这份 Skill。你的目标是**分清已确认事实与个人理解，再得到一个新的共同安排**。不是选出“记错的人”，也不把沉默算成同意。

若实际参与人数、天气或场地后来变了，先按那些变化核对容量或替代方案。本 Skill 只处理大家对同一安排的不同理解；工作指令、合同争议、收费责任、欺骗、胁迫和危机不在范围内。

### 准备与输入

写下各方当前会采取的不同动作，例如 A 会周六到旧地址，B 会周日到新地址。找到可核对的共同信息：原邀请、群消息、预订确认或后来明确更新。只看与这次行动有关的部分，不把私人对话整段转发到群里。标出谁有权确认场地、订位或时间，以及最晚何时必须定案，才能避免有人白跑。

### 执行

1. **暂停会扩大误会的动作。** 在日期、地点、人数或必要分工未定时，不让人按未经确认的版本出发或替大家下不可退的单。先发一句：“现在有两个版本，先别按旧消息行动；我会在 [时间] 发确认结果。”
2. **把事实、理解和提议分开。** 例如“预订邮件写周六 18:00”是可核对事实；“我以为后来改到周日”是理解；“要不改周日”是新提议。原记录也可能缺少后来口头更改，不把单一截图当万能判决。
3. **只问会改变安排的问题。** 向掌握信息的人核对：“你确认过店家周日可用吗？”向参与者问：“你现在能接受周六 18:00，还是需要新时间？”让每个人有机会纠正自己的版本；不要求公开解释为什么记错。
4. **明确谁能决定什么。** 场地或服务方确认其可用时间，组织者确认自己能负责的预订，参与者决定自己是否参加。若分工涉及别人的时间或物品，请本人答应；群里点赞不能替代必要的具体同意。
5. **形成一个当前版本。** 发一条包含日期、时间、地点、各自明确答应的事项、仍待确认项和版本时间的短消息。例如：“截至今晚 8 点：店家确认周六 18:00，4 人确认参加；周日没有预订。请仍想去的人在 9 点前回复；未确认的人不计入订位。”需要改变原计划时，让受影响者有退出选择。
6. **逐一处理缺口。** 若关键人未回复或预订不可用，在决策截止前只标“待确认”；到截止仍无法形成可执行安排，就延期或取消，并通知会因此改变行程的人。不要同时保留两个“最终版”。

### 完成条件

相关人收到同一个有时间标记的当前安排，并且其中的关键时间、地点和承诺都经实际有权者确认。待确认项和下一次核对时间也写明。若大家不能同意，清楚暂停、延期或取消，比制造表面一致更可执行。并非每个人都必须认同你对旧对话的解释。

### 常见报错与补救

- **两个截图都看似正确：** 查消息的发送时间和后续明确更改；若仍不清楚，就不再争旧约定，提出一个新选择并征求必要同意。
- **群聊里只有一半人表态：** 把已确认和未回复分开；涉及座位、钱或交通时，不替未回复者作承诺。用合适的直接渠道联系关键人一次。
- **有人已经按旧版出发：** 直接联系并给出当前确切状态；不要以为新群消息会及时到达。可能造成的白跑或费用要诚实说明。
- **有人不同意新版本：** 允许其退出，或检查另一个真实可行的时间/地点；不要把多数人的方便说成所有人的同意。
- **负责预订的人也不确定：** 由其向实际服务方查证，查询期间保持“尚未确认”。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里是自愿、普通的小型活动，各方可以选择参加或退出。文化、关系和沟通渠道会影响确认方式；对行动、听觉、视觉或阅读支持有需求的人，应以其能接收和回复的方式确认。你可能发现自己的记忆也不准确，或必须改期；让事实和新的选择可见，比赢得聊天记录审判更重要。

## English

Load this Skill when an ordinary group event was arranged, but some people expect Saturday and others Sunday, or disagree about place, roles, items to bring, or start time. Your goal is to **separate confirmed facts from individual interpretations and arrive at one new shared arrangement**. You do not need to identify “the person who remembered wrong,” and silence is not agreement.

If the actual headcount, weather, or venue later changed, check capacity or alternatives for that change first. This Skill addresses different understandings of the same arrangement. Work instructions, contract or payment disputes, deception, coercion, and crises are outside scope.

### Preparation and inputs

Write what each person would currently do: A may arrive Saturday at the old address while B expects Sunday at a new one. Find shared information that can be checked: invitation, group message, booking confirmation, or later explicit update. Use only the part relevant to this action; do not forward entire private conversations to the group. Identify who can confirm the venue, booking, or time, and the last decision time that prevents a wasted trip.

### Execution

1. **Pause actions that spread the mismatch.** While date, place, count, or essential role is unsettled, do not send people to an unconfirmed version or place a nonrefundable order for them. Say: “We have two versions right now. Please hold off on the old message; I will confirm by [time].”
2. **Separate facts, interpretations, and proposals.** “The booking email says Saturday at 6” is checkable; “I thought we later moved to Sunday” is an interpretation; “Could we use Sunday?” is a proposal. The original record may omit a later spoken change, so one screenshot is not a universal verdict.
3. **Ask only what changes the plan.** Ask the person with the information: “Did the restaurant confirm Sunday?” Ask attendees: “Can you now do Saturday at 6, or do you need a new time?” Give each person room to correct their version without requiring a public explanation of why they misremembered.
4. **Identify who can decide each part.** The venue or provider confirms its available time; the organizer confirms a booking they control; participants decide whether they attend. Ask the person who would take on a role or bring an item to agree to it. A reaction in the group chat is not a substitute for a necessary explicit yes.
5. **Make one current version.** Send a short time-stamped note with date, time, place, individually accepted responsibilities, pending items, and the version time. For example: “As of 8 tonight, the restaurant confirms Saturday at 6, four people have confirmed, and Sunday is not booked. Please reply by 9 if you still want to join; unconfirmed people are not included in the booking.” Let affected people opt out if the original plan changes.
6. **Resolve gaps one by one.** If a necessary person has not replied or a booking is unavailable, mark it pending until the decision deadline. If there is still no workable shared plan then, postpone or cancel and tell anyone whose journey changes. Do not keep two “final” versions alive.

### Success

Affected people receive the same time-stamped current arrangement, with critical time, place, and commitments confirmed by whoever can actually confirm them. Pending items and the next check time are visible. If the group cannot agree, a clear pause, postponement, or cancellation is more workable than apparent consensus. People do not have to accept your interpretation of the old conversation.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Two screenshots both look right:** Check when each was sent and any explicit later change. If the history stays unclear, stop arguing over the old plan and propose a new choice that needs real agreement.
- **Only half the group answers:** Separate confirmed from unanswered people. Do not commit seats, money, or transport for the latter. Try one suitable direct contact with a necessary person.
- **Someone already travelled under the old version:** Contact them directly with the exact current status; a fresh group message may arrive too late. Explain any wasted journey or cost honestly.
- **Someone declines the new version:** Let them leave or check one other genuinely feasible time or place. A majority's convenience is not everyone's consent.
- **The booking owner is unsure too:** Have that person check with the actual provider. Keep the booking unconfirmed while they ask.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This is a voluntary ordinary small gathering where people may attend or leave. Culture, relationships, and channels affect how confirmation works. Use a way each person can receive and answer if they need mobility, hearing, vision, or reading support. You may discover your own memory was wrong or need to change the date. Making facts and the new choice visible matters more than winning a trial by chat log.
