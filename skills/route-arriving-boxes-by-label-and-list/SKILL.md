## 中文

当普通搬家箱实际到达新住处、第一轮需要把箱子送到正确区域时加载。你会对**眼前每只箱的标识与目的地清单逐一核对**，让能确认的箱分送、不能确认的箱暂存待问；不必为找目的地拆箱。这里不替代搬运安全判断、承运方正式收货记录、损失交涉或长期收纳。

### 准备与输入

拿到本次搬迁的最新 `箱 ID → 第一站` 表、谁有权回答去向、目标区域的实际名称，以及双方可识别的暂存位置。问实际搬运者如何处理其正式清单；你的分送表与该清单是不同记录。不要靠“这只看着像厨房箱”开始搬运。

### 执行

1. **逐只看实际箱，不预填已到。** 从可安全查看的位置读 ID 和第一站；若箱体破损、漏出或需要超出你能力的搬动，先停下并请合适的人或服务方处理。只记录你实际看到的箱，未见的留为未见。
2. **将箱与清单做一对一核对。** ID 必须在清单里恰有一行，而且箱外第一站与该行一致。确认后把它记为“已见／待分送”。无 ID、重复 ID、清单缺行或两处目的地冲突的箱放进双方知道的待核区，不猜内容或擅自开箱。
3. **只分送已核对的箱。** 告诉实际搬运者第一站的区域名，按已确认的搬运能力与通道条件处理；本 Skill 不教提举，也不强迫你自己搬。送达后由接收者或可信协助者确认该箱在该区域，清单行再改为“已在第一站”。
4. **给待核箱一个负责人。** 在清单旁记实际可见的 ID 或外观、冲突是什么、谁会联系装箱者或有权接收者，以及暂存位置。核实后再更新箱外与清单，重新走匹配步骤；无法核实的仍保持待核。
5. **收束第一轮。** 逐行标出已在第一站、已见待分送、待核，以及清单有但现场未见的 ID。将未见或其他延误交给负责后续追踪的人；若使用服务方，按其实际正式交接流程记录事实。本次只证明第一轮分送状态，不声称所有内容齐全。

### 完成与停止

每只实际到达的普通箱都有确认后的第一站，或一个可追踪的待核位置和负责人；清单中未亲眼见到的箱仍标未见。目的地冲突、无法识别、疑似不安全的箱不得按猜测分送。不要用个人分送表替代承运方要求的清单或签收流程。

### 常见报错与补救

- **箱上是“厨房”，表里是“卧室”：** 暂存，找有权决定的人核对；同步改对箱标与表后再送。
- **两只箱共用一个 ID：** 两只都待核，联系装箱者辨认，再更正唯一编号；不凭摆放顺序分配。
- **箱子到了但表上没有它：** 记为未列箱、暂停分送，请有权者确认；不要擅自打开含私人内容的箱。
- **表中有箱，现场没看见：** 保持“未见”，告知后续追踪负责人；第一轮不能把预计到达写成已到达。
- **标签不适合某位接收者读取：** 用已约定的大字、语音、触觉标识或可信协助者共同读回，并让此人能核对相同的表行。

### 假设、替代与副作用

本 Skill 只做普通物品的首轮区域分送，不判断合同、索赔或物品内部状态。美国 FMCSA 关于正式货物清单的事项只适用于它描述的运输情境；其他地方和服务方要核对自己的规则。行走、视力或握持不便时，你可以在固定位置读表并指挥愿意且有能力的人观察箱标；不要为完成表格强行搬箱。副作用是“应该都到了”变成可检查的已见、未见和待核。

## English

Load this Skill when ordinary moving boxes have actually arrived at the new place and you need a first pass to send each one to the right area. You will **compare each box in front of you with its ID-to-destination list**, route confirmed boxes and hold uncertain ones. You need not unpack to find a first stop. This does not replace safe-handling decisions, the carrier's formal delivery records, loss discussions or permanent storage.

### Preparation and inputs

Get the latest `box ID → first stop` list, the person authorised to answer destination questions, real names for the receiving areas, and an agreed hold area. Ask the actual carrier how its formal inventory is handled; your routing list is a different record. Do not begin moving a box merely because it looks like a kitchen carton.

### Execution

1. **Look at each actual box; do not pre-fill arrival.** From a position where you can check safely, read its ID and first stop. If a box is damaged, leaking or needs handling beyond your ability, pause and ask a suitable person or provider. Record only boxes you actually see; unseen ones remain unseen.
2. **Match box to list one-to-one.** The ID must have exactly one list row, and the outside first stop must agree with it. Then mark it “seen / awaiting routing.” Put an unlabelled box, duplicated ID, missing row or conflicting destination in an agreed hold area. Do not guess its contents or open it without authority.
3. **Route confirmed boxes only.** Tell the actual handler the first-stop area. Use separately confirmed handling ability and route conditions; this Skill does not teach lifting or require you to carry the load. After arrival in that area, have a receiver or trusted helper confirm the box is there, then mark its row “at first stop.”
4. **Give every hold an owner.** Beside the list, record the visible ID or description, the conflict, who will contact the packer or authorised receiver, and the hold location. When resolved, update both box and list and repeat the match. An unresolved box remains held.
5. **Close the first pass.** Mark each row at first stop, seen but awaiting routing, held for a question, or listed but not seen on site. Pass unseen boxes or other delays to the person handling follow-up. If using a carrier, record facts through its actual formal delivery process. This pass proves routing status, not that every item inside is present.

### Success and stop

Every ordinary box actually received either has a confirmed first stop or a traceable hold location and owner; boxes you did not see stay marked unseen. Do not route an unidentifiable, conflicting or potentially unsafe box by guesswork. Your personal routing list does not replace the carrier's required inventory or receipt process.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Box says “kitchen”; list says “bedroom”:** Hold it and ask the authorised decision-maker. Correct box and list together before routing.
- **Two boxes share one ID:** Hold both, ask the packer to identify them and correct the unique IDs. Their order in a stack is not proof.
- **An arrived box has no list row:** Record it as unlisted and pause routing until an authorised person confirms it. Do not open private contents on your own.
- **A listed box is not in sight:** Keep it “unseen” and tell the follow-up owner. Expected arrival is not observed arrival.
- **One receiver cannot read the mark:** Use agreed large print, audio, tactile labelling or a trusted readback so that person can check the same list row.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers first-pass area routing of ordinary goods, not contract rights, claims or the condition of contents. US FMCSA formal-inventory details belong to the transport situation it describes; check the actual rules elsewhere. If walking, sight or grip makes direct handling difficult, stay in one place with the list and ask a willing capable person to observe the box marks. Do not force a lift to finish a checklist. The side effect is replacing “all boxes should be here” with checkable seen, unseen and held states.
