## 中文

当你在普通室内植物上看见疑似小虫、蛛网、黏性痕迹或扩大的斑点，尚不知道原因时，加载这份 Skill。结果是**把疑似问题与其他植物分开、留下可核对证据，并找到下一步辨认途径**；隔离本身不等于治好。

### 准备与输入

先确认这盆植物可以安全搬动，有一处仍满足其基本光温需求、却不碰到其他植物的位置。准备手机照片、纸笔或语音记录。未知植株、尖刺、刺激性汁液、大型重盆或可能触及宠物儿童的临时摆放，先安排合适协助和安全位置；不要为了“隔离”制造新的跌落或接触风险。

### 执行

1. **看见什么就记什么。** 检查叶背、新芽、叶柄与盆周围；记录发现日期、位置、虫体或斑点外观、是否可移动。照片尽量包含整株及局部。不要把一片黄叶直接诊断为虫害。
2. **先拉开距离。** 将疑似受影响的盆栽与其他植物分开，不让叶片和盆器接触；移动前后洗手，普通工具若接触到问题部位先按材质与制造商允许方式清洁。不要把脱落物带到其他盆中。
3. **检查邻近植物。** 用同样位置和症状清单观察它们并记录；只因相邻不等于已经感染。新买或刚从室外回来的植物也单独留意，不直接混入原有植株。
4. **核实身份再决定处理。** 查可信的园艺推广机构资料，或带清晰照片向可靠园艺服务询问。虫、螨、病害和光水问题的应对不同；不要把家用清洁剂或未核对标签的药剂喷上去。
5. **安排复查与去向。** 记下一次观察时间、需要确认的特征及求助渠道。若感染范围扩大或无法可靠辨认，维持隔离并求助；是否丢弃植株或使用获准产品，要基于真实辨认、当地规则和产品标签另行决定。

### 完成与停止

完成时，问题盆与其他植物实际分开，照片或文字足以描述观察结果，邻近植物已查看，下一次复查/求助有入口。你没有因为找到一张相似图片就声称已诊断或治愈。

### 常见报错与补救

- **隔离位置太暗或太冷：** 换一处既能分开、又满足该植物基本要求的位置；若做不到，找有条件的人或园艺服务帮忙。
- **几盆植物都有相似痕迹：** 分别记录，不共享未清洁的工具；请专业来源帮助辨认，避免同时乱喷。
- **只有黄叶、没找到虫体：** 继续观察并检查光、水、温度，别把“可能”写成确诊。
- **手无法翻看叶背：** 用放大照片、镜子或请人协助观察，不为看清楚而折断枝叶。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里负责普通盆栽问题的早期隔离和信息记录，不教杀虫剂、病原鉴定、植物检疫或涉及人体/宠物健康的判断。UC IPM 建议发现虫、螨或病害时及时隔离，并在选择控制方法前辨认问题；本文对尚未确诊的迹象采用同样谨慎的临时隔离。副作用是占用一处空间，并需要复查，不能靠一张照片自动解除。

## English

Load this Skill when an ordinary indoor plant shows possible insects, webbing, sticky traces, or spreading spots and you do not yet know the cause. The result is **separation from other plants, observable evidence, and a reliable identification route**. Isolation alone is not a cure.

### Preparation and inputs

Check that you can move the pot safely and have a stable place that meets its basic light and temperature needs without touching other plants. Prepare a photo, written note, or voice note. For an unidentified, spiny, irritating, or heavy plant, or a temporary spot accessible to children or pets, arrange suitable help and placement first. Containment should not create a fall or contact hazard.

### Execution

1. **Record only what you observe.** Look under leaves, at new growth, stems, and around the pot. Note the date, location, appearance, and whether anything moves. Capture both whole-plant and close-up views when possible. One yellow leaf is not a pest diagnosis.
2. **Separate the plant promptly.** Keep leaves and pots apart from other plants. Wash your hands after contact, and clean ordinary tools used on the suspect area as their materials and maker allow. Do not carry fallen debris to another pot.
3. **Inspect nearby plants.** Use the same observation points and record what you find. Proximity alone does not establish infection. Give extra attention to newly bought plants or those brought in from outdoors before mixing them with established plants.
4. **Identify before treating.** Compare observations with reliable extension guidance or ask a reputable horticultural service using clear photos. Insects, mites, diseases, and light or water problems need different responses. Do not spray household cleaners or an unchecked pesticide onto the plant.
5. **Set a review and next route.** Record when to inspect again, which features still need identification, and who can help. Keep the plant separate if signs spread or remain unconfirmed. Disposal or a labelled treatment requires a separate decision based on identification, local rules, and the actual product label.

### Success and stop

The suspect plant is physically separate, your evidence describes what was seen, nearby plants have been inspected, and a next review or advice route exists. A similar internet image has not been treated as proof of a diagnosis or cure.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The isolation spot is too dark or cold:** Choose another separated place that also meets this plant's basic needs. Seek horticultural or lifting help if none is available.
- **Several plants show similar marks:** Record each one and avoid sharing uncleaned tools. Seek sound identification instead of spraying all plants blindly.
- **There are yellow leaves but no visible pest:** Continue observing and check light, water, and temperature before calling it an infestation.
- **You cannot inspect leaf undersides:** Use an enlarged photo, mirror, or helper; do not break stems to get a view.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

This covers early containment and evidence for an ordinary potted plant, not pesticide use, laboratory diagnosis, plant quarantine law, or human and pet health advice. UC IPM recommends separating a plant with an identified insect, mite, or disease issue and identifying the problem before control. Here, suspicious signs justify temporary separation while the cause remains open. The side effect is a reserved space and follow-up work.
