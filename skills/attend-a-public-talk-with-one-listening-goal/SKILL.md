## 中文

当你想听一场公共图书馆、博物馆或社区机构的普通讲座，却不想结束后只记得“好像讲过很多东西”时，用这份 Skill 带**一个具体听讲目标**参加，留下一页能追溯来源的短笔记。结果不是取得课程资格或掌握整门学科，也不要求现场提问；提问另有流程。

### 准备与输入

从主办方当日公告核题目、讲者、时间、地点或正式线上入口、报名/费用/语言/字幕与可及服务；不要用旧转发帖推断还能入场。写一句“我今天想听懂____”，比如讲者如何解释一个例子或某个观点与反例的关系。备纸笔、可访问的便签或可信协助；按主办方规则处理手机、拍照和录音，默认不录音、不直播、不复制付费讲义。若这场内容涉及医疗、法律或财务决策，只把它当讲者观点的记录，不以讲座替代专业建议。

### 执行

1. **完成真正入场。** 按主办方指定的报名确认、签到或线上链接进入；确认你在正确场次，找到不妨碍他人的位置和必要的字幕/听力支持。无法进入或场次取消时记录事实并停下，不凭宣传资料假称听过。
2. **在讲前写目标与来源。** 记主办方、讲者/活动名和日期，把听讲目标写在最上面。讲者信息未知就标“待确认”，不要为了完整感猜名字。
3. **抓两处与目标有关的内容。** 听到讲者提出观点或示例时，用自己的短句记“讲者说/展示____；例子是____”。区分讲者的主张、他引用别人的资料和你自己的联想；不试图逐字抄全程，以免漏掉下一句关键解释。
4. **标一处仍不清楚的地方。** 写一个具体问号，如“这个结论只适用于哪种情况？”如果讲座后来回答了，就补一句“后来在____处解答”；没回答则留为后续检索/提问线索，不把沉默当默认答案。
5. **离场后做一分钟核对。** 看短笔记是否至少有两处实际听到的要点、一处未解或已解问题，以及能辨认的讲座来源。模糊词补成你自己的可懂句子；不把记忆中不确定的数字写成确定事实。依场馆路线离开或结束官方线上会话。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你实际参加了这场讲座，留下与一个目标有关的**两处讲者内容、一处问题状态和活动来源**。没有问到现场问题也可完成聆听任务；只有报名确认而没听见内容，不算参加完成。

- **讲者讲得很快，写不完：** 只保留与目标相关的一个观点和一个例子，空缺处标“未听清”，之后查主办方公开资料。
- **主题与公告不符：** 重新定一个今天实际可听的目标，或离开；不把原主题的预设写成讲者说过。
- **没有字幕或听力条件不适用：** 向主办方询问正式文字/辅助服务；得不到可理解内容时诚实标未完成。
- **引用信息不确定：** 标“讲者提及，来源待核”，不转发为已验证事实。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

公开讲座是否收费、可录制、能现场提问和使用辅助技术因主办方而异。纸笔、屏幕阅读、字幕或可信协助均可帮助你做自己的短笔记，但未经许可不录旁人。你离场后得到的是有来源的初步理解和问题，不是专业资格或真实性担保；需要继续核查的内容就按原话标出。

## English

Use this Skill when you want to hear an ordinary public talk at a library, museum, or community organisation and leave with more than “a lot was said.” Attend with **one specific listening goal** and make a short source-labelled note. The outcome is not a qualification or mastery of a subject. Asking the speaker a live question is a separate task.

### Preparation and inputs

Use the organiser's current notice to check title, speaker, time, location or official online access, registration, charges, language, captions, and access services. An old forwarded post may no longer describe admission. Write “Today I want to understand ____,” such as how one example supports a claim or how a counterexample was handled. Bring paper, an accessible private note method, or trusted help. Follow organiser rules for phones, photographs, and recording; default to no recording, live stream, or copying a paid handout. For medical, legal, or financial subject matter, record it as a speaker's view, not a replacement for professional advice.

### Execution

1. **Actually enter.** Use the organiser's registration, check-in, or official link, confirm the right session, and find a place and caption or listening support that works without blocking others. If entry fails or the event is cancelled, record that and stop. Publicity material is not evidence that you attended.
2. **Write goal and source first.** Note organiser, speaker or event title, and date. Put the listening goal at the top. If a speaker detail is unknown, mark it for checking rather than guess.
3. **Capture two relevant points.** When the speaker makes a claim or uses an example, write in your own short words “the speaker said or showed ____; the example was ____.” Separate their claim, someone else's source they cite, and your own association. Do not transcribe every word at the expense of the next explanation.
4. **Mark one uncertainty.** Write a concrete question such as “In which situations does that conclusion apply?” If the talk answers it later, add where or how; otherwise leave a research or Q&A lead. Silence is not an answer.
5. **Check for one minute afterward.** Confirm the note has at least two things you actually heard, one answered or open question, and a findable event source. Expand vague shorthand into words you understand. Do not turn an uncertain number from memory into a definite fact. Exit by the venue route or end the official online session.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You attended the talk and have **two speaker points related to one goal, a question state, and event source**. You need not ask in public to complete the listening task. Registration alone, without hearing content, is not completed attendance.

- **The speaker is too fast to copy:** Keep one claim and one example tied to your goal, mark “missed this,” and check the organiser's public material later.
- **The topic differs from the notice:** Set a goal for what is actually being said or leave. Do not write your expected topic as if the speaker delivered it.
- **Captions or hearing support are unusable:** Ask the organiser for an official text or access route. Mark the task incomplete if you cannot understand enough.
- **A cited fact is uncertain:** Mark “speaker mentioned; source to verify,” not a verified claim to pass on.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Charges, recording, Q&A, and assistive technology differ by organiser. Paper, screen reading, captions, or trusted help can support your private notes, without recording bystanders without permission. You leave with source-labelled provisional understanding and a question, not a credential or guarantee that every claim is true. Flag content that needs checking.
