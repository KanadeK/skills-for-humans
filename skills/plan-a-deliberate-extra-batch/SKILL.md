## 中文

当你正考虑把今天的普通家常菜多做一批，明确供另一次用餐，**还未开火**时，用这份 Skill 判断多做是否真的成立。它的结果是“本餐量 + 已分配的未来餐量”或“只做本餐”。它不处理已经煮好的剩菜；完成后的冷却、分装和保存仍要按适用安全流程实际执行。

### 准备与输入

确认今天实际吃的人与份量、未来哪一顿会吃多出的部分、未来用餐人数或所需量、届时是否能按该菜的说明安全使用。核对原配方能否按目标批量执行、锅具容量与注意力、干净且适合食品的浅容器、可靠冷藏或冷冻空间、实际冷却与标记时间。先检查食品本身的日期和保存状态；不能把不明历史的食材因“反正要煮”纳入批量。

### 执行

1. **给未来份取一个真实地址。** 写出“哪一天的哪一餐、几份、谁会取用”。没有具体下一餐，额外量就是意外剩余；先只做今天的需要。
2. **把两顿量分开核算。** 先锁定当前餐位和本餐需要量，再加未来已确认的份数。若配方需要扩大，使用可靠配方调整方法；锅、烤盘或炉具无法处理新总量时，改做两个原批次、缩小未来份数或取消额外批，不让满锅溢出成为烹饪技法。
3. **在开火前核对未来保存。** 多出的熟食能否按其具体类别和当地要求及时冷却、进入可靠冷藏/冷冻，且有足够浅容器和空间？未来吃法是否与保存方式相符？不能确认时不启动额外批，不能把室温餐桌当成计划的缓冲区。
4. **把未来份与上桌份分开设计。** 在菜单卡上标出哪部分供今天、哪部分应在**摆上桌前**单独转入干净容器，再按适用方法冷却和储存。这样未来份不必在一桌人吃饭期间等待或被反复接触。分装与冷却的实际操作由到时的食品保存步骤完成。
5. **核对会不会反过来伤害今天。** 多做是否让当前菜的熟度检查、上桌时间、器具或冷藏空间不成立？若会，就保持今天的小批量；另一次再做并不代表失败。
6. **留一张可执行决定。** 记下本餐份数、未来份数和日期、所用锅具、预留容器/冷藏位、摆桌前的分开点，以及如果设备或时间变更时优先取消哪一份。开工前再核对一次。

### 成功条件

额外批量有明确未来用途、真实可做的配方与器具、摆桌前分开点和可执行的低温保存路径；或者你明确只做今天的份量。写了“以后再说”的余量不算完成计划。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只想“一次多做点”，没有下一餐：** 改成当前需求量，或先定具体未来用餐再重新核算。
- **锅里装得下，冰箱装不下：** 缩掉额外份、分成不同日期的制作任务，或仅在确有可验证低温设备时保留；不能靠容器堆叠遮住容量问题。
- **未来份已经端上桌才想起来：** 停止把它称为预先留好的份；按已发生的剩菜与接触时间处理，不用本计划倒填记录。
- **配方扩大后时间或熟度不确定：** 用可信的目标份数配方或原批次分做；当前餐安全与可完成优先于提前准备未来一餐。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这只处理普通家庭菜和已确认的未来使用，不为医疗饮食、婴幼儿食品或过敏做批量建议。食品、地区和设备决定实际冷却、保存与复热要求；本 Skill 不给所有菜一个统一期限。看不清标签或没有能力搬一大锅时，可用大字计划、适用的小批次和可信协助，或放弃额外批。可能多用一个锅、几个容器和冰箱空间，也可能少做一次饭；这些成本在开火前应看得见。

## English

Use this Skill **before cooking** when you are considering making extra ordinary home food for one identified later meal. The result is “today's amount plus assigned future servings,” or “today only.” It does not handle food that has already been cooked; actual cooling, packing, and storage must still follow the applicable food-safety process afterward.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm who is eating today and their amounts, which later meal will use the extra food, how many future servings it needs, and whether that dish can be used safely after the intended storage. Check whether the recipe can make the total amount, your pan and attention capacity, clean shallow food-suitable containers, reliable refrigerator or freezer room, and time to cool and label food. Check the ingredient's date and handling history first. “It will be cooked anyway” does not make an unknown history acceptable.

### Execution

1. **Give the future servings a real address.** Write the date, meal, number of portions, and who will use them. With no actual next meal, the extra is accidental surplus. Make today's quantity instead.
2. **Count the two meals separately.** Fix today's diners and amount first, then add only confirmed future servings. If the recipe must grow, use a supported resizing method. When the pan, tray, or cooker cannot handle the total, make separate original-size batches, reduce the future amount, or skip it. Overflow is not a cooking technique.
3. **Check storage before turning on heat.** Can this cooked food be cooled promptly under its food-specific and local guidance, placed in reliable cold storage, and held in enough suitable shallow containers? Does the intended future use fit that storage? If the path is unconfirmed, do not start the extra batch. A room-temperature table is not reserve cold storage.
4. **Designate future food before serving today's.** Mark on your meal card what is for today and what goes into a clean separate container **before food is put on the table**. The future share need not wait through dinner or be touched repeatedly. Carry out actual cooling and packing under the relevant later food-storage workflow.
5. **Check the cost to today's meal.** Does the extra volume make today's doneness checks, serving time, equipment, or cold space fail? If so, keep a small batch for today. Cooking a future meal another day is a valid result.
6. **Leave an executable decision.** Note servings and dates for each meal, pan, reserved containers and cold space, the before-serving separation point, and which future amount you would cancel if time or equipment changes. Recheck before starting.

### Success

The extra batch has an actual later use, a recipe and equipment that can produce it, a separation point before serving, and a workable cold-storage path. Choosing to cook only today's amount is equally complete. “Deal with it later” is not an assigned remainder.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You just want to “make more” with no next meal:** Return to today's quantity or establish a specific future meal before recalculating.
- **The pan is big enough but the fridge is not:** Remove the extra, cook on another date, or retain it only with verified cold capacity. Stacking containers cannot hide a capacity gap.
- **The future share already went on the table:** Stop calling it a reserved batch. Handle actual leftovers and contact time under the after-cooking workflow rather than backfilling this plan.
- **More food makes timing or doneness uncertain:** Use a tested recipe for the target yield or repeat the original batch. The current meal's safe completion takes priority over an advance future meal.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary family food with an identified future use, not medical diets, infant food, or allergy batch advice. Food, place, and equipment determine actual cooling, storage, and reheating rules; this Skill supplies no universal duration. If labels are inaccessible or carrying a large pan is difficult, use a large-print plan, suitable smaller batches and trusted help, or skip the extra. Another pan, containers, and refrigerator space may be used; you may also avoid cooking later. Make those costs visible before starting.
