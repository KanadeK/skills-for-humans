## 中文

别人请你做一件普通、可自愿选择的小事，你决定不做，或只能承担其中明确的一部分时，用这份 Skill。目标是**说清“不做”或“只做这部分”以及必要的交接点**，不靠羞辱对方、编造借口或含糊拖延。对方赞同你的决定不是完成条件。若这是工作职责、危险操作、专业义务、威胁或持续骚扰，先用对应制度或支持渠道，不把它简化成普通人情拒绝。

### 准备与输入

先确认你听懂了对方的真实请求：做什么、需要多久、何时、是否要用你的物品或账号，以及对方是否已据你先前答复作了安排。区分三种状态：完全不做；能做一个有边界的部分；目前信息不够，先问清。不要为了显得好说话把“我可能有空”写成确定答应。已经明确承诺过且对方有所依赖，及时承认变化并处理真实后续，而不是假装从未同意。

### 执行

1. **给出能被执行的结论。** 完全不能或不愿做时说“这件事我不能帮”；无需准备一套必须通过对方审核的理由。若只能部分做，先说清“我能〔具体动作〕，但不能〔余下部分〕”，不要用“我看看”承载实际的否定。
2. **把边界写到可操作。** 对时间、数量、地点、材料和责任，只说真正愿意承担的范围。例如“我能在周四前帮你看两段错字，但不能编辑整份稿子；修改仍由你做。” 若这个部分对对方没有用，他们可以拒绝你的部分提议；这不是你的答复失败。
3. **只给真实可行的替代。** 你知道合适的正式渠道或愿意另约一次，才提出；不要为了缓和尴尬就说“随时找我”或保证别的同事会帮。若对方已经交来物品、材料或订位，说明你会如何把它们退回/停止处理；未完成的事不写成已交接。
4. **给对方回应空间并保持结论。** 对方问一个具体问题，你可以澄清范围；对方提出新方案，重新判断是否真的愿意。若只是反复追问理由或想把未同意的部分塞回来，简短重复边界并结束普通协商。不要攻击对方的人品来证明自己的“不”。
5. **做完自己已同意的部分才标完成。** 同意一小块后，记下具体交付和时点；任务扩大时重新征得你的同意。若你最终没法完成自己已接受的部分，尽早告知并移交真实状态，而不是让对方到截止时才发现。

### 成功条件

对方已收到明确的“否 / 有范围的部分同意 / 需先澄清”，你自己知道是否有需要退还的物品、已经接受的小部分或下一次答复点。**拒绝已表达**不等于对方满意；**部分同意**不等于整件事已办完。沉默和含糊都不能替代已作出的决定。

### 常见报错与补救

- **实际想拒绝，却说“有空再说”：** 在对方依赖这个可能性前补上明确答复。
- **只说“这个不行”，对方以为你同意其他全部：** 说清可以做和不能做的各自范围，或直接拒绝整项。
- **对方说你“不够意思”，你开始辩论人格：** 回到这次请求和边界；对方的评价不改变你可承担的实际范围。
- **你此前已答应且拿了材料：** 承认变化，及时退还或说明交接方式和剩余进度，不用一句“不帮了”掩盖材料去向。
- **部分帮忙变成无限追加：** 停下新工作，说明原范围已满；新部分须重新商量。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

适用于自愿的普通帮忙，不裁定雇佣职责、法律义务、照护义务或危险任务。权力差异、文化习惯和关系会影响表达方式与拒绝的现实成本；本文不保证说一句话就消除这些影响。无法使用文字可用对方能接收的口头或辅助方式，仍让范围具体。对方可能失望或需要换方案；你能做的是给准确状态并负责自己先前真正接受的部分，不需要羞辱或说服对方认同你的决定。

## English

Use this Skill when someone asks for an ordinary optional favor and you decide not to do it, or can genuinely do only one defined part. State **no or the exact part you can do and any necessary handover**, without shaming the requester, inventing an excuse, or delaying with a false maybe. Their approval is not required for your answer to be clear. Employment duties, hazardous tasks, professional obligations, threats, and persistent harassment need their own rules or support path.

### Preparation and inputs

Make sure you understand the real request: action, effort, timing, use of your things or accounts, and whether the person already relied on something you said. Separate three states: no to the whole task; yes to a bounded part; or insufficient detail to decide. Do not turn “maybe I have time” into a firm yes for politeness. If you already clearly committed and the other person relied on that, acknowledge the change promptly and handle real follow-up rather than pretending no agreement existed.

### Execution

1. **Give an actionable answer.** If you cannot or do not want to do it, say “I can't help with this task.” You do not need a reason that passes their approval test. For partial help, start with “I can do [specific action], but not [remaining part],” rather than using “I'll see” to carry an actual no.
2. **Make the limit operational.** State only the time, quantity, place, material, and responsibility you truly accept. For example: “I can check two paragraphs for typos by Thursday, but I can't edit the whole draft; you'll make the changes.” They may decide that partial offer is not useful. That does not make your answer a failure.
3. **Offer only real alternatives.** Suggest a formal channel or another time only if you know it is viable and genuinely want to. Do not soften the moment with “ask me anytime” or promise that another colleague will help. If you already hold their item, file, or booking, say how you will return it or stop processing it. An unfinished handover stays unfinished.
4. **Allow a response while keeping your answer.** Clarify the scope for one specific question. Assess any genuinely new proposal afresh. If the exchange becomes repeated demands for reasons or an attempt to add work you did not accept, briefly repeat the limit and end ordinary negotiation. Do not attack the person's character to justify no.
5. **Mark only actual work completed.** For an accepted small part, record its deliverable and time. A wider task requires new agreement. If you cannot complete even the accepted part, tell them early and hand over the real state instead of letting the deadline reveal it.

### Success

The person has a clear “no / bounded partial yes / detail needed first.” You know whether an item must be returned, an accepted part remains, or a response point is pending. **A stated refusal** does not mean they are pleased; **partial agreement** does not mean the whole job is done. Silence or vagueness cannot stand in for a decision you have already made.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You mean no but say “maybe later”:** Send a clear correction before they rely on that possibility.
- **Only “that part won't work” makes the rest sound accepted:** State both can-do and cannot-do parts, or decline the entire request.
- **They call you unhelpful and you start a character debate:** Return to this request and its limit; their judgment does not change your real capacity.
- **You had agreed and now hold their material:** Acknowledge the change, return it or give the actual handover and remaining progress. “I won't help” alone loses the material's whereabouts.
- **Partial help grows without end:** Stop new work at the agreed scope and discuss any new part separately.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers voluntary ordinary favors, not deciding employment, legal, care, or hazardous duties. Power differences, customs, and relationships change how you speak and the real cost of refusing; one sentence cannot guarantee those pressures disappear. If text is inaccessible, use a spoken or assisted channel the other person can receive, still making scope concrete. They may be disappointed or need a different plan. Your job is an accurate state and responsibility for what you actually accepted earlier, not humiliation or persuading them to endorse your choice.
