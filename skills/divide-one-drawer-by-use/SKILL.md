## 中文

当一个普通小件抽屉每次都要翻到底才找得到笔、便签或封口用品时，加载这份 Skill。你只处理**一个有权整理的抽屉**，按物品被取出、一起使用和放回的方式分区。结果是能找得到、用后放得回，抽屉仍能关上；不是把所有小件按颜色排队，也不替别人决定丢掉什么。

### 准备与输入

确认这个抽屉属于你或你已获同意整理；列出最近确实在这里取过的两三种动作，例如写便条、封包或拿备用小物。腾出一块不会让小件滚落的台面，使用已有浅盒、纸盒或简单分隔物即可。若抽屉很深或东西多，只处理一半，留一半在原位；不要为“整理”把整间房的物品都倒出来。

看不清、分辨不出颜色或难以够到抽屉深处时，可用固定前后顺序、大字、触觉或朗读记录辨认，不必买专用设备。带来但身份不明、属于他人或需要专门处置的物品先单独留好，不把普通抽屉 Skill 当成处置许可。

### 执行

1. **先确认这个抽屉要支持什么。** 以实际取用动作命名两三组，例如“写便条”与“封口”，而不是先买十个格子再找东西填。若物品几乎从不在此使用，记下它原本该去的位置；不确定就暂留待问。
2. **分小批拿出与核对。** 一次取出一小部分，保持你能辨认物品及其配件。能安全、明确放回旧位置的先放回；别人的、身份不明或去留未定的先留在一个可找回的待确认区，不丢弃、不混入你的分类。
3. **按动作而非外形配对。** 会在同一动作中一起拿的物品相邻；高频组放在你容易取到的前方或合适高度，低频组放后方。形状相似却用途不同的东西要能区分，不能只因为都是小圆片就住一起。
4. **用现有东西维持边界。** 浅盒、折纸隔片或抽屉本身的隔格能让小件不互相滑入即可。确认隔片不会卡住抽屉，容器打开后能拿到里面的物品。看不清的组可加简短大字或触觉标记；完整的盒外索引另作收纳任务，不为这一步建立标签数据库。
5. **做一次取还测试。** 关上抽屉，再按每组一个真实用途取出物品、完成最小动作或模拟组合，按同一路径放回。若要翻遍每个盒子才找得到，调整分组或标记；若关不上，减小分区占用或把不属于这里的物品送回原处。
6. **留下待确认事项。** 对来源、归属或去留不明的小件，写“要问谁 / 要查什么 / 放在哪”；暂时不要让它们占满常用组。测试通过就停止，之后按实际找不到的证据再改，不为统一风格反复重排。

### 完成与停止

你能从每个常用组取出一件、用后放回，抽屉能正常开关，你或经常使用它的人知道分区。未知物品还在可找回的待确认处，未被擅自丢弃。若抽屉卡住、分隔物不稳或物品有你不能识别的风险，先停用相关位置，恢复可安全关闭的状态；不要拆修家具或硬压抽屉以通过测试。

### 常见报错与补救

- **“杂物”变成最大的分区：** 再问这些物品实际服务哪两三种动作；没有共同用途的不要硬并，未知的写待确认。
- **隔片总倒或小件乱滑：** 换已有的稳固浅容器，减少每格数量；没有合适容器时先用抽屉原隔格，不增加摇晃的叠层。
- **抽屉关不上：** 取出新加入或堆高的物品，恢复原有开合，再按真实取用决定是否需要另一个合法位置。
- **另一个使用者找不到东西：** 告诉对方新分区并请其按自己的取用动作试一次；若位置不合适，调整约定，不把对方的物品私自转移。
- **中途被打断：** 留下已完成的组、未整理的一小批和待确认区；下次从未整理的一批继续，不把桌上的临时摊开状态当成果。

### 假设、替代与已知副作用

抽屉深度、同住者权限、手部动作、视觉和可取用高度都不同。这里以你实际的取还动作定位置；固定顺序、较大字或触觉提示都可以替代颜色编码。没有新分隔物也能先用现有空间做少量稳定分区。副作用是整理过程中台面会暂时被占用，最后必须把抽屉以外的物品送回有权使用的位置或留有明确下一步。

## English

Load this Skill when an ordinary small-items drawer must be searched to the bottom each time you need a pen, note paper, or sealing supplies. Handle **one drawer you are allowed to organize** and group its items by how you take them out, use them together, and return them. Finish when things can be found and put back and the drawer still closes. You need not arrange every small object by colour or decide what another person must discard.

### Preparation and inputs

Confirm the drawer is yours to arrange or you have permission. Name two or three actions you actually used it for recently, such as writing a note, sealing a packet, or finding a small spare. Clear a surface that will keep little items from rolling away; existing shallow boxes, folded card, or the drawer's own compartments are enough. If it is deep or crowded, work on half at a time and leave the rest in place instead of emptying the whole room.

If sight, colour distinction, or reaching deep into the drawer is difficult, use a stable front-to-back order, large print, a tactile cue, or an accessible spoken record. No special purchase is required. Keep items you cannot identify, that belong to someone else, or that need specialist handling separate; an ordinary drawer Skill does not authorize their disposal.

### Execution

1. **Name the drawer's jobs first.** Use real actions to name two or three groups, such as “write a note” and “seal a package,” before buying ten dividers to fill. If an item is hardly ever used here, note its existing home. Keep an uncertain item to ask about rather than assigning it by guesswork.
2. **Lift and check in small batches.** Take out a few things at a time so you can identify items and their parts. Return clearly identified things with known homes first. Keep another person's, unidentified, or undecided items in one retrievable “confirm” area rather than discarding them or mixing them into your groups.
3. **Pair by action, not shape.** Put items used together for one action near each other. Put frequent groups at the front or another height you can reach comfortably, and rare groups farther back. Similar shapes with different jobs still need a way to tell them apart; “small round things” is not a complete address.
4. **Keep boundaries with what you have.** A shallow box, folded-card divider, or existing drawer section only needs to stop small items sliding into the wrong group. Check that a divider does not jam the drawer and that you can reach inside a container. Add a short large-print or tactile cue when needed. A complete outside-box index is another task, not a database prerequisite here.
5. **Test one retrieval and return per group.** Close the drawer, then take one item for a real use from each group, do or simulate its smallest action, and put it back along the same route. If you must open every box, change the group or cue. If the drawer will not close, reduce the space taken by dividers or return items that do not belong there to their known homes.
6. **Leave a next action for unknowns.** For anything with unclear origin, owner, or fate, record who to ask or what to check and where it waits. Do not let unknowns fill the common-use groups. Stop after the test works; revise later only if actual retrieval fails, not for a uniform look.

### Success and stop conditions

You can retrieve and return one item from every frequent group, the drawer opens and closes normally, and you or its regular users can understand the zones. Unknown items remain findable and have not been discarded without authority. If the drawer jams, dividers are unstable, or an item's risk is unclear, stop using that part and restore a safely closing drawer. Do not dismantle furniture or force it shut to pass the test.

### Common errors and recovery

- **“Miscellaneous” becomes the largest group:** Ask which two or three actual actions those items serve. Do not force unrelated or unknown things together; mark unknowns to confirm.
- **Dividers fall or items slide around:** Use a stable shallow container you already have and put fewer items in each section. Without one, start with the drawer's existing compartments rather than an unstable stack.
- **The drawer will not close:** Remove the newly added or tall items, restore normal movement, then decide by real use whether another permitted place is needed.
- **Another user cannot find things:** Tell them the new zones and let them try their own retrieval route. Agree on an adjustment rather than silently relocating their property.
- **You are interrupted:** Leave the completed groups, one unprocessed batch, and the confirm area identifiable. Resume with that batch instead of treating everything spread on the desk as a finished system.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Drawer depth, shared permissions, hand movement, sight, and reachable height vary. Let your actual retrieval and return actions determine the places; consistent order, larger text, or touch can replace colour coding. You can create a few stable zones with existing space rather than buying dividers. The work surface will temporarily hold the drawer's contents. Before stopping, return anything outside the drawer to a place you may use or leave a clear next action.
