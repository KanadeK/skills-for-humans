## 中文

当你的一件普通衣物局部出现**连在原布纤维上的小毛球**，想改善表面但不损伤面料时，用这份 Skill 选择适用工具，先小处试，再有限清除。毛球是纤维磨损和摩擦后的结果，清掉可改善外观，却不能把已经失去的纤维补回去或保证以后不再出现。松散浮毛只需轻取，不应拿刮削工具处理。

### 准备与输入

先让衣物干燥、平放，读标签及制造商关于梳理/除球工具的说明。看毛球是否真的连在布面、所在处是否薄得透光、已有抽丝/破洞、针织结构是否易勾，以及纽扣、刺绣、印花、缝线是否紧邻。备好适合该面料的毛球梳或带护网的除球器及其说明书、明亮桌面和收集纤维的容器。若贵重羊毛、长绒、松针织、弹力面料或功能涂层的工具适用性不明，停下查制造商/专业护理；H054 的羊毛洗护流程不被本篇替换。

### 执行

1. **先分清能摘的是什么。** 用好光线看它是与原布连着的小球，还是纸屑/外来浮毛、松出的线头、破洞。仅对附着毛球继续；外来浮毛走轻取路径，松线和破洞不能用除球器“修平”。
2. **选最温和的相容办法。** 少量松毛球且说明允许时，可用手轻捏取；对更多附着毛球，选制造商允许的面料梳或带护网的除球工具，按工具说明核对适用面料和操作姿态。没有相容工具就停止，不用普通剃须刀、砂纸或剪刀贴着布面碰运气。
3. **在不显眼处小样试。** 把衣物放平，稍微展平但不拉到变形；在一小处按工具允许的方向和压力轻轻移动。确认毛球减少、布面没有拉丝、变薄或小孔。若工具抓住线圈、发出异常拉扯感或带下完整长纤维，立即停用。
4. **只修需要的区域。** 小范围、低压力地逐步处理显眼毛球，避开缝线、褶皱、纽扣与装饰；不要在同一薄处反复刮到“完全像新布”。收走掉下的纤维，按说明清洁梳子或关闭/清空除球器。用光线和手感复核局部是否平整、没有新勾丝。
5. **决定何时停。** 看起来已经可接受或再操作会碰到原纤维，就停止。反复大面积起球或磨薄时记录高摩擦部位、查面料与穿洗条件，不把除球当可无限重复的重置键；破洞或持续抽丝交材料修补/专业护理。

### 成功条件

局部附着毛球减少，衣物没有新增破洞、抽丝、薄斑或损坏装饰，工具已安全收好。只确认了面料不适合手边工具而停止，也完成了正确判断；不承诺恢复“全新”外观或永不再起球。

### 常见报错与补救

- **把松散浮毛当毛球刮：** 停止切削，改用柔软刷或适用粘毛工具轻取。
- **除球器卡到针织线圈：** 立即关停并轻柔移开工具，检查有没有抽丝；不要加大压力冲过去。
- **一处越修越薄：** 停止反复操作，记录磨损并考虑专业意见；更多切削不会长回纤维。
- **梳子勾住装饰、缝线或绒面：** 抬离工具，换更小的可操作区域或停止，别猛拽。
- **手摘时带出长线头：** 不继续拉；它可能已是原织物的一部分，按勾丝/破损另行判断。
- **工具使用说明和衣物材质不匹配：** 不试图以“轻一点”覆盖禁用条件，先查制造商或专业护理。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里只做普通非安全关键衣物的局部外观维护，面料和工具都必须允许该动作。不同针织、绒面、长纤维、弹力或技术涂层受损风险不同；少量毛球本来也可能是正常磨损。视力不便时用明亮灯、放大镜或可信帮助；手部稳定性不足时用平放和小范围操作，不能控制工具就停。清除的纤维不会再长回衣物，少量可见毛球比挖出洞更可接受。

## English

Use this Skill when small **fibre balls remain attached to an ordinary garment's surface** and you want a tidier area without harming the cloth. Choose a compatible tool, trial out of sight, and remove only a limited area. Pills follow friction and fibre wear. Removing them may improve appearance, but it cannot replace lost fibres or guarantee no future pills. Loose outside lint needs gentle lifting, not a cutting tool.

### Preparation and inputs

Work with a dry garment laid flat. Read its care and maker advice for combs or de-pilling tools. Check that the balls are attached, whether the patch is thin or already snagged, whether knit loops can catch, and whether buttons, embroidery, print, or seams sit nearby. Have a compatible fabric comb or guarded de-piller and its instructions, a lit table, and a place for removed fibres. Stop for valuable wool, long nap, loose knit, stretch, or coated fabric when tool suitability is unknown; check the maker or professional care. This does not replace H054's wool washing route.

### Execution

1. **Identify what you would remove.** In good light, distinguish attached fibre balls from paper, outside lint, a loose thread, or a hole. Continue only for attached pills. Loose matter follows the gentle lifting path; a thread or hole is not flattened safely with a shaver.
2. **Choose the gentlest compatible method.** Where guidance allows, a few loose pills may be lightly lifted by hand. For more attached pills, use a maker-compatible fabric comb or guarded de-pilling tool and check its fabric and handling instructions. With no suitable tool, stop. An ordinary shaving blade, sandpaper, or scissors held flush against the cloth are not a substitute.
3. **Trial an inconspicuous area.** Support the cloth flat and smooth it without stretching it out of shape. Move the allowed tool lightly in the direction and with the pressure its instructions permit. Confirm pills diminish without a pulled thread, thin spot, or hole. Stop at once if the tool catches a loop, pulls unusually, or removes a long sound fibre.
4. **Work only where needed.** Move through small visible areas with light pressure. Avoid seams, folds, buttons, and trim. Do not repeatedly shave a thin patch until it looks like new fabric. Collect loose fibres, then clean the comb or turn off and empty the de-piller as instructed. Recheck the area for acceptable smoothness and no new snag.
5. **Know when to stop.** Stop when appearance is useful or the next pass would touch original cloth. For widespread recurring pills or thinning, record the friction spot and investigate material and wear/wash conditions rather than treating trimming as an endless reset. Holes or continuing pulls belong to material repair or professional care.

### Success

Local attached pills are reduced without a new hole, pulled thread, thin patch, or damaged trim, and the tool is put away safely. Establishing that the available tool is unsuitable and stopping is also complete. This does not promise a new-fabric look or permanent prevention.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Loose lint is scraped as if it were pills:** Stop cutting and use a soft brush or compatible lint roller.
- **The de-piller catches a knit loop:** Switch it off and lift it gently. Check for a pull; pressing harder will not free it safely.
- **One area grows thinner with each pass:** Stop repeated trimming and record wear for professional advice. More cutting cannot regrow fibres.
- **A comb hooks trim, stitching, or nap:** Lift it off and choose a smaller safe area or stop. Do not yank.
- **Hand-picking draws out a long thread:** Do not pull farther; it may be part of the original fabric. Assess the snag or damage separately.
- **Tool instructions do not fit this fabric:** Do not override a prohibition by promising to be gentler. Check the maker or professional care.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is local appearance care for ordinary, non-safety-critical clothing, with a tool and fabric that both permit the operation. Knits, nap, long fibres, stretch, and technical coatings carry different damage risks; a few pills may be normal wear. Use strong light, magnification, or trusted help if detail is hard to see. Support the item flat and work small areas if hand steadiness is limited; stop if tool control is unreliable. Removed fibres do not grow back. A few remaining pills are preferable to a hole.
