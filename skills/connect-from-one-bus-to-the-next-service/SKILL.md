## 中文

你从普通公交下车，要接另一段公交或运营方明确标出的普通换乘服务时，用这份 Skill 将**实际下车位置**与下一段正确方向的上车站、当前班次接起来。目标是安全到达下一段正确站点并确认能搭哪班；若错过连接，得到一条可核验的新计划。它不教具体街道过街路线、不保证赶上、不推断免费换乘，也不接管铁路站内换乘流程。

### 准备与输入

从第一段的实际站名/站号开始，不继续使用已经与现实不符的预估时间。查运营方当前行程规划器、站牌或客服：下一段的线路、方向/终点、站点编号或字母、实时出发与改道/停站公告。把下一段票务或换乘资格视为该运营方当期规则，而非“同一张卡一定免费”。没有手机可读现场站牌、纸图或问官方工作人员；无法安全独自走到另一站时先选择可及替代或协助，不为赶车奔跑穿过不明路口。

### 执行

1. **确认实际交接点。** 在第一辆车停稳下车后核对站牌/官方信息，确定自己实际落在哪个站。若因改道或晚点不在计划换乘站，先重算下一段，别直接追旧截图上的车。
2. **核对下一段班次与方向。** 用实时站点出发信息或现场站牌，确认所选线路的方向/终点确实从当前换乘区域的某个站先经过上车站再到你的后续目标。相同线路号的对向车、短程车和临时改道不能混用。记下下一段的上车站编号/字母，而不只是“在这附近”。
3. **到达正确站点，不抢时间冒险。** 依当地站方/街道合法且可及的路径走到该上车点；站点在道路对面时按正式过街设施和信号处理，无法安全到达就放弃这一班。到站后再比对站牌的线路和驶往方向。不要因为一辆车先到就跳上去。
4. **判断连接是否仍可行。** 查看下一段实际出发、停站公告、票务/换乘条件及你能否按运营规则上车；所需刷卡或换乘凭证按当期规则准备。赶不上、站点关闭、资格或路线不明时，在安全位置查后续班次或向运营方求助，记录新的上车站与时间；不把预计时刻当承诺。
5. **完成下一段交接。** 只有车辆标识与下一段方向/变体相符时，才按该运营方的站台、上车与支付规则开始下一段。若选择等待下一班，明确等的是哪条、哪个方向、哪个站点。后续下车另按该段目标站判断。

### 成功条件

你在实际下车站重新核对了下一段，安全到达正确方向的上车点，并搭上符合计划的下一班，或明确选择了仍可行的后续班次及等待位置；若连接不可行，记录新计划而不是越过安全/票务边界强赶。

### 常见报错与补救

- **计划显示同站换乘，现场却分两个站牌：** 对照运营方站号/字母与驶往方向，再走可及的合规路径，不能只按距离判断。
- **第一段晚到：** 查实时下一班和服务公告；错过原连接不是让车“等你”的依据。
- **下一辆车号对但终点不对：** 留在正确站点查这一班实际站序，等符合方向/支线的车。
- **临时关闭下一段站点：** 查官方替代站及可及路径；不去封闭站牌下等车。
- **票务/换乘资格不明：** 在上车前核对运营方当期规则，必要时准备另一种允许的支付方式；不从别城经验推断免费或免刷卡。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里是普通、可安全退出的城市公交连接，下一段若进入大型铁路站、机场或特殊预约服务，应使用其专属流程。伦敦、新南威尔士与温哥华运营方工具仅说明如何查实际站点和下一班，不提供你所在城市的实时承诺。无智能手机可读现场公告或问工作人员；行动、视觉或听觉受限时按该服务的真实可及站点/协助方案重排，而不靠冲刺。可能会多等一班，这是比走错方向更小的副作用。

## English

Use this Skill after leaving an ordinary bus when you need another bus or a simple operator-marked next service. Connect your **actual exit stop** to the correct next boarding point, direction, and current departure. The result is reaching the right stop and identifying the service you can take, or a verified later plan after a missed connection. This does not give street-crossing directions, guarantee a connection, assume a free transfer, or run a railway-station interchange.

### Preparation and inputs

Begin with the actual first-leg stop name or ID, not an estimated arrival from an old plan. Check the operator's current planner, signs, or support for the next route, direction/terminus, stop ID or letter, live departure, and diversions/closures. Fare and transfer validity follow the operator's current rules; sharing a card does not automatically make a transfer free. Without a phone, use stop signs, a printed map, or official staff. For an inaccessible path, seek a supported alternative rather than sprinting across an unfamiliar crossing.

### Execution

1. **Confirm the real interchange point.** Once the first bus is stopped and you have alighted, identify its actual stop from a sign or official information. A diversion or delay can move you away from the planned interchange, requiring a new next leg.
2. **Verify the next run and direction.** Use live departures or stop signs to check that the chosen service from this interchange serves your onward destination in the right direction. Do not mix the opposite-side version of the same number, a short-working, or a diverted variant. Record the next boarding stop's ID or letter, not just “nearby.”
3. **Reach the correct stop without rushing.** Use a legal, accessible local path to that boarding point. If it is across a road, follow the crossing facilities and signals; abandon this bus if you cannot reach it safely. At the stop, compare signed routes and “towards” direction again. Do not board only because a bus arrived first.
4. **Decide whether the connection still works.** Check actual next departure, closures, fare/transfer conditions, and whether you can board under the operator's rules. Prepare any required tap or transfer proof under current local rules. For a missed departure, closed stop, or unclear eligibility, recheck later services or ask the operator from a safe position, then record new stop and time. A predicted minute is not a promise.
5. **Hand over to the next leg.** Board only a service whose display matches the next direction and variant, following that operator's stop, boarding, and payment rules. If waiting, know exactly which route, direction, and stop you await. Its eventual exit is a separate decision.

### Success

From the actual exit stop, you verified the next leg, reached the correct-direction boarding point safely, and took a matching next service; or you deliberately wait there for a feasible later service with its identity clear. An impossible connection ends in a recorded new plan, not a risky dash past safety or fare conditions.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Planner says same-stop transfer but signs show two points:** Compare operator stop IDs/letters and directions, then use an accessible legal path rather than distance alone.
- **First bus arrives late:** Check live next departures and alerts. A missed connection does not require the next driver to wait.
- **Right route number, wrong terminus:** Stay at the appropriate stop and inspect this run's served-stop sequence; wait for the correct direction/branch.
- **Next stop is temporarily closed:** Check the official alternative stop and accessible route; do not wait beneath a closed sign.
- **Fare or transfer validity is unclear:** Check the operator's current rules before boarding and have another permitted payment method if needed. Another city's free-transfer habit is no evidence.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is an ordinary, safely interruptible city-bus connection. A large railway station, airport, or special booking uses its own process. London, NSW, and Vancouver tools show how to verify a current stop and next departure, not what your city's buses will do. Without a phone, read signs or ask staff. For mobility, sight, or hearing barriers, replan around actual accessible stops and assistance rather than rushing. Waiting for another bus is often a smaller side effect than taking the wrong direction.
