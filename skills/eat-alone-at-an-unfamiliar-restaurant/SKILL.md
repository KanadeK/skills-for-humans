## 中文

当你想第一次独自去一家不熟悉的餐馆，又担心进门后不知道下一句是什么时，加载这份 Skill。目标不是表演“经常一个人吃饭的人”，而是完成一次你可以随时缩小或退出的用餐。

### 准备与输入

出发前确认：

- 地址、营业时间、是否需要预约，以及进门/排队方式；
- 菜单大致价格、你的总预算和付款方式；
- 你必须遵守的过敏、宗教、文化或饮食边界；
- 交通、回程、洗手间和无障碍入口/座位需求；
- 你愿意停留多久，以及不舒服时的退出方案。

若线上菜单不存在或不可访问，只准备一句提问：“你好，我第一次来，可以告诉我怎么点餐吗？”这不是暴露系统版本，是正常获取接口说明。

### 执行

1. **选择低负荷时间。** 第一次可以避开最繁忙时段，选一家价格和菜单大致可预期、交通方便的店。你不是在证明能处理最高并发。
2. **进门后先找流程。** 看是否有前台、取号机、自助点单或“请自行入座”标识。看不懂就直接问工作人员。
3. **说明人数。** 说“你好，一位”或 “Table for one, please.” 一是完整整数，不是等待第二个人上线的临时状态。
4. **确认座位需求。** 需要安静、靠边、轮椅空间、避免高脚椅或更清楚照明时，礼貌提出；店家无法满足时，你可以换店。
5. **先解决必须知道的问题。** 对过敏或严重饮食限制，只在店家能可靠确认配料与交叉接触时点餐；信息不足就不要冒险。
6. **选择一顿可完成的餐。** 第一次不必点“招牌全套”。选一到两样你理解、预算内且份量合理的食物；对辣度、份量或点餐方式不清楚就问。
7. **等待时做你愿意做的事。** 看环境、读一点东西、记笔记、使用手机或什么都不做。独自坐着不是需要修复的空闲进程。
8. **收到食物先核对。** 确认大致是你点的内容，并处理明显错误、遗漏或必须的饮食问题。小偏好可以决定接受，也可以礼貌提出。
9. **按自己的节奏吃。** 不为占桌而过快，也不需要故意拖延证明自在。需要打包时先问本店是否提供及是否收费。
10. **结账前观察本地流程。** 有些地方桌边结账，有些去柜台；服务费、小费、税和付款习惯因地区不同。看账单，确认项目和金额，再付款。
11. **离开。** 收好物品，必要时说谢谢，然后走出去。没有人会在门口发放“独自用餐资格证”，这次本身就是记录。

### 成功条件

你完成了进门、说明人数、点餐、处理必要问题、吃或决定停止、结账和离开。成功不要求没有紧张、没有沉默，也不要求你喜欢这家店。

### 常见报错与补救

- **店关门、满座或只收预约：** 换到预先准备的第二选择，或把任务降级成外带。一次店铺状态不代表你失败。
- **流程看不懂：** 用准备好的那句问法。工作人员每天解释流程，接口文档就在现场。
- **菜单完全不适合：** 点一杯允许的饮品后礼貌离开，或在点单前直接离开；不为“都坐下了”支付一顿不合适的饭。
- **上错菜或账单不对：** 平静说“我点的是……，麻烦帮我核对一下”。只描述事实和需要的修正。
- **突然很不舒服：** 把任务降级为结账/取消、打包（若安全合适）并离开。你不需要完成整顿饭来证明任何事。
- **语言沟通困难：** 指菜单、写下菜名、使用店家认可的翻译方式，或选择沟通更可访问的餐馆。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

排队、座位、结账、小费、外带和独自用餐文化差异很大。这里不把任何地区习惯写成普遍礼仪；现场标识和礼貌询问优先。预算、行动、感官或沟通条件不合适时，外带、熟悉的小店、商场餐区或与朋友先走一遍都是有效替代。

现实副作用是一顿饭、一笔消费，以及你获得了“下次不再是第一次”的本地缓存。它不自动意味着你从此喜欢独处。

## English

Load this Skill when you want to try an unfamiliar restaurant alone and worry that the interface after the door has not been documented. The goal is not to perform “experienced solo diner.” It is to complete a meal you can shrink or exit at any time.

### Preparation and inputs

Before leaving, confirm:

- address, opening hours, reservation needs, and entry/queue method;
- approximate menu prices, total budget, and payment method;
- allergy, religious, cultural, or dietary boundaries you must keep;
- transport home, restroom, accessible entrance, and seating needs;
- how long you want to stay and your exit route if it feels wrong.

If no accessible menu exists, prepare one sentence: “Hi, it is my first time here. Could you explain how ordering works?” This is normal interface discovery, not a version disclosure.

### Execution

1. **Choose a lower-load time.** For a first attempt, avoid peak service and select somewhere with understandable prices, food, and transport. You do not need to prove maximum concurrency.
2. **Find the entry flow.** Look for a host, ticket machine, counter, self-order screen, or seat-yourself sign. Ask staff when it is unclear.
3. **State the party size.** Say “One, please” or “Table for one, please.” One is a complete integer, not a temporary state waiting for another person.
4. **Name seating needs.** Ask politely for quiet, edge seating, wheelchair room, a non-bar-height chair, or clearer light. You can choose another place if the restaurant cannot provide an essential condition.
5. **Resolve necessary food questions first.** For severe allergies or strict restrictions, order only when staff can reliably confirm ingredients and cross-contact. Leave rather than guessing.
6. **Choose a meal you can finish.** You do not need the signature banquet. Pick one or two understood, affordable, reasonable-size items; ask about heat, portion, or ordering customs when needed.
7. **Wait in your own way.** Observe the room, read, write, use your phone, or do nothing. Sitting alone is not an idle process that requires remediation.
8. **Check the arrival.** Confirm it broadly matches your order and raise wrong items, omissions, or necessary dietary problems. You may accept or politely correct a minor preference issue.
9. **Eat at your pace.** Do not rush only to free the table or linger to prove comfort. Ask whether takeaway is available and charged before relying on it.
10. **Observe the local payment flow.** Payment may happen at the table or counter. Service charges, tips, tax, and payment customs vary. Read the bill and verify items and amount before paying.
11. **Leave.** Collect your belongings, say thanks if appropriate, and walk out. No certificate is issued at the door; the attempt is already recorded.

### Success

You entered, stated one person, ordered, handled necessary questions, ate or chose to stop, paid, and left. Success does not require zero nerves, zero silence, or liking the restaurant.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Closed, full, or reservation-only:** Use a prepared second option or downgrade to takeaway. One restaurant state is not your failure.
- **The flow is unclear:** Use the prepared question. Staff explain the interface every day.
- **Nothing on the menu works:** Order an appropriate drink and leave politely, or leave before ordering. Sitting down does not require buying an unsuitable meal.
- **Wrong food or bill:** Calmly say what you ordered and ask for a check. Describe the fact and needed correction.
- **You become uncomfortable:** Downgrade to cancelling/paying, taking away only when safe and suitable, and leaving. Finishing every bite proves nothing.
- **Communication is inaccessible:** Point to the menu, write the item, use a restaurant-accepted translation method, or choose a more accessible venue.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and known side effects

Queueing, seating, tipping, payment, takeaway, and solo-dining norms vary widely. This Skill does not promote one region's manners to a universal protocol; posted rules and polite questions win. Takeaway, a familiar café, a food hall, or first visiting with someone are valid alternatives when budget, mobility, sensory, or communication access requires them.

Known side effects are one meal, one bill, and a local cache entry called “next time is not the first.” It does not automatically make you enjoy being alone.
