## 中文

你要把**尚未完成**的一项普通工作、学习或志愿协作任务交给别人继续，而接手者不能只凭“文件都在里面”知道当前状态时，用这份 Skill。目标是交出一份对方能找到材料、看清已验证成果、障碍和第一步的短记录，并确认谁真正接手。它不把未完成说成已验收，不替组织决定新负责人的权限，也不替代 H250 在自己仍负责时向别人求助。

### 准备与输入

拿出最初确认的产物、边界、截止、验收者与当前任务位置。确认你为什么需要交接、谁有权指定接手人，以及对方能否访问材料；没有接手人或授权时先把状态报给原负责人，不往群里随意转发私有文件。区分“我已经做过”“我实际核实过”“我打算做却还没做”。

### 执行

1. **写一个真实状态行。** 用日期和任务名称开头，标“进行中、等待输入、受阻或待审”；说明已完成的可观察产物及最后一次实际检查结果。不要只写百分比或“基本好了”；如果有旧版和新版，指出哪份是当前工作版本、哪份只是参考。
2. **列未完成与障碍。** 每项写剩下什么、为何还没完成、已尝试什么和仍缺谁的输入或决定。把“发现的问题”与“猜测原因”分开；没有解决的错误留原样，不用静默默认值或漂亮截图遮住。截止和依赖关系按原约定标明，推测的完成日不要写成正式承诺。
3. **给能动手的第一步。** 对接手者写“先打开〔获准位置/版本〕，核对〔关键事实〕，然后联系〔实际负责人〕处理〔未决项〕”；附必要材料的真实名称或权限内链接，而不是给一串可能过期的目录。需要凭据、敏感文件或系统权限时指向组织正式申请路径，不在交接正文复制密码或私人资料。
4. **读回责任边界。** 与被指定接手者核对他能否打开材料、是否理解第一步、哪些后续由他承担、哪些仍由你或原负责人承担。若对方不能接或无权限，标“交接待接收”，继续向有权者报告；不能把消息已发送当责任已转移。
5. **留下可追踪的最终交接状态。** 记录交接版本、发送渠道/日期、接收方的实际答复和仍未解决的事项。若原任务需要通知验收者或协作者，按真实权限发简短状态；不要同时维护两份互相冲突的“最新进度”。接手后才发生的新问题由实际负责人按组织规则处理，不把你的旧猜测留作不可修改的指令。

### 成功条件

接手者能找到正确版本与获准材料，知道已做、已核实、未做、障碍、真实时限和第一步；责任转移范围由有权者与接手者实际确认，或明确仍是待接收。完成的是**可用交接**，不是原任务完成、验收通过或接手者已解决障碍。

### 常见报错与补救

- **“东西都在共享盘”但没有版本和入口：** 给当前文件的实际路径/名称、最后核对时间与下一步。
- **把计划中的工作写进已完成项：** 拆为已做与待做，并说明证据或缺口。
- **文件链接接手人打不开：** 走正式授权或提供获准可访问版本；没解决前标交接待接收。
- **有阻塞却只写“等别人”：** 写清等谁、等什么、对原截止的影响和何时再查。
- **接手人没回复，旧负责人以为责任已转：** 更正状态为“已发送、待接收”，联系有权协调的人，不替对方同意。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

适用于低风险普通协作的在制任务，不作劳动职责、人事、法律、财务、医疗或专业系统的交接裁定。Atlassian 的项目进度说明列完成项、风险、障碍、决定和下一步；英国项目管理指南强调未结项及责任归属。这些只是信息结构参照，真实移交权限按你的组织制度。不能使用同一在线工具时，可给纸面或可访问文字版本，但仍要有唯一当前版本。诚实列出未完成工作，可能让进度显得不那么漂亮，却能让下一位从真实位置继续。

## English

Use this Skill when you need someone else to continue an **unfinished** ordinary work, study, or volunteer task and “everything is in the folder” will not tell them where it stands. Leave a short record that lets them find the work, distinguish verified progress from blockers, and take the first step, with a real receiver. It does not claim completion or acceptance, assign authority for an organization, or replace H250's request for help while you still own the task.

### Preparation and inputs

Find the originally agreed output, scope, deadline, reviewer, and current task location. Know why it is being handed over, who may assign the next owner, and whether that person can access the material. Without a receiver or authority, report the state to the original owner instead of broadcasting private files. Separate “I did,” “I verified,” and “I planned but have not done.”

### Execution

1. **Write one honest status line.** Start with task name and date. Mark “in progress, awaiting input, blocked, or awaiting review.” State observable work done and the last actual check result. Avoid only a percentage or “almost there.” If old and new versions exist, name the current working version and which is reference only.
2. **List remaining work and blockers.** For each, say what remains, why it remains, what was tried, and whose input or decision is missing. Separate observed error from guessed cause. Leave unresolved errors visible rather than covering them with a default or a polished screenshot. Keep real deadlines and dependencies; a guessed finish date is not an accepted commitment.
3. **Give a first action someone can perform.** Say “Open [permitted location/version], check [key fact], then contact [actual owner] about [open item].” Include real names or permitted links for necessary material, not a trail of stale folders. For credentials, private files, or system rights, point to the organization's formal access path; do not paste passwords or private records into the handover.
4. **Read back responsibility.** Ask the designated receiver to confirm access, first action, what they take on, and what remains with you or the original owner. If they cannot accept or lack permission, mark “handover awaiting receiver” and tell the authorized coordinator. Sending a message does not transfer responsibility by itself.
5. **Leave a traceable handover state.** Record version, send channel/date, the receiver's actual answer, and unresolved items. Notify reviewer or collaborators under real authority where needed. Do not maintain two conflicting “latest” progress accounts. New issues after receipt go to the real owner under the organization's rules, not to a frozen guess from the old owner.

### Success

The receiver can find the current version and permitted material, and identify done, verified, undone, blocked, actual deadline, and first action. The scope of responsibility was confirmed by the authorized people and receiver, or is explicitly still awaiting acceptance. The completed result is **a usable handover**, not finished work, approval, or resolution of the blocker.

### Common errors and recovery

- **“It's on the shared drive” has no version or entry point:** Give the real current file, last check time, and next action.
- **Planned work appears under completed:** Split done from pending and name evidence or gap.
- **The receiver cannot open the link:** Use formal permission or an authorized accessible copy. Keep handover pending until resolved.
- **“Waiting on others” hides a blocker:** Name who, what input, effect on the deadline, and when to check again.
- **No receiver replied but the old owner assumes transfer:** Correct to “sent, awaiting acceptance” and contact someone authorized to coordinate. Do not consent for them.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is for low-risk in-progress collaboration, not deciding employment duties, personnel, legal, financial, medical, or specialized system transfer. Atlassian's project-progress guide uses completed items, risks, blockers, decisions, and next steps; UK project guidance points to owners for outstanding changes, risks, and documents. These are information examples, not authority rules for your organization. Paper or accessible text can replace a shared tool while keeping one current version. Honest unfinished work can look less polished, but it lets the next person continue from reality.
