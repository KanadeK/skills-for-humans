## 中文

当你负责**已安置并熟悉室内空间的自有兔或稳定兔群**，要让它们进入已有活动区前，加载这份 Skill。结果是可见咬线、误食植物、其他宠物、逃逸和打滑风险按原方案被控制，兔有可回的躲藏处；若不能控制就不放行。这不是设计新兔笼、安排新兔见面或诊断兔的恐惧。兔若不吃或没有新粪便，立即联系兽医，不用活动去“振作”。

### 准备与输入

读原主人/兽医对这只兔的活动范围、同伴、可碰材料与监督条件。兔仍留在其熟悉安全区，你先检查指定空间；拿可收起的线缆、小植物、清洁用品、纺织细绳及容易咬碎的杂物清单。看门窗、低处缝隙、地面是否过滑、躲藏箱能否进出；猫狗等其他宠物按原管理方案隔开，不凭“平时很友好”无人监督共处。

### 执行

1. **先控物理边界。** 关好可逃出口，移走或用兔无法接触的合用方式隔离电线、电源、危险植物、药品/化学清洁品和易吞小件；不能可靠隔离就缩小获准活动范围。别临时把兔放进狭小笼子当替代长期活动空间。
2. **看脚下和藏身点。** 地面按本兔原方案有不易滑的可走区，睡/饮水/厕所入口保持畅通；至少有熟悉可逃离人视线的安全躲藏处，不把兔堵在单出口盒子里。原伴兔仍能按既有组合一起或分区，不临时改变配对关系。
3. **安排在场监督与撤回路。** 确认你或已同意的熟悉照料者可在场，看见活动区主要角落、能在兔自愿回熟悉区域时不强抓。若你不能观察且门线边界不可靠，今天不开放该区。
4. **只在检查通过后放行。** 兔可按原方案自行进入，观察有无继续咬到不该碰的物件或被其他宠物惊扰；出现风险就安全关闭该区并修正，不追兔、拖耳朵或强抱。

### 完成、常见问题与恢复

活动区的边界、可咬物、脚下、藏身处、伴兔/其他宠物及监督都已按本兔方案核实后才开放，或一个无法消除的风险使活动暂缓。只是开门让兔跑，事后再找电线，不算准备完成。

- **发现兔可咬到电线/危险植物：** 先隔离或关区，不靠口头喊“别咬”。
- **其他宠物会进入：** 分区与现场监督不能保证时暂不开放。
- **地面太滑或盒子只一出口：** 改回本兔已熟悉的可走/可藏布局，不强行试。
- **兔活动中躲藏或紧张：** 保留其退出通道、降低干扰，不强拉出来展示。
- **视力/行动限制：** 清单、可信协助先查低处与门缝；无法持续安全监督就缩小范围。

### 假设与边界

兔需要空间、遮蔽和安全咀嚼环境，但此篇不替代福利组织的完整居住面积/同伴评估。已有主人与兽医方案优先，检查通过也不保证绝对无风险。异常食欲和排便为兽医紧急入口，不在这里处理。

## English

Load this Skill before an **already-owned rabbit or established bonded group enters its familiar indoor exercise space**. Finish with visible cable, plant, other-pet, escape, and slip risks controlled under the existing plan and a route back to a hide, or do not open the area. This does not design new housing, introduce new rabbits, or diagnose fear. If the rabbit is not eating or produces no new droppings, contact a vet immediately rather than trying exercise as a cure.

### Preparation and inputs

Read the owner's or vet's actual allowed area, companions, chewable materials, and supervision plan. Keep rabbits in their familiar safe space while you inspect. List cords, small plants, cleaners, strings, and swallowable items to move. Check doors, low gaps, slippery floor, and a hide with usable entrance and exit. Separate cats/dogs under the established plan; “usually friendly” does not authorize unsupervised contact.

### Execution

1. **Control physical boundaries.** Close escape routes and remove or reliably shield cords, power, unsafe plants, medicines/cleaners, and small chewable items. Shrink the approved activity area if barriers cannot work. A tiny temporary cage is not a substitute for adequate ongoing space.
2. **Check footing and hide access.** Use non-slip routes under this rabbit's plan and keep access to sleeping, water, and toilet. Retain a familiar hide it can use to leave people's view, without a trapped one-exit box. Keep established rabbit companions in their usual arrangement, not a surprise bonding experiment.
3. **Arrange supervision and a return route.** You or a consenting familiar carer must be able to watch main corners and let the rabbit return voluntarily. With no observer and unreliable door/cord boundary, do not open the area today.
4. **Open only after the check.** Allow entry under the plan and watch for new reachable hazards or other pets startling it. Close the area safely and correct a hazard rather than chase, drag by ears, or force-hold the rabbit.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

Boundaries, chew hazards, footing, hides, established companions, other pets, and supervision are checked under this rabbit's plan before access, or an unresolved hazard defers it. Opening the gate first and finding cables afterwards is unfinished preparation.

- **Cords or unsafe plants remain reachable:** Isolate them or close the area, not a verbal “don't chew.”
- **Other pets can enter:** Delay without reliable separation and supervision.
- **Floor is slippery or hide traps the rabbit:** Return to its familiar safe layout instead of forcing the trial.
- **Rabbit hides or appears stressed:** Keep an exit path and reduce disturbance, without pulling it out to display.
- **Sight or movement is limited:** A checklist and trusted low-level inspection help; shrink the area if you cannot supervise safely.

### Assumptions and limits

Rabbits need space, shelter, and safe chewing conditions, but this is not a full welfare housing-size or companionship assessment. Owner/vet plans govern; a passed check is not zero risk. Abnormal eating or droppings are urgent veterinary exits, not part of this routine.
