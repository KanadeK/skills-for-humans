## 中文

当你看完一个带解法的例子，觉得自己会了，但还没在**另一道题**上动手时，加载这份 Skill。做一次不看示例解答的独立尝试，并核对实际做到哪一步。只用于普通学习练习；不替代专业操作训练，也不拿正在评分、禁止合作或限制资料的作业破坏课程规则。

### 准备与输入

拿到已学示例和一项**相近但不相同**的新任务，例如同一概念的另一道习题、同一写作要求的新短段落。找得到答案、评分标准、可靠教材解释或可询问的教师/助教，才能在尝试后核对。先确认新题改变了什么条件、哪些要求相同；若只是把示例数字或词语机械替换，换一项真正需要自己选择步骤的题。需要读屏、大字、语音输入或较长作答时间，就预先采用；这些可及工具不等于偷看解法。

### 执行

1. **写明本次要检验的动作。** 用一句话写“读到这种条件时，我能自己选择方法并完成什么”，并给本次练习一个合理停点，例如 20 分钟或完成一个短答。看懂解释不是这个检验动作。
2. **收起示例的步骤与答案。** 保留题目要求和课程允许的参考资料；答案、视频演示、他人的成品先不看。若这是开卷技能，按真实允许的资料练，不把禁用资料当作独立完成。
3. **先读新题再选路。** 标出已知、要交的结果以及与示例不同的条件。先说或写你准备采用的方法及原因，再动笔、操作普通练习材料或作答。不要把示例句子逐行搬过去。
4. **做完整的一次尝试。** 每一步留下足够痕迹，使你之后能看出在哪一步作了决定；短答可写出判断依据。卡住时先写“卡在什么选择、试过什么”，给自己一次回想机会。到达停点仍做不出，就标“独立未完成”，停止本轮；求助之前保留原尝试。
5. **完成后才核对。** 用答案、标准或可信解释比对结果和关键条件。只有结果相同但理由不通，标“结果碰巧对”；没有可靠核对途径，标“暂未验证”，不要自判掌握。圈出一个具体差异供下一次修正；系统分析错因归 H256。
6. **留下可复查的结论。** 记录题目出处、是否使用了提示、完成/未完成/未验证、一道新题改变的条件和下一动作。一次成功只说明这次在这些条件下做到了，不担保换题、考试或长期保持；隔一段时间再测归 H257。

### 完成、常见问题与补救

你留下了一道与例子不完全相同的新题、未看解答时的原始作答，以及一次可核对的完成状态；即使做错，只要真实记录卡点，本次检验也完成了。

- **新题只换数字或名词：** 换一题有不同条件或需要自己选择方法的练习；只有机械替换不能证明迁移。
- **写第一步就看答案：** 标记“用了提示”，收起答案后再找另一道可核对的新题；不把本次写成独立成功。
- **看不懂题意或缺少先备知识：** 记下具体词、条件或步骤，回课程材料核实；需要人帮忙时带着原尝试提问。
- **没有答案或评分标准：** 保留原尝试为“暂未验证”，找可靠核对人或材料；没有途径就不声称正确。
- **疲劳、疼痛或过载：** 暂停并记下位置，换可及形式或短一点的任务；不要为了计时继续不适合的操作。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

可用的相近练习和核对渠道因课程、语言与资源而异；纸笔、可访问电子文档或口述录音都能保留独立作答。教材和教师可给不同的正确表达，按本任务真正的条件与评分点判断，不按一个词逐字匹配。你可能发现示例对你的支持比想象中多；这是下一次学习的输入，不是人格报告。

## English

Load this Skill after a worked example feels clear but before you have tried a **different task** yourself. Make one attempt without viewing the example's solution, then check what you actually managed. This is for ordinary study practice, not a substitute for professional hands-on training. Respect assignment rules about grading, collaboration, and permitted resources.

### Preparation and inputs

Have an example you have studied and a **comparable but different** task, such as another exercise using the same concept or a fresh short paragraph with the same writing requirement. Find an answer, rubric, trustworthy course explanation, or teacher or tutor who can check your attempt afterward. Identify what changed and what stayed the same. If the new task merely swaps the example's numbers or words, choose one that requires you to select a step yourself. Arrange screen reading, large print, speech input, or extra time if useful; access tools are distinct from seeing the solution.

### Execution

1. **Name the action being tested.** Write one sentence: “Given these conditions, I can choose an approach and produce this result.” Set a reasonable stopping point, such as 20 minutes or one short response. Recognising an explanation is not the action being tested.
2. **Hide the example's steps and answer.** Keep the task instructions and resources your course allows. Do not view the answer, demonstration, or someone else's finished work yet. For an open-resource skill, use the resources actually allowed; forbidden material does not count as independent completion.
3. **Read the new task and choose an approach.** Mark what is known, what you must produce, and the condition that differs from the example. Say or write which method you will use and why before solving or drafting. Do not copy the example line by line.
4. **Make one full attempt.** Leave enough of each step to see where you made decisions; a short answer can include its reason. If stuck, name the exact choice that blocks you and what you tried, then give yourself one chance to recall it. At the stopping point, mark “not completed independently” and stop this round; keep the original attempt before asking for help.
5. **Check only afterward.** Compare the result and key conditions with an answer, rubric, or trustworthy explanation. If the outcome matches but the reasoning fails, mark “correct by chance.” Without a reliable check, mark “not yet verified” rather than declaring mastery. Circle one specific difference for the next attempt; detailed error diagnosis belongs to H256.
6. **Leave a checkable conclusion.** Record the task source, whether you used hints, completed/not completed/unverified, the changed condition, and one next action. One success shows what you did under these conditions, not future grades or lasting retention. A later check belongs to H257.

### Completion, common errors, and recovery

You have a different task, your original response made without seeing its solution, and a checkable completion state. A wrong attempt still completes this test when its sticking point is recorded honestly.

- **The task only changes a number or noun:** Choose one with a different condition or a genuine method choice; mechanical substitution says little about transfer.
- **You open the answer after step one:** Mark “hint used,” then try another checkable new task with the answer hidden. Do not label this attempt independent success.
- **The wording or prerequisite is unclear:** Record the precise term, condition, or step and check course material. Bring your attempt when asking someone for help.
- **There is no answer or rubric:** Keep the attempt “not yet verified” and find a reliable person or source to check it. Do not claim correctness without one.
- **Fatigue, pain, or overload:** Pause with your place recorded and switch to an accessible format or shorter task. A timer does not require unsuitable work.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Comparable tasks and checking routes differ by course, language, and access. Paper, an accessible document, or a spoken recording can preserve an independent attempt. A textbook or teacher may accept several correct expressions; judge the actual conditions and criteria, not one exact phrase. You may discover the example was doing more work for you than you thought. That is input for the next session, not a verdict on you.
