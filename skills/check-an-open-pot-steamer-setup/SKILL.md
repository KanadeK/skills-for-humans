## 中文

当配方要求你用**普通锅内的蒸汽**处理食物，而锅、盖、蒸篮或容器尚未组合好时，加载这份 Skill。目标是得到一个稳固、食物不泡在水里、蒸汽能到达食物且水不会轻易烧干的**开火前布置**。它不把普通锅密封成压力锅，不处理罐藏、热油、设备维修或具体食物的安全熟度。

### 准备与输入

读本次食物的可靠配方或包装说明，确认食物量、是否需要蒸篮/耐热盘、加水方式、盖盖、计时起点与完成条件。查看锅、盖、蒸篮和任何盛食容器的制造商说明：它们是否适合当前热源与蒸汽温度、重量是否在设计范围内。不要把外观像塑料餐盒或普通碗当作“肯定耐蒸”。锅具和篮架有裂损、摇晃或把手不稳就先换。

### 执行步骤

1. **先安置空锅和支撑件。** 普通锅平稳放在可用炉头上；蒸篮或架放入后不摇、不倾、不触碰不该触碰的热源。把手朝内，留出盖子可安全移开的空间。若用盘盛食，盘须按其说明耐受这种蒸制并稳稳坐在架上，不遮住配方需要的蒸汽通道。
2. **用冷水核对水位。** 按配方与锅具说明加入足以持续本次蒸制的水，同时让水面低于食物或蒸篮底部的工作面；若一加水就浸到食物，这不是该配方的蒸法。加水过少又无法安全补水，也不能开火。没有适用的通用“几厘米”数字；按本器具和配方的实际标线决定。
3. **试放本批食物和锅盖。** 在开火前确认食物量放得下、盖可按说明闭合、不压住食物或摇晃。食物太多就分批，别靠挤压盖子封住蒸汽。盖子不等于压力锁；普通锅不尝试密封增压。
4. **计划观察与结束。** 找到安全可达的火力开关、计时器、合适的隔热工具和放热篮的耐热位置；确认你能在配方要求时检查水位、关闭热源并取出食物。锅或水量超出你的搬运能力时，用壶或量杯在炉上加水，取食时让热水留在锅中。
5. **开火前给出结论。** “器具适用、支撑稳定、水不浸食物且足够、盖与蒸汽通路可用、我能看守并安全结束”五件事都成立才开始；少一项便换器具、减量或停止。

### 完成与停止

蒸锅组合满足上述五项，且你知道具体配方如何计时和检查食物，就完成准备。器具适用性不明、盛食容器可能变形、篮具不稳、盖压食物、水位矛盾或无法安全取出时，不加热。烟、明火、燃气异味或设备故障退出本日常流程，按当地紧急指导处理。

### 常见报错与补救

- **蒸篮直接泡在水里：** 按器具说明改水位或换有合适高度的篮/架；不要把水煮误标为蒸制。
- **水只够一会儿：** 先核对配方和锅的容量；换适合本次时长的锅或分批，不靠“到时再想办法加水”开火。
- **临时盘材质不明：** 查确切型号的耐热/蒸汽说明；查不到就换明确适用的容器，不以饭后没碎作为验证。
- **篮具装满后盖不稳：** 减量分批，保持盖按说明放置；不压住盖子冒险。
- **握锅或站立困难：** 开火前调整到可及的炉头，用量杯加水、稳定篮具或现场协助；不能安全完成取出路径就暂缓。

### 假设、替代与副作用

这里专指**非压力的普通锅蒸制**。不同锅、篮、炉头和食物的水位、耐热能力与时间不同；具体器具说明和配方优先于通用例子。不能只靠手摸判断耐热，视觉或握持不便时可请可信的人读说明、用大字计时器、在开火前冷态试放。可能的副作用是你得换一只符合说明的篮具；这比发现餐盒在热蒸汽里改造自己更容易收尾。

## English

Load this Skill when a recipe calls for **steam from an ordinary pot** but the pot, lid, basket, or food container has not yet been assembled. Produce a **preheat setup** that is stable, keeps food above the water, lets steam reach it, and has enough water for the intended job. Do not seal an ordinary pot into a pressure cooker. Canning, hot oil, equipment repair, and food-specific safe doneness are outside this Skill.

### Preparation and inputs

Read the reliable recipe or package for this food: amount, basket or heat-safe dish, water method, lid, timing start, and completion check. Consult the maker's directions for the pot, lid, basket, and any dish holding food: suitability for this heat source and steam, plus supported load. A plastic-looking lunch box or ordinary bowl is not automatically steam-safe. Replace anything cracked, rocking, or fitted with an insecure handle.

### Execution steps

1. **Place the empty pot and support first.** Keep the ordinary pot stable on a usable burner. A basket or rack must not rock, lean, or touch a heat source it should not. Turn handles in and leave room to lift the lid safely. A food dish must be approved for this steaming setup, sit securely on its rack, and leave the steam path the recipe needs.
2. **Check the water level while cold.** Add enough water under the recipe and equipment directions to support the cooking period, while keeping it below the food or basket's working base. Food already submerged is not the steaming method requested. Too little water with no safe way to replenish is also a stop. There is no universal centimetre depth for every pot; use this equipment's marks and directions.
3. **Test the batch and lid before heating.** Check that food fits and the lid closes as directed without crushing food or rocking. Divide an oversized batch instead of forcing the lid down. A lid is not a pressure lock; do not try to pressurise an ordinary pot.
4. **Plan observation and removal.** Identify a safely reachable heat control, timer, suitable heat protection, and a heat-safe landing place for the basket. Make sure you can check water when required, switch off heat, and remove food under the recipe. If moving the pot is beyond your capacity, add water with a jug while it rests on the hob and leave hot water there when lifting food.
5. **Make the preheat decision.** Start only if the tools are suitable, supports stable, water is sufficient without submerging food, lid and steam route work, and you can attend and end the process safely. Change equipment, reduce the batch, or pause if any element is missing.

### Success and stop

The steamer meets those five conditions, and you know how the food-specific recipe will time and check the food. Do not heat when equipment suitability is unclear, a dish may deform, the basket rocks, the lid crushes food, water level is contradictory, or removal cannot be safe. Smoke, flame, gas odour, or equipment failure exits this everyday flow under local emergency guidance.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The basket sits in water:** Adjust to the equipment's supported level or choose a basket or rack with appropriate clearance. Do not relabel boiling in water as steaming.
- **Water appears too little for the job:** Check the recipe and pot capacity before heating. Change pot or divide the work instead of relying on an improvised refill.
- **A temporary dish has no material information:** Check the exact model's heat and steam directions or use a clearly suitable container. “It survived last time” is not a specification.
- **A full basket makes the lid unstable:** Divide the batch and use the lid as directed, rather than pressing it down.
- **Gripping or standing is difficult:** Choose an accessible burner, jug filling, stable basket, or in-person help before heating. Pause if there is no safe removal path.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is **non-pressure open-pot steaming**. Pot, basket, hob, and food alter water depth, heat tolerance, and timing; the exact equipment instructions and recipe outrank generic examples. Touch is not a heat-safety test. For visual or grip limits, have a trusted person read the instructions, use a large-display timer, and test the layout cold. A properly specified basket may be needed; that is easier to manage than a lunch box changing shape in hot steam.
