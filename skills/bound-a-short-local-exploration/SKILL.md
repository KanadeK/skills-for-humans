## 中文

当你想在住处或熟悉交通点附近随意看看一个街区、公共艺术或几处公开设施，却不想把“再拐一个弯”变成无终点漫游时，用这份 Skill 先定**探索范围、返回条件与联系办法**，再在范围内自由走一小段。结果不是精确导航到某地址，也不是野外徒步；H291 的公园固定路线和 H161–H170 的具体找路由它们自己处理。

### 准备与输入

选你知道如何到达和返回的公共起点，写一片小范围的边界（如几条有明确路名的街道围成的区域），和一个可辨认的返回点/公共交通站。只包含允许进入的开放区域；不把工地、封闭馆舍、私人楼道、深夜偏僻地或水边危险地带纳入。看当地正式步行地图或现场标识中的人行道、过街点、坡度/无障碍情况与临时封闭，按自身行动与感官条件缩小范围。定最晚返回时点，设备电量和常用联系方法；独自去不熟悉的区域时，可让可信联系人知道大致范围和预计结束时间，并约定到达后报平安，不必公开实时位置或私人日程。

### 执行

1. **给“随意走”上边界。** 在地图/纸上圈起点、范围边缘、一个最远的回头标识和回程站点。你可以在圈内改变看到什么，但不临时扩大范围；若必须越界才能看到目标，留到另一次重新计划。
2. **核返程与通知。** 算进公共交通班次或常用回程时间、休息和路线余量，写“到____时无论看到什么都返回”。对同行或可信联系人说明最晚结束和联系渠道；若人未回复，不把“消息发出”当已约好接应。
3. **只从公开路径观察。** 走到一个你感兴趣且允许接近的公开点，停下看一处细节或设施说明；要进入馆舍、参加活动或拍别人，另按相应场所和隐私规则。沿途在岔路先看路名/指示，不用猜捷径穿私人院落。
4. **每次改变方向先核边界。** 问“我还在圈内吗？从这里能沿公开路线返回吗？时间够吗？”若路面/坡道不合、公共通道封闭、天气或感官负担变化，就从最后确认的公共点返回；需要实际找路时用本地正式标识或向工作人员问路，本篇不教陌生城市救援。
5. **在真实返回点结束。** 到起点/回程站确认自己能返回，按约定通知同行/可信联系人已结束。记“一处看到的东西、一个没走的分支和原因”；没完成原想看的全部也可成功，只要范围、时间与返程已守住。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你有明确的**起点、外围边界、折返点/时间、返回方式及必要联系**，实际在公开范围内完成一个短观察并回到可返回的位置。手机显示一串步数、却不知道怎么回去，不算这项任务完成。

- **发现一处更有趣但越界：** 标记下次单独计划，不为“都来了”扩大当次范围。
- **地图路线被临时封住：** 回到已知公共节点，查现场公告或向工作人员确认可行返回；无明确路线时终止探索。
- **手机电量不足或网络断了：** 用已记的公共边界与现场标识回熟悉点，不继续往新方向走。
- **同行临时分开：** 按预先约定的公共会合点与时间联系；联系不上就停止自由探索并按当地正式求助渠道处理，不靠猜追人。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

街区开放、交通、无障碍与治安环境因城市/时段变化；本篇只适合白天或普通开放时段、短距离、容易返回的低风险公共区域，不做犯罪风险评估或紧急搜救。可信联系是边界工具，不要求向应用公开持续定位。可用纸图、大字标识、官方无障碍地图或可信协助；你仍要能指出出口。天气条件不再适合时按天气改期流程结束，不把探索当作必须完成的挑战。

## English

Use this Skill when you want to wander through a nearby block, public art area, or a few public amenities from home or familiar transit without letting “one more turn” become an open-ended trip. Set an **exploration perimeter, return condition, and contact method** first, then move freely inside it for a short time. This is not turn-by-turn navigation to an address or wilderness hiking. H291 covers a chosen park path, and ordinary wayfinding is another task.

### Preparation and inputs

Choose a public start you know how to reach and leave. Define a small boundary, perhaps a few named streets, with a recognisable return point or transit stop. Include only places open to the public; exclude work sites, closed venues, private halls, remote late-night areas, or hazardous waterfront. Use an official walking map or on-site signs to check sidewalks, crossings, slope or access, and temporary closures, shrinking the area to fit your mobility and sensory conditions. Set a latest return time, device battery, and usual contact method. If exploring an unfamiliar area alone, you may tell a trusted person the broad area and expected end and agree to check in when back; you need not publish live location or private schedule.

### Execution

1. **Put a boundary on wandering.** Circle start, outer edges, one farthest turn marker, and return stop on paper or map. You may change what you look at inside, but do not enlarge the zone on impulse. A target requiring a new area belongs to another separately planned outing.
2. **Check return and contact.** Allow for transit timing, rest, and a route margin. Write “at ____ I return regardless of what else appears.” Give a companion or trusted contact the end time and method if relevant. A sent message with no acknowledgement is not an arranged pickup.
3. **Observe from public routes.** Reach one interesting public point and stop for a detail or facility sign. Entering a venue, joining an event, or photographing people follows its own rules. At forks, read streets and signs instead of cutting through a private courtyard.
4. **Before each new direction, check the boundary.** Ask whether you are inside the zone, can return by public route, and have enough time. For unsuitable surface, closure, weather, or sensory load, return from the last confirmed public point. Use official local signs or staff for real wayfinding; this Skill does not teach unfamiliar-city rescue.
5. **Finish at an actual return point.** At the start or transit stop verify you can return and tell companions or your trusted contact the outing has ended as agreed. Note one thing seen, one branch not taken, and why. Missing other sights does not undo a successfully bounded trip.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You had a clear **start, perimeter, turnaround marker or time, return route, and needed contact**, made one short observation in permitted public space, and reached a place from which you can return. A step count with no way home is not this task.

- **Something interesting lies outside the perimeter:** Save it for a separately planned next visit instead of stretching this one because you came so far.
- **A mapped passage is closed:** Return to a known public node and check notices or ask staff for a return. End exploration without a clear route.
- **Battery or connection fails:** Use recorded boundaries and signs to reach the familiar point rather than take new turns.
- **Companions separate unexpectedly:** Use the agreed public meeting place and time. If contact fails, end free wandering and use local official help instead of chasing a guessed direction.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Open streets, transit, access, and local safety change by place and time. This is for short, easy-return public areas in daylight or ordinary open hours, not crime-risk assessment or emergency search. A trusted contact is a boundary tool, not a requirement to broadcast location to an app. Paper maps, large print, an official accessible map, or trusted assistance can help, while you still identify the exit. When weather no longer fits, end under the separate weather-change workflow rather than turn exploration into a challenge.
