## 中文

当你有一批**来源和保存历史已知、确实适合冷冻**的普通食物，未来会分次使用，却不想每次解冻整包时，使用这份 Skill。结果是每份能单独取出、封好、看得懂的冷冻包。它不负责判断已发生的熟度或冷却过程，也不能用冷冻修复已经不安全的食物。

### 准备与输入

先读这件食物的包装或适用的可信处理说明，确认可否冷冻、是否需先做特定预处理，以及开封、日期和烹调要求。记录它是生的还是已经按适用流程处理好的食物、原包装上的准确名称与必要说明、你已知的购买/处理/冷冻日期。检查冰格确实正常、没有塞满，并准备干净、适合该食物及冷冻使用的袋或盒、可读的标签。若保存时间/温度史不清，或不知道它是否能安全冷冻，先停下核实；不要把“冻上”当修复按钮。

### 执行

1. **确定每份的使用场景。** 写下一次要取多少，例如一顿会用完的量；过大要反复解冻，过小则每次多开包装。具体份量按你真实用途，不用统一克数。
2. **保留关键原始信息。** 打开原包装前记下准确品名、生/熟状态、必要的储存或烹调说明，以及你确知的日期。商品批号或原标签若影响识别或追溯，可保留清晰照片或与新包装对应的记录。不要把重新装包日期冒充生产日期或原冷冻日期。
3. **在合适条件下分份。** 用干净台面和工具处理；生的动物性食品与即食食品、工具分开。只分已经按该食品说明完成所需预处理的部分。若产品必须按原包装直接烹调或不适合拆封冷冻，停止拆分并按原说明处理。
4. **封成独立单元。** 把每份放进该包装说明允许的食品适用冷冻袋或盒；排出可安全排出的多余空气并封严，液体按容器说明留膨胀空间。不要把不知是否适合冷冻的玻璃瓶或普通薄购物袋当作万能容器。
5. **在入冰格前写标签。** 每份至少有能区分的食物名称、份量或用途、真实装包/冷冻日期，以及读者以后需要知道的生熟和关键处理说明。日期含义写清楚；若某项历史未知，就明确写“未知”并停止把它当作已证实可长期保存的份，不编一张新证据。
6. **检查可取用性再冻结。** 包装能关合、标签在结霜后仍可读、每份能单独拿出，冰格仍能正常关闭并按当地/产品要求维持低温，才把食物及时放入。将来按具体食物和当地说明决定解冻与使用，标签不是延长安全期限的许可证。

### 成功条件

你能从冰格拿出任一份，直接知道**这是什么、一次用多少、何时按什么状态装入、还需遵守什么原说明**；包装封好且冷冻条件可满足。若关键事实或设备不成立，明确停止冷冻这一批，也是合格结果。

### 常见报错与补救

- **标签只写“肉”和“今天”：** 补上能区分的准确名称、生熟状态、份量与日期含义；原始信息已丢失时不要编造，转入保存信息不明的处理。
- **分完才发现冰格放不下：** 不把易腐食物留在室温等待空间；只有可靠冷藏/冷冻或及时安全使用途径时才继续，否则按当地安全指导处理。
- **袋口没封严或容器裂漏：** 在仍能确认食品状态且可安全操作时换合适包装；原料泄漏接触其他食品时先隔离，按食品安全要求处理受影响物。
- **想先解冻整块再切成份：** 不靠室温解冻完成分装。若需改变已冻食品的处理方式，先核对该食品和当地适用的安全说明；无法确认就维持原包或停止。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

不同食物的预处理、冷冻适用性、质量保持和使用时间各不相同；本 Skill 不给全部食品一套期限，也不处理罐藏、发酵、婴幼儿食品或过敏替代。看不清小字可放大拍照、用易读标签或请可信的人读出关键说明；握力或搬运受限时选更小的适用容器并在可及高度操作。冷冻可能改变质地，包装与标签也会占用空间和材料。

## English

Use this Skill when you have ordinary food with a **known source and storage history**, confirmed suitable for freezing, and you will use it in separate sessions. The result is sealed portions you can take out one at a time and identify without guessing. It does not decide whether earlier cooking or cooling was safe, and freezing cannot repair unsafe food.

### Preparation and inputs

Read this product's package or a trusted food-specific guide. Confirm that freezing is suitable, whether pretreatment is required, and the opening, date, and cooking directions. Record whether the food is raw or already prepared under an appropriate process, its exact original name and essential directions, and only the purchase, preparation, or freezing dates you actually know. Check that your freezer works and has room. Have clean freezer-suitable food bags or containers and readable labels. If the temperature history or freezing suitability is unknown, stop and check rather than treating freezing as a repair button.

### Execution

1. **Choose one-use amounts.** Write what you expect to take out at once, such as enough for a real meal. A portion too large invites repeated thawing; a tiny one adds openings. Use your own needs rather than a universal weight.
2. **Keep the source facts.** Before removing the original pack, record the exact food name, raw/cooked status, required storage or cooking directions, and dates you know. If the lot or label matters for identification or traceability, keep a clear photo or linked record. A new packing date is not the manufacturing date or an invented earlier freezing date.
3. **Divide under suitable conditions.** Use clean surfaces and tools. Keep raw animal food and its tools apart from ready-to-eat food. Portion only food that has completed any required food-specific pretreatment. If the product must be cooked in its original pack or should not be opened for freezing, stop dividing and follow its directions.
4. **Seal independent units.** Put each portion in food-suitable packaging approved for that food and freezer use. Remove excess air where safe and seal tightly; allow expansion room for liquids according to the container directions. An unverified glass jar or thin shopping bag is not a universal freezer container.
5. **Label before freezing.** Give each unit an identifying name, amount or intended use, real packing/freezing date, and any raw/cooked or preparation fact needed later. State what each date means. If a key part of the history is unknown, label it as unknown and do not count the portion as proven safe for long storage. Do not manufacture evidence with a fresh date.
6. **Check retrieval and freeze promptly.** Confirm that the seal holds, the label will remain readable, each unit can be removed alone, and the freezer can close and maintain the condition required locally and for this product. Then put the food in promptly. Use food-specific and local guidance for later thawing and use; a label does not extend a safety limit.

### Success

You can take any unit from the freezer and know **what it is, how much one use contains, when and in what state it was packed, and which original directions still apply**. It is sealed and the freezer can hold it properly. A clear stop when a critical fact or appliance is missing is also a complete result.

### Common errors and recovery

- **The label says only “meat” and “today”:** Add an exact identity, raw/cooked status, amount, and meaning of the date. If the original facts have gone, do not invent them; use the separate unknown-storage-information workflow.
- **The freezer is full after you portion the food:** Do not leave perishables at room temperature waiting for space. Continue only with reliable cold storage or prompt safe use; otherwise follow local safety guidance for the affected food.
- **The seal fails or a container leaks:** If the food's condition is still known and handling remains safe, replace the packaging. Isolate raw leaks and handle any food they touched under food-safety guidance.
- **You want to thaw a frozen block on the counter to cut it up:** Do not use room-temperature thawing to divide it. Check instructions specific to the food and your location before changing its handling; if uncertain, keep the original pack or stop.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Pretreatment, freezing suitability, quality, and use periods differ by food. This Skill gives no universal shelf life and does not cover canning, fermentation, infant food, or allergy substitution. If small print is inaccessible, enlarge a photo, use large labels, or ask a trusted person to read the key instructions. If gripping or lifting is difficult, use smaller suitable containers on a reachable work surface. Freezing can change texture, and packages and labels take space and materials.
