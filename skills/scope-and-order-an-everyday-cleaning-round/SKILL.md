## 中文

当你看到家中几处普通小污垢，想做**一轮有限的日常清洁**却不知道先从哪儿开始时，用这份 Skill 决定本轮范围、顺序、各处的专属做法入口和停止条件。它不把整间房变成永远清不完的项目，也不重复教擦地、洗浴室、去污或清理碎玻璃的具体动作。霉害、体液、虫害、危险化学品、浸水电器、高处或无法安全到达的污染先排出普通范围。

### 准备与输入

列出你实际看见的区域和问题：湿滑/正在扩散的水、餐后食物、明显手摸表面、地面尘土等；估算今天真实可用的时间、体力与材料。核对表面材质和清洁产品是否有可读说明，准备能分开处理厨房/浴室/其他区域的工具与晾干位置。和同住者共享的区域先确认进入与物品移动权限；没有授权不处理他人物品。身体或行动限制影响范围时直接缩小本轮，不以拖到失控为“完成”。

### 执行

1. **先把普通与必须停止的分开。** 持续漏水、电器进水、刺激性化学气味、体液、可见霉害、面积大到无法控制或高处作业不进入本轮；隔离危险并交物业/专业或当地紧急路径。普通已知小泼洒和湿滑通道则先隔开人行，再由对应泼洒任务处理，不等排到清单最后。
2. **选今天够完成的一段。** 从可见且会影响接下来使用的区域中，选可在实际时间/能力内完成的几项；每项写一个可观察的“完成/停在何处”，例如“台面无残渣且干了”“地面恢复可走”。需要未知材质、专业工具或别人同意的，单独列待确认，不边擦边猜。
3. **让依赖决定顺序。** 先止住会继续扩散或使人滑倒的事，再移走会挡清洁的可移动物和松散碎屑；同一区域通常先处理上方可及表面和干尘，再在表面许可时湿擦，最后处理会接住落尘或滴水的地面。具体材料步骤交对应 Skill 和产品标签，不能为遵守“从上到下”去爬高或让湿布碰电器。
4. **避免把脏工具带回干净区。** 厨房食物接触区、浴室、普通居室工具按用途区分，换区前按工具说明清洁或换一块；不要把一瓶清洁剂倒进另一瓶，也不凭“消毒”字样跳过先去污。准备让湿工具、地面和家具真正干燥的时间与空间。
5. **每完成一项就验收、决定下一项。** 对照事先写的可见结果，未干、仍滑、产品残留或材质受损就留在待处理/停用状态，别为了打钩移交给下一人。时间到了，将未做的具体项和原因记录到下次，而不是把未知风险包装成“清洁完成”。清洗/晾放本轮用过的工具，恢复安全通道。

### 成功条件、常见问题与补救

本轮每项都有实际完成或明确停止/下次去向，优先风险已隔离，已清表面与工具不会把污物带回，通道可以安全使用。并不要求全屋一尘不染或每个未确认材料都当场处理。

- **列了十件事，时间只够两件：** 先做扩散/滑倒和今天必须使用的两件，其余写下，不用强效产品换速度。
- **想拿同一块布从浴室擦到厨房：** 停下按用途换布/洗净工具，不把可见干净当成无交叉污染。
- **打算先拖地再清上方积尘：** 若没有材料原因，重排避免重新弄脏地面；但不为固定顺序爬高。
- **途中发现插座湿或化学品不明：** 立即退出普通清洁、隔离并走合格帮助，不靠清单优先级压过安全。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这是一轮小范围、可识别、日常居室清洁的安排，不是物业验收或卫生认证。材质、房间布局、天气、同住权利和体力不同，顺序可随真实依赖改变。视力/记忆/行动受限时用大字短清单、计时提醒、可及范围或可信协助，不要求一口气做完整屋。未做项有记录，不把清洁计划变成重复真源；每个表面的具体完成证据由其专属任务负责。

## English

Use this Skill when several ordinary small messes are visible at home and you want **one bounded everyday cleaning round** but do not know what to do first. Decide scope, order, which material task to use, and where to stop. This does not turn a room into an endless campaign or reteach floors, baths, stains, or glass-shard cleanup. Mold, bodily fluid, pests, hazardous chemicals, wet electrical equipment, heights, and unreachable contamination leave ordinary scope first.

### Preparation and inputs

List the actual places and observed problems: a spreading or slippery spill, post-meal food, high-touch surfaces, floor dust. Estimate today's real time, strength, and supplies. Check whether surfaces and products have readable directions. Have a way to separate kitchen, bathroom, and other tools and to dry them. Confirm access and permission before moving items in shared spaces; another person's things are not yours to clear by default. Shrink scope when mobility or energy makes a plan unrealistic.

### Execution

1. **Separate ordinary work from stops.** Continuing leaks, water in equipment, irritating chemistry, bodily fluids, visible mold, uncontrollable area, or height work leave this round. Isolate and use property, professional, or local emergency routes. For a known small spill or slippery path, warn people and use its containment task promptly rather than leave it last on the list.
2. **Choose a finishable slice today.** Pick the visible items that matter for next use and fit actual time and ability. Give each an observable finish or stop, such as “counter free of crumbs and dry” or “floor walkable.” Hold a task needing unknown material care, special equipment, or another person's permission rather than guess while wiping.
3. **Let dependencies set order.** Stop things spreading or slipping first. Move allowable objects and loose debris that block cleaning. In one area, accessible upper surfaces and dry dust generally precede permitted wet wiping, while floor work follows what may fall or drip onto it. The material Skill and label govern the actual method. Do not climb high or touch an appliance wet just to obey a top-down slogan.
4. **Do not carry dirty tools back into clean zones.** Distinguish food-contact kitchen, bathroom, and ordinary-room tools. Clean or replace a cloth under its directions before switching areas. Never pour one cleaner into another bottle or let a product marked disinfectant replace removal of dirt. Reserve real space and time for tools, floors, and fabric to dry.
5. **Check each result before moving on.** Compare the observable finish you set. A still-wet, slippery, product-coated, or damaged area remains held or out of use rather than checked off for convenience. When time ends, record specific unfinished items and reasons for the next round. Wash or air-dry this round's tools and reopen safe paths.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

Every selected job has an actual finish or an explicit stop or next-time destination. Immediate risks are isolated, cleaned areas and tools will not carry soil back, and paths are safe to use. A spotless whole house or forced treatment of unknown materials is not required.

- **Ten tasks fit only two real slots:** Take spreading/slip risks and what must be used today; record the rest rather than use a stronger product to hurry.
- **One cloth is about to go from bath to kitchen:** Stop and change or clean it by its use; visible cleanliness does not remove cross-zone handling.
- **You plan to mop before cleaning dust above:** Reorder when no material reason argues otherwise, but never climb high to preserve a rule.
- **You discover a wet outlet or unknown chemical midway:** Leave ordinary cleaning, isolate, and seek qualified help; a checklist cannot override safety.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This plans one small, identifiable household cleaning round, not a property inspection or hygiene certification. Material, room, weather, shared permissions, and ability vary, so actual dependencies may change order. Use a large-print short list, reminders, reachable zones, or trusted help for vision, memory, or mobility limits. Record unfinished items without creating two competing inventories; each surface task owns its specific completion evidence.
