## 中文

当你已经做好一份**安全状态可确认**的普通酱汁，却发现它对这顿饭来说过稠或过稀时，用这份 Skill 决定要收浓、稀释、用可信方法增稠，还是改变用途。结果是能按预定方式淋、裹或蘸的酱汁，或及时放弃这次补救。这里处理的是**整体浓稀**；油水分层、乳品结块、焦糊和不安全的食物不是同一种故障。

### 准备与输入

先确定酱汁要淋、裹、拌还是蘸，目标是能流动还是能附着；看它在实际**上桌温度**下怎样从勺上流下。确认原配方或材料、当前味道是否已偏咸/酸/甜、还能失去多少体积、可否继续安全加热，以及有无相容液体、可靠的增稠做法、锅和储存空间。备好干净小碗、小勺与适用搅拌器，留一份原样作对照。

若材料、烹调或放置史不明，停止尝味与修复，按当地食品安全指导处理。罐藏、发酵、医疗饮食和未熟食材的安全判断不在这里完成；浓稀不证明熟度或保存安全。

### 执行

1. **先判别故障。** 在可安全尝的阶段搅匀一小份、稍微降温试味，并看它对目标食物是流得太快、黏成团还是恰好。若是可见油水分层、颗粒凝结或焦味，停止套用浓稀方案；分别查成分/分离或决定不用。不要把分层搅成暂时均匀就宣称修好了。
2. **保留原份，小样只改一件事。** 舀出少量到干净碗，记下起始量、味道和流动状态。用另一口原味对照。所有调整先在能放弃的小样里试；原锅暂不继续收汁，也不直接倒整杯水或干粉。
3. **太稠：先试相容液体。** 在小样中逐次加入少量与原配方相容的可食用液体，搅匀后按这道酱汁需要的上桌状态检查流动和味道。水、无盐底液、奶或原配方液体并不通用；选已知适合这一份的材料。需要加热或重新达到安全状态的新增材料按其实际食品要求处理；若味道被稀释到不可用，样本判失败。
4. **太稀：先看能否安全收浓。** 只有酱汁结构和可信做法允许继续加热、味道不已过浓、仍能留够需要的份量时，才在可看守的小锅里试少量、温和蒸发并反复检查。水走掉会同时集中盐、酸、甜等味道。乳化酱、容易分离的乳品或已接近烧干的酱汁，不靠猛火硬收。
5. **收浓不合适时，再核对增稠做法。** 若有针对这份酱汁的可信配方或材料说明，按它的小份增稠方法操作并完成所需加热。例如配方明确用淀粉浆时，先在规定的冷液中分散再入热酱，不能把干粉直接撒入成品。没有适用方法时，跳过增稠；一次性加大量粉不是证据。
6. **用目标用途验收，而非只看挂勺。** 小样实际试淋、裹、拌或蘸，确认味道、稠度、可用量和安全状态都合适；必要时问会吃的人。只有样本合格，才分次调整原酱汁的一部分，边做边检查。若仍不能完成原用途但仍安全，可少量作别的相容搭配；若无人愿意吃或无法安全保存，就停止并丢弃。剩余按当地食品安全指导及时处理。

### 成功条件

你有一份仍安全、味道可接受、在实际上桌状态下能完成这顿饭预定用途的酱汁，并知道新增或剩余份量怎么处理。若调整会使味道、份量、结构或安全失控，明确换用法、换酱或停止也算完成判断。

### 常见报错与补救

- **过稠就整锅兑水：** 停下，在小样里试相容液体；流动变好但味道消失时，不把整锅继续扩大。
- **过稀就一直收汁：** 先查味道和剩余量；过咸、过酸、过甜或快烧干时停热，改用可信的适用增稠方法或改用途。
- **淀粉直接入热锅结粉团：** 停止加粉；只按可靠做法处理可挽回的小量，必要时放弃，不把粉团当“额外浓稠”。
- **刚离火看着合适，上桌却太稠：** 在真正的上桌状态检查小样；只对这份配方允许的酱汁按其方法微调。
- **表面油层或明显结块被误认为太稀：** 这是另一种结构问题，停下查原因；单纯收浓或加粉可能让故障更难处理。
- **食物时间/温度史不清：** 停止口感修复并按当地食品安全指导处理；稠度不是安全检测仪。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

只讨论普通、无酒精、能确认安全的成品酱汁；不同淀粉、酸、糖、脂肪和乳品结构不会按同一个比例响应。若观察流动、握锅或看守热液不方便，缩小样本、选稳定锅具、让可信的人协助，不能安全操作就停止。收浓会减少总量并强化味道；稀释会增加份量与储存任务；增稠可能改变质地。这三者都不免费。

## English

Use this Skill when an ordinary finished sauce is **known to remain safe** but is too thick or too thin for this meal. Decide whether to reduce it, thin it, use a supported thickening method, or change its use. The result is sauce that can pour, coat, or dip as intended, or a timely stop. This handles **overall consistency**. Visible oil-and-water separation, curdling, scorching, and unsafe food are different failures.

### Preparation and inputs

Decide whether the sauce must pour, coat, mix, or dip, and whether it should flow or cling. Observe how it falls from a spoon at its actual **serving state**. Identify the source recipe or ingredients, current salt/sour/sweet strength, how much volume can be lost, whether further heating is safe, and any compatible liquid, supported thickening method, pan, and storage space. Have a clean small bowl, spoon, and suitable stirrer; keep some unchanged sauce for comparison.

If ingredients, cooking, or holding history is uncertain, stop tasting and follow local food-safety guidance. This process does not establish safety for canning, fermentation, medical diets, or undercooked ingredients. Thickness is not proof of doneness or safe storage.

### Execution

1. **Name the actual failure.** At a safe tasting stage, stir a small portion, cool a taste, and check whether it runs too fast, clumps together, or works on the intended food. If there is a distinct oil layer, curdled particles, or burnt flavour, stop applying a simple consistency fix. Find the separate cause or decide not to use it. Briefly stirring a split sauce smooth is not proof of recovery.
2. **Keep the original; change one sample at a time.** Put a small amount in a clean bowl and note its starting quantity, flavour, and flow. Keep another mouthful unchanged. Trial only an amount you can abandon. Do not keep reducing the original or pour in a cup of water or dry powder.
3. **Too thick: trial a compatible liquid.** Add a little edible liquid suited to this recipe to the sample in stages. Mix, then check flow and flavour in the sauce's intended serving state. Water, unsalted base, milk, or the recipe liquid are not universal options; choose what fits this sauce. Handle any addition that needs cooking or a return to a safe serving state according to its actual food requirements. If flavour becomes unusably weak, reject that trial.
4. **Too thin: check whether careful reduction is possible.** Try gentle evaporation in a small pan you can watch only if the sauce's structure and a trusted method permit more heat, its flavour is not already too strong, and enough sauce will remain. Check repeatedly. As water leaves, salt, acid, sweetness, and other flavours become more concentrated. Do not force an emulsion, split-prone dairy sauce, or nearly dry pan to thicken over fierce heat.
5. **If reduction does not fit, check a supported thickening method.** If a trusted recipe or ingredient instruction fits this sauce, follow its small-batch method and required heating. For example, where the recipe specifies a starch slurry, disperse it in the specified cold liquid before stirring it into hot sauce; do not sprinkle dry powder into the finished pan. Without a suitable method, skip thickening. A large amount of flour added at once is not evidence.
6. **Test the intended use, not just a coated spoon.** Actually trial pouring, coating, mixing, or dipping with the sample. Check flavour, consistency, usable amount, and safety; ask the intended diner if useful. Only after the sample works should you adjust part of the original in stages, checking as you go. If the original use fails but the sauce is still safe, offer a small compatible alternative use. If nobody wants it or it cannot be stored safely, stop and discard. Handle leftovers promptly under local guidance.

### Success

You have a safe, acceptable-tasting sauce that works for this meal at its actual serving state, and a destination for added or leftover portions. If adjustment would damage flavour, quantity, structure, or safety, an explicit new use, replacement sauce, or stop is a complete decision.

### Common errors and recovery

- **You water down the whole thick batch:** Stop and trial a compatible liquid in a small sample. If flow improves but flavour disappears, do not scale it up.
- **You endlessly reduce a thin sauce:** Check flavour and remaining amount first. Stop heat if it is already too salty, sour, sweet, or close to scorching. Use a suitable trusted thickening method or change its use.
- **Dry starch forms lumps in the hot pan:** Stop adding powder. Handle only a small recoverable batch under a reliable method, or abandon it. Lumps are not bonus thickness.
- **It looks right off heat but is too thick when served:** Check a sample in its actual serving state, then make only recipe-supported small adjustments.
- **An oil layer or obvious curds are labelled “too thin”:** This is another structural failure. Stop and find its cause; reducing or adding powder may make it harder to handle.
- **Time or temperature history is uncertain:** Stop texture repair and follow local food-safety guidance. Consistency is not a safety meter.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This covers ordinary, alcohol-free finished sauces of known safe condition. Starches, acid, sugar, fat, and dairy do not all respond to one ratio. If observing flow, holding a pan, or watching hot liquid is difficult, shrink the sample, use stable equipment, or ask a trusted person to help. Stop if heat cannot be handled safely. Reduction lowers volume and strengthens flavour; thinning increases volume and storage work; thickening can change texture. None is free.
