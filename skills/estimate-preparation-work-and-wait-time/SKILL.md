## 中文

当你已经选好一项普通非紧急任务，想安排它，却总只估算“真正动手那几分钟”，漏掉找材料、等别人、路上转换和最后检查时加载。目标是**分开估准备、动手、外部等待、转场与收尾，得到可调整的开始和复查时点**。这不诊断拖延或注意力，也不保证准确预言完成时间。决定先做哪一项任务属于另一流程。

### 准备与输入

用一句话写这次做成后能看见什么，例如文件发到正确接收人、普通物品归位或预约得到答复。记真实截止/服务时段、材料与工具在哪、需谁回复或开放、是否要走到另一地点、你自己可能需要的休息/可及准备。没有依据的持续时间写区间或“未知”，不用“通常很快”抹掉等待。

### 执行

1. **按实际阶段列一行时间。** ①准备：取得材料、打开场所/文件；②动手：真正制作或办理；③等待：机器、服务、他人答复；④转场：步行/交通/交接；⑤收尾：检查并把结果放到接收人可用的位置。没有某阶段就删掉，不为填满五格虚构步骤。
2. **给各阶段一个依据和范围。** 有类似任务的真实记录就借来估一个范围；有服务方公开办理/预约窗口就按当期信息；首次任务或外部回复只能标不确定并留更宽余量。把“预计十分钟”和“十分钟后必完”区别写清。
3. **标出能并行与不能并行的等待。** 等文件上传时可做与任务不冲突的轻事，不等于等待从日历消失；等待他人批准后才能继续的部分必须停在依赖点。不要把两个阶段都算整段时间又说总时长是简单相加，也不要假定所有等待都能被其他工作抵消。
4. **从真实截止倒推启动点。** 用截止前必须完成的最后检查、转场和已知办理时段倒推可开始的时点；外部等待放进计划的范围并设复查点。若最晚启动已过，别用“快一点”重写现实，及时缩小可交范围或与负责人协调。
5. **开工后用实际情况更新一次。** 准备比估计久、回复没到或路程变化时，改写剩余阶段和下一复查时刻；有相关人依赖结果就同步真实新预期。任务完成要以实际完成信号核对，而不是计时器响了就关单。

### 完成与停止

你有一张说明各阶段范围、外部依赖、能否并行、计划启动与复查点的短时间表，便完成这次估时。**估算不是完成承诺，计时结束不等于任务成果出现。** 外部服务要求、专业工时或安全条件没有可信信息时，标未知并向负责方求证，不由这份普通估时 Skill 指挥专业作业。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只填“动手十五分钟”：** 补实际取材料、转场、等待和提交后检查。
- **等待被算成零，因为那时可做别的：** 对最终完成仍是依赖，给它一个复查/截止点；可并行工作另列。
- **把准备和转场重复相加：** 看它们是否其实同一段或可重叠，只保留一次真实时间。
- **外部答复一直没来：** 更新状态为待对方，问何时复看或换可行步骤，不填一个猜想的必到时刻。
- **定时器响就报已完成：** 对照最初的实际成果与接收状态，缺了就继续或诚实交接未完部分。

### 假设、替代与副作用

适用于普通可退出的个人或小型协作任务，不做心理、睡眠、医疗、人事法律或专业项目工时判断。活动、设备、交通和服务等待差异很大；纸上五格、简单日历或可读清单都能用，不要求安装效率 App。视觉、行动、语言或注意力条件不同，可按自己的真实准备/休息需要设区间并用可访问提醒，不把差异当失败。副作用是计划可能显得比“做一下”长，但更接近现实。

## English

Load this Skill after choosing one ordinary nonurgent task whose schedule you want to plan, but you usually count only a few minutes of hands-on work and miss setup, replies, travel and final checks. Separate **preparation, active work, external wait, transition and closeout** to choose a start and recheck point you can revise. This does not diagnose procrastination or attention or predict an exact finishing time. Selecting which task to do belongs to another flow.

### Preparation and inputs

Write one sentence describing the observable finish for this task: a file reached the right recipient, an ordinary item was put away or an appointment got a real reply. Note the actual deadline or service window, where materials and tools are, who or what must respond or open, whether you need another location, and your own rest or access preparation. For time without evidence, use a range or “unknown,” not “usually quick.”

### Execution

1. **List the real phases on one line.** Preparation: gather materials and open the place or file. Active work: make or perform the thing. Waiting: machine, service or other person's reply. Transition: travel or handoff. Closeout: check the result and put it where the recipient can use it. Delete absent phases rather than inventing steps to fill five boxes.
2. **Give each a basis and range.** Use a recent comparable task when you have one. For a service's published booking or processing window, check its current information. A first-time task or external reply deserves an unknown or wider range. “Estimated ten minutes” is not “guaranteed done in ten minutes.”
3. **Mark parallel and blocking waits.** You may do an unrelated small action while an upload runs, but the wait still exists on the task's calendar. If another person's approval must arrive before you can continue, mark that dependency. Do not count two overlapping phases twice, and do not assume all waiting can be cancelled out by other work.
4. **Work backward from a real due point.** Allow for the required final check, transition and known service hours before the deadline, then choose a start. Include external-wait uncertainty and a recheck point. If the latest feasible start has passed, do not rewrite reality as “go faster”; reduce what can honestly be delivered or coordinate with the owner.
5. **Update once using actual progress.** For slower preparation, no reply or changed travel, revise the remaining phases and next recheck. If someone relies on the outcome, share the current expected time. Verify the original finish signal before marking complete; a timer ringing is not the deliverable.

### Success and stop

You have a short plan with phase ranges, dependencies, possible overlap, a start and a recheck point, so the estimate is complete. **An estimate is not a completion promise, and elapsed time is not an outcome.** For unknown external rules, specialist labour or safety conditions, mark uncertainty and ask the responsible source rather than directing professional work from this ordinary timing Skill.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Only “fifteen minutes of work” is listed:** Add real material pickup, travel, wait and post-work check.
- **Waiting is zero because you can do something else:** It still blocks completion; give it a review or cutoff point and list any parallel work separately.
- **Preparation and transition are added twice:** Check whether they are the same period or overlap and count actual time once.
- **An external reply never arrives:** Mark waiting on that owner, set the next check or do an independent step rather than inventing a guaranteed reply time.
- **The timer rings and you report complete:** Compare against the original deliverable and recipient state; continue or hand off the open part honestly.

### Assumptions, alternatives and side effects

This covers ordinary exit-able personal or small collaborative tasks, not psychological, sleep, medical, employment-law or professional time estimates. Activities, devices, travel and services vary. A paper five-cell note, simple calendar or readable list works without a productivity app. For sight, mobility, language or attention needs, include your actual setup or rest time and use accessible reminders rather than treating differences as failure. The schedule may look longer than “just do it,” but closer to reality.
