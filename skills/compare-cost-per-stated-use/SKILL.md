## 中文

当两件**已确认适用的普通消耗品**因浓度、形态或建议用量不同，按每升、每千克或每盒比较会误导你时，加载这份 Skill。例如两种适用于同一洗衣任务的洗涤剂，包装给出了可对应的每次用量。这里得到的是**按包装标示剂量计算的每次费用**，不是清洁效果证明，也不是擅自改变剂量的说明。药品、保健品、医疗用品及危险化学品不在范围内。

### 准备与输入

先确认两件商品都适用于你要做的**同一件事**，并找到包装或制造商对这件事所列的用量条件，例如同样的洗衣负载、污渍程度和水质设定。记下本次可用的总价、每包净量、每次标示用量及单位；不要把“最多可用 X 次”直接当成在你条件下的保证。若用途或条件不相同，先核对商品规格，而不是硬算一个赢家。

### 执行步骤

1. **固定一次的定义。** 写明每个候选对应的那次任务是什么；只有条件相近且商品说明都支持时才进入数值比较。两种产品使用方法或效果不同，不会因为都叫“洗涤剂”就自动等价。
2. **读包装本身的剂量。** 对每件商品分别找适合上述条件的用量，不把 A 的瓶盖、浓缩比例或“少量即可”套到 B。说明缺失或条件不同，就停止每次费用排名；实际使用仍须服从各自说明。
3. **统一每件商品内部的单位。** 例如 1 升是 1000 毫升；包装净量除以该商品每次所需毫升数。粉剂用克与克，片剂用片与片，不凭感觉把毫升换成克。若只给包装声称的使用次数，先核对其适用条件并标注“包装声称”，不要伪装成你已实测次数。
4. **算完整次数与费用。** 只数能完成的整次使用，余量另记。例：A 为 1000 毫升、现价 10、每次标示 50 毫升，可完成 20 次，每次 0.50；B 为 750 毫升、现价 9、每次标示 30 毫升，可完成 25 次，每次 0.36。B 按这些标示条件每次少付 0.14，但这个数字没有验证两者实际效果相同。
5. **写清比较的边界。** 记录每件的价、净量、使用条件、剂量、完整次数和每次费用。若包装价差很小而条件或效果无法等价，输出“标示费用可算，实际使用价值不可直接排名”；交由用途核对或一次真实使用记录再决定。

### 完成与停止

你能重算两件商品在**同一使用任务和各自适用标示剂量**下的每次费用，并注明这只是标示条件下的估算，就完成了。缺少可靠剂量、单位不匹配、使用条件不同、商品并不适用时停止数值比较；不通过多倒或少倒来制造更好看的价格。

### 常见报错与补救

- **只比每升或每千克：** 回到每次标示用量；浓度和剂量不同，原包装单位未必反映一次任务的费用。
- **把“最多 X 次”当实测：** 找适用条件；无法核对时仅记为商家声称，不拿它压过可计算的对照。
- **把 26.7 次算成 27 次：** 若只够 26 次完整用量，就按 26 次并记剩余量；下次是否能使用余量另看说明。
- **标签太小或只提供模糊剂量：** 放大照片、查同型号官方可读说明或请店员读出完整条件；仍无用量就暂停。
- **两件产品效果或兼容性不明：** 先核对用途；价钱较低不能修复不适用。

### 假设、替代与副作用

这里仅进行普通商品的购前算术，不教学配制、混合或改变用量。用量可能随设备、任务、污渍和水质改变；不同地区和产品说明也不同。读屏、放大、纸笔、计算器或可信的人朗读包装均可替代小字与心算；数字仍缺失就停止。现实副作用是一瓶看似贵的商品可能每次更便宜，反过来也可能；包装上的“次数”并不是你的实际使用日志。

## English

Load this Skill when two **already suitable ordinary consumables** have different concentrations, forms, or recommended amounts, so a price per litre, kilogram, or pack would mislead you. Two detergents with comparable directions for the same laundry job are one example. The result is a **cost per use at each pack's stated dose**, not proof of equal performance or permission to change the dose. Medicines, supplements, medical products, and hazardous chemicals are outside this Skill.

### Preparation and inputs

First confirm that both products suit the **same job**. Find the pack or maker's dose for comparable conditions, such as the same laundry load, soil level, and water setting. Record the current price, net pack quantity, stated amount per use, and units for each. “Up to X uses” is not a promise for your conditions. If the jobs or conditions differ, settle product suitability before calculating a winner.

### Execution steps

1. **Define one use.** Write down the task each candidate must complete. Rank numbers only when the conditions are comparable and both sets of directions support that task. Sharing the word “detergent” does not prove two products work in the same way.
2. **Read each product's own dose.** Find the amount specified for those conditions. Do not carry A's cap size, concentration, or “a little goes a long way” claim across to B. If the directions are missing or describe different conditions, stop the per-use ranking. Follow each product's own directions in real use.
3. **Match units within each product.** One litre is 1000 millilitres; divide that pack amount by its own stated millilitres per use. Use grams with grams for powder and tablets with tablets for tablets. Do not guess a conversion from millilitres to grams. If only an advertised number of uses is available, check its conditions and label it as a pack claim, not your tested count.
4. **Calculate complete uses and cost.** Count only full stated doses and record any remainder. For example, A has 1000 ml for 10 and states 50 ml per use: 20 uses at 0.50 each. B has 750 ml for 9 and states 30 ml per use: 25 uses at 0.36 each. Under those stated conditions B costs 0.14 less per use; the figures do not establish equal real-world performance.
5. **Record the limit of the comparison.** Keep price, net quantity, task conditions, dose, full uses, and cost per use for each product. If conditions or performance cannot be equated, say “stated costs can be calculated; actual use value cannot be ranked yet.” Check suitability or gather a genuine use record before going further.

### Success and stop

You can recalculate each cost for the **same kind of job at the applicable stated dose** and say that the result is an estimate under labelled conditions. Stop the numerical ranking if reliable doses are missing, units cannot be matched, conditions differ, or a product is unsuitable. Do not pour more or less merely to improve the price on paper.

### Common errors and recovery

- **Comparing only per litre or kilogram:** Return to stated doses. Pack units do not necessarily describe the cost of one task when concentrations or doses differ.
- **Treating “up to X uses” as tested:** Find the qualifying conditions. If you cannot, record a seller claim rather than using it to outrank a calculable alternative.
- **Rounding 26.7 uses up to 27:** If there is enough for only 26 full doses, count 26 and record the remainder. Whether it can be used later depends on the directions.
- **Tiny print or vague dose:** Enlarge a photo, check the exact product's readable maker directions, or ask staff for the complete condition. Pause if the amount remains unknown.
- **Unclear performance or compatibility:** Confirm suitability first. A lower cost does not repair an unsuitable product.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

This is pre-purchase arithmetic for ordinary goods. It does not teach mixing, preparing, or changing doses. The appropriate amount may vary by appliance, task, soil, and water; products and regional directions differ. A screen reader, magnification, paper, calculator, or trusted person's reading can replace tiny text or mental arithmetic. Stop if a key number is still missing. A larger-looking bottle may be cheaper per use, or the reverse; its stated use count is not your own use log.
