## 中文

当一件**已经选好的普通任务**在清单上停了很久，而你现在有一小段可用时间，却仍不知如何真正动手时，加载这份 Skill。目标是完成一个能留下痕迹的第一动作，并在事先设定的停点决定继续或暂停。选“清单里哪件先做”归 H262；这里不诊断拖延、注意力或心理状态，不处理紧急安全任务。

### 准备与输入

说出具体任务，如“给这份普通说明写第一段”，而非“处理所有积压”。确认当下具备开展**一个最小动作**所需的材料、权限、空间与身体条件；缺关键输入时先记缺口并向合适的人/来源求取，不把无依据猜测当开工。给自己一个适合的时间或产出停点，例如“写三句可修改的草稿”或“把一件材料放到工作区”，而不是必须坚持某个固定分钟数。

### 执行

1. **写出第一个能看见的动作。** 把“做报告”改成“打开正确文件并写本轮要回答的一个问题”；把“收拾物品”改成“把桌面上三件可归位的普通物品放回已知位置”。动作必须直接改变任务状态，不能无限准备工具或重排清单。
2. **核它真的此刻可做。** 问“我现在有需要的输入和许可吗？会不会要等别人决定？”若答案是否，就把第一动作改为**获取缺失输入的一次明确请求**，并记等待状态；别继续假装正文可写。物品查找、专门修理或敏感事项转其对应流程。
3. **清出这一次动作的入口。** 放好唯一必要材料、打开实际工作位置，暂时放下无关资料。环境调整只做到足以开始；控制干扰是 H264 的专门任务，不要求任何人靠意志克服可及限制。
4. **执行并留下痕迹。** 真正写三句、完成一项分类、发出一次明确请求或做任务本身的第一步；留一份可回看的稿、记录或状态。若做到一半发现方向错，标出具体卡点，不因不完美删除全部原始进度。
5. **到停点作选择。** 看见初步产出后，按剩余时间和条件选择继续、暂停或等待输入。暂停时留“已做 X、下次从 Y 开始”的短句；中断前更完整的恢复点归 H265。完成第一动作不等于整件事做完。

### 完成、常见问题与补救

你完成了一个确实改变任务状态的第一动作，留下可观察痕迹与下次入口；若关键条件缺失，你发出/记录了获取它的具体动作和等待状态。只把清单改写得更细，且实际任务没有动，不算启动。

- **第一步仍写“开始处理”：** 改成可动手的动词、对象和小产出，例如“给文件写三句开头”。
- **准备工具用掉全部时间：** 保留必需项，直接做一个任务动作；精致工具不是开工凭证。
- **一动手就发现缺资料：** 写下准确缺口和向谁/哪里求，发一次请求后停在待答，不编事实。
- **开头很糟：** 保留可改的草稿并标下一处要修；第一版的工作是存在，不是获奖。
- **疲劳、疼痛或环境不可及：** 改用可用形式或停止；本篇不要求硬撑，不把一次未启动当健康诊断。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

普通任务的第一动作和停点由实际任务决定，可用纸、可访问软件、语音或他人认可的协助。小起步可能帮助看清任务，但不保证之后自动完成，也不替代对重要期限或依赖的正式协调。你手上会多一个真实的未完稿；比只在脑中不断“准备开始”更可检查。

## English

Load this Skill when **one ordinary task is already chosen** and has sat untouched, you have a small usable window now, but still do not know how to actually begin. Complete one first action that leaves evidence, then decide to continue or pause at a preset point. Choosing which task from a long list belongs to H262. This does not diagnose procrastination, attention, or mental health, or handle urgent safety work.

### Preparation and inputs

Name the task concretely, such as “draft the first paragraph of this ordinary note,” rather than “handle all backlog.” Check that the materials, permission, space, and physical conditions for **one smallest action** are available now. If a required input is missing, record it and request it from the right person or source; do not start by inventing facts. Choose a time or output stopping point that fits you, such as “three revisable sentences” or “one item placed in its known work area,” not a universal timer length.

### Execution

1. **Write a visible first action.** Turn “do report” into “open the right file and write one question this round must answer.” Turn “tidy items” into “return three ordinary desk items to their known places.” The action must change task state directly rather than endlessly preparing tools or reshuffling the list.
2. **Check that it can really happen now.** Ask, “Do I have the input and authority? Must I wait for someone else's decision?” If no, make the first action **one clear request for the missing input** and mark waiting. Do not pretend the main draft can proceed. Item-search, specialist repair, and sensitive matters use their own routes.
3. **Open only this action's entry.** Put out the necessary material and open the actual work location, then put unrelated material aside. Adjust the environment only enough to start. Reducing distraction is H264's separate task; no one must overcome an access barrier by force of will.
4. **Do it and leave evidence.** Write the three sentences, finish one categorisation, send the concrete request, or perform the task's first step. Keep a revisitable draft, record, or status. If the direction seems wrong halfway through, mark the specific sticking point instead of deleting all imperfect progress.
5. **Choose at the stop.** With a visible initial result, continue, pause, or wait for input based on remaining time and conditions. On pause, leave “Done X; next start at Y.” A fuller interruption checkpoint belongs to H265. The first action is not the completed whole task.

### Success, common problems, and recovery

You performed one action that actually changed task state, with visible evidence and a next entry. If a prerequisite is missing, you made or recorded the specific action to obtain it and the waiting state. Rewriting a list more finely while the real task remains untouched is not a start.

- **The first step still says “get started”:** Use an actionable verb, object, and small output, such as “write three opening sentences in the file.”
- **Tool preparation consumes the whole window:** Keep only what is necessary and perform one task action. Beautiful tooling is not proof of work.
- **Missing material appears once you start:** Name the exact gap, where or whom to ask, send one request, and pause pending the answer rather than inventing facts.
- **The opening is rough:** Keep a draft you can change and mark the next fix. Version one needs to exist, not win an award.
- **Fatigue, pain, or an inaccessible environment:** Switch to a usable form or stop. This Skill does not require pushing through or treat one stalled attempt as a health diagnosis.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

The first action and stop depend on the actual ordinary task. Paper, accessible software, speech, or agreed assistance may work. A small start can reveal the task but does not guarantee the rest will complete, and it does not replace formal coordination about significant deadlines or dependencies. You may now have a real unfinished draft, which can be checked more easily than a plan to “start soon.”
