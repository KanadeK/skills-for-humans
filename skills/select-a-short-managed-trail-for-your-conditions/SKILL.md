## 中文

当你看见一条名字好听或照片漂亮的步道，却不知道**长度、路面、坡度和返回条件**是否适合自己时，用这份 Skill 在出发前筛一两条管理方公布的短步道，得到可走/暂不走的有据判断。结果是选路而非完成徒步；城市公园内部的普通短散步归另一任务。这里只有公开、维护、短时、容易退出的低风险步道，不进入野外求生或挑战路线。

### 准备与输入

先写你实际可用的总时间、能够接受的路面/坡度/台阶、是否需要无障碍入口或座椅/厕所、同行与通信条件。这里不制定体能指标或医疗建议；你自己最清楚哪些条件可行。只看管理方官方步道页、当前公告和入口地图，不拿第三方照片当实时路况。若步道涉及悬崖、水边、攀爬、无人区或危险天气，直接退出本篇范围，另选容易退出的路径。

### 执行

1. **列出候选的硬条件。** 对每条记官方长度及是否单程/往返/环线、预计时间的计算口径、路面材质、坡度/高差、宽度或障碍（如有说明）、入口、出口和当日开放状态。缺关键数据就标“未知”，不能因为写着“easy/简单”就自动补齐。
2. **和个人限制逐项对照。** 把你不能接受的一项条件标出来，特别看最陡/最窄处而不只看平均值。官方“可及”描述也要读细节；平整入口不保证全线平整。若自己无法判断某处是否可通过，问管理方或选更明确的候选。
3. **检查可退出结构。** 优先明确的短环线或沿原路可返的路，标起点、最晚转身点和最近可回公共区域。只知道总里程而不知道返回方式，不做出发决定。估时应留休息与返程余量，管理方估时不是你个人保证。
4. **核当日变化。** 出发前查封闭、维修、设施和普通天气公告；到入口再对照实际路面、标牌和工作人员指引。与预期不同就不进入或改走另一条明确开放路线，不绕围栏。
5. **写一行决定。** “我选____，因为____条件适合；到____即折返；如果____就取消/退出。”若没有符合条件的步道，写“本轮无合适候选，原因____”，停止比硬选一条更完整。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你能用管理方当前信息解释**为什么这一条短步道适合或不适合自己**，包括至少路面、长度/回程、坡度或障碍、开放状态及退出点。只有照片和评分，没有路况与回程事实，不满足本篇。

- **官方只写“简单”却无路面/坡度细节：** 找详细页、现场联系人或换信息充分的步道，不把标签当可及保证。
- **候选含一段超出你限制的台阶/陡坡：** 选允许的短支段或另选步道；不能用平均坡度抹掉这段。
- **路线图有岔路或似乎只能单程：** 先问管理方出口与返回方式；无法确认就不选。
- **临时封闭或现场湿滑：** 遵守公告并退出，天气改期的更完整决定归 H294，不越过封闭测试。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

管理方对“easy/accessible”的用词和测量方式不同，官方资料也可能落后于现场，因此要看具体数字/文字和最新公告。视力、行动或感官需求可用可及文字/电话、可信同伴或工作人员协助阅读；决定仍以你的实际条件为准。此处不提供医学适宜性、攀登、野外导航或紧急救援建议。最终可能没有可去的步道，这是筛选成功，不是失败。

## English

Use this Skill when a trail name or photograph appeals to you but you do not know whether its **length, surface, slope, and return path** fit you. Screen one or two officially managed short trails before going and make an evidenced go or no-go choice. The result is a trail decision, not a completed hike. An ordinary short path inside a city park is another task. This Skill covers public, maintained, short, easy-to-exit, low-risk trails only—not wilderness or challenge routes.

### Preparation and inputs

Write your real total time, suitable surface, slope and steps, any need for an accessible entrance, seating or restroom, and companion or communication conditions. This is no fitness or medical prescription; you know your own practical limits. Use official trail pages, current notices, and entrance maps, not a third-party scenic photo as current trail condition. If a route requires cliff exposure, water activity, scrambling, remote travel, or hazardous weather, leave this Skill's scope and choose an easy-exit option.

### Execution

1. **List each candidate's hard facts.** Note official length and whether it is one-way, out-and-back, or a loop; what the time estimate includes; surface; grade or elevation change; width or obstructions where supplied; entry, exit, and current open status. Mark missing information “unknown” instead of filling it in from an “easy” label.
2. **Compare with your limits.** Mark any condition you cannot accept, especially a steepest or narrowest part rather than only an average. Read detail behind an official “accessible” label; an even entrance does not make the whole path even. Ask the manager or choose a clearer candidate if you cannot judge a section.
3. **Check the way out.** Prefer a short known loop or a route you can reverse. Mark start, latest turn, and nearest return to a public area. A total distance without a return route is not enough to decide to go. Leave time for breaks and return; a manager's estimate is not your personal promise.
4. **Check day-of changes.** Before travel, check closures, maintenance, facilities, and ordinary weather notices. At the entrance, compare actual surface and signs with your plan and follow staff directions. If conditions differ, do not enter or use another clearly open route; do not bypass a barrier.
5. **Write one decision line.** “I choose ____ because ____ conditions fit; I turn at ____; if ____ I cancel or exit.” If no trail fits, write “no suitable candidate this time because ____.” Stopping is better than forcing a scenic choice.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You can use current manager information to explain **why one short trail does or does not fit you**, covering surface, length and return, slope or barriers, open status, and exit point. Photos and ratings without route and return facts do not meet this task.

- **Only “easy” is published, with no surface or slope:** Find detailed official information, ask the manager, or choose a documented trail; a label is no access guarantee.
- **One stair or steep section exceeds your limit:** Choose an allowed short segment or another trail; an average slope does not erase that section.
- **The map branches or looks one-way:** Confirm the exit and return with the manager before choosing. Do not select an unverified return.
- **A closure or slippery surface appears:** Follow notices and leave. A fuller weather-change decision belongs to H294; never bypass closure to test it.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Managers use “easy” and “accessible” differently and may measure in different ways. Official descriptions can lag real conditions, so read specific details and current notices. For sight, mobility, or sensory needs, use accessible text or phone information, a trusted companion, or staff help to read the facts; your own conditions govern the decision. This Skill gives no medical suitability, climbing, wilderness navigation, or emergency rescue advice. Finding no fitting trail is a successful screening result.
