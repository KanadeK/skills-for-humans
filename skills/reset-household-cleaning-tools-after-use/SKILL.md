## 中文

你刚完成一次普通居室表面清洁，手里留下用过的布、拖布头、刷子、簸箕或桶时，用这份 Skill 把它们按接触区域和材质**收尾到下次可用**。目标是污物被移除、可洗件按说明洗净并晾干、工具归回自己用途的位置；还没干的件明确留在“待干”，不塞进密闭柜里伪装成已完成。这里只处理已知的日常灰尘/普通污水，不处理体液、霉、虫害、尖锐物、危险化学品、电动清洁设备内部，也不把卫浴或食物接触工具混成通用工具。

### 准备与输入

先回想每件工具刚碰过什么：普通地面/家具，还是卫生间、食物接触面，或未知污染物。分开放，特别是后两类不要跟普通居室布一起直接投入清洗。看布或拖布头的洗涤标签及工具说明，确认可拆、能否手洗/机洗、允许的温度与晾干方式。找得到普通污水可按当地规则倒弃的位置、洗涤用品、干净可通风的晾放位。布还在滴水时不要跨过干净储物区搬运；沿途若会造成滑倒，先处理滴水。

若有碎玻璃、针尖、体液、不明强烈气味或无法识别的清洁剂残留，停止普通工具收尾，将工具暂时隔离并查该污染源/产品的专门说明或求助。不要为求“更干净”混合清洁剂或自配消毒液。本 Skill 的清洗和干燥不等于消毒证明。

### 执行

1. **按刚才的用途分组。** 把普通干扫工具、可洗的湿布/拖布头、硬质容器分开；把有专用用途的工具保留在其专属区。一次性纸巾按当地垃圾规则处理，不混进可洗布里。不要把一块刚擦过卫生间的布放进普通家具清洁布堆。
2. **先倒污物，不用手探。** 将簸箕中的普通灰尘倒入合适垃圾容器；用其自带齿梳或另一把可控刷子带走扫帚上的毛发和大颗粒，不用手摸索藏在刷毛里的东西。桶里的已知普通污水按所在地点允许的排水/弃置规则倒掉；桶中有未知或受限制产品时先读标签，不往不明排水处倒。
3. **按材料清洗。** 可洗布和可拆拖布头依其制造商/标签的洗法用洗涤剂洗净；允许机洗就按该件许可的温度，不把别的产品的“热洗”温度照搬。允许手洗而无法机洗时在合适区域单独洗。可水洗的硬质桶、簸箕和合成刷毛，先去可见污物，再按实际材料说明用适合的温和清洁品清洗、冲掉残留。自然纤维刷毛或不可浸水部件按其说明干式清理，不能一律泡水。清洗工具后把用过的水和清洗区按当地与产品要求收尾。
4. **先晾干，再归位。** 按标签摊平或挂起布和拖布头；桶倒置或打开晾干，刷毛离地并通风。不要把湿拖布头装回封闭桶或把湿布折进密闭柜。晾放位不能挡通道、碰电器或被人误作已洗净的可用工具。
5. **标记可用状态。** 目视和触摸工具可安全触及的表面，确认没有明显灰尘、积水、异味或松脱损坏；干燥后按用途归回固定位置。尚未洗、没干或损坏的件保留在“待洗/待干/待更换”，不以一个共同储物箱抹掉区别。最后清洁双手。

### 成功条件

普通居室清洁工具没有留着可见污物或脏水；可洗件按自己的说明清洗且完全干燥才回可用位，专用工具与普通工具分开。若暂时无法清洗或晾干，已清楚隔开放置并知道下一步，才是这次合理的暂停结果；“合上桶盖”不是晾干步骤。

### 常见报错与补救

- **湿布放进密闭桶后有味：** 拿出检查标签，重新按许可洗净并在通风处完全晾干；明显损坏或持续异味时更换，不靠香味遮盖。
- **把所有拖布头按同一个温度机洗：** 暂停，查该头的真实说明；微纤维、棉及带胶件可能不同。已变形时不把它当正常清洁面继续用。
- **自然纤维扫帚被浸湿：** 查制造商的材料护理；无法确认干透时保持离地晾放或更换，不在潮湿密闭处储存。
- **刚擦过不同用途区域的布混在一起：** 分开识别并按各自专用清洗要求处理；无法确定碰过什么的布不要直接归入普通“可用”。
- **桶里还有未知清洁液：** 不加另一种产品、不随意倒入排水口；保持容器稳定，核对原产品标签或咨询当地弃置渠道。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

这里假设工具只接触可识别的普通日常污物，洗涤标签可读，有安全的排水与通风晾放条件。洗衣机并非必需：产品允许手洗时可手洗；不能握持、弯腰或读清标签时，可用长柄工具、放大查看或请可信的人协助。不确定的工具宁可留在明确的待处理位。晾干会占空间和时间；本文不声称家庭工具被医疗级消毒。

## English

Use this Skill after an ordinary room-surface cleaning job leaves used cloths, a mop head, brush, dustpan, or bucket. Reset them **for their next permitted use**: remove dirt, wash washable parts as their own instructions allow, dry fully, and return them to the right purpose-specific place. A damp tool stays in a clear “drying” state instead of becoming mysteriously “ready” inside a closed cupboard. This covers known everyday dust and ordinary dirty water. It excludes body fluids, mould, pests, sharp debris, hazardous chemicals, and electrical cleaner internals. Bathroom and food-contact tools do not become general room tools by sharing a bucket.

### Preparation and inputs

Identify what each tool just touched: ordinary floor or furniture, bathroom or food-contact surface, or something uncertain. Keep special-use and uncertain tools separate from ordinary room cloths. Read each cloth or mop-head label and equipment guide for removal, hand or machine washing, allowed temperature, and drying. Find an appropriate place for ordinary dirty-water disposal, washing supplies, and clean ventilated drying space. Do not carry a dripping tool through a dry storage area; address a slippery drip route first.

If the tool may hold glass, a needle, body fluid, an unidentified strong-smelling substance, or unknown cleaner residue, stop this ordinary reset. Isolate the tool and use instructions appropriate to that source or ask for help. Do not mix cleaners or make an improvised disinfectant to make it “extra clean.” Washing and drying here do not prove disinfection.

### Execution

1. **Sort by recent use.** Separate ordinary dry sweeping tools, washable damp cloths and mop heads, and hard containers. Keep dedicated tools in their own zone. Discard single-use paper under local waste rules, not in washable cloths. A cloth used on a bathroom surface does not join ordinary furniture cloths merely because both are fabric.
2. **Empty debris without probing by hand.** Tip ordinary dust from a dustpan into a suitable waste container. Use a built-in comb or another controllable brush to dislodge hair and large particles from broom bristles; do not feel blindly inside them. Empty known ordinary dirty water where local and product rules permit. For an unknown or restricted cleaning product, read its label before disposal; do not pour it into an unverified drain.
3. **Wash by actual material instructions.** Wash reusable cloths and removable mop heads with detergent by their own labels. Use only a temperature that item permits; another brand's “hot wash” is not a universal setting. If hand washing is permitted and a machine is unavailable, wash separately in a suitable area. For water-washable buckets, dustpans, and synthetic bristles, remove visible dirt first, then use a mild compatible cleaner and rinse residue according to their guides. Clean natural bristles or non-immersible parts dry if their instructions require it. Finish handling wash water and the washing spot under local and product directions.
4. **Dry before storage.** Lay cloths or mop heads flat or hang them as their label permits. Drain and air-dry buckets open or upside down; keep bristles off the floor in ventilated space. Do not refit a wet mop head into a closed bucket or fold a damp cloth into a sealed cupboard. Keep the drying area out of walkways, away from electrical equipment, and distinct from ready-to-use tools.
5. **Mark the ready state.** Visually inspect and safely feel reachable tool surfaces for remaining obvious dirt, pooled water, odor, or loose damage. Once dry, return each item to its assigned-use place. Leave anything unwashed, damp, or damaged clearly in “to wash / to dry / to replace” rather than erasing those states in a shared bin. Clean your hands afterward.

### Success

Ordinary room-cleaning tools hold no visible debris or dirty water. Washable parts were cleaned under their own instructions, and only fully dry items return to ready storage. Dedicated tools remain distinct. When washing or drying is not currently possible, a clearly separated pending tool and a known next action are a valid pause. Closing a bucket lid is not a drying method.

### Common errors and recovery

- **A cloth smells after sitting wet in a closed bucket:** Take it out, check its label, wash again as permitted, and dry fully with airflow. Replace if damaged or persistently odorous rather than masking the smell.
- **Every mop head gets the same machine setting:** Stop and read that head's instructions. Microfiber, cotton, and glued parts can differ. Do not treat a deformed pad as a normal cleaning face.
- **Natural broom bristles were soaked:** Check the maker's material care. Keep it off the floor to dry, or replace it if you cannot confirm it has dried; do not seal it away damp.
- **Cloths from different-purpose areas are mixed:** Identify and separate them under their dedicated care rules. A cloth with uncertain exposure does not go straight to the ordinary ready pile.
- **Unknown cleaner remains in the bucket:** Do not add a second product or pour it into a random drain. Keep the bucket stable and check the original label or local disposal channel.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Tools have touched only identifiable everyday soil, care labels can be read, and a safe wash-water route and ventilated drying place exist. A washing machine is optional when the product permits hand washing. If grip, bending, or label reading is difficult, use a controllable long-handled tool, magnification, or trusted help. Keep uncertain tools in an explicit pending place. Drying takes space and time; this is no claim of healthcare-grade disinfection.
