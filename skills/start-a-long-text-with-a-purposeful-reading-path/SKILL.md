## 中文

当你拿起一本书或一篇长文，却在第一页前就被“要不要从头到尾逐字读完”困住时，用这份 Skill 先定**这次为什么读**，预览结构，再读完一小段实际相关的内容。结果是一条适合这次目的的阅读路径、一个真实读过的段落/章节及下次入口；浏览目录不等于读完，也不要求每种文本都跳着读。

### 准备与输入

确认文本来自你有权使用的纸本、图书馆借阅、开放网页或获准音频/电子版本；不绕过付费访问或复制整章给别人。写一句目的：为兴趣认识故事、找一个具体问题的解释，或为后续练习找材料。记录可用时间和你使用的版次/格式（纸页、章节、网页标题或音频时间码），方便下次定位。若视觉、阅读或注意条件需要大字、读屏、音频、安静空间或更短段落，按实际支持调整；本篇不诊断任何认知或阅读障碍。

### 执行

1. **只预览能帮你选入口的结构。** 看标题、目录或小标题、前言/引言、图表说明和结尾提示（若不介意剧透）；小说等叙事文本可只看封面说明、章节界线而不先读结局。问“哪一段最可能帮我实现本次目的？”预览是一张地图，不是阅读成果。
2. **选适当路径和一小段。** 想体验叙事就从开头按顺序读；为一个明确问题查资料，可先选相关章节，但要补上它引用的必要背景。段落长度按你的时间和文本难度，不设所有人统一页数。写“先读____，若它依赖____再返回”，避免在不知上下文时摘句当结论。
3. **真正读完选定段落。** 慢慢读到一个自然小停点，不只是滑到结尾。读图表时看标题、图例与正文怎样相连；听音频时记章节/时间码。碰到不认识的词先看本段是否给了解释；没解释就简单留待核，别让一个词拖走整段。
4. **用自己的话核一次。** 合上或暂停原文，写一句“这一段主要说____，我在____页/小节找到依据”；重新看文本，确认不是凭印象加的内容。若说不出，回看这一小段或缩到更小一段，不假称理解了整篇。
5. **留下下一入口。** 记“下次从____继续，原因____；还有____没搞懂”。若发现文本根本不回答目的，说明为什么，换一个有权使用的来源或重设目的；不因已经翻了几页就必须读完整本。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你有明确阅读目的、预览后选择的路径，**实际读过一个可指回的段落及自己的主旨句**，并能找到下一入口。只看目录或读了几页却无法说出读到哪里，是准备或时间记录，尚非本篇完成。

- **看目录看很久却没读正文：** 选最可能相关的第一小段，设一个自然停点，开始真正读。
- **专挑结论但不懂它依赖什么：** 回读引言、方法或前一节的必要上下文，不把孤立一句当全文证据。
- **第一段太难：** 缩小长度、换可访问格式或找入门来源；记困难点，不把速度慢归为失败。
- **版本/设备改变找不到位置：** 用章节标题加第一句关键词或音频时间码作入口，别只记“第 14 页”。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

叙事阅读、学术文章和工具书的合适路径不同，本篇让目的决定是否顺读或选读，不把某种阅读法写成普适定律。读屏、听书、触觉资料和可信协助可替代小字纸本，仍要区分实际读到与他人概括。读第一段不保证理解全篇，也不证明引用内容真实；更细的标记/提问、笔记复用和独立练习有自己的任务。

## English

Use this Skill when a book or long article is in front of you but “must I read every word from page one?” prevents you from starting. Decide **why you are reading this time**, preview its structure, and actually finish one relevant segment. The result is a path fitting your purpose, a part you truly read, and a next entry. Scanning contents is not finishing the work, and every text need not be read out of order.

### Preparation and inputs

Use a print copy, library loan, open page, or authorised audio or digital edition you may read. Do not bypass paid access or copy whole chapters for others. Write one purpose: enjoy and follow a story, answer a specific question, or find material for later practice. Note your available time and edition or format (page, chapter, web heading, or audio timestamp) so you can return. Use large print, screen reading, audio, a quiet place, or a shorter segment for your real access and attention needs. This Skill diagnoses no reading or cognitive condition.

### Execution

1. **Preview only enough structure to choose an entry.** Check title, contents or headings, preface or introduction, figure labels, and any ending summary if spoilers do not matter. For a story, you may inspect the cover and chapter boundaries without reading the ending first. Ask which part is likely to serve this purpose. A preview is a map, not reading evidence.
2. **Choose a path and one small segment.** For a narrative experience, begin and follow the story in order. For a defined information question, start with a relevant section but add the context it relies on. Size the segment to your time and text difficulty, not a universal page count. Write “read ____ first; return to ____ if it depends on that,” instead of treating an isolated sentence as the full argument.
3. **Actually read the chosen segment.** Reach a natural small stopping point rather than scroll to its end. For charts, connect title, legend, and prose. For audio, note chapter or timestamp. For an unknown term, first see whether the section explains it; otherwise mark it briefly for later instead of letting one word consume the whole pass.
4. **Check in your own words once.** Close or pause the text and write “this part mainly says ____; the support is at page/section ____.” Reopen to check you did not add something from memory. If you cannot say it, reread this small part or reduce the segment. Do not claim to understand the whole text.
5. **Leave a next entry.** Note “next time start at ____ because ____; still unclear ____.” If the text does not serve your purpose, say why and choose another authorised source or revise the purpose. Turning some pages does not oblige you to finish the whole book.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You have a purpose, a preview-based path, **one truly read and locatable segment with your own main-point sentence**, and a findable next entry. Contents alone or pages read with no record of where you stopped are preparation or elapsed time, not this completed first pass.

- **You keep scanning contents without reading prose:** Select the first likely useful segment, choose a natural stop, and read it.
- **You skim a conclusion but miss what supports it:** Return to necessary introduction, method, or prior section; do not cite one detached sentence as the whole work.
- **The first part is too difficult:** Shrink it, switch accessible format, or find an introductory source. Mark the sticking point rather than treat a slow pace as failure.
- **Page number changes with edition or device:** Use chapter heading plus first-phrase clue or an audio timestamp, not only “page 14.”

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

Stories, scholarly articles, and reference books call for different paths. Purpose decides sequential or selective reading; neither is a universal rule. Screen readers, audiobooks, tactile formats, or trusted help can replace small print while you distinguish what you actually read from someone else's summary. One segment proves neither whole-text understanding nor factual correctness. Detailed annotation, reusable notes, and independent practice have separate tasks.
