## 中文

当你已经有几条阅读/听课笔记，但要写一小段说明、准备一次普通讨论或做下一步时找不到关键内容，用这份 Skill 把它们整理成**服务这一个任务的短简报**。结果是少量可回查的要点、相互矛盾或未核的地方及下一步；不是把原笔记重新誊写得更整齐。H253 负责单条原文定位，H257 负责隔一段时间后检验你是否还记得。

### 准备与输入

写清当前任务与真正读简报的人（可以是下次的你），选至少两条与任务有关且能回原文/活动来源的已有笔记。只使用你有权保留和处理的普通学习资料；含个人敏感信息、他人私语或受保护长段文字时先删去不必要内容，不公开共享。没有来源位置的笔记标“线索待核”，不因写得像引用就当证据。医疗、法律、财务等高风险内容需专业核验，本篇只整理，不能把笔记变成专业意见。

### 执行

1. **按任务筛选，不按原顺序重抄。** 问“这条能帮我完成当前哪一步？”只保留直接有用的两三点；无关好句另放回原笔记。若连一个实际任务都说不出，先写用途，不造“万能知识库”。
2. **给每一点留可回查的出处。** 用“要点—依据所在文本/讲座与位置—我还不确定什么”三栏写简短自己的话。两个来源说法冲突时并列写“来源 A 说____、B 说____”，不静默选你喜欢的一边。
3. **排成读者能用的顺序。** 开头写这份简报要解决的问题，接两三条有出处的要点，结尾写一个未解条件和本次下一动作。若某结论只在特定条件下成立，把条件放在要点旁，而不是丢进脚注。
4. **用实际任务试读。** 暂时只看简报，问“我现在能写那一段、提出那一个问题或选择下一材料吗？我能按线索回原文核对吗？”不能就补缺的来源位置或改成“待核”；不追加十页材料掩盖当前空缺。
5. **保存一个明确版本。** 记任务名、整理日期和要点来源，放到下次能找到的位置；原笔记仍保留，不删真源。任务完成后可归档简报，新的任务应重新筛选，不把这一页到处复用成无条件正确。

### 成功、常见问题与补救

你有一份面向一个任务的短简报，至少两条**可回查的要点**、一处未知/冲突的真实状态和一个下一动作；从它能回到来源。只把原笔记按颜色排版，仍要翻找全部内容才能工作，不满足本篇。

- **笔记很多，简报又写成厚本：** 回到任务，只留最有用的两三条和出处，余下留在原处。
- **来源互相矛盾：** 并列差异与适用条件，标需核实，别平均成不存在的共识。
- **一条漂亮结论没有出处：** 标“待核线索”或撤出简报，不装成已证实事实。
- **换到另一任务时简报不够用：** 重新从原笔记筛选，不给旧简报改个标题当新材料。

### 假设、替代与现实副作用

“一页”表示一眼能用的规模，可是大字纸、可访问文档或语音提纲，不要求固定纸张尺寸。你可能发现笔记缺来源或相互冲突；暴露缺口是有效整理结果。此 Skill不替你确认来源真实性、完成最终写作/决策或保证成绩。听书、屏幕阅读与可信协助可提供不同输入形式，但要保留能回查的真实线索。

## English

Use this Skill when you have several reading or lecture notes but cannot find the useful material for a short explanation, ordinary discussion, or next step. Turn them into **a brief for one real task**: a few traceable points, genuine disagreement or uncertainty, and a next action. This is not recopying the old notes in prettier handwriting. H253 anchors one source passage; H257 later checks what you still retain.

### Preparation and inputs

Name the current task and who will use the brief, even if that person is your future self. Select at least two relevant existing notes with a way back to the original text or event. Use ordinary learning material you may retain and process. Remove unnecessary sensitive data, other people's private words, and long protected excerpts before sharing anything. Label a note without a source location “lead to verify,” not evidence merely because it resembles a citation. Medical, legal, or financial decisions need specialist verification; organising notes does not create professional advice.

### Execution

1. **Filter by task rather than recopy order.** Ask what step each note helps you perform now. Keep only two or three directly useful points; leave interesting but unrelated lines in the originals. If you cannot name a use, name one first instead of creating a general-purpose knowledge pile.
2. **Attach a return route to each point.** In your own short words use “point—source and location—what remains uncertain.” For conflict, write “source A says ____; B says ____” rather than silently pick the one you prefer.
3. **Order for the reader.** Start with the problem the brief serves, follow with two or three sourced points, and end with one open condition and one action. Put a point's condition next to it instead of burying it in a footnote.
4. **Try it on the real task.** Look only at the brief. Can you draft the intended paragraph, pose the intended question, or choose the next material? Can you reopen a source from the locator? If not, repair the locator or mark “to verify” rather than add ten pages to conceal a gap.
5. **Save one clear version.** Note task name, date compiled, and source trail and put it where you can find it. Keep original notes as the true source. Archive the brief after the task; a new task needs fresh selection rather than a retitled sheet assumed correct everywhere.

### Success, common errors, and recovery

You have a short brief for one task with at least two **source-retrievable points**, a visible unknown or conflict state, and one next action. You can return to the sources. Reformatting by colour while still searching all the original notes does not meet this result.

- **There are many notes and the brief becomes a book:** Return to the task and keep the two or three most useful points and sources, leaving the rest original.
- **Sources conflict:** Show both claims and conditions and mark verification needed rather than invent a compromise consensus.
- **An attractive claim has no source:** Mark it as a lead or remove it, not an established fact.
- **The next task needs different information:** Refilter originals instead of changing only the old brief's title.

### Assumptions, alternatives, and side effects

“One page” means usable at a glance; use large print, an accessible document, or a spoken outline as needed, with no fixed paper size. Organising may reveal missing sources and contradictions; exposing them is useful. This Skill does not validate all source facts, write the final deliverable, decide high-stakes action, or guarantee a grade. Audio, screen reading, and trusted help can change input format while preserving real return paths.
